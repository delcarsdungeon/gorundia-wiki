## South Desalle

_South Desalle Village; the Model Village_

> [!quote] “A real village has mud where people walk, smoke where people cook, and noise where children live. That place has none of it unless someone important is watching.”  
> - [[Captain Brennic Ashhook]]

South Desalle is an unnervingly immaculate village in the [[Barony of Desalle]], staged as a model settlement and presented to visitors as proof of the late baronial administration’s prosperity, order, and benevolence.

It is too clean.

That is the first thing most observant travelers notice, though many struggle to explain why.

The cottages are oversized. The tavern is oversized. The smiles are too ready. The shoes are too good. The windows are too full of flowers, and the flowers themselves are wrong for the soil and season.

When the Lord Marshal’s host rode through on the **8th of Coldeven, 1231 OR**, South Desalle greeted them with song, cider, sweetcakes, and a performance of village contentment so complete that it became grotesque.

---

## First Impression

At a glance, South Desalle appears prosperous.

The cottages are large, tidy, and well-painted. The roads are swept. The flower boxes are full. The villagers are clean, well-fed, and well-shod. The tavern stands large enough for a coaching inn on a trade road, though the village itself gives little sign of supporting such business honestly.

The people smile.

The people wave.

The people sing when important guests arrive.

This is the problem.

South Desalle does not feel lived in. It feels arranged.

---

## The Lord Marshal’s Passage

When [[Beringer|the Lord Marshal]] and his host passed through South Desalle, the village was ready for them.

The peasants wore coordinated clothing. The men had matching outfits and hats. The women wore matching garments and scarves. The effect was not festive so much as uniformed, as though someone had decided that poverty looked untidy and corrected it.

Villagers lined the way, smiling and singing.

Cider and sweetcakes were offered to the host. The halflings recognized the cakes as days old.

They ate them anyway, because halflings are a practical people and civilization is mostly snacks with consequences.

There were six cows visible in the village: long-haired, highland-style beasts, carefully brushed and presented almost like props. There were no dogs, chickens, goats, sheep, or other common village animals.

No barking.

No clucking.

No goat trying to ruin someone’s life, as gods intended.

The absence was louder than the singing.

---

## Signs of Wrongness

The first halfling scouts who passed through South Desalle noticed something wrong almost immediately, though they initially had trouble articulating it.

The village looked good.

That was the problem.

A real village has disorder. It has patched fences, muddy thresholds, working smells, kitchen smoke, livestock noise, children underfoot, dogs begging, chickens wandering where they please, and tools left where tired hands dropped them.

South Desalle had none of this.

Notably absent were:

- Dogs
- Chickens
- Goats
- Sheep
- A visible granary
- A working mill
- Ordinary crop fields
- Household gardens used for food
- Honest mud
- Visible poverty
- Unrehearsed villagers

The cottages had flower boxes filled with varieties that do not naturally grow in the region. The surrounding fields, instead of showing practical crops, were largely given over to flowers.

The blacksmith’s shop, glimpsed around the corner, was immaculate.

The blacksmith had clean hands.

This detail did not improve anyone’s confidence.

---

## Orchards on the Road to Blackbar

Later, when halfling scouts examined the road ahead toward [[Blackbar Post]], they found immaculate orchards.

The trees were well-kept. The rows were ordered. The scene looked prosperous from the road.

Then they found the fallen fruit.

It had been raked out of sight and left to rot.

To the halfling mind, this was not merely wasteful. It was sinful.

Food left to rot for appearances is the kind of thing that turns a tidy lie into a moral offense. The scouts may not have had the vocabulary for baronial theater, agricultural fraud, or staged prosperity, but they understood perfectly well that good fruit had been hidden rather than gathered.

That told them more than the singing villagers ever could.

---

## Unity Tavern

The village tavern is known as **Unity Tavern**.

It is too large for the village, too clean for its purpose, and too symbolic by half. Its sign bears both a manticore and a serpent, an unsubtle gesture toward [[Wilhelm Vogel]] and Desalle interests.

The tavern’s purpose was not merely hospitality. It was political staging.

South Desalle lies close enough to Vogel influence and [[Blackbar Post]] that its presentation was almost certainly meant to impress men from Blackbar and, by extension, those who wished to be seen as aligned with Gravendar power.

Whether Wilhelm Vogel himself ever gave a thought to South Desalle is doubtful. It is entirely possible he never knew more than that some minor Desalle fool was trying to flatter him with a painted village and free drinks.

The men of Blackbar who had enough status, nerve, or freedom to cross the line likely enjoyed the arrangement well enough.

Free drink has a way of softening political judgment, because men are tragic and ale is persuasive.

---

## Mayor Oswin Bellweather

[[Mayor Oswin Bellweather]] is the appointed civic head of South Desalle.

He is tidy, pleasant, obsequious, and aggressively rehearsed. His boots are clean, his hands are soft, and his smile has the strained polish of a man who has said “we are grateful for the Baron’s generosity” too many times in front of people who were not allowed to disagree.

Bellweather gave away nothing useful when the Lord Marshal’s host passed through. He pressed hospitality, encouraged the host to remain, and presented the village as prosperous, loyal, and content.

His chief talent is not leadership.

It is presentation.

That may have been exactly why he was chosen.

---

## Purpose

South Desalle appears to have functioned as a Potemkin village: a staged settlement used to display false prosperity, obedience, and gratitude under baronial rule.

It may have served several purposes at once:

- Impressing visitors from Desalle’s court
- Reassuring outside observers
- Flattering Vogel-aligned interests near Blackbar
- Hosting men from Blackbar with free food and drink
- Concealing local deprivation elsewhere in the barony
- Providing a controlled “happy village” for inspections
- Creating the appearance that Baron Desalle’s rule was orderly and beloved

Its falseness was not subtle once noticed.

The difficulty was noticing it beneath the flowers, songs, cakes, and smiles.

---

## Common Signs

South Desalle’s staged character can be recognized by several repeating details:

- Villagers dressed in matching “peasant” outfits
- Men wearing coordinated hats
- Women wearing coordinated scarves
- Everyone visibly well-fed and well-shod
- Oversized cottages
- An oversized tavern
- Imported flowers in every cottage box
- Flower fields in place of useful crops
- Brushed display cattle
- No ordinary village animals
- No visible grain infrastructure
- Too much fresh paint
- Too many rehearsed answers
- Too much smiling

The village does not lack life because it is abandoned.

It lacks life because life has been edited out.

---

## Reputation

Among officials friendly to the late Desalle administration, South Desalle was likely described as a “model village.”

Among locals with sense, it was something else.

A lie.

A trap for the eyes.

A painted cottage built over a cracked foundation.

[[Captain Brennic Ashhook]] of [[Ashmire Kilns]] tried to warn the Lord Marshal about the village’s falseness before the matter could be dismissed as peasant grumbling or rural jealousy. He lacked formal proof, but he knew what a real working village looked like.

South Desalle was not one.

---

## The Feeder Villages

South Desalle does not stand alone.

Hidden beyond the pretty roads, flower fields, and staged orchards are three ordinary feeder villages that supply the labor, food, bodies, and silence necessary to maintain the performance.

These villages are not shown to visitors.

They are where the real work happens.

They are where the pigs are kept, the grain is hauled, the mud gathers, the tools break, the shoes wear out, and the people look like peasants because they actually are peasants rather than civic decoration.

South Desalle draws from these settlements as needed, rotating selected villagers into the model village for appearances, inspections, visiting dignitaries, and Blackbar hospitality.

The arrangement is spoken of as “service.”

Most locals have other words for it.

---

## The Village Players

The peasants seen in South Desalle are not always permanent residents.

Many are selected from the feeder villages and brought in for a period of staged residence. While there, they are dressed, cleaned, rehearsed, and assigned roles in the village’s public face.

Women are chosen for what the mayor’s handlers call “earthy beauty”: healthy complexion, strong arms, good teeth, pleasant smiles, and the ability to look wholesome while wearing a scarf selected by someone who has clearly never milked anything angry.

Men are chosen for rugged peasant ideals: broad shoulders, sun-browned faces, steady posture, and the look of honest labor without too much visible misery attached.

The old, sick, visibly maimed, sharp-tongued, ugly, grieving, resentful, drunk, or insufficiently picturesque are kept out of sight.

This does not mean they are absent from the barony.

Only from the performance.

---

## Lines, Songs, and Clean Hands

Those rotated into South Desalle are expected to learn their parts.

They memorize greetings.

They learn songs.

They learn when to wave, when to smile, when to bow, and which questions require cheerful nonsense rather than honest answers.

They are taught which cottages are “theirs,” which tools may be touched, which animals may be mentioned, and which subjects are never to be raised in front of visitors.

They are also required to remain clean.

Hands washed.

Hair bound.

Shoes brushed.

Clothes kept in order.

No smelling like pig shit, stable straw, smoke, sour milk, or actual agricultural life.

Failure means removal from South Desalle and return to the feeder villages, where the work is real, the food is worse, the mud is honest, and nobody makes them sing unless there is a wedding or a body to bury.

---

## Why Some Leave Willingly

The strange truth is that many villagers cycle out willingly.

South Desalle offers better food, better shoes, cleaner clothes, lighter labor, and shelter good enough to impress visiting lords. For some, that should be enough.

For many, it is not.

The place is too watched.

Too false.

Too humiliating.

A person can only smile beside a fake flower box for so long before pig shit starts looking like liberty.

In the feeder villages, life is harder, dirtier, and poorer, but it is at least real. A woman can swear at a goat. A man can complain about rain. Children can be filthy. Dogs can bark. Someone can drop a bucket without worrying whether it damages the Baron’s image of rustic contentment.

South Desalle feeds the body and insults the soul.

Not everyone has the stomach for that trade.

---

## What This Reveals

The feeder villages make South Desalle’s lie more grotesque.

The barony had peasants.

It had farms.

It had livestock.

It had labor, hunger, anger, waste, and ordinary rural life.

All of it was simply pushed out of sight so a prettier version could be staged near the road.

South Desalle is not a village that became false because it prospered.

It is a false village built by stealing pieces from real ones.

## Summary

South Desalle is not merely a fake village.

It is a performance of prosperity.

Its clean roads, matching clothes, imported flowers, brushed cattle, and singing peasants were all part of a carefully managed image meant to reassure, impress, and deceive.

But the illusion failed in the details.

No dogs.

No chickens.

No grain.

No mill.

No real crops.

No honest dirt.

And somewhere beyond the road, good fruit raked out of sight and left to rot.

That was the truth of South Desalle: not hunger openly displayed, but abundance wasted so the lie would look prettier.
