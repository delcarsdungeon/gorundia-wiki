### Mercygate Hold

Mercygate Hold stands behind the Candle Line, where the hill roads bend west toward Three-Crooks Gate and the safer lands of Blackbriar. It is not a castle of honor, nor a prison of cruelty, though it has been called both by men who spent a night inside it. It is a registry fort: a place of ledgers, barred yards, guarded wells, priestly examinations, and questions asked until the answers stop changing.

Every body that comes through the Candle Line passes under Mercygate’s shadow sooner or later. Refugees from the hills. Half-orc families fleeing Dominion chains. Captured slave-soldiers. Human officers taken alive for questioning. Deserters. Looters. Men whose stories are too clean. Men whose hands are too soft. Men who know which direction to look when a Hextorite prayer is spoken.

House Amadis built Mercygate because mercy without vigilance is an open door, and vigilance without mercy is just tyranny with better masonry.

The fort’s Pelorites tend the wounded first, even when the wounded are enemies. The Heironeans take oaths and testimonies. The Pholtines test lies when their gifts are available. The wizards look for charms, hidden marks, false memories, and the little hooks by which the Dominion moves its pieces.

Most half-orcs who arrive in chains or panic are not executed. They are held, questioned, treated, and sorted. Some are sent west under supervision. Some are placed in temple custody. Some, after long proving, earn work, land, or service. But Dominion agents, Hextorite handlers, and oath-bound infiltrators are given no romance. If the proof is clean, the sword is cleaner.

Human prisoners fare better and worse. They are more likely to be kept alive, because they are more likely to know something worth knowing. That knowledge is taken carefully, lawfully when possible, harshly when necessary. Mercygate’s officers are fond of saying that a dead clerk answers no questions.

The soldiers call the place **the Sieve**.

The refugees call it **the Waiting Gate**.

The deserters call it worse.

And in Griefwatch, where the reports are read by candlelight under the skull above the hall, Mercygate is regarded as unpleasant, necessary, and proof that House Amadis still knows the difference between an enemy, a victim, and a fool who picked the wrong road.

## **113.046 — Mercygate Hold**

Formal name: **The Crown Registry and Holding Fort at Mercygate**  
Common name: **Mercygate**  
Soldier name: **The Sieve**  
Ugly nickname: **Halfway Hell**

It sits west/northwest of **Crookhorn Hold** and north of the Three-Crooks route, making it the natural place where survivors, captives, deserters, rescued slaves, and suspicious strays are marched after the Candle Line catches them. Crookhorn and Dead Lance send people there; Three-Crooks Gate can reinforce it; Griefwatch gets the reports.

It should not be on the front line. Its whole purpose is to **sort bodies after the alarm**, not become another glorious last stand because some clerk with a sword got ambitious.

## What Mercygate looks like

Mercygate is a low, ugly stone fort with too many gates and not enough trust. It has three outer yards divided by palisades, each with separate wells, latrines, and covered holding sheds. The inner keep contains records, interrogation rooms, a chapel of the Triad, mage cells, a small infirmary, and a gallows that everyone pretends is only there for deserters.

The architecture is brutally practical:

- **Refugee yard** for families, camp followers, escaped slaves, wounded noncombatants.
- **Iron yard** for armed captives, Dominion troops, suspected infiltrators.
- **Ash yard** for deserters, looters, bandits, and “unclassified scum.”
- **Priest house** for Pelorite healing, Heironean oath-taking, and Pholtine truth-work.
- **Mage lockroom** for scry-guards, spell checks, and controlled interrogations.
- **Record hall** where names, scars, brands, languages, clan marks, and route stories are copied into ledgers.

It is not kind. But it is not mindlessly cruel either. That distinction matters to Amadis, because without it, they are just Dominion with better wine.

## How fleeing half-orcs are treated

This is where Blackbriar gets ugly-but-interesting.

Half-orcs fleeing through the Candle Line are **not automatically put to the sword**. House Amadis knows too well that the Dominion uses half-orc slave-soldiers, forced auxiliaries, camp-bred laborers, and terrorized families. Killing every half-orc who runs west would be both evil and strategically stupid.

But they are not waved through with a sandwich and a “best of luck, tusky.” They are seized, disarmed, searched, separated by group, and marched under guard to Mercygate.

The basic policy:

> **Fleeing half-orcs are treated as dangerous refugees until processed. Armed half-orcs are treated as combatants until proven otherwise. Marked Dominion handlers, infiltrators, and oath-bound agents are executed.**

At Mercygate, priests and wizards begin the filtering.

### The screening process

First comes **disarmament and separation**. Families stay visible to one another when possible, but fighting-age adults are separated from unknown companions. Anyone with Hextorite brands, Dominion unit marks, priest scars, handler tokens, coded knots, or suspiciously clean stories gets pulled aside.

Then comes **triage**:

- Pelorites check wounds, malnutrition, abuse, and coercive drugs.
- Heironeans take sworn statements and test courage under oath.
- Pholtines conduct truth-reading where available.
- Wizards check for charms, compulsions, disguise, memory tampering, and courier-marks.

The key is time. A fresh arrival is unreliable. A starving escaped slave may lie because panic has eaten half his brain. A Dominion plant may tell the truth about small things and hide the knife in the one detail that matters.

So most half-orc refugees are held for days or weeks, not hours.

### Outcomes

**Cleared refugees** are moved west under escort, usually to labor camps, road crews, temple farms, or sponsored settlements where they can be watched without being caged forever.

**Former slave-soldiers** are a harder case. If they were coerced and surrender cleanly, they may be placed into bonded labor, military parole, or temple-supervised resettlement. Some eventually become scouts, laborers, or even soldiers under close sponsorship. Amadis is harsh, not wasteful.

**Spies dumb enough to get caught** are usually executed quickly after confirmation. The logic is simple: by the time someone gets caught at Mercygate, they are either bad at spywork or disposable. The useful ones will not be in the net. A new Kursk would already have a clean story, a false trauma, and a dead man’s name sewn into his coat.

So yes: the intel value is usually low. The point is not to squeeze them for grand secrets. The point is to prevent a knife reaching the villages.

## Human prisoners

Human Dominion prisoners are treated differently because they are more likely to be officers, handlers, priests, logisticians, oath-collectors, engineers, or literate agents.

They are captured if possible.

Not because Amadis is gentle. Because human prisoners are useful.

A captured Hextorite priest, clerk, engineer, or field officer might know:

- which house sponsored the push
- which Dominion ministry signed the paper
- whether the Synod or Secret Chancery was involved
- where supply caches sit
- which “orc horde” is actually being steered
- whether another wave is moving behind the first

So human prisoners go into the **Iron House** inside Mercygate: stone cells, no shared yards, no unsupervised contact, priest-warded doors, and interrogations by soldiers who do not confuse politeness with mercy.

Hextorite priests are gagged, bound, watched by clergy, and treated like loaded crossbows with opinions.

## Deserters and “other scum”

Mercygate also houses the less glorious garbage of wartime:

- deserters from Amadis or allied service
- looters caught behind the Candle Line
- camp followers accused of spying
- black-market suppliers
- false refugees
- captured bandits
- soldiers who abandoned a post before the beacon was lit

This gives Mercygate its nasty reputation. A starving half-orc mother, a captured Dominion clerk, an Amadis deserter, and a sheep thief may all pass through the same gates. The system sorts them eventually. The first few days, they all hear the same bars close.
