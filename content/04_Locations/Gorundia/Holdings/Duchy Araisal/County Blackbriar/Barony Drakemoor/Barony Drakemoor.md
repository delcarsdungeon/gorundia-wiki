# Barony Drakemoor

> “Blackbriar gives you wine, horses, and sunshine enough to make a bard unbearable. Drakemoor gives you fog, cold water, and the sense to keep your mouth shut after dark.”
> — [[Sorek Redmarch]]

## Overview

**Barony Drakemoor** is a baronial holding of [[County Blackbriar]] in the [[Duchy of Araisal]], ruled by [[House Draal]] from [[Drakemoor Keep]]. It lies within the Blackbriar sphere but does not share Blackbriar’s famous vineyard brightness. Where the county’s heartlands are known for rich thorned wines and fine horseflesh, Drakemoor is lake country and moor country: wet, old, watchful, and slow to forgive careless steps.

The barony is known for its **deep lakes** and **reputedly haunted moors**. Its roads are often raised causeways over marshy ground, its villages cluster near water and stone, and its old watchtowers still stare across the fog as if expecting something to come back.

Drakemoor is not poor, but neither is it showy. It is useful land, hard land, and old land.

## Place in Araisal

Drakemoor belongs to [[County Blackbriar]], under [[Count Alistair Amadis]]. Blackbriar itself is one of the notable counties of [[Duchy of Araisal]], famed for its vineyards and destrier breeding. Drakemoor forms one of its colder, stranger edges.

The barony’s ties to [[House Amadis]] are important. The Draals are not great lords in their own right, but they are stable, respectable, and woven into the Blackbriar order. A Draal’s word carries weight because they do not spend it cheaply.

## The Land

Drakemoor is defined by water and fog.

Its lakes are deep, dark, and cold, with shorelines broken by reeds, stones, and little fishing hamlets. Its moors stretch between them in long sweeps of grass, heather, peat, and standing mist. In summer, the land can be beautiful in a grim, silver-green way. In winter, it becomes the sort of place where even the wind sounds like it has unfinished business.

Common features of the barony include:

- deep black lakes
- reed beds and fishing villages
- peat marshes
- sheep-grazing uplands
- raised causeways
- old stone road markers
- half-sunken barrows
- lonely watchtowers
- moor ponies and hardy sheep
- marsh herbs and medicinal plants
- lights seen over bogs, not all of them natural

Locals do not laugh off the stories about the moors. They sort them into categories: ghosts, smugglers, marsh lights, old ward-stones, drunk shepherds, and things better left unnamed. This is not superstition, they will tell you. This is bookkeeping.

## Drakemoor Keep

[[Drakemoor Keep]] stands on a rocky rise overlooking one of the barony’s deeper lakes. It is not a decorative castle. It is a hard stone house built to endure wet weather, bad roads, and worse neighbors.

The keep likely includes:

- a lake-facing curtain wall
- a guarded causeway approach
- slate roofs
- a beacon tower visible through fog
- a boathouse or lake gate below the keep
- cold storage cellars cut into rock
- warm guest halls with heavy hearths
- a chapel with votive lamps for the drowned
- stables for moor horses and household mounts

The mood of Drakemoor Keep is quiet, severe, and practical. A guest is fed, warmed, and watched. Hospitality is real. So is caution.

## Economy

Drakemoor’s wealth is steadier than it is glamorous.

The barony draws income from:

- lake fishing rights
- peat cutting
- sheep and wool
- causeway tolls
- ferry and lake transport
- hunting rights
- marsh herbs and simples
- limited vineyard land on drier southern slopes
- trade service between Blackbriar proper and neighboring routes

Wine remains culturally important because of Blackbriar’s influence. Baron Henric Draal is known to praise Blackbriar’s wine, and that is not merely table talk; it reflects the pride of the county as a whole.

Still, no one mistakes Drakemoor for the vineyard heartland. Blackbriar sells warmth in a bottle. Drakemoor sells passage, wool, fish, peat, and the quiet promise that its roads will still be there after the fog lifts.

## Military Character

Drakemoor’s fighting strength is shaped by the land.

The barony is unlikely to field the grandest heavy cavalry in Araisal, but it produces hard practical troops suited to difficult ground.

Likely forces include:

- causeway guards
- lake patrols
- moor scouts
- archers used to fog and uneven terrain
- light cavalry on hardy moor horses
- household knights trained for defensive fighting
- boatmen and marsh guides useful during war

Drakemoor does not fight like a hammer. It fights like wet ground under your boots and an arrow from somewhere you cannot see. Invaders tend to become lost, tired, damp, and deeply unpopular with the locals.

## House Draal

[[House Draal]] is an old baronial house of Blackbriar: respectable, controlled, and more severe than splendid. The Draals are not famous for pageantry. They are famous for keeping order, holding their ground, and remembering what was said.

Known members include:

- [[Henric Draal]] — Baron of Drakemoor
- [[Elira Draal]] — Baroness of Drakemoor
- [[Lonric Draal]] — heir to Drakemoor
- [[Yselle Tarnen]] — wife of [[Lonric Draal]]
- [[Mariette Draal]] — daughter of the Baron and Baroness

House Draal’s manners are formal, but not fragile. Their hospitality is sincere. Their silences are not empty.

## Baron Henric Draal

[[Henric Draal]] is the current Baron of Drakemoor. He is a controlled and honorable nobleman, old enough to understand scandal, duty, and the difference between anger and judgment.

He likely knows [[Beringer]] through the Blackbriar and [[House Amadis]] connection, since Beringer was raised under [[Count Alistair Amadis]]. This gives Henric’s dealings with Beringer a more personal edge than a simple petition to the Lord Marshal. Henric may address the office in public, but in private he remembers the man.

A useful read on Henric:

> Baron Henric Draal is the kind of man who asks for law because he knows exactly what he would do without it.

## Baroness Elira Draal

[[Elira Draal]] is the Baroness of Drakemoor. If Henric is restrained steel, Elira is a quiet hand on the blade before it leaves the scabbard. She is likely the keeper of household memory, kinship obligations, guest politics, and all the small truths men often mistake for domestic details.

She should feel composed rather than soft.

Suggested table voice:

> “My husband wants names. I want order. Names can be added after.”

## Culture and Customs

Drakemoor culture is shaped by cold water, old roads, and the knowledge that fog hides more than weather.

Common traits include:

- formal hospitality
- respect for oaths
- strong family memory
- careful speech
- practical superstition
- dislike of waste
- patience with weather, not with fools
- lake and moor funerary customs
- votive lamps for the drowned
- deep respect for guides, ferrymen, and causeway guards

Drakemoor folk are not quick to panic. Panic wastes breath, and breath is useful when the road vanishes under fog.

## Rumors and Local Sayings

- “The moor keeps what the lake refuses.”
- “Never follow a light that answers your name.”
- “Drakemoor roads are honest. They tell you plainly they mean to kill you.”
- “A Draal may forgive late. They do not forget early.”
- “If the fog lifts too quickly, someone paid for it.”
- “Count your companions before the causeway. Count them again after.”

## Use in Play

Drakemoor is useful as:

- a grounded Blackbriar barony with strong [[House Amadis]] ties
- a moody lake-and-moor contrast to vineyard-rich Blackbriar
- a place for private noble meetings away from bright court settings
- a source of disciplined, restrained noble pressure
- a barony where old law, family honor, and local superstition overlap

Drakemoor should feel watchful rather than gothic-for-show. The ghosts may be real, but the living are usually the ones keeping accounts.
