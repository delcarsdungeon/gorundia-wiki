# County Blackbriar

> [!quote] [[Sorek Redmarch]]
> “Blackbriar looks gentle until you notice every vineyard road can move cavalry, every wool-house has a bow rack, and every old shepherd knows which hill burns first when the horns sound. Amadis folk smile with their teeth covered.”

![[Blackbriar Proper.png]]

## Overview

**County Blackbriar** is the hereditary county of [[House Amadis]], seated within the [[Duchy of Araisal]] under [[House Allaric]]. It is one of the most cultivated and quietly wealthy counties in western Gorundia: a land of thorned vineyards, horse pastures, sheepfolds, low ridges, managed woods, old watch-roads, and grim eastern fortifications.

Blackbriar has two faces.

The western face is civilized: wine, horses, old estates, mills, ledgers, chapel bells, and formal hospitality.

The eastern face is awake: [[Griefwatch Castle]], [[Mercygate Hold]], [[Crookhorn Hold]], and the [[Candle Line]]. That side of the county watches the border, receives the frightened, sorts the dangerous from the desperate, and remembers every old invasion corridor by name.

Blackbriar is not rich in the blunt Vogel sense. It has no great dwarven mine, no brutal toll-engine, no serf-choked grain basin. Its wealth is layered: wine, horses, wool, waterworks, small market communities, skilled freemen, and old legal privileges preserved by generations of Amadis counts.

The county’s reputation is civilized, chivalric, and somewhat severe. Its people are polite, but their roads, watchtowers, and ledgers all suggest that politeness is not the same thing as softness.

**House:** [[House Amadis]]  
**Tier:** Count

**Formal Seat:** [[Blackbriar Court]] / Blackbriar Castle  
**Usual Seat of Rule:** [[Griefwatch Castle]]  
**Direct Demesne:** 3 thirty-mile hexes  
**Vassal Baronies:** [[Barony Drakemoor]], [[Barony Ashwyn]]  
**Heir:** Alistair Amadis the Younger  
**Castellan of Blackbriar Castle:** [[Sir Edran Valehart]]

Although [[Blackbriar Court]] remains the formal hereditary seat and ceremonial heart of the county, Count Alistair usually rules and manages the demesne from [[Griefwatch Castle]]. This is not mere preference. It is policy.

Griefwatch gives House Amadis constant eyes on the eastern border, the old invasion corridors, Mercygate, the Candle Line, and the hard military roads feeding toward Ashwyn, Crookhorn, and the Orenic frontier. Blackbriar’s western estates may hold much of the county’s population and wealth, but Griefwatch holds the count’s attention.

Blackbriar’s wine grows in the west.

Its decisions are made in the east.

## Sir Edran Valehart, Castellan of Blackbriar Castle

[[Sir Edran Valehart]] holds Blackbriar Castle in the count’s absence. He is an older household knight of good family, not high enough to threaten the succession and not foolish enough to wish he were. He has served House Amadis for decades as steward-at-arms, host, records witness, and master of the formal household.

His duties include:

- maintaining Blackbriar Castle and its household staff
- receiving lesser guests when the count is absent
- preparing the castle for formal visits, feasts, marriage negotiations, and county courts
- preserving the wine ledgers, horse-breeding records, and old charters
- coordinating with Griefwatch when noble guests or civil delegations require the count’s attention
- ensuring visiting dignitaries feel honored without mistaking hospitality for access

Sir Edran is courteous, dry, exacting, and nearly impossible to hurry. He dresses well, keeps the castle immaculate, and has the unnerving ability to remember where every guest sat at dinner fifteen years ago.

During times of peace, Count Alistair relocates to Blackbriar Castle for major meetings, high-status guests, county ceremonies, marriage negotiations, and formal court business. He never remains long. After a week or two, sometimes less, he returns east to Griefwatch.

Sir Edran has never objected to this arrangement.

He likes clean halls, manageable nobles, and not being responsible for the border.

The direct Blackbriar demesne is **3 hexes**, not the full 7-hex Amadis sphere. Drakemoor and Ashwyn owe fealty, military service, toll coordination, and legal deference to the Count of Blackbriar, but they retain their own courts, officers, and local rights.

This distinction matters. The map, the tax rolls, and the local reeves all agree, which is a rare and suspicious miracle.

## Territorial Clarity

County Blackbriar is the overlord county of the Amadis sphere, but the Count of Blackbriar does not directly hold every settlement commonly associated with Amadis lands.

The **direct Blackbriar demesne** is the count’s personal territory: the western interior estates, the rugged eastern Griefwatch country, Mercygate, Crookhorn, the Whitefields, and the Candle Line.

The wider Amadis sphere also includes two vassal baronies:

- [[Barony Drakemoor]]
- [[Barony Ashwyn]]

Many road sites, market villages, mills, and wool settlements that serve the Amadis military system are legally baronial holdings rather than direct county land.

In particular:

- [[Hearthmere]], [[Loomford]], [[Larkspur Weir]], [[Ridgewool Watch]], and [[Three-Crooks Gate]] belong to [[Barony Ashwyn]].
- [[Griefwatch Castle]], [[Mercygate Hold]], [[Crookhorn Hold]], the [[Whitefield Estates]], and the [[Candle Line]] belong to the direct Blackbriar demesne.

## Demographics

County Blackbriar’s direct demesne covers **3 thirty-mile hexes**, with an approximate population of **101,002**. The population is not evenly spread.

The western clear country holds most of the people: vineyards, horse farms, mills, market villages, old estates, and the softer administrative life of Blackbriar. The eastern hexes are much rougher, dominated by uplands, military roads, watch posts, patrol ranges, Griefwatch’s command zone, and the border apparatus.

The west has villagers.

The east has soldiers, goats, signal fires, and people who think “comfortable” means “the wind only cut one cheek today.”

## Geographic Logic

The direct Blackbriar demesne should be understood as three unevenly populated hex-regions.

### Western Clear Hex

The settled heart of the direct demesne: vineyards, horse farms, mills, Blackbriar Court, market villages, and the densest population.

### Central Upland Hex

Transitional country: sheepfolds, ridge roads, patrol hamlets, smaller fortified settlements, and the roads that begin climbing toward the harsher eastern lands.

### Eastern Griefwatch Hex

Rugged border country: [[Griefwatch Castle]], Candle Line command roads, Mercygate access, scattered shepherds, military households, and low civilian density.

[[Barony Drakemoor]] and [[Barony Ashwyn]] are not part of the direct demesne, but they are vassal baronies under Blackbriar. Their two hexes each remain administratively distinct while still forming part of the wider Amadis military and political sphere.

## Character of the Land

Blackbriar sits where cultivated Araisal begins to meet rougher hill, moor, and forest country. Its best lands are not broad grain plains but **managed slopes, vineyard ridges, sheep pastures, horse meadows, and fortified road country**.

The land is organized, but not sterile. Hedgerows are thick with blackthorn and briar. Old elven boundary stones stand beside newer Oeridian mile markers. The roads are better than they need to be for trade alone, because in Blackbriar a road is also a cavalry lane, messenger route, supply line, and oath-path.

The county’s pride rests on five pillars:

- **Blackbriar wine**, especially thorn-vine reds and late-harvest white varietals.
- **Amadis destriers**, elegant, intelligent, and endurance-bred.
- **Fine wool and dyed cloth**, much of it tied to Ashwyn’s baronial economy.
- **Lawful road culture**, with tolls, patrols, wayhouses, and witness-stones maintained properly.
- **Border vigilance**, centered on Griefwatch, Mercygate, Crookhorn, and the Candle Line.

## Major Settlements and Sites

### Blackbriar Court

**Hex:** 107.040  
**Type:** Formal county seat / noble estate / castle-town  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar / [[House Amadis]]  
**Role:** Ceremonial seat, archives, household administration, noble hospitality  
**Held by:** [[Sir Edran Valehart]], Castellan of Blackbriar Castle

Blackbriar Court, often simply called Blackbriar Castle by outsiders, is the formal seat of [[House Amadis]]. It is where charters are sealed, marriages are hosted, wine is praised with suspicious seriousness, and visiting nobles are reminded that House Amadis is old, honorable, and very expensive to insult.

It remains the ceremonial heart of the county. The archives, household officers, wine ledgers, breeding records, and old family chapels are maintained here.

During times of peace, Count Alistair relocates here for formal court sessions, important guests, diplomatic meetings, feasts, marriages, and county ceremonies. These stays are brief. The household knows the rhythm: polish the silver, receive the guests, host the court, resolve the matter, watch the count ride east again.

Sir Edran Valehart holds the castle in his absence and keeps the formal face of House Amadis intact.

It is a thankless job, so naturally he is excellent at it.

### Fairbriar Fields

**Hex:** 106.039  
**Type:** Vineyard hamlet / estate country  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar  
**Role:** Vineyards, tenant farms, western estate supply

Fairbriar Fields lies in the softer western part of the direct demesne, where the land opens into vineyards, small farms, hedgerows, and low manor estates. It is not a great town, but a cluster of villages and noble leases that supply Blackbriar Court with wine, grain, fruit, poultry, and seasonal labor.

This is the Blackbriar most visitors remember: clean roads, orderly vines, stone walls, watchful reeves, and peasants who have learned to be polite without ever being unwary.

### Thornmere

**Hex:** 106.041  
**Type:** Wetland village / fishpond settlement  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar  
**Role:** Fishponds, reeds, kitchen gardens, herbs, waterfowl

Thornmere is a fen-edge village built around fishponds, reed beds, duck ponds, and kitchen gardens. It supplies the western estates with fish, herbs, rushes, eggs, preserved waterfowl, and seasonal marsh hay.

The village is quieter than the vineyard hamlets and less polished than Blackbriar Court, but its people know every wet path, sinkhole, reed channel, and old boundary ditch in the area.

If someone disappears near Thornmere, the locals will usually know whether the marsh took them, a person took them, or they were stupid enough to confuse the two.

### Greyfen Crofts

**Hex:** 107.043  
**Type:** Dispersed croft village / fen-edge settlement  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar  
**Role:** Peat cutting, sheep, marsh hay, scout families

Greyfen Crofts is not one village so much as a spread of crofts, sheepfolds, peat sheds, fenced gardens, and family towers along the edge of rougher wet ground. The people here are poorer than the vineyard folk and harder to impress.

Greyfen produces peat, wool, marsh hay, tough ponies, and stubborn scouts. It also produces rumors: lights over the fen, old elven stones under the reeds, lost riders, drowned bells, and figures moving where no sane person would walk after dusk.

### Briarford

**Hex:** 108.044  
**Type:** Road village / small bridge settlement  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar  
**Role:** Road crossing, local trade, messenger route

Briarford is a practical crossing settlement linking the western estates to the upland roads. It has a stone bridge, a toll-house, a shrine, a smithy, and enough inns to suggest that more people pass through than stay.

The village is not glamorous, but everyone uses it: merchants, drovers, riders, reeves, messengers, minor nobles, and patrols moving east toward Griefwatch or west toward Blackbriar Court.

Briarford’s people are proud of being useful, which is one of the less obnoxious forms of civic pride.

### Mournridge Steadings

**Hex:** 111.044  
**Type:** Scattered fortified farms / upland holdings  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar  
**Role:** Sheep, goats, hardy grain, family towers, patrol support

Mournridge is a thinly populated upland district of fortified farms, shepherd steadings, goat paths, stone walls, and old watch cairns. The families here live close to the border system and know what it means when horns carry over the hills.

Most homes are built stronger than comfort requires. Many have cellar stores, arrow slits, roof bells, or small family towers. The people are not unfriendly, but they do not ask strangers to stay long.

Mournridge is where Blackbriar stops looking prosperous and starts looking prepared.

### Saint Orveth’s Whitefield

**Hex:** 112.045  
**Type:** Pholtine agricultural colony  
**Jurisdiction:** Pholtine ecclesiastical administration under county tolerance  
**Role:** Main receiving estate from Mercygate

Saint Orveth’s Whitefield is the first and most watched of the Whitefield estates. Refugees cleared at [[Mercygate Hold]] often pass through here before being assigned to smaller farms or work details. It has the largest chapel, the most ledgers, and the highest concentration of clerks who believe handwriting is a moral virtue.

The estate is clean, whitewashed, orderly, and deeply supervised. Rows are straight. Dormitories are counted. Meals are plain but regular. Chapel bells mark the day.

For some, it is safety.

For others, it is a ledger with walls.

### Mercy Furrow

**Hex:** 112.046  
**Type:** Whitefield estate / road-labor colony  
**Jurisdiction:** Pholtine ecclesiastical administration under county tolerance  
**Role:** Road crews, ditching, wagon labor, supply work

Mercy Furrow is harsher than Saint Orveth’s. It supports the Mercygate-Griefwatch-Crookhorn road network through ditching crews, wagon labor, field work, stock handling, and road repair.

It receives more former slave-soldiers and fighting-age adults than the gentler estates. The discipline is harder, the labor heavier, and the sermons less forgiving.

Its people are also among the most likely to become future scouts, drovers, wardens, informants, or trouble. Sometimes all five.

### Mercygate Hold

**Hex:** 113.046  
**Type:** Registry fort / holding fort / refugee and prisoner screening site  
**Formal Name:** The Crown Registry and Holding Fort at Mercygate  
**Common Name:** [[Mercygate Hold]]  
**Soldier Name:** The Sieve  
**Ugly Nickname:** Halfway Hell  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar / Crown-recognized registry authority  
**Role:** Sorting refugees, captives, deserters, rescued slaves, and suspicious strays from the Candle Line

[[Mercygate Hold]] stands behind the Candle Line, where the hill roads bend west toward the safer lands of Blackbriar and the Ashwyn routes. It is not a castle of honor, nor a prison of cruelty, though it has been called both by men who spent a night inside it.

It is a registry fort: a place of ledgers, barred yards, guarded wells, priestly examinations, mage checks, and questions asked until the answers stop changing.

Every body that comes through the Candle Line passes under Mercygate’s shadow sooner or later:

- refugees from the hills
- half-orc families fleeing Dominion chains
- captured slave-soldiers
- human officers taken alive for questioning
- deserters
- looters
- men whose stories are too clean
- men whose hands are too soft
- men who know which direction to look when a Hextorite prayer is spoken

House Amadis built Mercygate because mercy without vigilance is an open door, and vigilance without mercy is just tyranny with better masonry.

The fort’s Pelorites tend the wounded first, even when the wounded are enemies. The Heironeans take oaths and testimonies. The Pholtines test lies when their gifts are available. Wizards look for charms, hidden marks, false memories, and the little hooks by which the Dominion moves its pieces.

Most half-orcs who arrive in chains or panic are not executed. They are held, questioned, treated, and sorted. Some are sent west under supervision. Some are placed in temple custody. Some, after long proving, earn work, land, or service. Others are sent onward to the [[Whitefield Estates]].

Dominion agents, Hextorite handlers, and oath-bound infiltrators are given no romance. If the proof is clean, the sword is cleaner.

In Griefwatch, where the reports are read by candlelight under the skull above the hall, Mercygate is regarded as unpleasant, necessary, and proof that House Amadis still knows the difference between an enemy, a victim, and a fool who picked the wrong road.

### Crookhorn Hold

**Hex:** 114.047  
**Type:** Connector fort / supply fort  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar  
**Role:** Links Candle Line, Mercygate roads, Ashwyn routes, and Orenic patrol logic

Crookhorn Hold is the southern connector of the line and the safest assignment among the eastern forts, which means everyone stationed there has to endure endless mockery from the men at Redtooth and Ashspur.

Unlike the hill towers, Crookhorn sits in flatter country where stone, timber, wagons, and masons can actually reach the site without requiring a prayer, a mule, and a grudge. Because of that, it is larger: a proper small fort rather than a tower-post. It has a broader yard, better stables, a smithy, extra grain stores, and a signal platform tied into Ashwyn roads and Orenic patrol routes.

Crookhorn’s battle history is less glorious and more useful. It is the kind of place that keeps a bad day from becoming a disaster.

Soldiers still call it safe, but only compared to the others. This is Blackbriar. Safe is graded on a curve, and the curve has teeth.

### Griefwatch Castle

**Hex:** 116.041  
**Type:** Mountain castle / war-seat  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar / [[House Amadis]]  
**Role:** Usual seat of rule, eastern command post, border surveillance, Candle Line command

[[Griefwatch Castle]] stands high in the eastern Blackbriar mountains, built into a knife-backed ridge overlooking the old invasion corridors from the orc hills and Dominion roads beyond. It is not the prettiest seat in Gorundia. It was never meant to be.

This is the true war-seat of [[House Amadis]].

Count Alistair usually rules from Griefwatch, not because the place is pleasant, but because it gives him constant eyes on the border. From here, orders ride to the Candle Line, reports come in from Mercygate, patrols are shifted, beacon drills are reviewed, and road closures can be commanded before rumors reach the western vineyards.

The great hall is long, severe, and mostly undecorated except for old banners, battle-scarred shields, and memorial plaques listing the dead of Amadis blood and service. At the far end stands the count’s chair: not quite a throne, but close enough to make the point.

Above the door mantle opposite it hangs the skull of a defeated enemy lord, cleaned, bound in black iron, and mounted where every Amadis ruler can stare at it during judgment, dinner, or brooding weather.

This is where [[Beringer]] grew up: under cold rafters, old grief, hard discipline, and that damn skull watching every meal like the world’s worst family portrait.

No wonder the man developed the emotional range of a locked armory.

## The Whitefield Estates

The [[Whitefield Estates]] are not one village, but a chain of Pholtine-administered agricultural colonies placed on safer lands behind the border system. They exist to receive cleared half-blood refugees from [[Mercygate Hold]] and similar registries.

Officially, they provide lawful work, food, shelter, moral instruction, and civic supervision to persons who would otherwise become vagrants, bandits, or casualties of the road.

Residents are not slaves. No writ of ownership is held over them, and any adult may petition to depart with travel papers.

This distinction is recited often.

Usually by people who have never had to choose between signing the ledger and watching their children starve.

Among half-orcs, the Whitefields are bitterly described as:

> “Slavery with fewer whips and more lectures.”

This is legally false and socially understandable. The food is real. The walls are real. The wages are real, though thin after deductions. So is the constant counting, the chapel discipline, the suspicion, and the quiet knowledge that leaving means stepping back into a kingdom that has not yet decided whether you are a person, a problem, or tomorrow’s corpse.

The Pholtines insist the estates are mercy given structure.

The Pelorites call them better than abandonment, and worse than welcome.

House Amadis tolerates them because fewer starving refugees become raiders.

And the half-orcs endure them because survival is still survival, even when it comes with a sermon before breakfast.

## The Candle Line

The [[Candle Line]] runs east and southeast of [[Griefwatch Castle]], along the yellow hills and old invasion corridors. Its formal name is **the Blackbriar Thornline Watch**, but no one calls it that unless they are writing orders, receiving funding, or trying to sound important in front of a duke.

The Candle Line is not meant to stop an invasion.

The posts are meant to see the swarm, confirm its numbers, force it to slow, and buy Griefwatch the hours it needs to wake the Thornward, summon riders, and close the roads behind the villages.

Each post is small, hard, and ugly: a squat stone tower, a walled yard, a cistern, a signal brazier, a mule-stable, a dry powder room, and bunks for men who learn to sleep in armor.

Life on the Candle Line is one of two things:

**boring or terrifying.**

The men prefer boring.

They complain anyway.

It is tradition.

### Greenbriar Downs

**Hex:** 111.038  
**Type:** Pasture district / horse country  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar  
**Role:** Grazing, remounts, hayfields, western ranch country

Greenbriar Downs is broad, open country by Blackbriar standards: rolling pasture, hayfields, hedged paddocks, and long horse runs broken by stone walls and windbreaks. It marks the beginning of the county’s ranch and vine country.

The Downs supply remounts, pack animals, oats, hay, and riding stock to both [[Blackbriar Court]] and the eastern war-seat at [[Griefwatch Castle]]. Many Amadis horses spend their early years here before being moved to more specialized yards.

It is peaceful in the way armed rural wealth is peaceful: gates are maintained, strangers are noticed, and every stable boy knows where the alarm horn hangs.

### Thornvale

**Hex:** 112.038  
**Type:** Vineyard village  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar  
**Role:** Wine production, cooperage, tenant farms

Thornvale is a vineyard village set among low slopes, blackthorn hedges, and old stone presses. The local thorn-vines produce one of the better-known Blackbriar reds: dry, dark, and sharp enough that visiting courtiers pretend to understand it.

The village supports vintners, coopers, tenant farmers, carters, and seasonal pickers. It is not grand, but it is prosperous, and its people carry themselves with the quiet arrogance of folk who know nobles care about their harvest.

Thornvale’s cellars are deeper than the houses above them. Some are old enough to have elven stonework in the lower courses. Naturally, no one has ever hidden anything questionable in a wine cellar. Civilization would never.

### Amberford

**Hex:** 113.038  
**Type:** Ford hamlet / road market  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar  
**Role:** Crossing, wagon stop, local market, horse watering

Amberford is a modest ford settlement on the west-east road through the interior demesne. It has a stone ford, a wagon green, watering posts, a shrine, a reeve’s house, and enough inns and stables to keep travelers moving without letting them linger too comfortably.

It is where vineyard carts, horse drovers, patrol riders, and messengers begin to mix with traffic headed toward the uplands. News travels quickly through Amberford, usually arriving disguised as gossip, complaint, or a suspiciously detailed stable bill.

Amberford is not important enough to be grand.

That is exactly why it is useful.

### Old Briarstead

**Hex:** 112.039  
**Type:** Manor cluster / old estate country  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar  
**Role:** Minor noble leases, orchards, vineyards, household supply

Old Briarstead is a cluster of long-held manor estates, tenant farms, orchards, and older Amadis leaseholds. The families here are not great lords, but many have served House Amadis for generations as reeves, horse-masters, vintners, clerks, and household knights.

It is one of the more comfortable parts of the direct demesne, with good stone houses, old wells, neat fields, and chapels that have been repaired so many times they are basically arguments with gravity.

The people here are loyal to House Amadis in the deeply annoying way of families who remember every favor, every insult, and every marriage contract since the kingdom had fewer roads.

### Rookmere Ponds

**Hex:** 113.040  
**Type:** Fishpond estate / wet meadow settlement  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar  
**Role:** Fish, reeds, ducks, eels, kitchen supply

Rookmere Ponds is a managed wetland estate of fishponds, reed beds, eel traps, duck blinds, and damp meadow pasture. It supplies freshwater fish, smoked eel, rushes, feathers, and waterfowl to the western estates and Blackbriar Court.

The ponds are old, orderly, and more engineered than they first appear. Low sluices control water levels, and small causeways cross the marshy ground between ponds.

Rookmere is quiet, misty, and full of birds that sound like old women judging you from the reeds.

### Saddlethorn Yards

**Hex:** 114.040  
**Type:** Horse yards / training fields  
**Jurisdiction:** Direct Blackbriar  
**Role:** Horse training, grading, remount preparation

Saddlethorn Yards is where many Amadis horses are broken, trained, graded, and assigned. The yards sit where the western pasturelands begin to rise toward harder country, giving trainers access to open runs, hill tracks, road drills, and controlled obstacle fields.

The best animals may go to household knights or noble buyers. The reliable ones go east as remounts for patrols, messengers, Griefwatch riders, and road wardens.

Saddlethorn’s horse-masters are famous for two things: producing calm, durable riding stock and insulting anyone who confuses a pretty horse with a useful one.
## Vassal Baronies

### Barony Drakemoor

[[Barony Drakemoor]] lies northwest of the direct Blackbriar demesne. Its lands are wetter, darker, and more haunted in reputation. It is ruled by [[House Draal]] and owes fealty, service, and coordination to the Count of Blackbriar.

Drakemoor is tied to Blackbriar through northern roads, old watch lines, and the uneasy trade between cultivated estates and moorland holdings.

### Barony Ashwyn

[[Barony Ashwyn]] lies along the southern and southwestern side of the Amadis sphere. It is ruled by [[House Helbron]] and contains several settlements that are economically and strategically important to Blackbriar, including [[Hearthmere]], [[Loomford]], [[Larkspur Weir]], [[Ridgewool Watch]], and [[Three-Crooks Gate]].

Ashwyn is especially important for wool, road traffic, tolls, mills, and the southern support routes feeding toward Mercygate, Crookhorn Hold, and the edge of the Candle Line.

Many outsiders blur the distinction between Ashwyn and direct Blackbriar holdings.

The locals do not.

The tax rolls certainly do not.

## Economy

Blackbriar’s economy is diverse and carefully managed.

### Wine

Blackbriar’s thorned vineyards are famous throughout Gorundia. The best estates produce structured reds and pale, floral whites. The finest bottles go to noble tables, temple feasts, and diplomatic gifts.

Amadis wine is not as aggressively marketed as some merchant-house vintages. That is partly snobbery, partly scarcity, and partly because the Amadis counts understand that people value a thing more when told they may not have it.

### Horses

House Amadis breeds exceptional destriers and riding horses. Their stock blends elven grace with hardier bloodlines, producing mounts known for endurance, intelligence, and discipline.

They are not as brutally strong as Vogel warhorses, but they are more adaptable, less temperamental, and much better suited to long campaign work.

### Wool and Cloth

The upland villages produce quality wool, much of it sold into Ashwyn’s textile networks. The count’s officers and Ashwyn’s baronial factors keep careful oversight of weights, dyes, and transport tariffs.

This cooperation is functional, profitable, and occasionally full of quiet knives.

### Border Logistics

Griefwatch, the Candle Line, Mercygate, Crookhorn, and the Ashwyn road system create a constant demand for grain, horseshoes, arrows, lamp oil, salted meat, wool blankets, mule tack, leather, pitch, and stonework.

This means the eastern demesne is not merely defended by the western economy.

It feeds on it.

A bad harvest in the west means fewer supplies in the east. A border scare in the east means higher prices in the west. Somewhere in the middle, a steward quietly ages ten years.

### Road Tolls

Blackbriar and its vassal baronies maintain lawful toll points on major roads. Unlike Vogel toll stations, Amadis and Ashwyn tolls are generally predictable, recorded, and appealable.

This makes merchants like them.

It also makes other nobles mutter darkly about “mercantile influence,” which is noble-speak for “someone else figured out how to make money without terrorizing peasants.”

## Military and Defense

Blackbriar’s military strength is not raw mass. It is **mobility, roads, horses, watch-lines, and prepared choke points**.

The county can field:

- household knights of [[House Amadis]]
- mounted retainers
- trained road wardens
- village bowmen
- watchtower garrisons
- Candle Line veterans
- shepherd scouts and drover auxiliaries
- limited elven and half-elven skirmish support
- baronial support from Drakemoor and Ashwyn when mustered

The defensive system relies on early warning. Griefwatch, the Candle Line, Mercygate, Crookhorn, the Ashwyn road gates, and the northern watch-chain can light beacons and move riders fast enough to gather local forces before raiders reach the richer heartland.

Blackbriar roads look like trade infrastructure.

They are also cavalry lanes.

## Law and Governance

Blackbriar law is more formal than harsh. The Amadis counts prefer charters, witnesses, seals, and written obligations. The county’s legal culture is proud of being predictable.

Local disputes are handled by reeves, manor courts, guild witnesses, and sworn elders. Serious matters go to the count’s court.

In the baronies, such as [[Barony Ashwyn]] and [[Barony Drakemoor]], local officers act under baronial authority first, but major toll records, musters, road disputes, and military issues can be appealed upward to Blackbriar authority.

Griefwatch complicates this further, because the count’s war-seat often receives reports faster than the civil court does. A matter may be legally Ashwyn, economically Blackbriar, militarily Griefwatch, and spiritually Pholtine within the same afternoon.

The Amadis style of rule is:

- lawful but not usually cruel
- chivalric but not naive
- merchant-friendly
- protective of freemen
- cautious with noble privilege
- deeply concerned with oaths, records, and precedent
- unforgiving toward border negligence

This makes Blackbriar popular among merchants, smallholders, and skilled tradesmen.

It makes hardline aristocrats call them soft.

Those hardline aristocrats should inspect the Candle Line more closely.

## Refugee Policy and the Border Sieve

Blackbriar’s eastern system is built on a brutal distinction:

Not every half-orc crossing the border is an enemy.

Not every refugee is safe.

Not every prisoner is useful.

Not every act of mercy is clean.

Half-orcs fleeing through the Candle Line are not automatically put to the sword. House Amadis knows the Dominion uses half-orc slave-soldiers, forced auxiliaries, camp-bred laborers, and terrorized families. Killing every half-orc who runs west would be both evil and strategically stupid.

But they are not waved through with a sandwich and a “best of luck.”

They are seized, disarmed, searched, separated by group, and marched under guard to [[Mercygate Hold]].

The basic policy:

> **Fleeing half-orcs are treated as dangerous refugees until processed. Armed half-orcs are treated as combatants until proven otherwise. Marked Dominion handlers, infiltrators, and oath-bound agents are executed.**

At Mercygate, priests and wizards begin the filtering. Cleared refugees may be sent west under escort, placed in temple custody, assigned to road crews, or sent onward to the [[Whitefield Estates]].

This is the kind of policy that lets every faction involved claim it is the only adult in the room.

They are all partly right.

That is what makes it ugly.

## Culture

Blackbriar culture is polished rural nobility with a hard eastern spine: wine, horses, wool, formal hospitality, chapel bells, market days, old stories, signal fires, border plaques, and children taught the names of the dead before they are old enough to understand why their parents go quiet afterward.

Common sayings include:

> “A good road tells the truth.”

Meaning: trade, patrols, news, and justice all depend on roads.

> “Mind the briar.”

Meaning: be polite, but remember where you are.

> “Griefwatch sees first.”

Meaning: the border notices trouble before the court does.

> “The candle burns for the house.”

Meaning: the watch-posts buy time by risking themselves.

## Political Significance

Blackbriar is one of Araisal’s stabilizing counties. It links the refined Allaric heartland to practical western trade, border defense, refugee control, and the old invasion corridors.

House Amadis’ current importance has increased sharply due to [[Beringer]]’s rise, the fall of [[Wilhelm Vogel]], and the Crown’s dependence on honorable but politically useful houses.

This creates tension. Blackbriar is respected, but now watched. Its old image as a chivalric wine-and-horse county is being replaced by something more dangerous:

A county close to the Crown.

A county with legal credibility.

A county with a functioning border apparatus.

A county whose house now stands near the center of succession politics.

That is not a comfortable place to be.

Comfort is mostly for people too far from the border to hear the horns.

## Public Rumors and Local Troubles

### The Whitefield Petition

A group of cleared half-orc families at Saint Orveth’s Whitefield petition to leave, claiming their placement debt has been illegally extended by a Pholtine rector.

The rector’s ledgers are perfect.

That is the first sign something is wrong.

### The Missing Mercygate Name

Mercygate’s ledgers show a half-orc refugee child processed, cleared, and transferred west.

No Whitefield received her.

No road warden escorted her.

No grave lists her.

Someone altered three records and forgot the fourth.

### Candle Out

Widow’s Lantern fails to light during a scheduled beacon test. Griefwatch sends riders. The tower answers late, claiming bad fuel and wet kindling.

The veteran sergeant posted there has never once blamed kindling in his life.

### The Blackglass Foal

A prized Amadis destrier foal is born with glassy black eyes after its dam drinks from a stream running down from Blackglass Mere. The horse is healthy, brilliant, and deeply unsettling.
