# Notable Taverns and Drinking Establishments of Bard's Gate

> Bard's Gate may no longer be the gleaming center of trade it once was, but a city endures by the strength of its stories and its watering holes. From weathered roadside inns to gnomish coffeehouses, each establishment below serves a role in the patchwork soul of the city.

## Guide to the Listings

- **Price:** `¢` inexpensive, `¢¢` moderate, `¢¢¢` expensive.
- **Noise:** hush, buzz, or raucous.
- **Trouble:** the likelihood that a peaceful drink becomes somebody else's problem.
- **House Rule:** the rule patrons learn before, during, or immediately after breaking it.
- **Hook:** an adventure seed associated with the establishment.

## Tradeton

### The King's Rest

> A humble tavern made famous after [[King Rimmand II]] shared a beer here with [[Orin Kharne|Sir Orin Kharne]] and [[Beringer Amadis|Sir Beringer Amadis]]. Its plain oak benches and thick-walled hearth have since become a pilgrimage site for local patriots.  Keep your feet OFF of the King's Bench.

- **Owner/Operator:** Adal Morn, retired city guardsman turned publican.
- **Clientele:** Guards, adventurers, loyalists, and anyone who wants to claim they sat where the King did.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢¢`, buzz becoming raucous on feast days, low.
- **House Rule:** No boots on the bench the King used, and no lying about having sat there. Adal checks.
- **Signatures:** *Rimmand's Rest*, an amber ale drawn from the so-called Royal Cask; fire-roast trencher with onion gravy.
- **Hook:** A supposedly royal splinter sold as a relic traces back to a missing holy object in Old Bardston.
- **Tags:** Patriots, Watch veterans, Champions sightings.

### The Weary Wheel

> Formerly a roadside coach stop, the Weary Wheel is now favored by teamsters and caravan guards. The broken wheel hanging outside groans in the wind like an old soldier's knees.

- **Owner/Operator:** Treda Lonn, one-legged former teamster with a sharp tongue and a sharper broom.
- **Clientele:** Muleteers, caravan hands, traveling merchants, and grizzled former sellswords.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, buzz, medium due to teamster grudges.
- **House Rule:** Park your wheels straight or Treda's broom finds your ankles.
- **Signatures:** Road Dust porter; peppered beef pit-ends.
- **Hook:** A missing caravan ledger encodes a smuggling route through Butcher's Block.
- **Tags:** Caravans, Road gossip, Fixers.

### The Tarnished Star

> A faded tavern with cracked tiles and a crumbling mural of a falling star. It is rough around the edges but stubbornly trying to recover its former glory.

- **Owner/Operator:** Maerin Gall, talkative, charming, and perpetually one good idea away from fortune.
- **Clientele:** Down-on-their-luck merchants, former scholars, honest city folk, and amateur adventurers.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, hush rising to a buzz, low.
- **House Rule:** Sing an old song and you buy the table a round.
- **Signatures:** Falling Star, a cheap brandy with lemon; herb-salt flatbread.
- **Hook:** Maerin's latest good idea requires a gnomish financier and slightly more credibility than he possesses.
- **Tags:** Nostalgia, Honest locals, Small stakes that matter.

## Gnomoria

### The Brass Thimble

> An ornate gnomish café with golden trim, glass display cases, and high-end imported beans. Every drink is calibrated to the gram and served with ceremony.

- **Owner/Operator:** Gildrim Tinklatch, obsessive about measurements and prone to lecturing patrons on the ancestry of their coffee.
- **Clientele:** Gnomish aristocrats, human assistants, tired financiers, and bored half-ogre valets.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢¢¢`, hush, low.
- **House Rule:** No free pours. Measurements must be respected.
- **Signatures:** Single-origin siphon coffee; pistachio-cardamom biscotti.
- **Hook:** A disputed bean shipment conceals a micro-automaton blueprint sought by rival guilds.
- **Tags:** Finance, Calibration cult, Quiet power.

### Rivets & Ristretto

> A tinker's dream of a lounge filled with ticking clocks, gleaming brass, and the constant aroma of espresso. Gears spin, cups clink, and deals are quietly struck.

- **Owner/Operator:** Harkla Bronzebuzz, gnomish caffeine alchemist and amateur philosopher.
- **Clientele:** Inventors, engineers, junior officials, and suspiciously well-dressed interns.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢¢`, buzz, medium due to patent disputes.
- **House Rule:** Schematics face down, cups right-side up.
- **Signatures:** Boiler-foam ristretto; copper-pan sugared almonds.
- **Hook:** A prototype valve has vanished, and the thief left a riddle tied to the clocks of Master's Mall.
- **Tags:** Inventors, Junior officials, Intern intrigue.

## Glimmer Green

### The Hollow Oak

> A serene, dimly lit tavern near the roots of the Great Tree. Lanterns of bark and amber provide soft light, and the scent of sage hangs permanently in the air. Iron is forbidden, and loud voices earn sharp looks.

- **Owner/Operator:** Callathiel Fernbrook, half-elf druid and poet who acts as an unofficial ambassador between the circle and the city.
- **Clientele:** Elves, half-elves, druids, herbalists, followers of the Old Ways, and travelers seeking wisdom or silence.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢¢`, hush, low.
- **House Rule:** **No iron** past the lantern line. Druidic steel chits are issued for eating knives.
- **Signatures:** Moon-sage mead; acorn-smoked trout on leafbread.
- **Hook:** A concealed metal charm awakens a guardian wisp demanding the return of something stolen from the Green.
- **Tags:** Old Ways, Circle envoys, Poets.

### Whispers in the Leaves

> An open-air wine terrace nestled among ivy-covered stone columns, where the wind seems to carry forgotten songs. It is known for tranquil views and wandering minstrels.

- **Owner/Operator:** Nirien of the Wind, an eccentric elven vintner who speaks to her wine before serving it.
- **Clientele:** Nature mystics, melancholic elves, lovestruck poets, and bardic dropouts.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢¢`, hush, low.
- **House Rule:** Songs must be finished. No half-verses.
- **Signatures:** Wind-Kissed White, a crisp elven wine; pear and goat-cheese tart.
- **Hook:** One particular vintage has begun answering questions through its bouquet, but only the recently jilted can understand it.
- **Tags:** Melancholy, Minstrels, Quiet omens.

## Outlander's Corner

### The Painted Jackal

> Colorful, loud, and only moderately respectable, the Jackal welcomes anyone with coin and a story. Bright fabrics hang from the rafters, and spicy stews are served beside potent ale.

- **Owner/Operator:** Saffa Dorran, a Tashari expatriate with a past she declines to discuss.
- **Clientele:** Foreign traders, retired adventurers, brash mercenaries, and scholars between appointments.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, raucous, high due to bravado and spice.
- **House Rule:** Tell a story or buy a stew.
- **Signatures:** Fire-jackal stew; clove-and-date ale.
- **Hook:** A corsair captain arrives carrying old debts and one fragment of a map to Lenor's Watch.
- **Tags:** Outsiders, Adventurers, Sea rumors.

### The Crossing Stone

> Named for the marker that once indicated the city's boundary, this inn has become a gathering place for outlanders trying to become insiders.

- **Owner/Operator:** Harlan Croft, carpenter turned innkeeper with a permanent grievance against the nobility.
- **Clientele:** Middle-class hopefuls, mixed families, hardworking immigrants, and off-duty members of the City Watch.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, buzz, medium.
- **House Rule:** Locals pay first and newcomers last, allegedly to teach patience.
- **Signatures:** Brick-oven barley loaf; alder-cask small beer.
- **Hook:** An off-duty confession reveals a shakedown ring targeting shops owned by mixed families.
- **Tags:** Immigrant pride, Watch off-duty, Mutual aid.

## Old Bardston

### The Red Kettle

> A working-class pub where arguments are loud but rarely violent. The kettle above the hearth never stops boiling, and the beer is always cheap.

- **Owner/Operator:** Grambly Tatch, widower, brewer, and determined minder of his own business.
- **Clientele:** City clerks, dockhands, older locals, minor craftsmen, and nosy neighbors.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, raucous when the kettle hisses, medium.
- **House Rule:** Take your argument outside or settle it at darts.
- **Signatures:** Kettle Bitters; corned turnip hash.
- **Hook:** A dockhand's dart pins a blackmail note concerning a beloved local midwife.
- **Tags:** Clerks and dockhands, Neighborhood aunties, Loud hearts.

### The Bell and Board

> Built into a former messenger waystation, the tavern retains its old posting board. It now carries jobs, notices, contracts, warnings, and occasional offers that should have been reported to the Watch.

- **Owner/Operator:** Winsey Grett, middle-aged half-elf with a soft spot for desperate youths and a formidable arm-wrestling grip.
- **Clientele:** Independent contractors, urban adventurers, low-level spellcasters, and locals with side work.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, buzz, low.
- **House Rule:** If you post it, you honor it. Winsey watches.
- **Signatures:** Runner's ale; sausage-and-mustard rolls.
- **Hook:** A job written in thieves' cant pays in burning-root sigils associated with forest raiders.
- **Tags:** Odd jobs, Urban adventurers, Soft spot for strays.

## Worker's Rest

- **Clientele:** Millers, cartwrights, laundresses, young families, and decent people who know when to leave trouble outdoors.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, buzz, low.

- **Hook:** Missing work lanterns are causing fatal accidents. Sabotage is suspected, though several workers insist the thief has teeth.

## Butcher's Block

There are no sanctioned, permanent taverns in Butcher's Block. The smell of blood, offal, smoke, and rendered fat discourages leisurely drinking. Most workers cross into Climber's Corner or Worker's Rest after their shifts.

There is, however, the Black-Pot.

### The Black-Pot

> Hidden behind Warehouse Nine, the Black-Pot is little more than an illegal stewpot, several barrels, and a collection of benches. Off-the-books butchers gather there for meat, dice, and sullen silence. It has no sign and officially does not exist.

- **Owner/Operator:** Uncertain. Different people appear to hold the ladle on different nights.
- **Clientele:** Butchers, meat porters, warehouse hands, smugglers, and those who do not intend to be remembered.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, hush, high if noticed.
- **House Rule:** No names, no knives on the table, and no seconds for strangers.
- **Signatures:** Off-cut stew; red-salt bread.
- **Hook:** Someone has replaced the red salt with a slow poison. The kitchen is now seeking volunteers to test each new batch, with predictable recruitment difficulties.
- **Tags:** Meat-guild shadows, Night dice, Hard stares.

## Bard's College

### The Broken Lute

> Once a haven of poetic brilliance, the Broken Lute is now a dive with peeling paint and broken dreams. Its walls are scratched with lyrics, and bards gather to complain, compete, and rewrite their failures as artistic persecution.

- **Owner/Operator:** Professor Tharnel Elth, failed lyricist who insists on grading every performance.
- **Clientele:** Washed-up minstrels, drama students, idealistic youths, and patrons buying drinks in exchange for gossip.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, shout-singing raucous, medium due to professional jealousy.
- **House Rule:** Anyone who sings receives a grade and must accept it or be booed.
- **Signatures:** Sour ale; Ink-Stain stew.
- **Hook:** A lyric scratched into the wall conceals both the location of a buried masterwork and the proof of a professor's disgrace.
- **Tags:** Washed-up brilliance, Drama, Cheap genius.

### The Second Verse

> A somewhat better tavern nearby, advertised as the place where the real talent drinks. Cozy and intimate, it is one of the few establishments where bardic students may perform without being devoured alive by their elders.

- **Owner/Operator:** Merra Softstep Lunil, retired elven contralto turned businesswoman.
- **Clientele:** Musical hopefuls, quiet composers, romantics, and professors who still care.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, buzz, low.
- **House Rule:** No heckling student performances.
- **Signatures:** Apple-rose wine; thyme crackers with soft cheese.
- **Hook:** Two prodigies are courting the same patron, who has demanded a dangerously memorable finale in the Dregs.

## The Dregs

### The Blood and Sand

> The largest and best-known half-blood tavern in the Dregs, the Blood and Sand is built around a central fighting pit. Its patrons range from local hardcases and professional fighters to knights, adventurers, and occasional royalty.
>
> The establishment was brought to wider attention during [[Wonderful|Wonderful's]] tenure. Since her elevation to Lord Mayor, its daily operations have passed into rougher but surprisingly capable hands.

- **Owner/Operator:** [[Gravel]] runs the house; [[Grenda]] controls the floor; [[Slab]] keeps the door; [[Uncle Fenby Goodbarrel]] has taken command of the kitchen.
- **Clientele:** Half-blood locals, criminals, fighters, adventurers, knights, laborers, and anyone willing to obey the room's rules.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, raucous, high by design but firmly managed.
- **House Rule:** Pit fights require sign-up. No blades in the ring. Gravel's word ends business disputes; Grenda's ends floor disputes.
- **Signatures:** Pit-Smoke lager; grilled spice skewers; Red Sand chaser; Troll Discharge; whatever Fenby decided everyone was eating that day.
- **Hook:** A visiting noble seeks a private match intended to humiliate a local hero and ignite a wider street conflict.
- **Tags:** Half-blood pride, Champions, Pit fights, Underworld.

> [!note] Special Drink: Troll Discharge
> A thick, syrupy combination of dwarven stone-spirit, burnt sugar, hot peppers, clove, and smoke-salt. It is poured into a squat clay cup so newcomers cannot see its consistency before making regrettable decisions.
>
> **Taste:** Candy-sweet at first, followed by rising pepper heat, numbing clove, and a campfire finish.
>
> **Custom:** A newcomer who keeps it down drinks free for the rest of the night. Anyone who vomits in the pit cleans the pit.
>
> **Variants:**
> - **Greenblood:** Mint and cucumber added. Cooler in flavor, less forgiving to the stomach.
> - **Granite Drip:** No syrup, only spirit and heat.
> - **Sweet Fang:** Honey replaces the burnt sugar. Considered a Thimblewick heresy.
>
> **Price:** `¢` per cup. First-timer challenges are free.
>
> **GURPS 4e:** Roll HT-2 to keep it down. Failure causes Nauseated for 30 minutes and forfeits the free-drinks reward. Success grants +1 to Fear Checks inside the Blood and Sand for the evening and +1 reactions from regulars who witnessed the attempt.

### The Fang's Rest

> A dim subterranean tavern serving as the primary stronghold of the Fangbloods. Damp stone, cheap ale, and crude carvings of fangs and serpents set the mood with all the subtlety of a knife on a table.

- **Owner/Operator:** Garrik the Fang Morlen, ruthless leader of the Fangbloods.
- **Clientele:** Fangblood members, their associates, and people conducting business with them.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, hush, high.
- **House Rule:** Show the mark or show yourself out.
- **Signatures:** Bitter Fang Brew; raw-spice jerky.
- **Hook:** A defecting Fangblood will cooperate if his death can be convincingly staged.
- **Tags:** Gang stronghold, Contraband, Dead-man deals.

### The Crooked Rat

> A barely standing shack with one room, three tables, and rats large enough to reconsider a person's ownership of their boots. The beer is cheap, which is the full extent of its defense.

- **Owner/Operator:** Old Fen, one-eyed former scavenger who drinks more than he serves.
- **Clientele:** Thieves, thugs, cutpurses, and desperate people on their last copper.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, thin buzz, medium.
- **House Rule:** Pay before you drink.
- **Signatures:** Rat-brown beer; pickled anything.
- **Hook:** Fen's bouncer has vanished and a nastier crew has arrived carrying a debt list.
- **Tags:** Thieves and cutpurses, Last-copper crowd, Bad-luck charm.

### The Barrelfire

> Named for the burning barrel that serves as its hearth, the Barrelfire is known for stolen beer, bloodstained benches, and a firm drink-first, ask-never policy.

- **Owner/Operator:** Nacka Redhands, a half-orc woman who throws out more patrons than she permits to remain.
- **Clientele:** Hired muscle, fugitives, pit fighters, and corrupt Watch officers on break.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, raucous, high.
- **House Rule:** Drink first. Ask never.
- **Signatures:** Barrel Scorch; Blood-Bench roast.
- **Hook:** Someone is testing a new street drug by adding it to the ale.
- **Tags:** Hired muscle, Fugitives, Crooked Watch.

### The Gutter's Cup

> A miserable pub beneath a collapsed causeway. The roof leaks, the drink is thin, and the food is not poisoned intentionally.

- **Owner/Operator:** Wink Taldo, cook, gambler, and man without visible shame.
- **Clientele:** Beggars, ratcatchers, grifters, outcast mages, and people unable to afford the Crooked Rat.
- **Price/Noise/Trouble:** `¢`, drip-hush, medium.
- **House Rule:** Keep to the dry stones.
- **Signatures:** Thin grog; salt-fried sprats.
- **Hook:** Wink's gambling debt lands on a patron's table, along with the demand to pay, flee, or deal with the lender.
- **Tags:** Beggars, Ratcatchers, Outcast mages.

# Sir Osric Fenwick's Public-House Reviews

> [!quote]
> The following reviews were apparently written by [[Sir Osric Fenwick]] during an extended attempt to "learn the city."
>
> Sir Osric denies intending them for public circulation, which ensured their immediate popularity.

## Tradeton

### The King's Rest

> **★★★★☆**
>
> The ale is honest, the food filling, and Adal Morn guards the King's old bench more fiercely than some men guard their daughters.
>
> I did not sit on it. I have survived too many campaigns to be killed by a retired guardsman with patriotic furniture.

### The Weary Wheel

> **★★★★☆**
>
> Good porter, excellent beef ends, and enough caravan gossip to plan an invasion or avoid one.
>
> Treda struck a man's ankle with her broom for parking his wagon crooked. His wagon was crooked. Justice remains alive in Tradeton.

### The Tarnished Star

> **★★★☆☆**
>
> The flatbread is better than the brandy. Maerin Gall has one good idea every quarter-hour and explains each as though it has already secured royal investment.
>
> Do not sing an old marching song unless prepared to purchase drinks for everyone who remembers losing someone to it.

## Gnomoria

### The Brass Thimble

> **★★★☆☆**
>
> The coffee arrived measured to a precision normally reserved for siege engines. Gildrim explained the bean's ancestry, elevation, temperament, and apparent moral philosophy.
>
> Excellent coffee. Exhausting biography.

### Rivets & Ristretto

> **★★★★☆**
>
> Strong drink, good almonds, and clocks everywhere, presumably so one may know exactly how much life has been lost listening to inventors argue over valves.
>
> Two gnomes challenged one another to a patent duel. No blood was shed, but several diagrams were called fraudulent.

## Glimmer Green

### The Hollow Oak

> **★★★★☆**
>
> Quiet, restful, and entirely free of iron. Surrendering my sword at the lantern line was uncomfortable, but less uncomfortable than having six druids stare at it simultaneously.
>
> The trout was excellent. The mead tasted like moonlight, sage, and decisions one should reconsider before dawn.

### Whispers in the Leaves

> **★★★☆☆**
>
> Fine wine, beautiful terrace, and more tragic poets per table than any military commander should permit in one location.
>
> A young elf began a song about his lost love. The house rule required him to finish it. By the sixth verse, I understood why she left.

## Outlander's Corner

### The Painted Jackal

> **★★★★☆**
>
> The stew is hot enough to qualify as defensive magic. The stories are louder than the music and approximately as reliable as cavalry scouts after their third drink.
>
> Three different men claimed to have killed the same corsair. The corsair was seated beside me.

### The Crossing Stone

> **★★★☆☆**
>
> Good bread. Acceptable beer. Newcomers are served last to teach patience.
>
> I have endured sieges, winter marches, and noble succession hearings. I did not require a carpenter to teach me patience, but he appeared pleased with the attempt.

## Noble's Rest

### The Swan & Scepter

> **★★★☆☆**
>
> Excellent wine and veal. Every patron watches every other patron as though choosing where to place the knife.
>
> A gentleman whispered an accusation at me, then remembered the house rule and had to repeat it using my name. This deprived the insult of nearly all its charm.

### The Veiled Chalice

> **★★☆☆☆**
>
> Masks are allowed, names are optional, and everyone present knows something they should not.
>
> The cordial was excellent. I left with three rumors, two invitations, and no certainty that the person serving me had ever worked there.

## Old Bardston

### The Red Kettle

> **★★★★★**
>
> Cheap beer, loud arguments, good darts. Grambly lets patrons settle disputes outside or at the board, which is more judicial flexibility than most courts manage.
>
> The turnip hash is much better than that phrase suggests.

### The Bell and Board

> **★★★★☆**
>
> Good ale, excellent sausage rolls, and a board full of employment opportunities for anyone insufficiently attached to keeping all their limbs.
>
> Someone attempted to hire me while I was reading the postings. The pay was insulting. The assassination target was worse.

## Craftsman's Crescent

### The Broken Tongs

> **★★★★★**
>
> Hot, loud, scarred, and honest. The tables look as though they survived the same wars as the patrons.
>
> Brokk serves the best sausage plate in Bard's Gate and does not ask a man to describe the wine before drinking it. Civilization may yet recover.

## Worker's Rest

### The Lantern's End

> **★★★★★**
>
> Dependable stew, warm cider, working people, and no one attempting to impress anyone after a twelve-hour shift.
>
> A child fell asleep against my arm while her parents finished supper. No one apologized. They were correct not to.

## Midtown Manors

### The Whispering Mantle

> **★★★★☆**
>
> Good claret, private booths, and enough old officers to turn any conversation into a campaign nobody officially lost.
>
> The curtains provide discretion. They do not provide soundproofing. This distinction has apparently ended several marriages.

### The Marigold Taproom

> **★★★★☆**
>
> Warm buns, mild ale, quiet music, and children behaving slightly better than their parents.
>
> Sella stopped a fiddler halfway through a flourish and informed him that it exceeded marigold volume. I have seen generals exercise less authority.

## Climber's Corner

### The Iron Pitcher

> **★★★☆☆**
>
> The ale is solid. The curtains are not velvet. The servers' cologne could be used to mark territorial boundaries.
>
> Everyone present is pretending to have recently become richer than they are. Some may eventually succeed.

### The Clinking Step

> **★★★☆☆**
>
> Half the room counts coins. The other half watches the door to see who noticed.
>
> The gin is adequate. The atmosphere resembles the five minutes before an ambush, except everyone is armed with account books.

## Hin Hideaway

### The Sugar Cask

> **★★★★★**
>
> Excellent mead, excellent pies, and several generations of halflings conducting family politics over one table.
>
> I asked about the prize pumpkins. This was a mistake. The resulting argument lasted longer than the Eastern Reclamation.

### Thimblewick's

> **★★★★★**
>
> Children underfoot, laughter overhead, and a proprietor who claims to have once been duke of a very small country.
>
> I do not believe him. I would nevertheless prefer his government to several I have served.

## Butcher's Block

### The Black-Pot

> **★★★☆☆**
>
> Unmarked door, no names, no knives on the table, and no second helping for strangers.
>
> The stew was remarkably good. I was disappointed not to receive seconds, though admittedly relieved they still considered me a stranger.

## Dwarven Dwell

### The Anvil's Bane

> **★☆☆☆☆**
>
> I was not admitted. My message was accepted at the outer stone and delivered efficiently.
>
> The refusal was courteous, absolute, and backed by a dwarf holding a hammer large enough to end the discussion. Consistent service.

### The Hammer's Thirst

> **★★★★☆**
>
> I entered as a guest and was required to give my name and lineage before drinking. My lineage took longer to explain than the first ale took to consume.
>
> Dagna remembered both. She also remembered a debt my grandfather apparently owed Clan Elgarin. One star removed for genealogical consequences.

## Master's Mall

### The Ledger & Lash

> **★★☆☆☆**
>
> Excellent wine. Convenient writing tables. Lockboxes built into every booth.
>
> I watched three guild officers negotiate a contract while separately preparing to blackmail one another. It made me nostalgic for battles, where hostility is less administrative.

### The Molten Measure

> **★★★☆☆**
>
> I signed a waiver before drinking something that hissed when placed on the table. It tasted of apple, smoke, copper, and poor judgment.
>
> Nothing exploded. The proprietor seemed disappointed.

## Bard's College

### The Broken Lute

> **★★★☆☆**
>
> Cheap ale, scratched lyrics, and performers who believe volume is a substitute for talent.
>
> Professor Tharnel graded my rendition of *The Mud at Valewatch* a C-plus. He has never marched through mud, and his criticism is therefore without standing.

### The Second Verse

> **★★★★☆**
>
> Good wine, earnest musicians, and a strict prohibition against heckling students.
>
> One young singer forgot half her song and began again. Everyone waited. That was kinder than applause would have been.

## The Dregs

### The Blood and Sand

> **★★★★★**
>
> The doorman calculated how quickly he could kill me at roughly the same moment I did. He advised me to sit near the wall. This was excellent advice.
>
> The food is very good, the room is honest about what it is, and the staff provide clear instructions when a patron is behaving foolishly. Navigation assistance may also be available at closing.
>
> I am informed that my opinion of the establishment is compromised. I reject this allegation while conceding that it may contain evidence.

### The Fang's Rest

> **★☆☆☆☆**
>
> I was asked to show the mark or show myself out. I possessed no mark and therefore demonstrated that I could follow simple instructions.
>
> The ale smelled poor from the stairs. The knives looked well maintained.

### The Crooked Rat

> **★★☆☆☆**
>
> The beer resembles something that once heard beer described from another room.
>
> A rat sat beneath my table for twenty minutes and behaved with greater courtesy than most of the clientele. I left it part of my supper.

### The Barrelfire

> **★★☆☆☆**
>
> The burning barrel provides warmth, smoke, and a useful means of destroying evidence.
>
> The roast was surprisingly good. A man was thrown through the door before I finished it. He had asked a question, contrary to policy.

### The Gutter's Cup

> **★★☆☆☆**
>
> The roof leaks, the grog is thin, and the food is not intentionally poisoned.
>
> This is not a ringing endorsement, but it is apparently the standard by which nearby establishments are judged. Keep to the dry stones.

## Sir Osric's Overall Assessment of Bard's Gate

> A city may be measured by its walls, laws, markets, or temples. This is how officials measure it because officials are rarely invited anywhere interesting.
>
> Measure it instead by where a tired person can sit, what they are served, and whether anyone notices when they fail to return.
>
> By that standard, Bard's Gate is battered, loud, suspicious, frequently dishonest, and still worth defending.
