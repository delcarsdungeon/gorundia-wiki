# Blood and Sand Crew

> [!important] In Brief  
> The Blood and Sand is no longer merely a rough tavern with a fighting pit. Under [[Gravel]], with [[Thalrik Vaultforge]] and [[Wonderful]] both owning pieces behind the counter, it has become a halfblood house, street office, pressure valve, refuge, warning bell, and profitable bad idea.

The **Blood and Sand** has always been loud.

That was never the remarkable part.

Every gutter tavern in [[The Dregs]] can manage noise. Noise is cheap. A drunk with two teeth and a grievance can make noise. The Blood and Sand has become something more useful and more dangerous: a place where noise gets organized.

[[Thalrik Vaultforge]] handed the keys to [[Gravel]] in full. Not as a joke. Not as a test. Not as a temporary favor until a more polished man could be found. Gravel runs the house now.

Thalrik and [[Wonderful]] still own pieces of it. That is understood by the people who need to understand it and not loudly explained to the people who enjoy misunderstanding things for sport. Gravel is the face, the fists, and the man behind the booth. Thalrik remains patron, backer, and shadow. Wonderful has enough civic interest in the place that clever men avoid calling it merely a tavern.

Among halfbloods, being called **boss** means something.

Sometimes it means a man is paying.

Sometimes it means he has earned respect.

In Gravel’s case, it is probably both.

## Gravel

Gravel first appeared as a young punk with a dragon’s mouth, kobold legs, and a talent for getting beaten senseless by men who were demonstrably better at violence than he was. [[Beringer]] and [[Orin Kharne]] both beat the hell out of him more than once.

This improved him only slowly.

Gravel could not shut his mouth. He could not back down. For a while, it was unclear whether this was stupidity, pride, or some tragic biological condition.

Then Thalrik took him under his wing.

That did not make Gravel gentle. It did not make him educated. It did not file the teeth off him. But it did polish him enough that people began noticing the difference between a loud idiot and a street-cunning young bastard learning when to hold his tongue.

Gravel is not sure how old he is. He recently went through another growth spurt, so good money puts him around seventeen, give or take the usual halfblood accounting where nobody kept a birth record because civilization was apparently busy disappointing itself elsewhere.

He is cunning, street-smart, and better with numbers than letters. He is slowly learning to read, with the grim patience of a man personally offended by the alphabet. He has a mostly-honest accountant, which in the Dregs is close enough to divine intervention that no one should inspect it too closely.

Gravel has money now.

He has respect.

He has friends.

He also has a woman, two children he pretends are not becoming his, a tavern full of halfbloods who watch his face when trouble starts, and a city beginning to realize that the Blood and Sand may be more stable than several official institutions wearing cleaner boots.

## Ownership and Authority

The Blood and Sand’s present structure is simple on paper and complicated everywhere else.

- [[Gravel]] runs the house.
- [[Thalrik Vaultforge]] owns a serious piece and remains the chief patron.
- [[Wonderful]] owns a piece and has civic reasons for keeping the place alive, watched, and useful.
- The staff answer to Gravel inside the walls.
- The Dregs listen when the Blood and Sand changes its posture.

Nobody uses formal titles much.

Gravel is not “proprietor” to the regulars. He is not “master of the house.” He is **boss** when someone means it, **Gravel** when they know him, and worse things when they are safely outside biting range.

His booth in the corner is not officially an office.

Everyone knows it is.

## The Pit

Gravel has expanded business by turning the pit fights into scheduled, regular events.

The old Blood and Sand fights were messy affairs: grudges, wagers, challenges, and the occasional fool who mistook loudness for courage. Gravel has made the thing more profitable by giving it rhythm.

Now there are set nights.

There are announced bouts.

There are returning fighters with reputations.

There are grudges allowed to ripen before being harvested in front of a paying crowd.

The fights are mixed-style brawls with fewer rules than a monastery would like and more theater than the fighters admit. A man may come to settle a score. He may also come to shout, posture, bleed artistically, and make sure the crowd remembers his name.

The Blood and Sand sells blood, but Gravel has learned it also sells story.

That may be Thalrik’s influence.

Or Wonderful’s.

Or simply Gravel discovering that half the city will pay extra if a beating has a beginning, middle, and end.

## Grenda

[[Grenda]] runs the back.

She cooks, though no honest person would call her gifted at it. Her food is fair at best. Heavy, plain, survivable. Nobody comes to the Blood and Sand because Grenda has discovered the higher mysteries of seasoning.

They come because she makes sure people eat.

That is different.

Grenda is a rarity among the local halfbloods: blonde, broad, and sharper than strangers expect. She was pulled out of a worse bar run by one of the gangs. Thalrik was good to her, good to her children, and dropped enough coin on her life that she had time to become solid instead of merely surviving.

She had strength before.

Now she has room.

She has been getting fat.

No one comments.

Not because they do not notice. Everyone notices everything in the Dregs. They notice boots, bruises, coin purses, exits, lies, hunger, weakness, and who looks too long at whose child.

They do not comment because Grenda’s softness means safety. Prosperity. A woman eating enough. A woman not running. A woman no longer measuring every door by whether she can get through it before the shouting starts.

In the Blood and Sand, that kind of weight is almost holy.

Grenda has become the backroom matron and survival manager of the house. She oversees kitchen hands, stores, cleaning, staff meals, and the complicated moral disaster of keeping halfblood workers alive when most of them were trained by the world to treat food as something someone else deserves more.

She makes sure [[Druuna Sunseeker]] sits down and eats at least one meal a day.

This is not easy.

When Grenda used to give Druuna bread, Druuna would thank her, walk out, and give it to someone “that needed it more.” Grenda solved this by making Druuna eat where she could see her.

This is the kind of theology the Blood and Sand understands: sit down, eat the soup, and stop being a problem.

### Sella and Brak

Grenda brought two children with her.

The girl is **Sella**.

The boy is **Brak**.

Half-orcs age fast enough that they can read older than their years. Sella looks near ten to most flat-teeth, though she is likely younger. Brak reads around four, give or take the usual chaos of halfblood childhood, nutrition, and nobody remembering exactly when anything happened.

Gravel pretends he is only looking after Grenda’s brats.

Nobody believes him.

Sella watches everything. She has already learned which regulars are safe, which are loud, which are sad, and which ones Toots is going to hurt before the night is out. She is hopeful without being foolish, which is a difficult balance and therefore probably doomed to attract plot.

Brak follows Gravel’s stance.

He plants his feet wide, folds his arms, and scowls at doors that have done nothing wrong.

Gravel has not noticed.

Everyone else has.

No one tells him, because watching him fail to understand fatherhood is one of the quieter amusements of the house.

## Toots

**Toots** chose the name herself, as most halfbloods do.

It sounds harmless.

That is the joke.

Toots has greyish skin, broad shoulders, a hard body, an ugly face, and an impressive pair of breasts which she displays with the casual confidence of a woman who knows the difference between being looked at and being touched.

She often waits tables topless.

Regulars learn the house rule quickly:

Look if you must.

Do not touch.

Newcomers learn the same rule when their hands heal.

Toots is tough, rough, and sweeter than she has any interest in advertising. She is part of the family in every way that matters. She dotes on Sella and Brak, watches the room for regulars who are too drunk, too sad, or too close to making a permanent mistake, and is fiercely protective of Druuna.

She is not stupid.

Men make that mistake because men continue being men even when provided with centuries of evidence that this is a poor survival strategy.

Toots has hopes. She has already bought herself one fine silk shirt. She wears it when the king comes slumming, which is to say when someone important enough visits that she wants the room to understand she is not just skin, muscle, and threat.

The shirt is not armor.

That is why it matters.

Someday she wants more soft things that are hers because she bought them, kept them, and nobody took them away.

## Druuna Sunseeker

[[Druuna Sunseeker]] remains a staple of the Blood and Sand.

Her arrangement is clear:

No proselytizing in the Blood and Sand.

She may preach one-on-one if approached by someone curious.

This agreement has probably saved more peace than any formal decree would have. Druuna does not shout doctrine over the ale. She does not stand on tables. She does not turn breakfast into a sermon unless someone asks a question and then has the misfortune of receiving an honest answer.

She prays with people.

She brings food.

She washes wounds.

She sits with the dying.

She continues being impossible in the quiet way that makes harder people rearrange their lives around protecting her.

The Blood and Sand claims her as its own. Gravel will say this simply, without ornament, and anyone wise will hear the warning inside it.

After the Solgard examination, Druuna is not recognized as polished. She is not learned. She cannot recite everything she should. She remains under instruction and is expected to present herself for proper temple teaching.

But she is not false.

That matters more down here than proper phrasing.

## Morg

[[Morg]] was once a regular fixture at the Blood and Sand.

He has packed his things and joined [[Orin Kharne]]’s army with a group of volunteers.

He is gone from the house now.

Not forgotten.

Gone.

If Kharne will not knight him or give him a real chance, Morg will likely return to the mercenary life. There is bitterness in that, but also wisdom. Morg has learned enough to know that respect spoken loudly is not always opportunity offered honestly.

Slab replaced the function.

He did not replace the man.

## Longjaw

**Longjaw** was once on the wrong side of a territory dispute with Wonderful and the adventurers.

[[Orin Kharne]] cut off his arm.

Longjaw survived, because some men are too stubborn to die when the plot would be cleaner without them. The infection was killing him afterward. He scraped together his last coin, took **Tinnet** with him, and came to beg Thalrik to take the girl in for work.

He was not begging for himself.

That helped.

Tinnet was the daughter of Longjaw’s best friend, **Dugger**. Dugger is gone. Longjaw is not the girl’s father, but in the Dregs that distinction matters less than whether a man stands between a child and the people pricing her future by the hour.

Puberty had begun to show on Tinnet, and a dwarven madam from the Butcher’s Quarter had started sniffing around.

Longjaw knew exactly what that meant.

He came to the Blood and Sand before the Hammer’s Rest could close its hand around her.

Longjaw was healed by [[Lyosha Ohotnik]]. His arm is mostly back now, though it aches, itches, and does not yet feel entirely like his. He keeps it wrapped when he works the street. The beggar disguise benefits from people thinking the limb is diseased, cursed, or simply unpleasant enough not to investigate.

Longjaw now watches the streets disguised as a beggar.

He hears things.

He sees things.

He is easy to ignore, which is the closest the Dregs has to invisibility.

### Tinnet

**Tinnet**, called **Tiny**, works in the kitchen at the Blood and Sand.

No last name.

She is young, sharp, and newly aware that adults looking at her can mean different things. Grenda watches her. Toots watches her. Longjaw watches the street. Gravel watches everyone who watches too long.

Tiny is learning kitchen work, house habits, and the difference between being sheltered and being owned.

The Blood and Sand is not gentle.

But it is better than the Hammer’s Rest.

That bar is low enough to trip over, but in the Dregs even low bars keep some monsters out.

## The Hammer's Rest

The woman who came looking for Tiny was **Morna Coalbraid**, dwarven madam of **The Hammer’s Rest** in the Butcher’s Quarter.

Morna is a former blacksmith, built like a forge anvil and twice as hard to move. She runs a brothel featuring half-orc girls and keeps iron discipline in the house.

She does not tolerate mistreatment by customers.

This makes her better than some.

It does not make her safe.

Morna’s bodyguard is **Mogda**, a female half-ogre who works the door. Mogda is silent, devastating when needed, and generally sufficient to end most negotiations before they become expensive.

Morna Coalbraid is not a cartoon villain. That would be easier. She may believe she offers protection, food, rules, and a roof to girls who would otherwise be chewed apart by the street.

The Blood and Sand does not care.

Tiny is theirs now.

## Slab

**Slab** is the new doorman.

He may be three-quarters orc.

He may be an orog with something else in the blood.

No one knows.

No one asks.

Slab is as communicative and movable as his name implies. He is neither nice nor cruel. He is patient. He moves with the inevitability of a landslide and the moral urgency of a locked gate.

Silk once tested him and enjoyed the result enough to relay it to Thalrik.

> “I came and got the attention of Slab. I understand he’s the new doorman. When I offered him a silver piece to sneak us into the common room before opening, he said no. I offered him two, he said, ‘Good. Now I’ll have two coins put on your eyes when I float your body down the river. Piss off.’ When I offered him a third, he told me he didn’t understand why I wanted to float my dead body down the river with coins on my eyes and one up my ass.”

Silk respects fearlessness.

Slab respects clarity.

Their relationship is probably healthy by Dregs standards, which is to say neither has stabbed the other and both would notice if someone else tried.

Slab is not clever in the quick way.

He is clever in the way walls are clever.

## Scrub

**Scrub** does a little of everything.

Mostly dishes.

He is not as large as Slab, but the locals have noticed he is trained. He once took out a bigger halfblood with a clean hold no one learns on the street unless the street has developed a curriculum and started charging tuition.

He did it without breaking a sweat.

This produced speculation.

The staff enjoy speculating.

That is permitted.

Scrub can play the ignorant dumb halfblood when needed. He hams it up well enough that outsiders dismiss him. The staff know better. They know he is more than he appears. They do not know exactly what.

That is also permitted.

> [!secret] GM Only  
> Scrub is Lantern Watch, one of Silk’s Cell.  
>   
> He is in the Blood and Sand with Thalrik’s blessing. His job is to keep an eye on the feeling in the Dregs, because the ward is porous enough for trouble to slip in and out before anyone respectable admits there is smoke.  
>   
> Privately, his deeper purpose is to help prevent another Elderbrook.  
>   
> Only the PCs know the truth. The staff know he is trained. They do not know he is Watch.

## Elderbrook

Elderbrook was once a halfblood refuge.

Then the Kursk, Theron, and Marvelous disaster kicked over the lantern.

Metaphorically.

Mostly.

The name remains a wound in Sylvanranth lore and a warning to anyone who thinks halfblood anger, civic neglect, romantic stupidity, noble power, and open flame can be safely stored in the same room.

Scrub’s presence at the Blood and Sand is not about arrests.

Not first.

It is about noticing when the air changes.

The Dregs burns quickly when fed the right lies.

## House Rules

The Blood and Sand has rules.

They are not painted prettily over the bar because most of the clientele read fists better than signs.

- Druuna may speak of Pelor one-on-one when approached. No shouting doctrine over the room.
- Staff eat. No exceptions. Grenda enforces this with terrifying practicality.
- Look at Toots if you must. Touch her and leave damaged.
- Children are not prey.
- Fights happen in the pit, not wherever some drunk decides his feelings need architecture.
- Debts are remembered.
- Outsiders may spend coin. Outsiders may not buy people.
- Trouble with the Blood and Sand becomes trouble with Gravel.
- Trouble with Gravel may become trouble with Thalrik.
- Trouble with Thalrik may become a civic matter, a religious matter, a Watch matter, or a river matter, depending on how stupid the offender has been.

## Current Role in the Dregs

The Blood and Sand is now several things at once.

A tavern.

A fighting pit.

A halfblood household.

A street office.

A refuge.

A business.

A listening post.

A place where Druuna can feed people before sunrise.

A place where pit fighters can become names instead of corpses.

A place where a young halfblood boss learns letters badly, numbers well, and power carefully.

A place where Thalrik’s old trouble has become Gravel’s responsibility.

A place where Wonderful’s civic interest and the Lantern Watch’s quiet concern both touch the same dirty tables.

It is not nice.

It is not safe.

It is becoming reliable.

In the Dregs, that is practically a cathedral.

With worse hygiene and better betting.

## Adventure Uses

- A scheduled pit night draws a fighter with political backing and a reason to start trouble.
- Morna Coalbraid tries to reclaim Tiny by legal pressure rather than force.
- Someone notices Longjaw’s regrown arm and begins asking what Lyosha can do for the desperate.
- Scrub hears the first hints of another Elderbrook-shaped fire.
- Gravel’s accountant finds a discrepancy that points toward someone using the Blood and Sand to launder Vogel coin.
- Toots’s silk shirt becomes a point of insult, admiration, theft, or unexpected courtly recognition.
- Brak calls Gravel something dangerously close to “Da” in front of the wrong person.
- Druuna’s monthly temple instruction draws attention from Pelorites who approve of her less than Solgard did.
- A pit fighter becomes a local hero, then a liability.
- Wonderful’s ownership piece becomes politically inconvenient.
- Thalrik’s old enemies decide hurting Gravel is a way to reach him.

## Tone Notes

The Blood and Sand should feel rough, funny, dangerous, and weirdly tender.

This is not a sanitized refuge. It is a dirty tavern in a hard ward run by people who have done violence, sold violence, survived violence, and occasionally found enough decency left under the bruises to protect someone else.

The crew are not saints.

Except possibly Druuna, and even she would object because she has chores.

The point is not that the Blood and Sand has become good.

The point is that it has become loyal.

In the Dregs, that may be better.B
