## The Candle Line of Blackbriar

Along the yellow hills east and southeast of Griefwatch runs a chain of small forts known formally as **the Blackbriar Thornline Watch**. No one calls it that unless they are writing orders, receiving funding, or trying to sound important in front of a duke.

The soldiers call it **the Candle Line**.

That name began as a joke, because each post is expected to light its beacon when danger comes. It stopped being funny after enough men proved the comparison too honest. A candle gives warning by burning itself down.

The Candle Line is not meant to stop an invasion. That would be fantasy, and House Amadis does not indulge in comforting nonsense unless it comes bottled from the western vineyards. The posts are meant to see the swarm, confirm its numbers, force it to slow, and buy Griefwatch Castle the hours it needs to wake the Thornward, summon riders, and close the roads behind the villages.

Each fort is small, hard, and ugly: a squat stone tower, a walled yard, a cistern, a signal brazier, a mule-stable, a dry powder room, and bunks for men who learn to sleep in armor. Veterans rotate through them in short watches. Green soldiers are rarely sent unless someone wants them ruined early.

Life on the Candle Line is one of two things: **boring or terrifying**. The men prefer boring. They complain anyway. It is tradition.

### North Fang Post — 115.042

North Fang Post is the coldest of the line, built where the yellow hills begin to climb into rougher stone beneath Griefwatch’s eastern shadow. It watches the goat tracks, high saddles, and broken hill-paths that raiders use when they are too few to risk the main passes but too ambitious to stay home and annoy their own mothers.

It is considered a lonely post rather than a doomed one, which is the kind of distinction soldiers make when they have run out of better jokes. Its beacon is visible from Griefwatch in clear weather, and its horn-codes carry strangely well through the upper gullies.

North Fang’s garrison tends to be small: scouts, bowmen, two or three hard sergeants, and one knight or senior man-at-arms who knows the difference between a wolf pack, a raiding party, and the first breath of a swarm.

### Widow’s Lantern — 116.043

Widow’s Lantern stands on a bare rise south of Griefwatch, its tower taller than its walls because the tower is the point. The beacon cage atop it is oversized, blackened, and visible for miles. Every man stationed there notices this immediately and takes the lesson personally.

The post’s name predates the current tower. Supposedly, an Amadis widow once lit the old signal fire herself after the garrison was found dead and the raiders were already moving west. Whether true or improved by several generations of tavern-weathering, the tale stuck.

Widow’s Lantern is less exposed than Redtooth but more important to the signal chain. If Widow’s Lantern goes dark, Griefwatch assumes the hills are already compromised.

### Redtooth Tower — 115.044

Redtooth Tower sits in the hard middle of the Candle Line, where the hill corridor narrows enough to force a moving horde into a shape that archers can punish. Its walls are low, thick, and scarred. Its yard is too small. Its cistern is deep. Its chapel alcove faces west, toward home.

Redtooth has been rebuilt twice.

The second rebuilding preserved a blackened section of inner wall, because House Amadis can turn even masonry into a guilt lecture.

This is the tower where **Sir Maelon Amadis** made his stand during the **Third Black Banner Push**, when a Dominion-backed orc horde came west under Hextorite priests and half-orc slave-soldiers. The Dominion did not march under its own banners. They rarely do when they can send someone else to bleed first. The horde came in layers: orc clans promised plunder, chained half-orc shock troops driven forward by priest-handlers, and black-robed Hextorite war-clerics chanting tyranny into courage and obedience.

Redtooth saw them first.

Sir Maelon was not supposed to be there. He had come east with his wife, two grown sons, and a younger daughter to inspect the post and review its accounts. It was meant to be a dull duty visit, the kind of noble housekeeping that keeps soldiers fed and quartermasters honest. The horde reached the hills two days early under storm cover.

Maelon sent three riders west. Only one reached Widow’s Lantern.

Then he barred the gate.

The garrison held through the first assault, then the second, then the night attack. Maelon’s sons fought on the wall beside the post soldiers. His daughter, Elira, carried bolts, water, and lamp oil until the dead became too many and she took up a spear herself. His wife, Lady Anwen, kept the wounded alive in the lower room until the priests began throwing fire through the arrow slits.

At dawn, the Hextorites offered terms: open the gate, surrender the Amadis blood, and the common soldiers would be spared.

Maelon answered by hanging the Dominion envoy from the beacon frame and lighting the signal beneath him.

Subtle? No. Effective? Extremely.

The tower burned for most of the morning. Redtooth’s defenders fought room by room after the outer wall cracked. When the relief column from Griefwatch finally arrived, they found the horde delayed, disordered, and trapped in the hill throat long enough for Amadis cavalry to hit them from the west and Orenic riders to strike their southern flank.

No member of Maelon’s household survived.

His line ended at Redtooth.

The tower did not.

Today, Redtooth’s chapel keeps a plain stone plaque:

> **Sir Maelon Amadis, Lady Anwen, Ser Caldus, Ser Rovan, and Elira of the Spear.**  
> **They did not yield the gate.**

Amadis children are taught the names before they are taught the wine vintages. Which explains a lot, frankly.

### Ashspur Post — 116.045

Ashspur Post guards the southern hill corridor, where the land is lower, drier, and easier for large bands to move through. The hills around it were burned bare during one of the older orc pushes, and even now the scrub grows thin and gray in patches. The soldiers say the ground remembers fire.

Ashspur is not as famous as Redtooth, but it has sounded more warnings than any other post in the line. Most incursions begin as movement near Ashspur: smoke too far east, birds lifting from the wrong ridge, shepherds vanishing, or scouts returning with that flat-eyed look that makes officers stop asking clever questions.

It has a reputation for producing excellent sergeants and miserable musicians. No one knows why the second thing keeps happening.

### Dead Lance Tower — 115.046

Dead Lance Tower is the southern hill anchor of the Candle Line. It stands where the last broken hills begin sloping toward easier ground and the approaches to Three-Crooks Gate. Its name comes from a failed relief charge during the **Red Rain Raid**, when mounted Amadis troops attempted to reach the old tower before the post fell.

They arrived too late.

The field below the tower was found littered with snapped lances, dead horses, and enough blood that rainwater ran pink in the gullies for two days. The current tower was raised on the same site, because House Amadis regards “this place killed us” as an argument for building there harder.

Dead Lance is considered dangerous but honorable duty. The garrison usually includes more cavalry scouts than the northern posts, because when Dead Lance lights, the threat is often already spilling toward the flats.

---

### Crookhorn Hold — 114.047

Crookhorn Hold is the southern connector of the line and the safest assignment, which means everyone stationed there has to endure endless mockery from the men at Redtooth and Ashspur. Unlike the hill towers, Crookhorn sits in flatter country where stone, timber, wagons, and masons can actually reach the site without requiring a prayer, a mule, and a grudge.

Because of that, it is larger: a proper small fort rather than a tower-post. It has a broader yard, better stables, a smithy, extra grain stores, and a signal platform tied into both **Three-Crooks Gate** and the Orenic patrol roads. It is the handshake between Blackbriar and the red lands: not quite Amadis alone, not quite Orenic business, but watched closely by both.

Crookhorn’s battle history is less glorious and more useful. During the **Cinder Moon Incursion**, its garrison did not face the main swarm. Instead, it caught three separate bands of Dominion-led half-orc slave-soldiers trying to slip west around the Candle Line after Redtooth and Ashspur had already lit. Crookhorn held its gates, launched riders to Three-Crooks, and trapped the raiders between its own walls and an Orenic patrol coming north.

That action made Crookhorn famous among quartermasters and scouts rather than bards. It did exactly what a connector fort should do: it kept a bad day from becoming a disaster.

Soldiers still call it “safe,” but only compared to the others. This is Blackbriar. Safe is graded on a curve, and the curve has teeth.
