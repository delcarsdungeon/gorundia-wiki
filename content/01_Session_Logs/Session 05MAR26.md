# Thalrik Arrives

The great doors of the hall swing inward with a thunder of iron and oak.

The herald steps forward, already pale. He unfurls a scroll.

It keeps unfurling.

A murmur rolls through the assembled lords.

He inhales.

“Presenting—Sir Thalrik Vaultforge, the Ironwind—”

“Knight-Captain of the Realm, dubbed by the sacred hand and sovereign blade of His Most August Majesty, King Rimmand the Second—”

A respectful hush.

“Admiral of the Gorundian Sky Fleet—”

There it is. A ripple. Suppressed smiles. Someone in Allaric actually nods, as though this is perfectly ordinary.

Thalrik strides forward through the double doors, bicorn set like a declaration of war. Gold braid flashes in torchlight.

The herald presses on.

“Vice-Admiral of the Gorundian Navy—”

From Grevandar’s benches, Countess Zara Vallytrex speaks, velvet over steel:

“Vice-Admiral,” she purrs.
“I do so appreciate the emphasis on vice.”

A few nobles go rigid trying not to laugh. One poor clerk nearly drops his quill.

Thalrik does not look at her.

He keeps walking.

The herald, voice tightening but committed:

“Captain of the Royal Airship Second Draft, Sovereign Vessel of the Upper Currents—”

A pause. Audible processing across the chamber.

“—and former Captain of the sea-going vessel Rum Runner, duly commissioned under Crown writ—”

A couple of Ravenster knights exchange a look that says of course he was.

“Bearer of the Royal Sky-Sigil and Commander of All Aerial Assets of the Crown—”

A tiny breath.

“—presently consisting of one commissioned warship.”

A ripple of restrained laughter.

He soldiers on.

“Holder of the Third Anchor Seal—”

“Defender of the High Winds and Warden of Crown Trade Routes by Air and Sea—”

“Legal Counsel to the Lord Marshal of Gorundia, entrusted with writ, charter, and maritime judgment—”

That lands differently. Eyes shift. A few nobles stop smirking.

“Sworn servant of Crown and Sky.”

The scroll snaps shut.

The herald, visibly relieved:

“Sir Thalrik Vaultforge.”

Silence settles.

Thalrik reaches the center of the chamber.

Bootheels strike stone.

Coat sweeping. Sabre at his side. Medallion catching the torchlight.

He does not bow.

He stands.

Fleet of one.

Knighted by the king.

Advisor to the Lord Marshal.

Vice-Admiral—emphasis noted.

Captain of a ship called Second Draft.

And looking, for all the world, like a man who expects history to require revision.

# Thalrik’s Opening Address Before the Court of Grundia

“My Lords and Ladies of the Court… Honored Magistrates… loyal citizens of Grundia.”

“I am Thalrik, Admiral of the Grand Fleet — sworn to defend our waters, our people, and the sanctity of the throne.”

“I stand before you today not to indulge in spectacle…”

“I stand here to enter into record an account of treason so profound that even speaking it aloud feels like summoning a shadow.”

Turns slowly toward Vogel.

“Wilhelm Vogel has not merely erred in judgment.”

“He has bartered this nation.”

“Bartered it in ink, in coin… and in blood.”

“I submit before you sealed correspondence, infernal contracts bearing diabolic marks, transactional ledgers, and burial records.”

“These documents prove that Wilhelm Vogel entered into covenant with devils — not metaphorical ones whispered about in taverns…”

“Literal entities of Hell.”

“And what was the price of his ambition?”

Pause.

“Virgin souls.”

“Impoverished young women he courted, married, and then conveniently laid to rest.”

“I present financial transfers executed immediately following their deaths.”

“I present correspondence confirming ‘deliveries fulfilled.’”

“He did not woo wives.”

“He procured sacrifices.”

“And when influence alone did not move him close enough to the throne…”

“He drafted contingencies.”

“Plans to unleash demons and devils in violent coups against this very nation — against this Court — against the sovereignty he pretends to serve.”

Steps forward, voice steady.

“As heinous as Wilhelm Vogel is… his antithesis would be a day at The Blood and Sand on God’s Day — where the first drink is on the house and the worst sin committed is overindulging in ale.”

“Compared to that honest debauchery…”

“He is calculated damnation.”

“Patriots defend their people.”

“They do not sell daughters of Grundia for infernal leverage.”

“They do not mortgage our future to Hell for a crown.”

“I place these dossiers into the record.”

“And I ask this Court — not as an Admiral, not as a rival…”

“But as a son of this nation…”

“If a man who bargains with devils, murders his brides for their souls, and prepares demons as instruments of rule is fit to sit upon our throne…”

“Then perhaps we should replace the crown with a collar.”

Long pause.

“Because if Wilhelm Vogel is innocent…”

“Then Hell itself is owed an apology.”

# In the Gallery

A flicker at the stair.

Aylassa arrives late.

Of course she does.

She beams.

Radiant, irrepressible, waving brightly at Fayge as if this were a spring festival instead of the Lord’s Muster.

Beringer sees her climbing toward the front row.

She spots him.

Waves.

Keeps climbing.

She drops neatly into the seat beside Caelina Redmarch, directly adjacent to Dana Sylvanranth and Dame Venom.

Dana’s eyebrows rise.

“How,” she asks evenly,“and why are you here?”

Aylassa smiles like a cat with documentation.

She produces a rolled parchment and hands it over.

Dana unseals it.

Venom leans in.

They read.

Venom titters softly.

Dana blinks once.

“Lady Silvershire?”

Aylassa nods.

“Yep.”

She leans toward Caelina — who did not ask — and murmurs conspiratorially:

“Those hin are productive and don’t need much oversight. You only need to whip them once a week, tops.”

Caelina turns slowly, warm eyes steady.

“Oh, I’m sure they’re very grateful for your kindness.”

The words are gentle.

The meaning is not.

Aylassa grins wider.

“I did a favor for a nobleman.”

A beat.

“Nothing scandalous. Just pruning the family tree.”

Dana exhales slowly.

Venom nearly laughs aloud.

Caelina tilts her head.

“How fortunate,” she says softly,“that the branches you trim never seem to fall on you.”

Aylassa laughs.

Then she pops to her feet.

A sharp whistle.

From her sleeve, she flicks something down toward the chamber floor — spinning fast.

A cookie arcs like a sugared shuriken.

Thranduil catches it without looking.

Glances up.

Smiles faintly.

Devours it.

Aylassa blows him a kiss and sits back down.

“See? A little passive income and a safe house tucked away in Araisal lands is… useful.”

She pats Caelina’s knee lightly.

Caelina shakes her head, amused despite herself.

Below, the King resumes his seat.

The Verdant Bench sits freshly blessed.

The hall breathes again.

And soon, the dukes will be announced in married pairs.

But for this moment —

The Crown has been moved.

And everyone saw it.
