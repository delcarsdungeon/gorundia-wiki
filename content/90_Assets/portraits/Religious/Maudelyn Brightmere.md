# Sister Maudelyn Brightmere

> [!quote] Sister Maudelyn Brightmere  
> “A shield left to rust is not honored. It is neglected.”

**Sister Maudelyn Brightmere**, usually called **Sister Maud**, is a young nun of Heironeous assigned as personal assistant to [[Cwenheld]] during her journey aboard the airship to join the [[Beringer|Lord Marshal]]’s host.

Officially, she is a reader, clerk, and devotional attendant.

Unofficially, she is a nurse, handler, minder, and the temple’s last desperate attempt to keep a half-blind holy terror from dying of infection, exhaustion, untreated swelling, or bravery. Because apparently sending elderly saints into active war zones is “tradition” and not “evidence for a kingdom-wide intervention.”

Cwenheld knows exactly why Maud is there.

Maud knows Cwenheld knows.

Neither of them has admitted this aloud, because there are forms of war too cruel even for Heironeous.

---

## Appearance

Maudelyn is plain-featured, serious-eyed, and usually looks as though she has already heard the nonsense you are about to say and found it wanting.

She wears a dark blue and white habit cut for travel, usually beneath a practical cloak. In war zones she wears mail or a breastplate marked clearly with the white shield of her order, but she does not carry a weapon in human battles.

Her satchel is always close at hand and contains bandages, salves, bitter tonics, wax tablets, writing charcoal, clean linen, a tiny devotional symbol of Heironeous, and a notebook full of medical observations written in a severe little hand.

---

## Personality

Maud is painfully earnest, intensely dutiful, and almost completely unable to lie with grace.

She is not sweet in the Pelorite sense. She is not warm, sunny, comforting, or inclined to tell wounded soldiers that everything will be all right unless she has strong evidence that it will be.

She is Heironean.

Her comfort sounds like:

> “You are not dead. Therefore you still have duties.”

This works better than it should.

She is deeply compassionate, but her compassion has a spine. She believes warriors deserve dignity, cleanliness, food, medicine, honest speech, and someone willing to tell them when they are being proud idiots.

---

## Role with Cwenheld

Maud’s duties include:

- Reading letters, reports, and orders to Cwenheld.
- Writing replies in a clean temple hand.
- Managing Cwenheld’s medicines, meals, rest, bandages, and private care.
- Watching for fever, infection, exhaustion, falls, confusion, pain suppression, and heroic foolishness.
- Ensuring Cwenheld is treated as a revered knight, not an inconvenient relic.

Cwenheld calls her **Little Marshal**.

Maud calls Cwenheld **Dame Cwenheld** in public, **my lady** when worried, and **you promised** when she has run out of other weapons.

That last one works.

Usually.

# Order of the Unbroken Shield

> [!quote] Order Saying  
> “We do not lift the sword. We keep the sword-arm alive.”

The **Order of the Unbroken Shield** is a clerical-medical order within the faith of [[Heironeous]], dedicated to battlefield rescue, trauma care, and the lifelong tending of warriors broken in righteous service.

They are not pacifists.

They are not Pelorites in blue.

They are Heironeans who believe that courage does not end when a knight falls, and that abandoning the wounded is a form of cowardice.

Pure healers are rare in Heironeous’ service. Most clergy of the Invincible are chaplains, war-priests, knightly confessors, or militant leaders. The Unbroken Shield fills a necessary and uncomfortable niche: they serve among the battle lines without making war their first act.

They are the people who run toward screaming.

Naturally, soldiers think they are insane.

Soldiers are not wrong.

---

## Duties of the Order

- Recover the wounded from active battlefields.
- Stabilize knights, soldiers, squires, bannermen, and camp followers.
- Maintain field infirmaries behind the line.
- Escort dying Heironeans through final rites when no chaplain can reach them.
- Record the names of the fallen and the last words of those who can still speak.
- Run hospices for Heironean veterans who will never return to the field.
- Preserve the dignity of maimed, blinded, burned, crippled, or aging warriors.

The Pelorites would gladly treat such veterans, and often do when asked. But the Unbroken Shield exists because Heironeans believe some wounds must be understood by one’s own.

A Pelorite may heal the body.

The Unbroken Shield tends the body that still dreams it is holding formation.

---

## Battle Conduct

In battles between mortal peoples, members of the Unbroken Shield are clearly marked and do not carry weapons.

They wear armor. Usually mail, breastplates, or reinforced medical habits.

They carry stretchers, satchels, water, bandages, hooks, rope, splints, and shields used for cover or hauling the wounded. Their shields are painted white with a blue upright sword wrapped in a bandage.

They do not strike mortal enemies except in immediate defense of the wounded.

They do not execute prisoners.

They do not finish fallen enemies.

They do not abandon fallen allies because the line moved.

This has gotten many of them killed.

This is why even hardened knights tend to lower their voices around them.

---

## Exception: Monsters and Unclean Foes

Against monsters, undead, fiends, constructs of slaughter, or creatures that do not respect surrender, ransom, mercy, or battlefield custom, the Unbroken Shield may arm themselves.

Their usual weapons in such cases are maces, staves, hatchets, or short blades intended for defense rather than glory.

Their doctrine is blunt:

> “Mercy belongs to those who can receive it. Monsters receive the shield’s edge.”

Even then, their first priority remains rescue and preservation.

---

## Hospices of the Unbroken Shield

The order maintains quiet hospices near major Heironean temples and fortress-shrines.

These houses serve:

- Blind knights.
- Amputees.
- Veterans with shattered nerves or recurring battle-fever.
- Elderly paladins no longer fit for campaign.
- War-priests whose bodies outlived their strength.
- Retired shield-bearers, squires, and standard-guards.

The hospices are not soft places, exactly.

They are clean, disciplined, prayerful, and direct. Residents are expected to rise when able, pray when able, eat properly, assist others if they can, and stop pretending pain is a personality.

This is considered mercy.

Heironeous has never been accused of making comfort too comfortable.

In fortress-monasteries, the hospice knights are not hidden away. When their injuries allow, they teach: tactics from chairs, formation discipline from benches, battlefield judgment from scarred hands laid over old maps. Some noble houses quietly tuck their maimed veterans into side estates or back rooms where polite guests need not be reminded what banners purchase. The paladins of Heironeous do the opposite. They make novices look directly at what war costs, because courage that cannot face the wounded is only pageantry with better lighting.

---

## Reputation

Among young knights, the Unbroken Shield are sometimes dismissed as “linen sisters” or “bandage monks.”

Among veterans, no such jokes are made twice.

Most soldiers know at least one story of an Unbroken Shield medic crossing killing ground to retrieve a man everyone else had written off.

Their courage is not theatrical. They do not roar. They do not call challenges. They do not seek songs.

They crawl through mud under arrows, drag the screaming into cover, press hands over bleeding wounds, and say prayers so steady they make panic feel embarrassed.

---

## Common Sayings

> “The line is not held if the fallen are forgotten.”

> “Courage brought him there. Duty brings him back.”

> “Bind the wound. Name the fallen. Carry the living.”

> “No sword today. Only the shield.”

> “If he can still hear us, he is not alone.”

---

## Maudelyn’s Place in the Order

Maudelyn is young for field assignment, but not inexperienced. She served first in a hospice attached to a Heironean veterans’ house, tending men and women who had survived battles their bodies never truly left.

There she developed the conviction that Gorundia honors old heroes very loudly and cares for them very inconsistently.

Her private project, never officially approved, is a manual titled:

> **The Care of Saints Who Refuse to Admit They Are Mortal**

It is part medical guide, part devotional text, part quiet institutional accusation.

The senior sisters have not confiscated it.

That means they either approve or are waiting to see who it offends first.

With Maud, either is possible.

### Vigil Draught

**Vigil Draught** is the common military name for **Antaeus**, the alchemical Elixir of Endurance. Soldiers also call it **Knight’s Wake**.

> [!info]- GURPS Notes
> In game terms, Antaeus allows the subject to feel no fatigue for 1d hours, though fatigue from the subject’s own spellcasting still applies normally.

The Order of the Unbroken Shield hates casual use of it. It does not grant true rest. It suppresses the warning signs of exhaustion and encourages commanders to mistake bodily debt for discipline.

> “Vigil Draught does not grant rest. It postpones the debt and adds interest.”

Maud will tolerate Antaeus in true emergency: evacuation, forced retreat, siege breach, rescue under fire, or holding a line until relief arrives.

She will not tolerate officers using it because they failed to sleep like mortal beings with organs.

---

## Secrets and Hooks

Maud once cared for a dying Heironean veteran who raved about a battle that officially never happened.

In his final fever, he named Cwenheld as one of the only survivors and whispered:

> “She heard the bells under the earth.”

Maud has never asked Cwenheld what that means.

She is afraid Cwenheld will answer.
