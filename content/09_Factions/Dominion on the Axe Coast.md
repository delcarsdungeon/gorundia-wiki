# **Dominion Presence on the Axe Coast**

## **Short Answer (Table Version)**

They are:

* **Not official**
* **Not absent**
* **Absolutely active**

They operate through **three layers simultaneously**:

1. **Legitimate trade cover**
2. **Covert intelligence & religious cells**
3. **Black operations / asset acquisition**

And depending on who’s looking:

> They are either “just merchants”… or the opening move of a war.

---

# **Layer 1 — The Overt Delegation (The Lie Everyone Accepts)**

## **The Khorovišk Trade Mission**

Located in: **Veyra Saltspire** (and lightly in Myldenär)

### Public Face:

* “Independent merchant consortium”
* Specializing in:

  * metalwork
  * grain contracts
  * “frontier logistics”
* Extremely disciplined, very polite, slightly unsettling

### Reality:

This is a **state-sponsored economic foothold**.

They are:

* mapping trade flows
* identifying chokepoints
* buying influence quietly

Because the Dominion learned long ago:

> You don’t conquer chaos. You *organize it first.* 

---

### Key NPC: **Envoy-Merchant Kazar Volen**

* Speaks like a diplomat
* Thinks like a quartermaster
* Keeps perfect records
* Never raises his voice

**Actual Role:**
Logistics intelligence officer for the Dominion

---

### What Thalrik Would Clock Immediately

> “Those aren’t merchants. Those are soldiers pretending they haven’t been told to kill anyone yet.”

---

# **Layer 2 — The Hidden Structure (The Real Game)**

## **Iron Hierarchy Cells**

Scattered through:

* Draeven’s Reach
* inland caravan routes
* certain “quiet” shrines

These are:

* **Hextorite cells**
* Led by:

  * Iron Prelates
  * Scourge Commissars in civilian cover

Their job:

* recruit (desperate, violent, or ambitious types)
* test ideological loyalty
* establish **obedience networks**

Because in Dominion doctrine:

> Control comes from hierarchy, not territory 

---

### What They’re Actually Building

Not an invasion.

A **framework**.

So that if the Dominion ever moves in force:

* supply lines already exist
* collaborators are already in place
* resistance is already mapped

---

## **The Quiet Truth**

Some of the “Hill Bastards”?

Yeah.

A couple of them are already:

> **On Dominion coin.**

They don’t even realize how deep that goes yet.

---

# **Layer 3 — Black Operations (What They Deny Exists)**

## **The Ash Hand**

Unofficial Dominion field group.

No banners. No uniforms.

Used for:

* artifact recovery
* sabotage
* “problem resolution”

---

### Their Priorities on the Coast:

1. **Draconis Island**
2. **Black Veil interaction (avoid / study / exploit)**
3. **Spelljammer tech (YOU)**

---

### Key Detail You Want to Use

They are **deeply uncomfortable** with the Black Veil.

Not afraid.

Not dismissive.

Just:

> “That is outside the system.”

Even the Dominion—who built itself on surviving horror—doesn’t like something that doesn’t obey rules .

---

# **So Where Are They, Exactly?**

## **Primary Hub**

* **Veyra Saltspire**
  → Trade mission (overt)

## **Secondary Nodes**

* **Draeven’s Reach**
  → black market interface, Dominion goods already leak here 

* **Caravan routes near Ironvale / Thorn Pact**
  → covert influence

## **Mobile Presence**

* Ships flying:

  * neutral colors
  * Salt Compact contracts

## **Hidden Sites**

* 1–2 inland fortified “warehouses” that are absolutely not just warehouses

# **How They Interact With the Party**

This is where it gets fun.

## **Phase 1 — Interest**

They approach as:

* polite merchants
* offering:

  * fuel
  * trade contracts
  * “secure landing rights”

## **Phase 2 — Assessment**

They test:

* discipline
* leadership
* internal fractures

## **Phase 3 — Decision**

They decide:

* **Asset?** → try to recruit or bind
* **Threat?** → contain or remove
* **Unknown?** → observe longer

---

# **Key Flavor Difference (Sell This Hard)**

Everyone else on the Axe Coast is:

> chaotic, reactive, opportunistic

The Dominion is:

> patient, structured, inevitable

They don’t rush.

They **prepare**.

# **The Real GM Lever (This Is Your Knife)**

You don’t introduce them as villains.

You introduce them as:

* helpful
* reliable
* *better organized than anyone else*

And let the players realize:

> “Oh… these guys are the only ones here who could actually take this place over.”
