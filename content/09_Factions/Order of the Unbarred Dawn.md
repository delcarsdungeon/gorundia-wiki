# Order of the Unbarred Dawn

> “Mercy opens the door. Valor keeps it open. Truth writes down who tried to bar it.”
> — Mother Eadlin Mercy-Gate

## Overview

The **Order of the Unbarred Dawn** is a small Triad-linked refuge, hospitaler, and witness order headquartered at [[Mercygate]].

It exists to receive and protect women, children, and other vulnerable persons fleeing unlawful confinement, domestic brutality, debt-bondage, forced service, war displacement, private punishment, and noble abuses hidden behind sealed doors.

The order is not large.

It is not famous.

It is not yet a national institution.

It is a practical mercy that became organized because the same suffering kept arriving at the same gate.

## Origins

## Origins

The Unbarred Dawn began as a problem of hospitality inside [[Mercygate]].

Most of Mercygate’s people were already refugees from the Dominion to the east: half-orcs, border folk, freed laborers, camp followers, widows, children, and broken families who had crossed into Gorundia with little more than fear, hunger, and whatever names they still dared to use.

At first, the sisters dealt mostly with the most vulnerable among them.

A half-orc woman freed from a war camp.

A child born in bondage and carried west under a blanket.

A mother who flinched at every raised voice.

A girl who had no language for freedom because no one had ever taught her she was allowed to refuse.

Each case was treated as individual mercy.

Food was given.  
Beds were found.  
Names were protected.  
Guards were placed at doors.  
Priests were asked to listen first and question later.

Then word spread.

Slowly, others began coming from the west.

Not Dominion refugees.

Gorundians.

A widow fleeing a cruel household.

A child smuggled out of debt-bondage.

A servant girl escaping a locked room no law had ever inspected.

A mother and daughter arriving under a false name, carrying bruises, fear, and no writ anyone wished to examine too closely.

That changed the shape of the work.

The sisters had known the Dominion made chains.

Everyone knew that. It was easy to hate evil when it wore a foreign crest.

But the western road brought quieter chains: family chains, debt chains, noble chains, household chains, and legal-looking papers used to make cruelty respectable.

Over time, the sisters and clerks of Mercygate realized these were not isolated misfortunes.

They were symptoms.

The roads were not producing accidents.

They were revealing systems.

## Formation

Around **1216 OR**, with the support of [[Count Alistair Amadis]], the informal refuge work at Mercygate was organized into a standing religious order under the shared witness of the Triad.

The order was given a small hall, a protected dormitory, a sealed record room, and authority to coordinate with Mercygate’s healers, chaplains, and household guards.

[[Duke Eldric Allaric]] and [[Duchess Maris Allaric]] later provided funds quietly through Allaric charitable channels, allowing the order to maintain wagons, attendants, and a limited number of safe beds without depending entirely on local parish collections.

The Allaric support was never proclaimed loudly.

Which, by Allaric standards, means it was probably discussed for seven years and then done correctly.

## Patronage

### Count Alistair Amadis

[[Count Alistair Amadis]] is the order’s principal secular sponsor.

His support gives the Unbarred Dawn:

- political shelter
- local legitimacy
- access to Mercygate resources
- protection from hostile claimants
- authority to coordinate with Amadis household officials

Alistair does not command the order.

He sponsors it, shields it, and lets it do its work.

### House Allaric

[[House Allaric]] provides quiet financial support.

Allaric funds help cover:

- beds and food
- winter stores
- physician stipends
- record materials
- discreet transport
- maintenance of safe rooms
- stipends for lay attendants

This support reflects Allaric’s broader concern for halfblood rights, vulnerable dependents, and those caught between noble authority and lawful protection.

## Triad Structure

The order draws from all three faiths of the [[Triad of Law and Light]].

### Pelor

Pelorites form the heart of the order.

They oversee:

- healing
- food and warmth
- sleep care
- care for children
- recovery from shock
- mercy rites
- long-term restoration

### Heironeous

Heironean sisters and lay-shields guard the threshold.

They oversee:

- protected rooms
- escort duty
- field discipline
- watch rotations
- courage rites
- witness protection
- cooperation with household guards

### Pholtus

Pholtan record-sisters preserve lawful witness.

They oversee:

- sealed testimony
- false writ review
- unlawful confinement records
- debt and custody disputes
- identity protection
- court packets
- lawful custody review

## Motto

**Mercy finds the living. Valor guards the door. Truth names the chain.**

A shorter field motto is also used:

**The Door Stays Open**

## Symbol

The formal badge of the order is:

**A golden sunrise behind an open silver door, with a white lamp above the threshold.**

A simpler field badge is sometimes used by traveling sisters:

**A silver key laid across a bandaged wrist.**

The formal badge emphasizes sanctuary.

The field badge emphasizes rescue and care.

## Headquarters

The order’s headquarters is in [[Mercygate]], where it maintains:

- a small refuge hall
- a women’s dormitory
- a children’s room
- a locked testimony chamber
- a sealed archive
- a kitchen
- a modest infirmary
- two guarded interview rooms
- space for lay attendants and widows in service

The headquarters is intentionally plain.

No grand façade.  
No dramatic tower.  
No marble monument to mercy, because apparently someone involved had taste.

## Current Scope

As of **10 Coldeven 1231**, the Unbarred Dawn remains limited in reach.

It has:

- one primary refuge at Mercygate
- a small number of trained sisters
- a handful of lay attendants
- limited escort capacity
- a modest wagon network
- restricted funds
- little ability to project aid far from Amadis protection

Its work is respected locally but not widely understood.

Many nobles see it as charity.

Some quietly see it as a nuisance.

A few understand exactly what it threatens.

## Rules of Refuge

The Unbarred Dawn maintains strict rules.

- No rescued person is questioned before care.
- No name is recorded publicly without consent.
- No family is contacted unless requested.
- No claimant may remove a protected person without review.
- No debt claim is honored without scrutiny.
- No armed man enters sleeping quarters without invitation or emergency need.
- No priest, knight, noble, reeve, or patron may compel confession from the protected.
- Testimony is witnessed, sealed, and protected.
- Mercy is not treated as evidence of guilt.

## Notable Members

### Mother Eadlin Mercy-Gate

Mother Eadlin Mercy-Gate is the current superior of the order.

A Pelorite hospitaler by training, she is practical, sharp-eyed, and almost offensively calm.

She speaks softly, which causes fools to underestimate her exactly once.

Her guiding principle:

> “A locked door is not proof of order. Sometimes it is only proof that no one wanted to hear what was happening behind it.”

### Sister Merewen Ashbrook

A Pholtan record-sister responsible for sealed testimony and lawful custody review.

She is meticulous, patient, and has personally ruined several fraudulent debt claims with ink, silence, and the moral warmth of a winter courtroom.

### Sister Hild Cædwyn

A Heironean hospitaler attached to the refuge watch.

Formerly trained among shield-medics, she oversees escorts, guard rotations, and the physical security of protected persons.

She is known for telling frightened children that courage does not mean “not being afraid.”

It means eating soup anyway.

## Reputation

Among common folk, the Unbarred Dawn is spoken of quietly.

A place to send someone.

A door that might open.

A bed where no one asks the wrong question too soon.

Among priests, the order is respected but sometimes considered difficult, because it insists mercy, law, and protection cannot be separated when dealing with the vulnerable.

Among nobles, opinions vary.

The kindly call it necessary.

The cautious call it delicate.

The guilty call it disruptive.

## Notes

The Order of the Unbarred Dawn is not a crusading order.

It does not hunt.

It does not raid.

It does not prosecute by itself.

It receives, protects, heals, records, and bears witness.

Its power lies in making the hidden visible without immediately handing the vulnerable back to the world that harmed them.

In time, the order may grow.

For now, it is small.

But small doors still open.
