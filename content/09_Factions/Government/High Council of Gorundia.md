# High Council of Gorundia

The **High Council of Gorundia** is the king’s senior advisory body, composed of the realm’s highest officers of Crown law, war, treasury, works, court administration, religion, naval affairs, and intelligence.

It is powerful.

It is influential.

It is also, despite what several of its members may privately wish, **not the Crown**.

The High Council advises the king, coordinates urgent matters of state, and may issue provisional instructions in the king’s name when delay would harm the realm. Such instructions are always understood to be **pending His Majesty’s pleasure**.

The king may confirm them.

The king may amend them.

The king may ignore them entirely while thanking the council for its diligence, because monarchy is apparently the art of letting important men speak before doing whatever you were going to do anyway.

## Authority

The High Council may issue:

- provisional suspension orders;
- emergency reviews;
- requests for reports and testimony;
- temporary funding holds;
- preliminary writs and instructions;
- preservation orders for documents, sites, or evidence;
- recommendations to Crown officers, commanders, stewards, and local authorities.

The High Council **cannot** permanently bind the king.

It cannot veto royal decision.

It cannot overrule royal judgment.

It may advise unanimously against a course of action, pound the table, cite law, doctrine, treasury, weather, and common sense, and still be met with:

> “So what I am hearing is, yes, Your Majesty.”

This is considered a normal function of royal government, because civilization enjoys dressing up absurdity in seals and good chairs.

## Current Standing Members

### Lord Chancellor Cadogan Merewyck

**Office:** Lord Chancellor of Gorundia  
**Duties:** Great Seal, royal writs, charters, proclamations, legal form of Crown decisions.

Lord Cadogan is the keeper of lawful form. He does not decide what the Crown should do. He explains what the Crown has accidentally said once the scribes are finished making it official.

He is precise, dry, and professionally allergic to sloppy wording.

### Lord Treasurer Alwyn Greaves

**Office:** Lord Treasurer of Gorundia  
**Duties:** Treasury, war debt, reconstruction funding, royal payments, emergency levies.

Lord Greaves is a **new appointee**, raised to the office after the recent shocks to the realm’s financial and political order.

This makes him both necessary and unfortunate.

He has inherited ledgers distorted by war, corruption, emergency spending, noble privilege, and the charming national habit of declaring something vital without identifying who will pay for it.

### Lord Marshal Beringer Amadis

**Office:** Lord Marshal of Gorundia  
**Duties:** Crown military authority, field command, noble musters, martial policy.

Lord Marshal Beringer holds one of the most important seats on the High Council.

He is also rarely in it.

At present, Beringer is far more likely to be found with the army than in a council chamber, which is probably why the army continues to function. Reports, instructions, and hard military positions are often carried to the council by deputies.

His absence is not taken as lack of influence.

It is taken as evidence that someone is doing actual work.

### Sir Odran Kell

**Office:** Marshal’s Deputy for Council Affairs  
**Duties:** Carries Lord Marshal Beringer’s positions when the Marshal is in the field.

Sir Odran is not a full councilor in his own right, but he is often present when the Lord Marshal cannot attend.

He is known for giving short, practical answers, many of which begin with some variation of:

> “My lord’s answer is no.”

### Lord Constable Eamon Blackmere

**Office:** Lord Constable  
**Duties:** Royal security, roads, fortresses, arrests under Crown writ, custody of dangerous nobles.

Lord Blackmere concerns himself with the hard edge of royal order: warrants, roads, armed men, locked gates, and whether a noble’s title will survive contact with a Crown arrest order.

He is not loved.

This may be because he is useful.

### High Justiciar Osric Fenhale

**Office:** High Justiciar of the Crown  
**Duties:** Treason law, noble standing, succession disputes, appeals to royal justice, writ review.

The High Justiciar oversees the lawful shape of royal justice in matters too large or dangerous for ordinary courts.

He is the man most likely to explain why something technically legal may still ruin three houses, provoke two duels, and give the Crown a clean road to seizure.

### Royal Steward Brynmor Ashcombe

**Office:** Royal Steward  
**Duties:** Crown demesne, palace logistics, royal estates, court access, provisioning, royal household administration.

The Royal Steward manages the machinery of court life and Crown property.

This sounds dull until one remembers that food, rooms, servants, stables, keys, letters, and access to the king all pass through someone’s hands.

Lord Ashcombe is soft-spoken, observant, and remembers far too much.

### Master of Works Torren Bexley

**Office:** Master of Royal Works  
**Duties:** Roads, bridges, fortifications, ports, public works, reconstruction surveys, royal construction priorities.

Master Bexley is responsible for reminding ambitious men that stone, timber, labor, roads, and maintenance schedules exist.

He is therefore widely resented by visionaries, which is one of the signs that he is doing his job.

He has become especially important in questions of post-war reconstruction, fortification repair, and proposed aerial or naval works.

### Sapphire Flame

**Office:** Acting Archmage / Acting Royal Thaumaturge  
**Duties:** Arcane consultation, magical threat review, dangerous artifacts, royal magical filings, emergency spellcraft matters.

There is currently **no permanent Royal Thaumaturge** or settled Archmage of Gorundia.

**Sapphire Flame** acts in the role for urgent council purposes.

This distinction matters. She has authority enough to advise, warn, and review dangerous magical questions, but the office itself remains politically unsettled. Naturally, this creates exactly the sort of ambiguity that wizards, courtiers, and bureaucrats all pretend to hate while quietly using.

### Lord High Admiral Varro Kent

**Office:** Lord High Admiral  
**Duties:** Navy, river forces, shipyards, ports, waterborne logistics, coastal and lake defense.

Lord High Admiral Varro Kent oversees the kingdom’s traditional naval forces and waterborne logistics.

Recent developments involving airships, irregular naval titles, and the heroic national decision to make “sky fleet” a phrase that people now have to say aloud have complicated his life.

He does not appear grateful.

### Father Halric Dane

**Office:** Patriarch-Advocate to the Crown  
**Duties:** Liaison for the Triad churches when joint religious counsel is required.

Father Halric speaks to the Crown when the churches of Pelor, Heironeous, and Pholtus are aligned enough to present common counsel.

He does not command the Triad.

No one commands the Triad.

He coordinates its public religious advice to the Crown, which is a difficult task made worse by the fact that all three faiths are often correct in mutually irritating ways.

### Lady Elowen Marst

**Office:** Lady Privy Listener  
**Duties:** Sealed reports, quiet intelligence, sensitive correspondence, foreign threats, matters requiring discretion.

The Lady Privy Listener is the council’s quietest and least openly discussed office.

Lady Elowen Marst is responsible for matters that do not benefit from shouting, banners, or noble speeches. Her work concerns warning, correspondence, hidden movements, foreign influence, and the sort of truths that become more dangerous when said too early.

She is polite.

This is not reassuring.

### Lord Speaker Taran Havel

**Office:** Lord Speaker of the Bannerhall  
**Duties:** Liaison between the High Council and the noble assembly.

Lord Havel speaks for the mood of the Bannerhall, or at least for the version of that mood safe enough to bring near the king.

When he says, “The Bannerhall is concerned,” this may mean anything from “several lords have questions” to “someone has already ordered armor polished.”

## Summoned Advisors

Not every matter requires every important person in Gorundia to sit in the same chamber and rediscover contempt.

For specialized questions, the council may summon additional advisors.

Commonly summoned figures include:

- **Lord Mayor Wonderful**, for matters concerning Bard’s Gate, civic order, refugees, trade, and the reeves.
- **Vice Admiral Thalrik Vaultforge**, for aerial operations, irregular naval matters, legal fallout from the Vogel affair, and questions involving the Crown’s more unusual instruments.
- **Count Vorias Serrin**, for prophecy, high magic, military arcana, and other matters where brilliance is required and regrettable behavior can be tolerated.
- **Lord Faelar Moonshade**, for ancient law, scholarship, archives, magical history, and written objections sharp enough to draw blood.
- **Dame Cwenheld**, when the Crown requires moral clarity from the faith of Heironeous rather than another polished argument.

## Relationship to the Bannerhall

The **Bannerhall** represents the assembled weight of noble opinion.

The **High Council** is smaller, more immediate, and closer to the king’s hand.

The Bannerhall may roar.

The High Council may advise.

The king may listen to both and then choose his own course, because crowns are funny that way and peasants are somehow expected to keep planting grain through all of it.

## Common Formula

Provisional council orders are often phrased along these lines:

> “By provisional authority of the High Council, acting in the King’s name and pending His Majesty’s confirmation…”

This means the council believes the matter cannot wait.

It does **not** mean the king has personally ruled.

A confirmed royal order is different, stronger, and considerably less fun for anyone hoping to argue later.

## Public Reputation

Among nobles, the High Council is respected, feared, mocked, courted, and blamed, often by the same person before lunch.

Among commoners, it is vaguely understood as the place where important men argue before taxes happen.

Among Crown officers, it is both useful and maddening.

Among the king’s enemies, it is watched carefully.

Among the king’s friends, it is watched even more carefully.
