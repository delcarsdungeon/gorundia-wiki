# Lesser Council of Gorundia

The **Lesser Council of Gorundia** is the Crown’s principal body for petitions, civic grievances, guild concerns, minority representation, and the problems of ordinary subjects who lack the titles, money, or armed relatives required to interrupt the [[High Council of Gorundia]].

It is called “lesser” because its authority is narrower.

It is not lesser because its work is less important.

The High Council concerns itself with war, treason, royal law, fortresses, foreign threats, and the survival of the kingdom.

The Lesser Council concerns itself with whether the kingdom remains worth surviving.

It hears complaints, investigates abuses, mediates disputes, gathers testimony, recommends remedies, and brings matters before higher royal authority when local officials cannot or will not act.

It does not govern independently.

It does, however, create records.

This has proven deeply inconvenient to officials whose preferred administrative method was waiting for poor people to become tired.

## Authority

The Lesser Council may:

- receive written or oral petitions;
- summon witnesses willing to appear;
- request records from reeves, guilds, temples, chartered towns, and Crown offices;
- appoint small investigative panels;
- mediate disputes between communities, guilds, landlords, tenants, and local officers;
- issue recommendations to local authorities;
- request written answers from Crown departments;
- propose temporary protective measures;
- refer matters to royal courts, the High Council, the Crown, or the churches;
- publish findings unless sealed by lawful authority;
- preserve documents and testimony pending higher review.

The Lesser Council may not:

- levy taxes;
- command royal armies;
- declare war;
- pass permanent law;
- revoke noble titles;
- impose capital punishment;
- overrule the king;
- compel a great lord to obey merely by looking disappointed.

That final limitation continues to frustrate several members.

## Current Standing Members

### Lady Elswyth Marrin

**Office:** Crown’s Listener and First Petitioner  
**Duties:** Presides over sessions, controls the petition docket, assigns investigations, maintains procedure, and presents formal findings to higher authority.

Lady Elswyth is the presiding officer of the Lesser Council.

She is not a great noble. Her title was granted for royal service rather than inherited land.

Elswyth began as a copying clerk in the Chancery, advanced through the petition office, and eventually became the official responsible for sorting the kingdom’s endless supply of pleas, complaints, accusations, requests, and carefully phrased descriptions of local officials stealing livestock.

She is methodical, quiet, and exceptionally difficult to bully.

Elswyth does not raise her voice when a witness becomes hostile. She asks the clerk to repeat the question and records the refusal to answer.

She has survived four chancellors, three administrative reorganizations, two palace purges, and enough aristocratic outrage to regard shouting as a form of weather.

Her authority during sessions is substantial. She may recognize speakers, call for documents, remove disruptive observers, appoint panels, and determine whether a matter is ready for formal recommendation.

She cannot decide the kingdom’s policy.

She can ensure that anyone deciding it must first read what actually happened.

### Mistress Mereth “Mera” Clayhand

**Office:** Advocate for Halfblood Affairs  
**Duties:** Represents half-orcs, half-elves, mixed families, refugees, and communities facing discriminatory law, custom, employment, taxation, or settlement practices.

Mera Clayhand is a half-orc woman in her early forties who rose through the guilds rather than noble patronage.

She began as a leatherworker’s apprentice and became a guild bookkeeper after demonstrating that she could manage accounts better than several men who regarded literacy as proof of leadership.

She later served as an arbitrator between the Leatherworkers’, Carters’, Warehousemen’s, and Tanners’ Guilds. Her patient handling of wage disputes, apprenticeship abuses, and injury funds brought her into contact with [[Wonderful]].

Mera does not possess Wonderful’s theatrical presence.

She does not bait hostile councillors, dominate chambers, or turn every dispute into a moral reckoning visible from the street.

Instead, she:

- remembers every promise made in committee;
- arrives with written testimony;
- cultivates competent clerks;
- accepts partial victories without mistaking them for final ones;
- makes discriminatory policies administratively expensive;
- understands when invoking Wonderful’s name will open a door and when it will make someone lock it.

Wonderful broke open the door.

Mera is installing hinges, drafting procedures, and making sure no one quietly bricks it over after midnight.

> “Lady Wonderful proved we could be heard. My task is making certain they cannot return to pretending otherwise.”

Mera openly credits Wonderful’s trailblazing, but she is building an office meant to survive after both of them are gone.

### Caerwyn Alderpath

**Office:** Elven Advocate  
**Duties:** Represents settled elves, woodland communities, elven refugees, treaty rights, forest boundaries, cultural protections, and disputes involving elven communities within Gorundia.

Caerwyn Alderpath is a middle-aged wood elf who previously worked as a treaty witness, boundary surveyor, and mediator between woodland settlements and neighboring human communities.

He has spent more time with foresters, charcoal burners, village reeves, hunters, displaced grugach, and farmers arguing over tree lines than he has in high-elven courts.

Caerwyn believes:

- elves possess legitimate ancient rights;
- ancient rights do not invalidate everyone born afterward;
- treaties must protect living communities rather than merely honor dead kings;
- high elves do not speak for all elves;
- grugach are political peoples, not embarrassing relatives;
- cooperation with humans is neither surrender nor cultural extinction.

He is patient, practical, and willing to compromise on method without abandoning substance.

Caerwyn replaced [[Ithildor Laevarion]] following Ithildor’s disgrace and appointment as Royal Liaison to the Thornbriar Clan.

His selection was deliberate.

King Rimmand wanted the elven seat to represent elves living within the kingdom rather than serve as a second embassy for the high-elven court.

The high-elven delegation described Caerwyn’s appointment as:

> “An unfortunate reduction in the dignity of the office.”

This helped confirm that the king had chosen correctly.

Caerwyn’s usual response to sweeping claims of ancient sovereignty is:

> “Ancient claims remain claims. They are not permission to ignore the people currently standing on the ground.”

Grugach communities outside direct Crown authority are not treated as ordinary domestic constituencies. They are addressed through treaties, recognized leaders, and diplomatic channels.

Individual grugach living under Gorundian law may still petition through Caerwyn or the Bailiff of the Commons.

### Master Hadrik Ironwright

**Office:** Dwarven Stonewarden  
**Duties:** Represents dwarven enclaves, clan charters, mining rights, subterranean claims, craft privileges, and disputes involving dwarven labor or customary law.

Hadrik Ironwright is a master mason and charter-reader from the dwarven enclave of Wyrmlea.

He began as a stone surveyor, spent several decades inspecting mines and foundations, and eventually became the preferred arbitrator when dwarven customary rights collided with human land grants.

Hadrik is blunt, suspicious of decorative language, and entirely unimpressed by noble ancestry unsupported by competent masonry.

He is especially vigilant regarding:

- unsafe mines;
- unpaid clan contracts;
- surface lords claiming rights beneath land they have never entered;
- human officials misunderstanding communal dwarven property;
- forged or poorly copied charters;
- proposals that begin with “the dwarves can simply move.”

Hadrik is conservative by temperament but not reactionary.

He believes agreements should be honored exactly as written, including those protecting non-dwarves.

> “A bad promise is still a promise. Write better ones next time.”

### Master Arvellin Crankwheel

**Office:** Gnomish Factor  
**Duties:** Represents gnomish guilds, workshops, scholars, inventors, apprentices, merchants, and communities affected by regulations involving magical or mechanical craft.

Arvellin Crankwheel is a city gnome, workshop owner, and former assessor for the Crown’s Office of Measures.

He has inspected mills, alchemical shops, clockworks, magical conveniences, experimental furnaces, and several devices whose inventors insisted the smoke was expected.

Arvellin is cheerful, exacting, and incapable of hearing the phrase “minor risk” without requesting diagrams.

He is a defender of invention but an enemy of preventable stupidity.

His office frequently handles:

- guild restrictions on independent artificers;
- confiscated inventions;
- workshop accidents;
- patent-like claims and stolen designs;
- magical licensing disputes;
- apprentices used as disposable test subjects;
- human officials outlawing machinery because it made an unfamiliar noise.

Arvellin is known to arrive at hearings with scale models.

This usually clarifies matters.

Occasionally, it causes evacuation.

### Tobin Underbough

**Office:** Halfling Hearthwarden  
**Duties:** Represents halfling settlements, caravans, farming communities, family rights, local charters, food security, and disputes involving itinerant or rural halfling communities.

Tobin Underbough is an older halfling scout, community organizer, and proprietor of the Underbough Hedge Company.

He has spent much of his life moving between villages, caravans, farms, military camps, and the neglected spaces between official maps.

Tobin has no patience for the belief that halfling concerns begin and end with kitchens.

His office regularly addresses:

- seizure of halfling farmland;
- harassment of caravans;
- village charter disputes;
- food requisitions;
- militia service;
- missing travelers;
- family guardianship;
- local officials assuming small people are politically harmless.

Tobin is pleasant, observant, and remarkably well informed.

Nobles sometimes mistake his friendliness for simplicity.

They generally make this mistake once.

> “A hearth is only peaceful because somebody notices who is walking toward it.”

### Kael “the Bookman” Rynne

**Office:** Bailiff of the Commons  
**Duties:** Represents laborers, tenants, servants, displaced families, unchartered workers, the urban poor, and petitioners lacking another recognized advocate.

Kael Rynne is a human commoner and former debt clerk whose nickname comes from carrying a ledger almost everywhere.

The ledger does not contain money.

It contains names.

Kael records petitioners, witnesses, promises, injuries, missing relatives, unpaid wages, evictions, confiscations, and officials who insist they have never heard of any of them.

He is neither charismatic nor particularly polite.

He is persistent.

Kael’s office is the broadest on the council and the easiest to overwhelm. It deals with people who have no guild, clan, temple, title, patron, or recognized community office willing to act for them.

The Bailiff of the Commons may:

- place petitions directly on the council docket;
- demand written responses from royal departments;
- speak for petitioners unable to appear;
- request investigations through the First Petitioner;
- publish findings unless sealed by royal order.

Kael is particularly hostile toward anonymous collective language.

> “There are no ‘displaced laborers.’ There is Bran Tallow, his wife, three daughters, and eleven other households whose names are written here.”

### Belda Wainwright

**Office:** Provost of Markets  
**Duties:** Oversees complaints involving market access, weights, prices, shortages, toll abuses, licensing, and unfair local monopolies.

Belda Wainwright is a former wheelwright, market inspector, and guild negotiator.

She understands how shortages are created, how prices are manipulated, and how quickly respectable merchants discover patriotism when accused of hoarding.

Belda’s office does not set prices throughout the kingdom.

It investigates whether prices, tolls, and market restrictions are being distorted through fraud, coercion, monopoly, or abuse of authority.

She is especially attentive to grain, salt, lamp oil, timber, iron tools, and transport animals.

These are the goods nobles forget until they become the reason armies stop moving.

Belda is practical, sharp-tongued, and suspicious of any merchant who begins a defense by explaining how much charity he performs.

### Harvek Dunnald

**Office:** Guildmaster of Coin  
**Duties:** Advises on guild finance, debt, apprenticeships, small merchants, craftsmen, credit, and the effects of royal policy on ordinary commerce.

Harvek Dunnald is a senior guildsman from a family of cloth merchants.

He is wealthy enough to understand creditors and ordinary enough to remember when one bad season could ruin his household.

Harvek represents chartered guilds and small commercial interests rather than the royal treasury or great merchant houses.

His office often hears matters involving:

- predatory debt;
- guild admission;
- apprenticeship contracts;
- workshop closures;
- confiscated stock;
- unfair tariffs;
- merchants trapped between conflicting local laws;
- Crown policies whose authors have never balanced a shop ledger.

Harvek is cautious and rarely supports dramatic reform on the first hearing.

He is not hostile to change.

He simply believes every policy eventually reaches someone’s pantry.

This frequently places him between Mera Clayhand’s demands for action and the more alarmed sections of the guild establishment.

They work together better than either expected.

### Ser Darlan Veyne

**Office:** Lord of Houses  
**Duties:** Handles petitions involving tenancy, lodging, domestic service, household obligations, urban property, abandoned estates, and emergency shelter.

Ser Darlan Veyne is a minor knight whose military career was spent largely in logistics and siege administration.

He knows exactly how many people can be packed into a hall, how quickly sanitation fails, and how easily temporary quarters become permanent misery.

His title causes some petitioners to distrust him.

His willingness to inspect cellars, servant quarters, tenements, and refugee barracks personally tends to correct that.

Darlan’s office hears disputes involving:

- unlawful eviction;
- unsafe housing;
- landlord obligations;
- domestic servants;
- overcrowding;
- abandoned property;
- billeting soldiers;
- emergency refugee shelter;
- inheritance disputes too small for noble courts but large enough to make families homeless.

Darlan is formal, patient, and uncomfortable with praise.

> “A roof is not charity if rent was paid for it.”

### Captain Merrel Farsen

**Office:** Keeper of the Watch  
**Duties:** Reports on civic security, local watches, banditry, corruption, urban disorder, and failures of law enforcement.

Captain Merrel Farsen is a veteran watch officer rather than a royal military commander.

He has served in market districts, river wards, noble quarters, and neighborhoods where the watch entered only in groups.

Merrel does not command the kingdom’s local watches.

He investigates how they are functioning and whether their failures arise from lack of men, lack of money, corruption, political interference, or simple cowardice.

His office frequently handles:

- watch brutality;
- failure to investigate crimes against unpopular communities;
- unlawful detention;
- missing prisoners;
- bribery;
- districts left unpatrolled;
- local reeves using sworn men as private muscle;
- officials describing organized violence as “a neighborhood matter.”

Merrel is terse, skeptical, and more willing than most officers to admit when enforcement has failed.

> “The law was present. It simply chose not to act.”

### Lady Corvessa Mandral

**Office:** Master of Ships  
**Duties:** Represents ports, river traffic, ferries, sailors, shipwrights, customs disputes, and merchant shipping below the level of national naval policy.

Lady Corvessa Mandral comes from a minor river noble family whose wealth depends more on barges than banners.

She has personally captained merchant vessels and understands currents, docks, crews, cargo loss, port fees, and the interesting creativity of customs officials.

Corvessa’s concerns overlap with those of the [[High Council of Gorundia|Lord High Admiral]], but the distinction is clear:

The Lord High Admiral concerns himself with fleets, defense, and royal naval power.

Corvessa concerns herself with whether grain barges can reach market without being taxed six times, detained twice, and seized once by a reeve who has recently discovered maritime law.

She is poised, direct, and less aristocratic in manner than her title suggests.

### Sister Magda Torrel

**Office:** Speaker of the Faiths  
**Duties:** Presents local religious grievances, temple disputes, burial concerns, charity failures, and community matters not requiring intervention by senior religious authorities.

Sister Magda is a Pelorite lay sister who spent years administering hospice kitchens, burial funds, and joint relief efforts involving all three churches of the Triad.

She does not command the faiths.

She does not issue doctrine.

She carries concerns from priests, congregations, shrine keepers, religious minorities, and ordinary worshippers to the council.

Her office often addresses:

- denial of burial;
- misuse of temple charity;
- disputes over shared shrines;
- local persecution;
- abandoned holy sites;
- conflicts between religious practice and guild or civic law;
- officials claiming divine sanction they have not actually received.

Magda is warm in private and formidable in session.

She has a particular talent for asking powerful clergy whether their position represents doctrine, custom, or personal convenience.

## Meetings and Schedule

### Full Sessions

The full Lesser Council meets on the **first and third Godsday of each month**.

Sessions begin at the second morning bell and continue until the docket is completed or Lady Elswyth determines that everyone has become too tired to distinguish justice from irritation.

Full sessions address:

- completed investigations;
- petitions involving multiple constituencies;
- recommendations requiring collective approval;
- referrals to higher authority;
- disputes between council offices;
- matters with kingdom-wide implications.

### Petition Panels

Smaller panels meet every Waterday.

A normal panel consists of:

- the First Petitioner or a delegated chair;
- the advocate most directly responsible;
- one civic or economic officer;
- a clerk;
- additional specialists when required.

Panels hear testimony, review documents, question officials, and attempt mediation.

Most routine matters are resolved or referred by panels rather than consuming the full council’s time.

### Open Tables

Each councillor must hold at least one public **Open Table** every month.

An Open Table is an informal session held in a guildhall, market room, temple court, public house, civic office, or community hall where constituents may speak directly with the councillor or their clerks.

No fee may be charged.

No petitioner is required to possess a seal, guild membership, noble sponsor, or written complaint.

This is considered one of the Lesser Council’s most important customs.

It is also the reason several members know more about the kingdom’s actual condition than officials with larger offices and cleaner boots.

### Emergency Sessions

Lady Elswyth may call an emergency session when:

- lives are immediately at risk;
- evidence may be destroyed;
- prisoners may disappear;
- communal violence is imminent;
- a local authority refuses lawful inquiry;
- delay would make the petition meaningless.

The king, Lord Chancellor, or High Council may also direct the Lesser Council to conduct an urgent inquiry.

## How Petitions Reach the Council

### Written Petitions

Written petitions may be submitted through:

- a local reeve;
- a guild clerk;
- a temple office;
- a chartered town council;
- a ducal petition office;
- the royal petition hall in the capital;
- the office of a specific Lesser Councillor.

A petitioner is not required to submit through a local official accused in the complaint.

The reorganization made this explicit after several reeves demonstrated confusion on the point.

### Oral Petitions

Illiterate petitioners may give oral testimony to a sworn petition clerk.

The clerk must read the completed petition back to the petitioner before it is marked or witnessed.

A petitioner may bring:

- a family member;
- a guild representative;
- a priest;
- a community elder;
- another trusted witness.

### Sealed Petitions

Petitions involving dangerous accusations may be submitted under seal to the Crown’s Listener.

The petitioner’s identity is recorded separately and may be withheld from ordinary council records when disclosure would create a clear danger.

Anonymous accusations without evidence are logged but do not automatically trigger formal action.

The council has learned that “everyone knows” is not testimony.

### Red-Thread Petitions

A petition involving immediate danger to life, disappearance of prisoners, destruction of evidence, or organized violence may be bound with red thread.

Misusing the designation is punishable.

Failing to examine one promptly is considered considerably worse.

## Procedure

### First Review

Petitions are initially reviewed by clerks under the Crown’s Listener.

They are classified as:

- local remedy;
- mediation;
- investigation;
- urgent protection;
- judicial referral;
- High Council referral;
- direct royal attention.

The First Petitioner then assigns the matter to the relevant advocate or panel.

### Preliminary Inquiry

The assigned councillor may request:

- local records;
- guild rolls;
- tax accounts;
- watch reports;
- witness statements;
- charters;
- contracts;
- temple records;
- inspection of the disputed site.

A request from the Lesser Council is not identical to a royal command.

Refusal is permitted in some circumstances.

The refusal is also recorded and forwarded with the case, which tends to make the next request less optional.

### Hearing

Hearings are less formal than royal courts.

Witnesses speak under oath when the issue warrants it, but ordinary petitioners are not expected to understand court procedure.

The chair may interrupt advocates, ask direct questions, or allow testimony to be given as a narrative.

The goal is to determine what happened, not to reward whoever hired the best-trained speaker.

### Findings

A panel may issue:

- a mediated settlement;
- a recommendation to local authorities;
- a request for corrective action;
- a public finding;
- a referral to a court or church;
- a temporary preservation request;
- a formal escalation.

Findings identify:

- the facts established;
- the facts disputed;
- the law, charter, custom, or royal interest involved;
- the proposed remedy;
- any refusal to cooperate.

## Matters Referred to Higher Authority

The Lesser Council refers a matter upward when it involves:

- treason;
- noble title or succession;
- permanent changes to royal law;
- interducal disputes;
- major expenditure from the royal treasury;
- military deployment;
- royal charters;
- major arcane threats;
- heresy or religious jurisdiction beyond local practice;
- foreign powers;
- confiscation of noble estates;
- widespread official corruption;
- refusal by a powerful lord to recognize lawful inquiry;
- any remedy beyond the council’s authority.

### Referral to the High Council

A formal referral to the High Council is called a **Memorial of Finding and Prayer**.

It contains:

- a summary of the petition;
- established findings;
- dissenting opinions;
- supporting testimony;
- the remedy requested;
- the reason Lesser Council authority is insufficient.

The High Council may act, request additional inquiry, forward the matter to the king, or send it back with instructions.

### Referral to the King

Matters may be presented directly to the king when:

- delay would endanger the realm or its subjects;
- the High Council is divided;
- the complaint concerns a High Councillor;
- the issue involves royal conscience rather than administration;
- the king has requested direct notice.

Lady Elswyth presents these matters personally or through the Lord Chancellor.

The Lesser Council does not demand that the king agree.

It does insist that he be accurately informed before disagreeing.

## Official and Unofficial Access

The Lesser Council is considerably more approachable than the High Council.

This is deliberate.

Its members are expected to maintain offices accessible to their constituencies, visit relevant communities, attend guild meetings, and appear in places where petitioners can reach them without first obtaining three introductions and a better coat.

A subject may encounter a Lesser Councillor:

- at a guildhall;
- inspecting a market;
- attending a shrine;
- visiting a workhouse;
- eating in a public tavern;
- walking a dock;
- examining a mine;
- hearing complaints in a village hall.

Unofficial conversations do not automatically become formal petitions.

They often become the reason a councillor asks a clerk to open a file the next morning.

This accessibility occasionally makes Lesser Councillors seem less prestigious than High Councillors.

It also makes them far harder to deceive about conditions outside the palace.

## The King’s Attendance

King Rimmand has attended Lesser Council sessions personally.

He once attempted to do so unannounced.

His intention was sincere.

He wanted to hear the council’s ordinary work without ceremony, rehearsed speeches, selected petitioners, or frightened officials cleaning the room before he arrived.

He entered during a routine hearing and said:

> “You will ignore our presence and proceed precisely as though we were not here.”

This was a direct royal instruction.

It was also impossible.

[[Krozen]] stood behind him.

[[Sapphire Flame]] accompanied him to ensure that no magical threat had been introduced into the chamber.

Krozen’s presence caused every guard, veteran, and person with functioning instincts to sit straighter.

Sapphire Flame’s quiet examination of the room caused several minor charms to hum, one clerk’s warded inkpot to smoke, and a petitioner to assume he had accidentally become part of a treason inquiry.

The councillors became painfully formal.

Witnesses began addressing every answer to the king.

One guild representative prefaced a complaint about spoiled flour with an assurance of his family’s eternal loyalty to the Crown.

A tenant withdrew her accusation against a landlord because she became convinced the king’s presence meant the man was secretly important.

Rimmand endured this for less than half an hour.

He then stood and said:

> “I have made this worse.”

Lady Elswyth answered:

> “Yes, Your Majesty.”

The king left.

He later apologized to Elswyth privately and issued a standing instruction:

> “The Crown will not listen from behind walls to those it has invited to speak.”

The king does not attend Lesser Council proceedings in disguise.

He does not secretly observe them through magic.

He does not send Sapphire Flame to scry upon them or Krozen to test their loyalty.

If he wishes to attend, the visit is now announced.

He sits visibly to one side, receives the same written docket as any observer, and does not speak unless asked or unless a matter is formally placed before him.

His purpose is to understand the council, not catch it behaving naturally.

Rimmand has made clear that he does not want councillors performing for him.

He also does not want them wondering whether every empty chair contains an invisible king.

## Recent Reorganization

King Rimmand reorganized the Lesser Council after the removal of [[Ithildor Laevarion]] and the elevation of [[Wonderful]] to Lord Mayor of Bard’s Gate.

The king used the vacancies to:

- eliminate duplicate racial advisory positions attached informally to the High Council;
- place community advocacy clearly within the Lesser Council;
- make the Bailiff of the Commons a permanent office;
- strengthen the petition clerk system;
- establish regular Open Tables;
- formalize written escalation procedures;
- appoint councillors with working constituencies rather than ceremonial prestige.

Wonderful’s former office passed to Mera Clayhand.

Ithildor’s former office passed to Caerwyn Alderpath.

The appointments signaled that the council would no longer treat high-elven rank or noble approval as substitutes for actual representation.

## Relationship to the High Council

The High Council advises on the machinery and survival of the Crown.

The Lesser Council advises on how that machinery affects the people beneath it.

The High Council may ask:

> “Can this be done?”

The Lesser Council is expected to ask:

> “What happens to everyone when it is?”

The two councils frequently irritate one another.

This is healthy.

A government in which senior officials never hear inconvenient reports is not harmonious.

It is merely approaching surprise.

## Public Reputation

Among commoners, the Lesser Council is one of the few royal institutions considered reachable.

Among guilds, it is useful when favorable and meddlesome when not.

Among nobles, it is alternately dismissed as a complaint hall and feared as a place where complaints acquire dates, names, witnesses, and royal seals.

Among local officials, receiving a letter from the Lesser Council is not necessarily dangerous.

Receiving a second letter usually is.

Among the king’s reform-minded supporters, it is evidence that the Crown listens.

Among the king’s enemies, it is evidence that the Crown is teaching ordinary people where to complain.

Both interpretations are correct.
