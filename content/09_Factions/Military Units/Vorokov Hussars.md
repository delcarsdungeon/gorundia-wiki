# Vorokov Hussars

> “They are silent as monks until the horn sounds, then loud enough to make a priest consider drink. I have never seen such disciplined lunatics.”
> — Sorek Redmarch


## Overview

The **Vorokov Hussars** are the prestige cavalry of [[Barony Vorochev]] and one of the most famous fast-response forces in [[Duchy Royonad]]. They are inseparable from [[House Vorokov]], from [[Vorochev Hall]], and from the barony’s horse-breeding economy. The hussars are not merely stationed in Vorochev. They are the reason Vorochev matters.

They ride as elite cavalry: scouts, raiders, flankers, pursuit troops, mounted archers, lancers, and shock cavalry when the moment is right. They can deliver glorious cavalry charges or spend a day harassing a stronger enemy until the poor bastards start praying for infantry, which is how one knows morale has collapsed into comedy.

Their horses are famously swift and agile, bred from Dominion and Gorundian stock. Their gear is excellent. Their discipline in action is terrifying. Their behavior off duty is a public health concern with better tailoring.

The hussars are culturally tied to Royonad and House Orenic, but they are not closed by bloodline. Not every man who earns his wings is from Vorochev. Not every rider is even from Orenic territory. They care less about genealogy than courage, riding skill, discipline under signal, and the ability to do dangerous work without needing a song written first.

That said, the regiment attracts so many young nobles that commoners must usually do something exceptional to be admitted. Blood does not guarantee a place.

But money, a good horse, and a family name do get a man close enough to be tested.

After that, the horse, the lance, and Old Red-Spur decide.

## The Barony and the Regiment

The Vorokov Hussars cannot be separated from [[Barony Vorochev]] without misunderstanding both.

Vorochev breeds the horses, trains the grooms, houses the remount yards, maintains the tack workshops, feeds the farriers, pensions the widows, and raises children under the shadow of red pennons. The barony’s roads are cavalry roads. Its chapel yards are messenger yards. Its feasts are full of saddle talk. Its economy is not merely “supported” by the hussars.

It is organized around them.

This is why the regiment is politically permanent in a way the family is not.

If the Vorokov line were wiped out, or if a Vorokov baron proved unworthy, Royonad would not disband the hussars or relocate them. That would be madness with paperwork, and even nobles have limits.

Instead, a new baron would be installed.

The title serves the barony. The barony serves the regiment. The regiment serves Royonad.

In theory, this is all very orderly.

In practice, everyone understands that whoever rules Vorochev must be acceptable to the hussars, the remount families, the officer clans, House Orenic, and Old Red-Spur if he has not had the courtesy to die yet.

## Reputation

The hussars have a reputation for impossible contrasts.

When they are on duty, they are precise, silent, and brutally controlled. They ride in exact spacing, respond to hand signals instantly, maintain formation through broken terrain, and shift from scouting screen to harassment to charge without the shouting and flailing lesser cavalry mistake for command.

When they are off duty, they are among the wildest men in the army.

They drink too hard, sing too loudly, gamble too recklessly, race horses where horses absolutely should not be raced, and hold insult contests with elaborate rules. They are famous for smashing taverns and then paying the owner more than the damage was worth, as if destruction is just a violent form of tipping.

This contradiction is not accidental.

The hussars live under pressure. They are often the first to see the enemy, the first to be cut off, the first ordered into uncertain ground, and the last ones back when an army needs eyes behind it. Casualties are high. Horses are hard to replace. Glory comes quickly and then asks for blood like a landlord.

So when the bit is out of their mouths, they run.

Stanislav permits more disorder from them than most commanders would tolerate because he understands the bargain.

A hussar may sing, boast, drink, flirt, gamble, and make a public idiot of himself.

He may not miss watch.

He may not mistreat his horse.

He may not abuse civilians.

He may not steal.

He may not draw steel in camp without cause.

He may not disgrace the pennon.

The first category gets laughter.

The second gets punishment.

The distinction is explained exactly once.

## The Practical Rank Structure

On paper, the Vorokov Hussars have ranks, commissions, squadrons, officers, clerks, payroll, standards, remount ledgers, and all the other little rituals humans invented to pretend war is not chaos wearing boots.

In practice, the regiment’s true hierarchy is simpler:

1. The Gods
2. The Baron
3. Old Red-Spur
4. Everyone else

There are arguments about whether Old Red-Spur belongs above or below the Baron in practical matters. These arguments are usually held quietly, far from both men, because hussars are brave, not suicidal in an administrative sense.

The formal answer is that Baron [[Stanislav Vorokov]] commands the regiment.

The functional answer is that Stanislav commands the war, while Old Red-Spur commands the men when they are becoming hussars, failing to remain hussars, or about to do something magnificently stupid.

Both are necessary.

Neither is decorative.

## Formal Rank Structure

The hussars use a Royonad rank pattern influenced by Orenic battle-honor culture and older cavalry custom.

### Baron-Proprietor

The noble patron and commander of the regiment.

The Baron of Vorochev is expected to command, fund, protect, and embody the hussars. A baron who does not ride, does not understand cavalry, or cannot command respect from the regiment is a political problem waiting to become a succession crisis.

The current Baron-Proprietor is Baron [[Stanislav Vorokov]].

### Rotmistrz

A squadron captain.

Usually noble-born or druznikh-born, though battlefield promotion is possible. A rotmistrz commands a banner or squadron-sized body of hussars and is expected to know tactics, horses, terrain, and noble tempers. The last requirement is the most dangerous, because horses can at least be trained.

### Poruchik

A lieutenant or deputy officer.

A poruchik handles immediate squadron command, carries orders, maintains formation control, and acts when the rotmistrz is dead, absent, wounded, or being dramatic somewhere else.

### Khorunzhy

A cornet or standard officer.

The khorunzhy carries or guards pennons, signal flags, and battlefield markers. This is not ceremonial. In fast cavalry work, signals are life. A khorunzhy who loses a pennon may survive the battle and still wish he had not.

### Druznikh

An honored battle-companion, roughly equivalent to a knight in Gorundian usage.

A druznikh is not merely a nobleman with a horse and a sharp stick. Among Orenics, the title carries the weight of battle honor. Bloodline may open the door, but battle earns the chair.

Many druznikhs are noble-born.

Not all.

A commoner who becomes druznikh has usually done something everyone agrees was brave, useful, witnessed, and inconvenient to ignore.

### Druznikh-Starshy

A senior druznikh, elder battle-companion, and regimental authority.

The most famous current holder is [[Druzhnikh-Starshy Radovan Belik]], called [[Old Red-Spur]]. In western terms, he functions as the regiment’s sergeant major. In Vorokov terms, he is the man who turns mounted aristocrats into soldiers and survives the process out of spite.

He oversees discipline, drill, watch standards, recruit trials, camp behavior, remount condition, and the education of young nobles who arrive believing courage is a substitute for listening.

It is not.

They learn.

### Desyatnik

A ten-rider leader.

The desyatnik is a practical field NCO, usually an experienced hussar trusted to keep a small file alive. Desyatniks are often the backbone of patrols, screens, and raids. Officers issue the plan. Desyatniks notice when the plan has begun smoking.

### Full Hussar

A full rider of the regiment.

A full hussar has earned his spurs and wings. He is no longer merely attached to the regiment, training under it, or wearing its colors on hope and credit. He has passed the required trials and been accepted by the unit.

### Young Spur

A probationary hussar or newly admitted rider not yet fully blooded.

Young Spurs may be noble sons, foreign volunteers, local riders, or rare common-born candidates. They ride, serve, train, and suffer until the regiment decides whether they are becoming hussars or simply making the horses sad.

## Recruitment

The hussars recruit from three broad sources:

### Vorochev Blood

These are men from the barony: sons of officer families, horse-breeding households, veteran lines, grooms who grew into riders, minor landholders, and cavalry families whose children learned to ride before they learned tact.

They understand the culture early. That helps.

It does not guarantee acceptance.

The hussars are cruelest to local sons because local sons should know better.

### Royonad Nobility and Battle Families

The regiment draws heavily from Orenic and Royonad noble families. Young nobles seek the hussars because service brings prestige, scars, officer access, fashionable danger, and the faint possibility of being admired by people whose approval is terrible for one’s life expectancy.

This creates a practical barrier for commoners. Not a legal one. A social one.

There are only so many mounts, instructors, officer slots, and chances to be tested. A noble arrives with a horse, kit, letters, and family pressure. A commoner arrives with courage and a problem.

The commoner must be better.

Sometimes he is.

When that happens, the hussars remember.

### Foreign Riders and Outsiders

Not all hussars are from Orenic territory. A rider from another duchy, a border family, a refugee line, or even a former enemy culture can earn a place if he proves himself.

This is rare, but celebrated when it happens cleanly.

The regiment likes the story it tells about itself: blood matters less than courage, ability, and the seat to survive full speed under a lance.

The fact that noble blood still crowds the stable yard is a separate injustice, naturally filed under “human society continues being human society.”

## Earning Spurs and Wings

A man does not become a full Vorokov Hussar by appointment alone.

He must earn both **spurs** and **wings**.

### Spurs

Spurs mark that a rider has proven basic hussar worth.

To earn spurs, a Young Spur must demonstrate:

- controlled seat at speed
- lance handling
- saber work from horseback
- bow or javelin use while mounted
- remount discipline
- tack inspection under pressure
- obedience to silent signals
- ability to recover formation after disruption
- ability to care for his horse before himself

A man who neglects his mount is failed. Immediately.

No appeal.

### Wings

Wings mark that a rider has proven himself under real danger or an accepted equivalent.

This may come from:

- surviving a dangerous patrol
- holding formation under fire
- recovering a fallen comrade or standard
- breaking through encirclement
- returning with vital intelligence
- riding a pursuit without losing discipline
- saving a horse line or remount train
- standing fast when ordered not to charge

That last one is important.

The hussars admire courage.

They revere controlled courage.

A rider who charges without signal may be brave. He may also be a liability with hair.

## The Wings

The famous hussar wings are not merely decoration. They are earned regimental signs.

A full hussar may wear the wing frame in battle or parade. The exact form varies by rank, wealth, and honor:

- simple dark feather frame for newly winged hussars
- red-bound feather frame for veterans
- white-tipped feathers for riders who survived being unhorsed and returned to battle
- gold pins or fittings for officers
- black binding for mourning
- broken-feather marks for a rider whose horse died under him in action
- paired full wings for elite officers and highly decorated veterans

The wings are dramatic because cavalrymen are incapable of not being dramatic when given access to feathers, metalwork, and impending death.

## Unit Grooming and Style

Vorokov Hussars are famous for their dress and grooming almost as much as their riding. The unit does not allow complete personal freedom. That would be infantry behavior with better boots.

Instead, the hussars practice regulated individuality.

Long mustaches are customary. Junior men wear them plain. Veterans and officers may weight them with small beads, rings, or metal drops, a fashion copied from Baron Stanislav himself. Exact imitation of the baron’s style is considered vulgar unless earned by scars, rank, or a spectacular act of nerve.

Hair is worn long enough to bind, braid, or tie under a helm. Small side braids are common, especially temple braids bound with colored cord.

Common braid signs include:

- **Red cord:** sworn service under the Vorokov pennon
- **White cord:** unhorsed in battle and returned to saddle
- **Black cord:** mourning for a dead lance-partner or horse
- **Gold thread:** officer privilege or duel honor
- **Horsehair wrap:** memorial from a fallen mount
- **Iron bead:** survived a failed pursuit or encirclement
- **Silver bead:** recognized by Old Red-Spur for discipline under pressure

The hussars are vain about this.

Openly.

Proudly.

Obnoxiously.

But the vanity has rules. Hair must not foul the rein. Jewelry must not interfere with the bit, bowstring, or lance. A man may look like a peacock in camp, but once the horn sounds, every braid, bead, and cloak-pin had better stay exactly where discipline put it.

## Heraldry and Badge

The hussars derive their badge from the arms of [[House Vorokov]].

**House Vorokov Arms:**  
*Gules, a horse courant argent barded for war; on a chief argent, a lance Or fesswise.*

In plain speech: a red shield bearing a white warhorse in motion, armored for cavalry service, beneath a white chief charged with a gold lance.

### Hussar Badge

The regiment uses a simplified badge:

**A gold lance over a white horse’s head on red.**

The full arms belong to the house.

The badge belongs to the regiment.

This distinction matters to heralds, officers, and the sort of people who think war can be improved by marginalia.

## Standards and Pennons

The hussars use several levels of battlefield signs:

### The Baronial Standard

The full House Vorokov arms, carried when the Baron rides in person or when the regiment acts in formal service to the barony.

Losing this standard would be catastrophic.

Recovering it would be the kind of deed that turns a commoner into a druznikh while nobles pretend they always supported him.

### The Regimental Pennon

Red field, white horse’s head, gold lance.

This is the common symbol of the Vorokov Hussars. It flies over the regiment’s camp, marks their place in a host, and travels with major detachments.

### Squadron Pennons

Smaller pennons identify squadrons and rotmistrz commands. These often include personal marks, colored cords, or battle streamers.

### Lance Ribbons

Individual riders carry red-and-white lance ribbons. Veterans may add black, gold, or white knots by honor. Outsiders think this is decoration. Hussars can read a rider’s history from a lance if they are close enough and sober enough, which is not a guaranteed combination.

## Discipline

Hussar discipline is sharp because it has to be.

Loose cavalry dies.

Loose elite cavalry dies expensively.

Their wildness off duty is tolerated only because their obedience under signal is absolute. When the pennon drops, the laughter stops. When the horn sounds, dice remain where they fall. When the commander gives the hand sign, a hundred bright, arrogant, half-drunk cavalrymen can become a single moving blade.

This is the part outsiders struggle to understand.

The hussars are not undisciplined.

They are disciplined enough to survive being wild.

## Camp Customs

### The Quiet Tack Rule

Before any mission, there is no singing, no jokes, no dice, and no unnecessary speech. Tack is checked in silence. A horse making more noise than its rider is considered a bad omen.

### The Red Cup

After a successful return from dangerous scouting or pursuit, the surviving riders drink from a shared red-lacquered cup. The first drink is poured out for those who did not return.

### The Empty Saddle Toast

No hussar sits in a dead man’s place at table until his name has been spoken aloud. The toast is brief.

The drinking afterward is not.

### The Spur Debt

A hussar who is saved by another rider owes that rider a spur-token. These tokens are traded, worn, gambled, stolen back, and sometimes sent home to widows.

### The Three Forbidden Boasts

A hussar does not boast that he cannot be unhorsed, cannot be outrun, or cannot be killed.

These are considered invitations to the gods.

The gods are known cheats.

### Pay Before Breaking

When entering a friendly town, senior hussars sometimes pay innkeepers and taverners in advance. This is called courtesy.

Everyone else calls it evidence.

### The Laughing Return

After a successful raid, pursuit, or screen action, the hussars return loudly. Songs, horns, insults, and red pennons announce survival. It reassures allies, unnerves rivals, and advertises that the red lunatics came back again.

### The Silent Mile

The final approach before a surprise action is taken in silence. No speech, no metal rattle, no unnecessary movement. Even the young nobles learn to shut up, which many priests consider evidence that miracles still occur.

## Lance Duels

Among hussar officers and ambitious veterans, formal lance-dueling is a tradition of prestige, lunacy, and social sorting.

The duel is fought mounted, at speed, with blunted but still dangerous lances. Armor is worn. Death is discouraged but not unknown. Facial scars from these duels are not hidden. They are displayed, because apparently cavalrymen saw “avoid being hit in the head” and decided it needed social nuance.

The duel tests:

- seat
- nerve
- timing
- lance control
- pain tolerance
- ability to stay mounted while regretting one’s ancestry

A duel may settle insult, test nerve, mark promotion, or demonstrate readiness for officer command. Old Red-Spur has authority to forbid a duel.

He rarely does.

He prefers to let fools explain themselves to physics.

## The Baron

The Baron of Vorochev is the formal commander, patron, and political shield of the hussars.

The Baron is expected to:

- ride with competence
- understand cavalry war
- fund the regiment
- protect the remount families
- defend the honor of the pennon
- discipline officers without weakening initiative
- represent the hussars before House Orenic
- keep the regiment useful to Royonad, not merely glorious

The current Baron, [[Baron Stanislav Vorokov]], embodies the ideal almost too well: scarred, cold-eyed, fast, disciplined, and visibly dangerous.

He is what young hussars imagine becoming before time, grief, and Old Red-Spur begin editing the fantasy.

## Old Red-Spur

Druzhnikh-Starshy [[Radovan Belik]], called **Old Red-Spur**, is the senior practical authority of the regiment.

He is not the Baron.

He does not outrank the Baron.

Everyone agrees on this publicly.

Privately, the regiment understands that some matters belong to Stanislav, some belong to the gods, and some belong to Old Red-Spur because the alternative is letting young aristocrats manage themselves, which is how units die in colorful clothing.

Old Red-Spur controls:

- recruit trials
- daily discipline
- watch standards
- horse care enforcement
- tack inspections
- internal punishments
- veteran honors
- the Empty Saddle Roll
- the correction of officers too young, too noble, or too stupid to know better

A noble officer may technically outrank him.

A wise noble officer remembers that “technically” is a word often found near shallow graves.

## Doctrine

The hussars fight in layers.

First they see.

Then they sting.

Then they turn.

Then they sting again.

Then, when the enemy has opened a gap, lost formation, or committed too far, they charge.

Their officers train constantly in controlled aggression. The ideal Vorokov officer is not fearless. Fearless men die quickly and make horses nervous. The ideal is a man who feels fear, measures distance anyway, and can still put a lance through a target while traveling at an unreasonable speed.

### Common Tactics

**The Glass Spur:** A sudden acceleration from apparent withdrawal into a flank charge.

**The Wolf Circle:** Mounted archery harassment around slower infantry or supply trains.

**The Broken Rein:** Feigned command confusion meant to bait enemy cavalry into pursuit.

**The Red Pennon:** A signal that the hussars are shifting from harassment to lance charge.

**The Empty Saddle Drill:** Every hussar trains to recover, lead, or remount a riderless horse under pressure.

**The Silent Mile:** A stealth approach before contact.

**The Laughing Return:** A deliberate noisy return after success, used to signal morale and survival.

## Horses

A hussar’s horse is not equipment.

It is status, partner, weapon, responsibility, and social witness.

Mistreating a horse is one of the fastest ways to be expelled, beaten, or made into an educational example. The hussars tolerate vanity, gambling, brawling, and singing that would make a dock priest resign. They do not tolerate poor horse care.

Vorochev horses are bred for:

- speed
- agility
- endurance
- courage under noise
- responsiveness to knee and rein
- recovery after hard pursuit
- ability to carry armored riders without becoming sluggish

They are not as massive as Vogel chargers and not as elegant as Blackbriar destriers. They are faster than the former, harder than the latter, and generally mean enough to deserve Royonad citizenship.

## Foreigners and Commoners

The hussars like to say:

> “The saddle has no ancestors.”

This is noble-sounding and only partly true.

A noble has advantages. He can arrive with a horse, armor, letters, training, and enough family pressure to annoy three officers into watching him ride.

A commoner has to force the regiment to notice.

That usually means an act of courage or ability so clear that ignoring it would insult the pennon. Pulling a wounded hussar from a collapsing flank. Riding down a courier no one else could catch. Saving a remount line during a raid. Holding formation under circumstances where better-born men broke.

Once accepted, however, a hussar is a hussar.

There are still snobs.

Obviously.

Humans did not build feudalism to become emotionally healthy overnight.

But within the regiment, earned wings carry real force. A foreign-born or common-born hussar with proven wings may still be mocked, but he is mocked as one of them.

That is an important distinction.

Also a very loud one.

## Succession and Permanence

The Vorokov Hussars are tied to [[House Vorokov]], but they are more deeply tied to [[Barony Vorochev]].

If the baronial line fails, the regiment remains.

If the baronial line is disgraced, the regiment remains.

If a Vorokov heir proves unworthy, the regiment remains and politics begins sharpening knives.

House Orenic would install a new baron before it allowed the hussars to collapse, relocate, or become a wandering military household. The remount yards, roads, families, contracts, shrines, training fields, and entire local economy are in Vorochev. Moving the regiment would be like moving a forest because one tree offended someone.

Possible replacements could come from:

- a cadet Vorokov line
- a proven hussar officer
- a druznikh with enough battle honor
- a marriage claim through Baroness Mila’s network
- an Orenic-backed cavalry noble
- a politically useful outsider who could survive being accepted by the regiment

The last option is dangerous.

The hussars do not require a baron to share their blood.

They do require him to understand what he is sitting on.

## Relationship with House Orenic

House Orenic values the hussars as a prestige force, fast-response arm, and visible symbol of Royonad’s independence from Dominion methods.

The hussars are especially useful because Royonad cannot rely only on heavy formations or static defenses. Its political memory is full of border pressure, deniable raids, bad-faith treaties, and enemy houses that know how to make war without admitting they started one.

The Vorokov Hussars are an answer to that world.

They can move before a court finishes arguing.

Naturally, this makes courts nervous.

Good.

## Relationship with Other Gorundian Forces

### Infantry

Infantry resent the hussars until the hussars save their flank.

Then they resent them with affection.

### Heavy Knights

Heavy knights often consider the hussars overdressed, underarmored, and insufficiently committed to proper impact. Hussars consider heavy knights brave furniture.

Both are sometimes correct.

### Scouts

Professional scouts respect the hussars more than they admit. The hussars are louder, richer, and more dramatic, but the good ones know terrain, distance, weather, and enemy behavior.

### Priests

Priests appreciate their courage and dislike their tavern habits.

The hussars consider this a fair review.

## Adventure Hooks

### The Commoner’s Wings

A common-born rider performs an undeniable act of courage and earns nomination for wings. Several noble families object quietly. Old Red-Spur does not object quietly.

### The Unworthy Heir

A Vorokov relative attempts to leverage blood claim over demonstrated incompetence. The hussars obey on paper while the entire barony watches for Stanislav’s response.

### The Foreign Rider

A non-Orenic rider seeks admission. He is brilliant, brave, and politically inconvenient.

### The Broken Pennon

A squadron pennon is lost in a disastrous pursuit. Recovering it becomes a matter of honor, but the truth of how it was lost may ruin an officer.

### The Paid Tavern

The hussars enter a town and pay damages before drinking. The townsfolk panic because that means the hussars expect either celebration or grief by morning.

### Old Red-Spur’s List

Old Red-Spur carries names that should not be on the Empty Saddle Roll yet. He knows which young men are brave, which are doomed, and which officer is going to get them killed.

### The New Baron Problem

A succession crisis in Vorochev forces House Orenic to consider installing a new baron. The hussars will accept merit, courage, and competence. They will not accept a decorative idiot with a seal ring.

## GM Notes

The Vorokov Hussars should feel like elite cavalry with institutional personality.

They are not generic horsemen.

They are disciplined lunatics, noble peacocks, common-born legends, frontier patriots, grief-drinkers, horse worshipers, and dangerously competent raiders in the same red-cloaked package.

Play the contradiction hard:

- in camp, they are loud, stylish, insulting, vain, and half-feral
- before action, they go silent
- under signal, they obey instantly
- after action, they explode back into noise
- around horses, they become almost priestly
- around cowards, they become cruel
- around genuine courage, they become surprisingly welcoming

Their culture should attract young idiots for the wrong reasons and forge a few of them into terrifying adults.

That is the trick.

That is also the tragedy.
