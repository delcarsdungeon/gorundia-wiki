Here’s the **“they’ve earned it” version**: the Iron Covenant are not just elite hell-knights. They are **contract-bound anti-hero suppression troops**, built specifically to stop champions, giants, paladins, and inconvenient seven-foot problems with opinions.

# The Iron Covenant, Brutal Siege Version

# Core Brutal Upgrade

Every serious Iron Covenant member gets this:

## Contract-Bound Flesh

These men are not undead, not devils, and not fully mortal anymore. They are living men with infernal clauses stapled to the soul. The legal system continues to disappoint.

**Effects:**

- High Pain Threshold
    
- Hard to Kill +3 minimum
    
- Hard to Subdue +3 minimum

- Resistant to Mind Control +3

- Cannot be forced to surrender by ordinary morale checks

The armor is **Fine or Very Fine blackened plate**, engraved with Vogel manticore marks and infernal contract script.

**DR:**

- Torso: DR 11
    
- Skull/helm: DR 12
    
- Face: DR 8
    
- Arms/legs: DR 10
    
- Hands/feet: DR 8
    
- Eyeslits: DR 4

- Fortify +2

If commanded by a Prefect or Marshal, they gain:
- +1 to active defenses when adjacent to another Iron Covenant knight.    
- +2 to resist Feints, Beats, Ruses, and Intimidation.    
- May use **sacrificial parries** for the Prefect or designated objective.

### Dirty Tricks

## Shield-Pin

If an Ashmantle hits with Shield Bash and wins a Quick Contest of ST or Shield skill, target is pinned or partially pinned against a wall, gate, ally, or obstacle.

- Target suffers -2 DX until breaking free.
    
- Adjacent Covenant get +1 to attack that target.
    
- Huge targets like Orin get +3 to resist but can still be slowed.
    

## Hook the Knee

Polearm attack to leg at -2.

If it hits and the target fails defense:

- Resolve damage normally.
    
- Contest Polearm vs target’s ST or DX.
    
- Failure means target falls, kneels, or loses Step next turn.
    

Against Orin, this should not remove him from the fight. It should ruin his rhythm. That’s enough to make him mad, which is the closest thing to joy a GM gets.

## Chain-Binder Techniques

### Lock the Limb

Attack arm, leg, weapon, or neck at normal hit location penalties.

If hit and defense fails:

- Target is grappled by that location.
    
- Chain-Binder rolls Chain skill in future contests.
    
- Target suffers normal grapple penalties.
    

### Set the Teeth

Requires one Ready maneuver after a successful chain grapple.

- Target is at -3 to break free.
    
- Moving away inflicts 1d+2 cutting damage unless armor fully protects the bound area.
    
- If bound by two set chains, target loses Step unless winning a Quick Contest.
    

### Bring Down the Giant

If two or more Chain-Binders have chains on a target:

- They may combine effort.
    
- Each assistant adds +2 to the lead Chain-Binder’s Contest, max +6.
    
- Huge or very strong targets get +3 to resist.
    

If Orin loses:

- By 1–3: loses Step.
    
- By 4–6: falls to one knee.
    
- By 7+: falls prone or is slammed into terrain.
    

If Orin wins by 5+, he yanks one binder off balance.

If Orin wins by 10+, he pulls one binder into reach and may make an immediate free attack at -2.

That is the fun part. Let the trap bite both ways. Players love tactical traps that can reverse into slapstick homicide.

## Manticore Guard Special Moves

### Manticore Rush

Once every 10 seconds.

- Move up to half Move.
    
- Slam at +3 damage.
    
- If target is knocked back into wall/gate/statue, add 2d crushing.
    
- Target must roll DX or fall.
    

Against Orin, this becomes a cinematic strength collision. Let him contest it. Let the table lean in like idiots watching two trucks argue.

### Barbed Counter

If the Guard parries a melee attack by 3+, he may immediately attempt a bind, beat, or disarm at -2.

### Crush the Opening

If an enemy fails an attack against the Guard by 5+, the Guard may take a free Step and shield-check or shoulder-check at -4.

This lets him punish reckless All-Out Attacks without making him invincible.

### Hold the Threshold

When defending a doorway, stair, bridge, gatehouse, or narrow hall:

- +1 Block
    
- +1 Parry
    
- Cannot be forced backward unless losing a ST Contest by 5+
    

This is where he becomes a real problem.

# Set-Piece Encounter: The Covenant Breach Lock

This is the “they’ve earned it” encounter.

## Composition

- 1 Ash-Prefect
    
- 2 Manticore Guards
    
- 6 Ashmantle Veterans
    
- 3 Chain-Binder Adepts
    
- 1 Infernal Censer-Magus
    
- Optional: 8 lesser Vogel men-at-arms with crossbows, mostly there to die and block movement
    

This is not a random encounter. This is a siege set-piece. Use it at a gatehouse, bridge, inner courtyard, stairwell, or under the overhang with the burning sand pots.

# How They Fight Orin

## Opening Round

- Censer-Magus starts **Ash-Breath** and **Pain Hymn**.
    
- Prefect commands **Take His Leg**.
    
- Chain-Binders target Orin’s legs.
    
- Ashmantles form a shield crescent.
    
- Manticore Guards hold the center.
    

## Round 2

- If Orin is chained, the second Chain-Binder goes for weapon arm.
    
- Third Chain-Binder goes for opposite leg or torso.
    
- Manticore Guard uses **Manticore Rush** if Orin tries to break the line.
    
- Prefect uses **Bleed the Giant**.
    

## Round 3

- If Orin is slowed, they drop sand, fire, rubble, or gate machinery on his position.
    
- Ashmantles stab from Reach 2.
    
- Manticore Guard attacks legs or weapon arm.
    
- Prefect stays just out of Orin’s happiest murder-zone.
    

## If Orin Breaks Free

They do not panic.

- One Manticore Guard intercepts.
    
- Ashmantles All-Out Defense and create a wall.
    
- Chain-Binders reset.
    
- Prefect uses **Close the Iron**.
    
- Censer-Magus switches to **Blood-Kindle** if the line is bleeding badly.
    

## If Orin Reaches the Censer-Magus

The Censer-Magus spends everything on escape, smoke, or defensive magic.

If he dies, the Covenant loses its buffs. Let the players feel that. Otherwise the fight becomes just numbers wearing hats.

# Anti-Orin Kill-Zone Terrain

To make this brutal without feeling like GM cheating, give the battlefield obvious features.

## 1. Iron Chain Anchors

Set into walls, floor, and gatehouse pillars.

Chain-Binders using an anchor gain:

- +3 to resist being pulled.
    
- +2 to hold a restrained target.
    
- But if Orin destroys the anchor, all attached binders must roll DX or fall.
    

Anchor stats:

- DR 12
    
- HP 25
    

## 2. Murder-Slit Crossfire

Crossbowmen above fire at restrained targets.

Use bodkin bolts.

- Skill 14
    
- Damage 1d+5 impaling
    
- Armor divisor 2 if you want them relevant
    

They are not meant to kill Orin. They are meant to make being chained feel like a bad life choice.

## 3. Burning Sand Pots

This is where it gets spicy.

Dropped from overhangs.

### Burning Sand Pot

**Area:** 2-yard radius primary, 4-yard splash  
**Attack:** Dropped object, use Artillery/TL3, Traps, or Soldier depending on crew  
**Dodge:** Allowed if target has room and sees it coming  
**Damage:**

Primary zone:

- 3d burning
    
- 1d crushing
    
- Continues 1d-1 burning per second for 1d seconds unless cleared
    

Splash zone:

- 1d+2 burning
    
- Continues 1 burning per second for 1d seconds
    

**Armor Effects:**

- Sealed armor protects better.
    
- Open-faced helmets, gaps, cloth padding, exposed skin? Bad day.
    
- If damage penetrates DR, target suffers -2 to Vision and DX for 1d seconds from pain, grit, and heat.
    

**Clearing Sand:**

Requires one Ready maneuver and DX roll.

- Success: stop ongoing burn.
    
- Failure: reduce remaining duration by 1 second.
    
- Assistance gives +2.
    

**Nasty Siege Rule: Sand in Plate**

If burning sand penetrates armor gaps, the victim must roll HT.

Failure:

- -1 DX until armor is removed or cleaned.
    
- Critical failure:
    
    - -2 DX and -1 Move.
        
    - Also, they scream. Because they are not furniture.
        

Against Orin, this is useful because it forces action cost. It probably won’t delete him. It will piss him off and make him choose between murder and not cooking in his own gear.

## 4. Portcullis Drop

If Orin pushes too deep:

- Falling portcullis: 4d crushing
    
- Pinning effect if it traps a limb or blocks passage
    
- DR 9, HP 45 to break through
    

## 5. Narrow Bastard Stairs

Large characters suffer:

- -1 to attack
    
- -1 to defense
    
- Cannot Retreat unless descending into open space
    
- Polearms from above get +1
    

Orin should hate stairs. Everyone should hate stairs. Stairs are civilization’s passive-aggressive attack on knees.

- Chain-Marshal Durn Halvek

- 2 burning sand pot crews
    

## Battlefield

- Narrow gatehouse throat
    
- Overhead murder gallery
    
- Two chain anchors on each wall
    
- Portcullis behind the Covenant
    
- Rubble piles restricting movement
    
- One side stair to reach the gallery
    

## Objective

The Covenant is not trying to chase the party.

They are trying to:

1. Hold the breach for 12 rounds.
    
2. Keep Orin pinned under the gallery.
    
3. Force the party to split between:
    
    - killing the Censer-Magus,
        
    - stopping the sand crews,
        
    - freeing Orin,
        
    - and breaking the line.
        

This is how you make it hard without just saying “the enemy has ST 40 and your fun is cancelled.”

---

# Victory Conditions

Do not run this as “kill every enemy” unless you want combat to last until everyone’s beard grows.

Use battlefield goals.

## Party Wins If:

- Prefect dies or retreats.
    
- Censer-Magus dies and at least one Manticore Guard falls.
    
- Chain-Marshal dies and the portcullis is breached.
    
- They hold the gate for allied troops to reinforce.
    
- Orin breaks through the line and reaches the inner yard.
    

## Covenant Wins If:

- They delay the party 12 rounds.
    
- They force Orin out of the breach.
    
- They drop the portcullis and isolate 1–2 PCs.
    
- They bloody the party badly enough that the next phase of the siege gets harder.
    

This lets the Covenant “win” without a TPK. Villains with objectives. Revolutionary concept, I know. Someone alert the bards.
