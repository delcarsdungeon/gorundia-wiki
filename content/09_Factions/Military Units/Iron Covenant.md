# Iron Covenant Hell-Knights

_Vogel’s Infernal Shock Troops, also called Ashmantles_

These are the bastards Wilhelm Vogel was saving for the kind of problem normal knights solve by dying politely. They are not “duel Orin one at a time” enemies, because that is how you make expensive plate-armored soup. They fight in **locked files**, use **chains and polearms**, and are built to make a monster like Orin burn actions, fatigue, and patience.

They are not individually stronger than Orin. They are disciplined enough to make that matter less, because apparently hell also discovered middle management.

---

# Common Iron Covenant Traits

All Iron Covenant troops have some version of the following:

## Infernal Drill

They are trained to fight in formation under supernatural pressure.

**Game Effects:**

- +2 to resist Intimidation and Fright Checks.
    
- May use **Shield Wall Training** if appropriate.
    
- Will not break formation unless:
    
    - their Prefect dies,
        
    - they suffer 50% casualties,
        
    - or they are magically commanded/compelled.
        

## Blood-Fed Plate

Their armor is black iron veined with ember-red script. It is not fully alive, but it behaves like it resents being dead metal.

**Game Effects:**

- While the wearer has spilled fresh blood in the last 10 seconds, they gain:
    
    - **Regeneration: Slow**, 1 HP per 12 hours normally, but in combat it manifests as **1 HP every 10 seconds**, max 5 HP per fight.
        
    - +1 to HT rolls against stunning and knockdown.
        
- This stops immediately if the wearer is:
    
    - disarmed and held,
        
    - unconscious,
        
    - exorcised,
        
    - or removed from the armor.
        

This is not enough to make them trolls. It is enough to make players say, “Oh, come on.”

## Infernal Obedience

They are not mindless. Worse, they are obedient.

**Game Effects:**

- Fanaticism: Iron Covenant / Infernal Contract
    
- Sense of Duty: Unit
    
- Resistant to Fear +3
    
- Resistant to Mind Control +3 when obeying direct orders from a Prefect.
    

## Weaknesses

They are terrifying, but not unbeatable.

- Holy weapons bypass the blood-fed regeneration.
    
- Sun-blessed or Pelorite consecrated weapons suppress regeneration for 1 minute.
    
- Their formation tactics degrade badly in broken terrain.
    
- They are slow in stairs, rubble, mud, and cramped interiors.
    
- Killing or banishing the Prefect can cause a morale shock.

Against Orin, their job is to **not die immediately** and let heavier assets do their job.

### Gear

**Weighted Barbed Chain**

- Reach 1–4
    
- Swing: 2d+1 crushing
    
- Entangle limb: Chain skill vs target’s active defense
    
- On success, target is grappled by the limb.
    
- Contest of ST to pull, trip, or force posture change.
    

**Infernal Lock-Hooks**

Once a chain grapple lands, the Chain-Binder may take a Ready maneuver to set the hooks.

- Target suffers -2 to break free.
    
- If target moves away without breaking free, takes 1d cutting damage.
    
- Against bare flesh or light armor, this is disgusting. Medieval design, naturally, decided “worse” was a feature.
    

### Special Technique: Hamstringing Pull

Attack leg at -2. If the chain hits and the target fails defense:

- No immediate crippling unless damage penetrates.
    
- Target must win a Quick Contest of ST or DX vs Chain-Binder’s Chain skill.
    
- Failure means target falls or is forced to kneel.
    
- Large targets get +2 ST, but the Chain-Binder may receive help from nearby Ashmantles.
    

### Against Orin

Use 2 Chain-Binders and 4 Ashmantles if you want a real scene.

They aim to:

1. Chain one leg.
    
2. Chain weapon arm or shield arm.
    
3. Force Orin into contests.
    
4. Let polearms jab him from Reach 2.
    
5. Make him spend turns smashing chain instead of smashing men.
    

Chain DR: 8  
Chain HP: 12  
Orin can absolutely break them, but it costs actions. That is the point. Annoying? Yes. Tactical? Also yes. Humanity limps onward.

### Special Ability: Command the Line

Once per turn, the Prefect may give one command.

Choose one:

**Hold**  
All Iron Covenant within 8 yards gain +1 Block or Parry until next turn.

**Close**  
One nearby Ashmantle may Step and Attack immediately after the Prefect’s turn.

**Bind**  
Chain-Binders gain +2 to the next Quick Contest to pull, trip, or hold a target.

**Bleed Him**  
All Iron Covenant attacks against one named enemy gain +1 damage for one second if at least two attackers engage that target.

### Improved Blood-Fed Plate

The Prefect heals **1 HP every 5 seconds**, maximum 10 HP per fight, if blood has been spilled nearby.

Holy damage prevents this for 1 minute.

### Against Orin

The Prefect should not try to out-muscle Orin. He should fight like a cruel chess player:

- Defensive Attack.
    
- Retreating parries.
    
- Command Chain-Binders.
    
- Use Wait to punish overcommitment.
    
- Let Orin win individual exchanges while losing position.
    

A Prefect with 4 Ashmantles and 2 Chain-Binders is a nasty encounter for Orin and friends.

### Special Ability: Manticore Rush

Once every 10 seconds, the Manticore Guard may make a brutal shield or shoulder rush.

- Move up to half Move.
    
- Slam at +2 damage.
    
- If target fails defense, resolve knockback normally.
    
- If target is knocked back into a wall, add 1d crushing.
    

This is mostly there so Orin can get knocked into masonry once and everyone at the table can make that “oh shit” face. Worth it.

### Special Ability: Barbed Counter

If the Guard successfully parries a melee weapon attack by 3 or more, it may immediately attempt a free bind/disarm at -2.

Against huge weapons, this becomes a Quick Contest of weapon skill or ST-based weapon skill.

### Against Orin

One Manticore Guard can give Orin a real fight.  
Two can threaten him badly if they coordinate.

They should:

- Use Evaluate and Defensive Attack early.
    
- Force slam contests.
    
- Try to pin him against architecture.
    
- Attack weapon arm, legs, or shield.
    
- Punish All-Out Attacks.
    

If Orin goes full beast mode and has PC support, he can still wreck them. Good. He should. But he should have to work for it.

# How to Make Them Challenging to Orin Without Cheating

Do not just inflate damage forever. That gets boring fast.

Use:

## 1. Reach

Polearms and chains make Orin spend movement and positioning.

## 2. Control

Leg binds, weapon binds, shield hooks, trips, forced kneeling.

## 3. Formation

The Covenant should fight like a machine.  
A horrible machine.  
A LinkedIn machine.

## 4. Terrain

Doorways, stairs, murder holes, overhangs, rubble, smoke.

## 5. Objectives

They do not need to kill Orin. They need to:

- delay him,
    
- split him from allies,
    
- keep him off a gate,
    
- force him into a killing lane,
    
- buy time for a ritual,
    
- protect a Prefect,
    
- or hold him under the burning sand pots.
    

## 6. Attrition

Orin wins the duel.  
The Covenant tries to make sure there is never just one duel.

# Orin-Specific Rule: The Beast Draws the Chain

If the Iron Covenant has studied Orin, give them this doctrine:

When fighting a Huge or giant-class melee threat, Chain-Binders may combine efforts.

Each additional Chain-Binder or Ashmantle assisting a chain hold gives:

- +1 to the Quick Contest to restrain, max +4.
    
- If using two separate chains on different limbs, Orin must break each separately.
    

However:

- Orin may attack a chain directly.
    
- Any successful attack dealing 12+ cutting or 16+ crushing damage to a chain destroys or ruins that chain.
    
- If Orin wins a Quick Contest by 5+, he may pull one binder prone.
    
- If he wins by 10+, he may pull the poor bastard into melee range.
    

This is fun because it makes the chain tactic strong but risky. If they mishandle him, he turns a Chain-Binder into a weather event.

# Recommended Named Mini-Boss

## Prefect Malrec Voss, the Eighth Ember

Former mortal knight. Now mostly contract and spite.

Use **Ash-Prefect** stats with:

- Broadsword-19
    
- Tactics-17
    
- Will-17
    
- Add Trademark Move: **“Kneel the Giant”**
    

### Kneel the Giant

If two Chain-Binders have a target restrained, Voss may attack the target’s knee or lower leg at only -1 instead of normal hit location penalty.

Damage is normal, but if any damage penetrates DR, target rolls HT at -2 or suffers temporary crippling for 1d seconds.

Against Orin, this is not “haha he is crippled forever.” It is “for three seconds, the armored murder refrigerator has a problem.”

That’s enough.

# Quick Reference: Difficulty Dial

Use this when adjusting at the table.

## Easier

- Remove Ember-Censer Bearer.
    
- Reduce Ashmantles to ST 14, skill 15.
    
- Blood-fed armor only heals after combat.
    
- Chain-Binders cannot combine bonuses above +2.
    

## Standard

Use as written.

## Hard

- Add Prefect.
    
- Give Chain-Binders Balanced chains, +1 skill.
    
- Allow blood-fed healing up to 8 HP per fight.
    
- Use terrain and smoke.
    

## Brutal

- Add Manticore Guard.
    
- Give Prefect Penetrating Weapon 1.
    
- Let Censer Bearer maintain two effects at -2.
    
- Use prepared kill-zone terrain.
    

Do not start with Brutal unless the party has earned it, or unless you’re trying to teach them the ancient lesson: “Maybe charging the infernal phalanx alone was not technically strategy.”
