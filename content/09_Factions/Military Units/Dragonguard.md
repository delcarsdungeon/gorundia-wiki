# The Dragonguard

> “A man comes to the Dragonguard when every other name he owns has failed him. If he survives, he gets a new one. If he doesn’t, well… the old one was probably already ruined.”
> — Sir Sorek Redmarch

## Overview

The Dragonguard are the sworn martial household of House Sylvanranth: helmed soldiers, adopted-name men, oath-bound outcasts, and reformed criminals forged into one of the strangest elite companies in Gorundia.

They are not knights, though some fight as well as knights.

They are not mercenaries, though they are paid better than most.

They are not monks, though their discipline borders on religious severity.

They are not jailers, though many first came to them because a jail was the next likely destination.

They began as Lord Theron Sylvanranth’s private steel: a disciplined force apart from the City Watch, apart from noble levies, and apart from the usual rot of bribes, favors, blood-ties, and convenient incompetence. In Bard’s Gate, they were feared as the Dragon’s faceless men.

That reputation has followed them.

It has not always helped.

## Current Status

Current operational strength: **approximately 200**.

The Dragonguard are, on paper, the household troops of [[Dana Sylvanranth]], Countess of Blackvale.

In function, they remain something closer to an independent military order. They follow Dana’s orders to the letter, without hesitation or visible doubt, but they are not culturally or institutionally absorbed into Blackvale. Their habits, discipline, names, rituals, and internal justice remain their own.

This makes them intensely useful.

It also makes them politically disastrous, because apparently no one in Gorundia can own a sword without also becoming a constitutional problem.

## Recent History

When Ponzio Bordella was made Lord Mayor of Bard’s Gate, the Dragonguard packed their arms, emptied their barracks, and left the city.

To a man, they went west to Westhold.

They did not swear themselves to the Warden of the West.

Not then.

But when [[Theron Sylvanranth]] bent the knee and entered service under [[Orin Kharne]] as a knight, the Dragonguard followed him. They went not as a civic guard, nor as a mayoral weapon, but as the household force of a man who had given them names when the world had mostly given them gallows.

Theron’s death shamed them deeply.

Not because they failed in any simple way. The world is rarely that courteous. But the Dragonguard are men trained to carry blame like kit. If their lord died, then somewhere in the hidden arithmetic of duty, they were found wanting.

Afterward, they remained at Westhold, mostly purposeless but not useless. Under [[Braga Kharne|Baroness Braga Kharne]], Warden of the West, they served with honor. They were trusted by Braga, worked well with [[Harlan Vayne]] and the village leaders, and earned a quiet reputation among Westhold folk as hard men who did hard things without stealing, swaggering, or turning hungry eyes toward the locals.

Dana Sylvanranth’s elevation to Countess of Blackvale gave them their next purpose.

The Dragonguard swore to her to a man.

## Public Reputation

The Dragonguard are respected by soldiers, distrusted by civilians, hated by corrupt officials, and feared by anyone who depends on being difficult to identify in a crowd.

Their discipline is real.

Their loyalty is real.

Their lack of charm is also real, and apparently incurable by ordinary medicine.

They do not explain themselves well. They do not flatter local pride. They do not smile for frightened villagers. They do not understand why a lawful warrant becomes less comforting when delivered at midnight by men with hidden faces.

This makes them useful.

It also makes them dangerous.

In Blackvale, their presence beside Countess Dana is politically explosive. To Dana, they are the only force she can absolutely trust not to be bought by old Bordella coin, frightened by local pressure, or compromised by Vogel loyalties.

To many in Blackvale, they are faceless Sylvanranth soldiers enforcing a Crown seizure of the county.

Both readings are understandable.

Naturally, everyone involved prefers the least helpful one.

## Armor and Names

Dragonguard armor is well-made, green-enameled or green-accented, and usually worn with full helms in public duty. Their cloaks vary by posting, but dark green remains traditional.

Their faces are hidden.

Their birth names are abandoned.

Their current names are not shouted, painted, or displayed for common convenience. Instead, each Dragonguard bears a small nameplate worked subtly into the armor, engraved in Elvish and integrated into the design.

Most Gorundians never notice the plates.

Many who notice them assume they are decorative script.

Even a non-elf fluent in Elvish may miss them unless trained to look, because the plate is not presented like a label. It is folded into the armor’s patterning: along a collar-rim, under a pauldron lip, worked into the border of a breastplate scale, or set into the greenwork of the helm.

Dragonguard are trained to read these plates quickly.

Most elves spot them almost immediately.

This has led to the uncomfortable habit of elves addressing Dragonguard by name while nearby humans still think they are dealing with anonymous iron statues. The Dragonguard rarely clarify.

This does not help their reputation.

Because why would it. That might be useful.

## Adopted Names

A Dragonguard name is not a disguise in the ordinary sense. It is a legal and spiritual severing.

The old name belonged to the thief, deserter, bastard, murderer, debt-runner, failed squire, broken soldier, disgraced son, or doomed fool who came to the gates.

The new name belongs to the man who survived the oath.

Dragonguard names are usually short, practical, and easy to speak through a helm. They are not grand titles. They are not heroic decorations. They are work names.

That would imply the universe is kind.

A released Dragonguard may keep his adopted name, lay it down, or take a third name. His birth name remains his to reveal or bury. Speaking another Dragonguard’s birth name without leave is treated as a grave betrayal.

## Sealed Rolls

The adopted names of sworn Dragonguard are recorded in sealed rolls at [[Dragon's Rest]].

These rolls are witnessed by Stern Alia’s devout and Sylvanranth authority. They are not public records. They are not made available to reeves, guilds, local courts, tavern investigators, offended cousins, or anyone else suffering from the tragic condition of curiosity.

This arrangement worked well enough when the Dragonguard served as a private household force.

It works less well when Dragonguard are used for county enforcement in Blackvale.

A man may not need to know a Dragonguard’s birth name to bring complaint against him. But he must be able to identify which adopted-name soldier struck him, arrested him, seized his grain, or stood warrant at his door.

Dana may need to establish a Blackvale civil roll of operational Dragonguard names: not birth names, not private histories, but enough legal identity for complaint, witness, discipline, and law.

The Dragonguard will hate this.

They may still obey.

Those are not the same thing.

## Command Structure

The Dragonguard avoid flamboyant titles. Their hierarchy is clear, practical, and deliberately unromantic.

At current operational strength, the force is organized into four companies of roughly fifty men each, with support staff and rotating veterans attached as needed.

### Commandant

The senior officer of the Dragonguard under Countess Dana Sylvanranth is **Commandant Slate**.

Slate answers directly to the Countess and is responsible for discipline, deployment, internal justice, and liaison with noble command.

He is not a courtier. He is not a noble captain playing at household glory. He is a Dragonguard officer: exact, quiet, literate, difficult to bribe, and even harder to frighten.

This makes him useful.

It does not make him pleasant.

### Captains

Each company is led by a captain. Captains command in the field, execute county or household orders, and keep their men from becoming either sentimental or stupid.

Typical captain names include:

- Captain Flint
- Captain Gray
- Captain Hart
- Captain Morrow
- Captain Yew
- Captain Barrow

### Sergeants

Sergeants are the spine of the Dragonguard. They drill recruits, punish sloppy discipline, lead small detachments, and act as the first real authority most soldiers fear.

Typical sergeant names include:

- Sergeant Tallow
- Sergeant Brindle
- Sergeant Pike
- Sergeant Cinder
- Sergeant Fen
- Sergeant Crow
- Sergeant Anvil
- Sergeant Reed

### Troopers

Rank-and-file Dragonguard are simply called troopers, guardsmen, or “helms” by outsiders.

Within the unit, a trooper is addressed by adopted name only.

A typical field exchange:

> “Moss. Left gate.”
>
> “Seen.”
>
> “Kettle. Count the wagons.”
>
> “Counting.”
>
> “Bitter. If that reeve moves again, sit him down.”
>
> “Gladly.”

This is the Dragonguard at their most conversational.

## [[Dragon's Rest]]

The formal home of the Dragonguard is [[Dragon's Rest]], a small fortified holding in the King’s Demesne. It is one of Lord Theron Sylvanranth’s quieter properties: well-funded, well-supplied, and almost never advertised.

No bright road sign points to it.

No recruiter drinks in taverns promising glory.

No bard sings young fools toward its gates.

But men find it.

They hear enough. They ask the wrong people. They limp through rain. They arrive hungry, hunted, desperate, or angry enough to believe becoming someone else might hurt less than remaining themselves.

Those who serve there call it Dragon’s Rest.

The recruits call it **the Green Pit**.

## The Green Pit

The Green Pit is the training yard, barracks, trial-ground, punishment field, classroom, language hall, and spiritual meat grinder of Dragon’s Rest.

The name is not poetic. It is descriptive.

Recruits are lowered into the life of the Dragonguard and then ground against it until something either breaks or holds.

The first lesson is simple:

No one is coming to save you.

Once a recruit commits, he has no rights inside the training program beyond those granted by the Dragonguard articles. His old name gives him no protection. His old rank means nothing. His old crimes may explain him, but they do not excuse him.

The Triad will not intervene.

No kindly priest will pull him out because the drills are cruel, the food is thin, the instructors are merciless, or the work has stripped him down to shaking bone and regret.

He may quit.

That is the mercy.

Any recruit may walk out. If he does, he leaves with what he came with, sometimes less pride, sometimes more sense. He is not hunted for quitting. He is not murdered for weakness. He is simply not Dragonguard.

Those who remain discover that the Pit is not merely brutal.

It is educational.

A man may enter the gates an illiterate thief and leave fluent in Elvish, conversational in Gnomish, drilled in Gorundian history, able to identify fifty noble arms by sight, and capable of explaining why a supply column dies faster from bad math than enemy arrows.

If that sounds merciful, wait until the sword-work begins.

## Recruitment

The Dragonguard recruit men.

Only men.

This is not treated inside the order as a glorious principle or a sermon on nature. It is brutal practicality.

The Green Pit is filled with violent men under extreme stress: criminals, outcasts, failed soldiers, desperate killers, frightened boys pretending to be hard men, and hard men pretending not to be frightened. There are too few suitable trainers, officers, priests, and facilities to guarantee the safety of women inside that environment.

So the Dragonguard do not pretend otherwise.

Women who make the long walk to [[Dragon's Rest]] are not simply cast into the road. They are given another path through the church of [[Stern Alia]], where their courage, desperation, guilt, or hunger for purpose can be tested under different discipline.

Some resent this.

Some are saved by it.

Some likely would have made better Dragonguard than half the fools who survive the Pit.

The institution is aware of this.

It does not change the policy.

## Screening and Infiltration

Dragon’s Rest heavily screens recruits for infiltrators.

This is not done gently.

A candidate may be watched before he ever reaches the gate. His story is checked where possible. His scars are read. His lies are catalogued. His reactions to hunger, insult, isolation, pain, and temptation are observed.

Some recruits are rejected because they are too broken.

Some because they are not broken enough.

Some because they are loyal to someone else.

Some because they enjoy cruelty.

That last category is removed quickly. The Dragonguard recruit desperate men, guilty men, angry men, and men with bloody histories. They do not knowingly recruit men who love hurting the helpless.

Not twice, anyway.

The trials are designed to catch spies, sadists, cowards, zealots, and men who mistake anonymity for permission.

The Dragonguard have plenty of sins.

They are very specific about which sins are useful.

## Training

Dragonguard training is built on the theory that a man who has already failed one life must not be allowed to fail the next through ignorance.

Instruction includes:

- literacy in Common
- Elvish fluency sufficient for armor-plates, orders, and field signals
- basic Gnomish, especially engineering and sapper terms
- Gorundian history
- noble heraldry and precedence
- field law and warrant procedure
- strategy and small-unit tactics
- formation fighting
- shield-wall discipline
- mounted and dismounted escort work
- prisoner handling
- riot line discipline
- night movement
- ambush survival
- dirty knife-work
- grappling in armor
- disabling strikes
- sentry removal
- fighting in alleys, stairwells, granaries, kitchens, bridges, and other unheroic places where men actually die

Many of these techniques would disgust a noble knight.

This is intentional.

A knight is trained to embody honor.

A Dragonguard is trained to make sure honor survives contact with the cellar stairs.

## Stern Alia’s Shrine

Dragon’s Rest contains a small shrine of Stern Alia and a handful of her devout.

This surprises outsiders who imagine the Dragonguard as godless hard men. They are not godless. They are simply allergic to being comforted.

Stern Alia’s role in the training program is not rescue.

It is judgment.

Her devout help administer oaths, witness renunciations of old names, instruct recruits in duty, endurance, and lawful suffering, and remind them that pain is not automatically purification.

A recruit may kneel in the shrine and confess.

He may weep.

He may shake.

He may beg the gods to make him clean.

Then he is sent back to drill.

This is not because the priests are cruel.

It is because forgiveness does not teach footwork.

## Veterans and Retired Staff

Dragon’s Rest maintains a core of approximately forty veterans rotating out of field duty. These veterans serve as instructors, evaluators, armor-masters, language tutors, formation captains, and living warnings.

A smaller number of working-retired Dragonguard remain full-time staff. Some are missing hands, eyes, or the ability to sleep through bells. Others are simply too old for field service and too useful to release.

They tend the armories, teach recruits how not to die stupidly, maintain the name rolls, and identify which young fools are confusing stubbornness with courage.

A working-retired Dragonguard may no longer be fast.

He is still usually right.

## Terms of Service and Release

The Dragonguard are not slaves.

That distinction matters.

A man may quit the training program. A sworn Dragonguard may petition for release after his term or under extraordinary circumstances. Completion of service may earn coin, arms, a horse, a letter of standing, and, in rare cases, recommendation for household service or minor land.

But while serving, a Dragonguard has surrendered much of himself.

He does not keep his old name.

He does not pursue old feuds.

He does not claim old rank.

He does not use the guard to settle private debts.

He does not reveal another Dragonguard’s past.

He does not remove his helm before outsiders without order or cause.

He does not take bribes.

He does not refuse lawful command because it wounds his pride.

He belongs to the oath.

That oath may save him.

It may also spend him.

## Discipline

Dragonguard discipline is severe, quiet, and internal.

Under current operations, crimes and failures by Dragonguard are handled within the order whenever possible. The punishments can be brutal: confinement, hard labor, loss of standing, stripped duties, public beating before the company, reassignment to the worst watches, or rotation away from trusted work.

Yet the Dragonguard are often more forgiving than outsiders would expect.

They were built from failed men.

The institution believes a man can do wrong, be broken against the articles, and still be made useful again. So long as he returns to discipline, accepts punishment, and does not betray the oath, he may be rotated, watched, punished, and kept.

To the Dragonguard, this is hard mercy.

To outsiders, especially in Blackvale, it can look very much like the helmed men protecting their own.

Both interpretations have teeth.

This may become one of the first great legal conflicts between Countess Dana’s need for order and Blackvale’s demand for visible justice.

## The Oath

The precise form of the Dragonguard oath is not publicly recorded. Known fragments survive through released men, priests of Stern Alia, and those with enough wine in them to become temporarily educational.

Commonly repeated lines include:

> “I lay down the name that failed.”
>
> “I take up the name that serves.”
>
> “My face is not my honor.”
>
> “My blood is not my excuse.”
>
> “I will be judged by the oath, the order, and the work.”
>
> “Let the Dragon remember me if I stand.”
>
> “Let the Pit forget me if I break.”

The final line is traditionally spoken only after the helm is placed.

## Conduct

Dragonguard do not consider themselves police.

This creates problems when they are used for civil order.

They are at their best when assigned to:

- protect a ruler
- secure a gate
- escort dangerous prisoners
- hold a bridge
- guard evidence
- break an ambush
- defend a settlement under threat
- enforce a high warrant under hostile conditions

They are at their worst when expected to:

- reassure villagers
- soothe insulted elders
- explain tax seizures
- manage public grief
- perform ceremonial humility
- distinguish between lawful fear and active resistance quickly enough for everyone to remain happy

They can learn these things.

Most would rather be stabbed.

## The Blackvale Problem

When Dana Sylvanranth became Countess of Blackvale, the Dragonguard swore to her to a man.

To them, this was loyalty restored. Theron was dead. His daughter stood in lawful need. The house had purpose again.

To Blackvale, the meaning was murkier.

Ponzio Bordella had been executed. Cornelia and Allec Bordella had been exiled under Bertram Moffet to Zara Vallytrex’s lands. The old county order had been broken.

In its place came a half-elven Sylvanranth countess, Crown-backed and grim, accompanied by two hundred masked soldiers with adopted names.

That is not a transition.

That is an image people can hate before breakfast.

The Dragonguard are not unaware of this. They are simply bad at caring in a useful way.

Their position is blunt:

> “We are sworn. We are lawful. We are here.”

Blackvale’s reply is becoming just as blunt:

> “Then show us who ‘we’ are.”

The phrase **“No Masks in Blackvale”** has begun to spread in taverns, market yards, mill walls, and chapel gossip. It is partly fear, partly prejudice, partly legitimate legal concern, and partly Dominion-fed poison.

The Dragonguard are capable of holding Blackvale by force.

Whether they can help Dana rule it is another question.

## Relationship with Dana Sylvanranth

Dana does not flatter them.

They prefer this.

She does not ask them to be better men than they are.

They respect this.

She gives clean orders, expects obedience, and does not waste much breath pretending the work is prettier than it is.

They understand this perfectly.

The danger is that Dana and the Dragonguard share the same flaw: both believe competence should speak for itself.

It does not.

Competence has terrible public manners.

Under Dana, the Dragonguard may become something new: not merely a private army, not merely a household guard, but the hard core of a county regime trying to survive famine politics, border war, old noble resentment, and Dominion interference.

Or they may become the symbol that ruins her.

Both futures are standing very close together.

## Relationship with Westhold

The Dragonguard’s time in Westhold matters.

They arrived there after Bard’s Gate, after Ponzio’s appointment, and after Theron’s oath to Orin Kharne. They were outsiders, but they were not conquerors. Under Baroness Braga Kharne, they served with honor.

Westhold villagers learned that a Dragonguard might be humorless, ugly-tempered, and impossible to gossip with, but he would stand a watch in freezing rain without stealing a chicken.

Harlan Vayne and the other village leaders found them difficult but dependable. The Dragonguard, in turn, learned that village leaders are often more useful than minor nobles and usually less impressed by helmets.

This experience may become Dana’s best argument in Blackvale.

If anyone thinks to make it.

## Typical Dragonguard

A typical Dragonguard trooper is not a shining warrior.

He is a hard-trained soldier in good armor, literate, multilingual enough to follow specialized orders, drilled in formation fighting, experienced in close violence, and conditioned to obey under fear, insult, and confusion.

He may once have been:

- a thief
- a deserter
- a debtor’s runaway
- a failed squire
- a disgraced guardsman
- a killer seeking lawful use
- an orphaned sellsword
- a bastard with no inheritance
- a former gang knife
- a peasant strong enough to be dangerous and desperate enough to need structure

After training, he is not innocent.

He is directed.

That difference is the entire Dragonguard.

## Sample Ordinary Dragonguard

### Commandant Slate

Senior field authority of the Dragonguard under Countess Dana. Controlled, cold, precise, and almost aggressively unromantic about command. Slate understands law better than most soldiers and people worse than most reeves. This is not a harmless combination.

### Captain Gray

A steady company captain with a battered helm and a reputation for never raising his voice. Reads supply tallies better than most reeves. Former life unknown. Does not ask men to do anything twice unless the second time is an autopsy.

### Captain Hart

Dismounted field captain, good with village defense and road watches. Has a habit of standing very still while angry men exhaust themselves talking. Westhold elders found him tolerable, which from them counts as a love poem.

### Captain Morrow

Harder edge. Efficient, severe, excellent in raids and prisoner transfers. Poor choice for tense civil duties unless paired with someone who remembers frightened people are not automatically enemies.

### Sergeant Tallow

Training sergeant. Broad, patient, and merciless. Teaches shield work, formation discipline, and how to fall without dying. Recruits hate him until they survive their first real fight.

### Sergeant Brindle

Keeps the name rolls and armor plates. Reads Elvish faster upside down than most clerks read Common. Known for catching recruits lying from tiny inconsistencies in their own invented pasts.

### Sergeant Pike

Field sergeant used for escorts and gate control. Blunt even by Dragonguard standards. Good at keeping men alive. Bad at saying anything that would make civilians less afraid.

### Trooper Moss

Young by Dragonguard standards, still too eager to prove the old name is dead. Useful runner, good eye for terrain, prone to standing too close to trouble.

### Trooper Kettle

Former kitchen thief, or so everyone assumes from the name. Surprisingly educated now. Tracks stores, camp kettles, ration fraud, and poison risks. Terrifyingly serious about soup.

### Trooper Bitter

Older recruit who came late to the guard. Calm in riots, unpleasant in conversation, very good at not hitting first. The kind of man no one likes until the room gets dangerous.

### Trooper Wren

Small, fast, quiet. Used as a scout and messenger. Speaks clean Elvish and enough Gnomish to insult engineering decisions. Claims not to enjoy this. Lies poorly.

## Campaign Use

The Dragonguard are not meant to be loved.

They are meant to be trusted under terrible conditions, which is much more uncomfortable.

They can be saviors in one village and occupiers in the next. They can prevent massacre and still create rebellion by how they prevent it. They are proof that redemption can be disciplined, armed, and socially disastrous.

In Blackvale, they give Dana the strength to rule.

They also give her enemies the image they need to resist.

A faceless army can hold a gate.

It cannot easily win a harvest.
