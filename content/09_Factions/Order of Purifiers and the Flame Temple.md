# The Order of Purifiers

The **Order of Purifiers** is an ancient fire-bound order dedicated to the keeping, tending, and restraint of sacred flame.

Their temple is known by several names: the **Flame Temple**, the **Temple of Bound Ash**, and, since its restoration, the **Rekindled Temple**.

To outsiders, the Purifiers are often mistaken for fire-priests, elemental mages, or a peculiar order of temple-wardens. All three descriptions are partly true and therefore insufficient, which is the usual fate of simple answers.

The Order teaches that fire is not merely destruction.

Fire reveals.

Fire purifies.

Fire consumes what cannot endure it.

Fire makes space for what comes after.

For this reason, the Purifiers do not worship uncontrolled flame. They do not praise wildfire for its hunger or mistake burning for wisdom. Their duty is containment, judgment, and transformation.

A common saying of the Order is:

> Fire is not mercy.  
> Fire is not cruelty.  
> Fire is revelation.

---

## The Living Flame

At the heart of the Rekindled Temple burns the **Living Flame**, an ancient sacred fire said to remember oaths, corruption, sacrifice, and truth.

The Flame is not treated as a simple altar-fire.

It is addressed as a presence.

Some names associated with it include:

> **Nárë Aerlindor**  
> _The Flame That Remembers the First Song_

And:

> **Kha'zir al-Keth**  
> _Keeper of Bound Ash_

Whether these are names, titles, translations, or merely mortal attempts to speak something older is a matter debated by scholars who enjoy being disagreed with by candles.

The Living Flame does not speak often.

When it does, witnesses describe its voice as layered: coals shifting beneath ash, distant thunder, and pages turning in a library that has never known winter.

The Flame is said to ask, not command.

This distinction matters greatly to the Order.

It may also matter less than outsiders would prefer.

## The Rekindled Temple

The temple stands within a wide circle of ash.

No birds cross it.

No insects hum within it.

Even the wind seems to slow at its edge.

Those permitted to enter describe the temple as warm rather than hot, alive rather than comfortable. Blue-gold braziers burn in the halls. Ash rests in carved basins along the walls. The stone smells faintly of cedar oil, heated brass, and old lightning.

The Rekindled Temple is not only a place of worship.

It is also:

- a sanctuary    
- a library    
- a place of binding    
- a house of judgment    
- a refuge for fire-touched mortals    
- a prison for dangerous flame-relics    
- a warded site for elemental rites    

The Purifiers insist that sacred fire must be tended by discipline, not appetite.

Their critics often say this is easy to claim while standing inside a temple full of things that would very much like to burn.

---

## The Purifiers

The **Purifiers** are the mortal members of the Order.

They may be men or women, elf or human, highborn or lowborn, so long as they can endure the rites and submit to the discipline of the Flame.

Purifiers are trained as:

- ritual keepers    
- fire-wardens    
- ash-readers    
- elemental handlers    
- guardians of dangerous relics    
- witnesses of truth    
- students of sacred destruction    
- keepers of controlled burn and cleansing flame    

Not every Purifier is a spellcaster.

Some are scholars.

Some are guards.

Some are mystics.

Some are simply steady enough to stand between a dangerous flame and the rest of the world.

The Order values restraint above power.

A Purifier who can call fire but cannot govern it is considered more dangerous than useful.

---

## The Ember-Sovereign

The restored temple is led by **Sabry**, who bears the title:

> **Ember-Sovereign of the Rekindled Temple**

Her fuller ceremonial style is:

> **Keeper of Bound Ash, Voice Before the Living Flame**

The Ember-Sovereign does not own the Flame.

She does not command it.

She stands before it, answers it, interprets it, restrains what must be restrained, and carries its authority among mortals.

The title is deliberately severe. It does not mean queen, priestess, or servant.

It means that when the Flame asks, someone mortal stands close enough to answer.

And strong enough, perhaps, to refuse.

## Fire-Blood Initiates

In recent days, fire-touched elves have begun arriving at the Rekindled Temple seeking instruction.

Some carry old elemental inheritance in their blood. Their gifts may appear as instinctive command of flame, heat resistance, ember-sight, fire-dreams, or the ability to understand tongues they were never taught when the braziers speak too loudly.

The Order does not treat such gifts as proof of holiness.

It treats them as proof of danger.

Fire-blood initiates are taught discipline before power, silence before invocation, and ash before flame.

The first lesson is simple:

> The fact that fire answers you does not mean it approves.

## The Daughters of Flame

Among the most striking figures of the Rekindled Temple are the **Daughters of Flame**.

They are not Purifiers.

They are servants of the temple and attendants of the Living Flame.

The Daughters appear as grey-skinned, elf-featured women with ember-bright eyes, slow-burning grace, and an unsettling beauty that has caused more than one visiting scholar to forget why he was holding a notebook.

Their origins are not openly discussed.

Certain old songs, carefully avoided genealogies, and the occasional impolite comparison to efreeti courts have led to persistent speculation. The Order neither confirms nor denies such claims.

The Daughters themselves do not appear interested in explaining.

That has not stopped anyone from being curious, because mortals remain committed to being unwise in groups.

They serve as:

- attendants    
- sentinels    
- reliquary bearers    
- threshold guardians    
- ash-preparers    
- ritual aides    
- keepers of temple protocol    

They are calm, disciplined, and dangerous to underestimate.

At the temple threshold, two towering Daughters may appear as living flame-sentinels, twelve feet tall and shaped from layered light and heat. Those they permit to pass describe the sensation as being measured by warmth beneath the skin.

Not burned.

Measured.

---

## Fire-Walking

It is whispered that the Daughters of Flame can pass from one fire to another.

This is not common teleportation.

Nor is it safe.

The Order speaks of such travel rarely, and never casually. Fire-walking is said to require a flame strong enough to receive them, a path hot enough to survive passage, and a will disciplined enough not to scatter into ash between one breath and the next.

A Daughter who enters flame in this way does not merely step through distance.

She risks becoming part of the road.

Those who have witnessed fire-walking describe a sudden narrowing of all nearby flames, a flash of grey skin behind the blaze, and the scent of myrrh, brass, and rain on hot stone.

The Daughters do not use this gift lightly.

When they do, something has usually gone very wrong.

## Rites of the Order

### The Rite of Ash

Those who come before the Flame in solemn petition may undergo the Rite of Ash.

The supplicant’s hair is unbound and then braided tightly. Ash from fallen Purifiers is pressed to the brow, throat, and palms. Oil of Truth may be placed at the hollow of the throat.

The ash is not symbolic only.

It carries memory.

The rite is said to make lies difficult, evasions costly, and hidden motives visible to the Flame.

### The Oil of Truth

A sharp ceremonial oil smelling of myrrh and metal.

Those anointed with it often describe the sensation as burning inward rather than outward, as if some crooked branch within the chest has been forced straight.

### Bound Ash

The Order uses the phrase **bound ash** for dangerous fire, relics, or elemental forces that have been contained rather than destroyed.

To the Purifiers, not every dangerous flame should be extinguished.

Some must be remembered.

Some must be watched.

Some must be denied escape until the world is ready to judge them.

## Doctrine

The Order teaches that fire has three sacred faces:

### Fire as Witness

Fire reveals what is hidden.

It exposes corruption, falsehood, weakness, and endurance.

### Fire as Purifier

Fire burns away rot, disease, poison, and spiritual corruption.

But the Purifiers warn that destruction alone is not purification.

Purification requires judgment.

### Fire as Transformation

What survives the flame is changed.

What does not survive may still feed what comes after.

The Order does not teach that all burning is holy.

It teaches that all burning must be answered for.

## Common Sayings

> Fire is not mercy.  
> Fire is not cruelty.  
> Fire is revelation.

> What calls the flame must be answered carefully.

> The flame that obeys too easily is usually lying.

> Ash remembers shape.

> A clean burn is not the same as a righteous one.

> Never bargain with hungry fire.

> The first duty of the hand that holds flame is not to close.

## Reputation

The Rekindled Temple is treated with respect, caution, and occasional alarm.

Common folk may see the Purifiers as holy fire-keepers, strange but necessary.

Mages tend to regard them as dangerously specialized.

Nobles tend to wonder whether the Order can be useful.

The Order tends to notice that last part immediately.

The Purifiers are willing to advise, cleanse, contain, and witness.

They are not eager to become anyone’s private arsenal.

The Ember-Sovereign’s restoration of the temple has made the Order newly relevant. Fire-touched initiates have begun arriving. Old records are being reopened. Dormant rites are being relearned.

And the Living Flame is watching the world again.

This comforts almost no one sensible.

## Player-Facing Summary

The Order of Purifiers is a restored fire-bound temple order led by Sabry, the **Ember-Sovereign**.

They tend an ancient Living Flame, preserve dangerous fire-lore, contain elemental threats, train fire-blooded initiates, and perform rites of ash, truth, and purification.

The beautiful grey-skinned Daughters of Flame serve the temple but are not Purifiers. Their origins are mysterious, though many suspect ancient ties between elves and efreeti. They may be able to pass through fires at great risk.

The Order believes fire is sacred because it reveals, transforms, and purifies.

It also believes fire must be governed.

Especially when it wants to help.
