# Priesthood of Wee Jas in Gorundia

> “The dead are not property.  
> The soul is not evidence to be misplaced.  
> Magic is not absolution because the caster used prettier ink.”  
> — attributed to [[Canoness Velatha Durn]]

## Overview

The priesthood of [[Wee Jas]] occupies an uneasy but respected place in Gorundia. While [[Pelor]], [[Heironeous]], and [[Pholtus]] form the visible religious spine of the kingdom, Wee Jas governs quieter and more dangerous jurisdictions: death, burial, lawful magic, necromantic boundaries, soul violations, beauty under discipline, and the proper handling of the dead.

Her clergy are rarely loved.

They are, however, listened to.

In Gorundia, Wee Jas is honored as patroness of death, magic, law, and beauty. Her temples are not usually popular public shrines. They are vaults, courts, libraries, mortuaries, salons, and magical registries wearing the skin of a church.

Where Pelor comforts, Heironeous commands, and Pholtus judges, Wee Jas records.

And then she collects.

## Role in Gorundian Society

The Jasite priesthood serves several overlapping functions:

- custodians of lawful death
- overseers of contested burials
- investigators of corpse misuse and soul violation
- temple authorities on necromancy and undead threats
- keepers of dangerous magical records
- legal witnesses in magical inheritance, binding, and death-oath disputes
- ritual specialists when a soul, body, name, or magical identity has been tampered with
- quiet patrons of noble mourning customs, beauty rites, and preserved dignity

Their authority is narrow but sharp.

A Jasite priestess does not outrank a bishop of the Triad in ordinary religious matters. She does not command a king’s chapel, bless armies, or oversee public justice. But when a corpse has been animated, a soul has been bound, a ghost has been wrongfully held, a magical death has violated sacred order, or an inheritance turns on the proper handling of the dead, even proud Heironeans and icy Pholtans tend to step aside.

Not happily, obviously. Priests are still people, and people are what happens when theology gets trapped in meat.

## Theology

Wee Jas is understood in Gorundia through four primary aspects.

### The Ruby Lady

The beautiful, terrible keeper of death’s dignity.

This aspect governs funerary rites, the lawful passage of souls, and the sanctity of remains. The dead are not to be used, mutilated, mocked, animated, or trapped for convenience.

### The Witch-Goddess

The divine patroness of disciplined magic.

Magic is not forbidden, but it must be ordered. Knowledge is sacred. Reckless use is not. Jasite theology is especially concerned with spellwork that alters death, memory, soul, identity, or binding.

### The Ruby Beauty

The patroness of composed beauty, elegance, adornment, and dignity beneath the gaze of death.

This aspect governs beauty as discipline rather than vanity. To the Jasite mind, adornment is not merely decoration. It is order imposed upon the mortal form. A properly dressed body, a perfectly kept face, a preserved portrait, a widow’s veil, or a noblewoman’s ruby mourning jewel may all be acts of reverence.

The Ruby Beauty is especially honored by nobles who understand that appearance is not trivial.

Appearance is testimony.

### The Final Magistrix

The cosmic judge of names, debts, endings, and oaths that survive the flesh.

This aspect dominates the church’s legal identity in Gorundia. A death is not merely an event. It is a transition with rules, witnesses, rights, and consequences.

## The Beauty of Wee Jas

The beauty of Wee Jas is not the warm, life-giving beauty of spring, fertility, or courtly romance. It is composed, deliberate, and exacting.

Jasite beauty is the beauty of the final portrait.

The mourning veil.

The perfect signature beneath a death writ.

The ruby at the throat of a widow who has not cried in public once.

It is elegance under restraint, beauty sharpened by discipline, and the refusal to decay into vulgarity simply because grief, age, or danger has entered the room.

Among some nobles, especially older noblewomen, widows, court mages, duelists, and houses with strong traditions of inheritance law, Wee Jas receives quiet favor as a patroness of poise, dignity, and controlled presentation.

Her worship does not usually appear as open devotion. A noble may never call herself Jasite in public, yet still keep:

- a ruby mourning ring
- a sealed beauty mirror blessed in her name
- a private funerary compact
- a portrait veil used only for death rites
- a spell-preserved gown worn at wakes and inheritance courts
- a cosmetic box marked with a tiny ruby skull, dagger, or crescent

To these nobles, beauty is not frivolous.

Beauty is order made visible.

A face properly composed is a court document.

A body properly dressed is a final argument.

A funeral properly staged is political force wearing black silk.

This quiet noble favor gives the priesthood of Wee Jas access to salons, widow-courts, private libraries, mourning chambers, and inheritance disputes that the Triad often reaches only through formal authority.

The Jasites do not sneer at beauty.

They regulate it.

Which is somehow more alarming.

## Doctrine of the Body and Soul

The Gorundian Jasite doctrine makes a hard distinction between body, soul, name, and memory.

A body may be dead, but it is not ownerless.

A soul may have departed, but it is not irrelevant.

A name may be recorded, but it is not merely ink.

A memory may remain, but it is not always the person.

This makes the priesthood especially dangerous in cases involving:

- undead animation
- soul gems
- soul coins
- magical possession
- revenants
- phylacteries
- necromantic research
- unlawful resurrection
- magical impersonation of the dead
- binding spirits as witnesses, weapons, or fuel

Their recurring legal maxim is:

> “Death ends breath.  
> It does not end jurisdiction.”

## Organization

The Gorundian priesthood of Wee Jas is small but highly structured.

### The Ruby Matron

The senior known authority in Gorundia is:

**Arch-Jasmin Velyara Karsyne**  
*The Ruby Matron of the Third Vault*

Also styled:

- Keeper of the Last Name
- Registrar of Lawful Deaths
- The Woman Who Signs the Writs No One Can Appeal

Arch-Jasmin Velyara Karsyne oversees the principal Jasite temple authority in the capital. Her jurisdiction includes high-order funerary law, soul-ledger disputes, major undead matters, noble death compacts, and cases where arcane practice violates the rights of the dead.

She is not loud.

She does not need to be.

When she appears in person, the implication is that something has gone badly wrong and is about to become properly documented.

### Senior Priestesses

Several senior priestesses serve beneath or alongside the Ruby Matron, depending on jurisdiction and assignment.

**Magistra Arkhava Velis**  
A senior doctrinal and legal officer of the temple. Earlier records style her as a high priestess, but current canon treats her as a ranking magistral priestess beneath Arch-Jasmin. She likely oversees internal doctrine, court procedure, and formal temple opinions.

**Sister Ilyra Thorne**  
*Warden of the Silent Hall*

Responsible for mortuary custody, disputed bodies, pauper remains, criminal corpses awaiting judgment, and bodies that cannot yet be buried because the legal or magical situation is still screaming.

**Sister Nysera Valyx**  
*Priestess-Magus, Mistress of the Veil*

A specialist in necromantic law, soul-bound anomalies, spirit wards, and magical violations involving the dead. She is more likely than most Jasites to work directly with arcane scholars, maesters, or Ruby Magistrates.

**Acolyte Mira Vell**  
A junior initiate and ritual assistant. Useful as a messenger, witness, scribe, or terrified young cleric holding a ruby lantern while older women calmly discuss whether the corpse is legally a victim, evidence, or plaintiff.

## The Ruby Triune

The **Ruby Triune** is a field tribunal of Wee Jas that appeared at [[Castle Stormarrow]] during the crisis surrounding corpse misuse and soul violation.

They are not the entire priesthood. They are a special investigative and ritual delegation, sent when an offense is serious enough to require immediate Jasite presence outside the capital.

### Members of the Ruby Triune

**Canoness Velatha Durn**  
*Ruby Judge of Tombs*  
*Keeper of the Final Ledgers*

Senior authority of the trio. She speaks in law, theology, and verdict. Her concern is not sentiment but jurisdiction: what happened, what order was broken, and who now answers.

**Mistress Rhiassa Vale**  
*Whisperer of Ashes*  
*Voice of the Unfinished Dead*

Spiritual medium and funerary investigator. She is most likely to hear the unsettled dead, interpret revenant behavior, or determine whether a soul is absent, trapped, misdirected, or wrongfully silenced.

**Acolytrix Nerisa of the Shard**  
*Bearer of the Silent Flame*

The youngest of the trio, though “junior” is doing a lot of work there. Nerisa carries ritual implements, ruby flame, soul-lanterns, or sanctified vessels. She is the one people underestimate once.

Usually once.

### Castle Stormarrow Declaration

At Castle Stormarrow, the Ruby Triune gave a joint pronouncement:

> “The body was misused.  
> The soul was not present.  
> The offense is against the order of death itself.”

This line should be treated as canonical.

It cleanly establishes the Jasite position: the problem was not merely murder, desecration, or magic. It was an offense against the lawful structure separating body, soul, and death’s passage.

## The Ruby Magistrates

The **Ruby Magistrates** are a lay and arcane branch of the Jasite order: sanctioned wizards, scholars, maesters, legal witnesses, corpse-law specialists, and magical auditors who serve Wee Jas without being ordained as priests.

They are sometimes called:

- Sanctioned Arcanists
- Ledger-Bearers
- Quiet Adjudicators
- Ruby Magistrates

Their guiding principle is simple:

> “Those who do not cast more spells… but cast them correctly.”

Ruby Magistrates exist because not every matter of death-magic can be handled by prayer alone. Some questions require a spellbook, a legal mind, and the kind of person who can look at a necromancer’s ritual circle and say, with professional disappointment, “This is improperly filed.”

They do not outrank priestesses of Wee Jas.

They are not clergy in the full sacramental sense.

But within their sanctioned domain, their testimony carries weight.

A Ruby Magistrate may be asked to:

- examine necromantic rituals
- determine whether a spell violated the rights of the dead
- authenticate magical causes of death
- inspect soul-binding artifacts
- review magical contracts involving death or oaths
- assist in cases involving undead, revenants, ghosts, demons, or spirits
- advise courts and temples on arcane impropriety

In practice, they are auditors with spells.

This is somehow worse than either thing separately.

## Ruby Magistrate Talent

Also called:

- Sanctioned Arcanist
- Ledger-Bearer
- Quiet Adjudicator

**Cost:** 5 points/level  
**Maximum:** 4 levels

### In-World Description

Not all who serve Wee Jas wear vestments.

Some carry spellbooks.

Some carry scars.

And a few carry something rarer:

> The sense that every spell cast in their presence is being observed.

Ruby Magistrates are wizards who have accepted a simple truth:

> Magic is not merely power.  
> It is property.  
> And all property is accounted for.

They are not stronger than other wizards.

They are harder to deceive, harder to surprise, and far more dangerous to lie to.

### Game Effects

Each level of Ruby Magistrate gives **+1 per level** to:

- Thaumatology
- Hidden Lore (Undead)
- Hidden Lore (Spirits/Demons), or combined at GM discretion
- Occultism
- Research
- Detect Lies
- Diplomacy, only in matters involving magic, death, oaths, or supernatural effects
- Intimidation, only in matters involving magic, death, oaths, or supernatural effects

The social bonuses apply only when the character is leveraging magical authority, knowledge of death, or calling out supernatural wrongdoing.

### Reaction Bonus

Each level gives **+1 reaction bonus** from:

- wizards and arcane scholars
- necromancers, through respect, fear, or both
- priests and priestesses of Wee Jas
- spirits, undead, and death-associated entities

> “They can tell what he is. Even if they don’t like it.”

### Flavor Effects

At GM discretion, a Ruby Magistrate may:

- notice when magic feels off, corrupted, or improperly performed
- be taken seriously when accusing others of magical wrongdoing
- be invited into conversations normally closed to ordinary wizards
- recognize when a soul, name, corpse, or death-right has been mishandled

## Associated Advantages and Perks

These are commonly taught or granted in Jasite circles, but are not required.

### Clerical Investment (Wee Jas)

You are recognized as an agent of Wee Jas and her temples.

You gain:

- access to restricted libraries and records
- hospitality within Jasite temples
- legal or social standing in matters of magical law and death rites

> “You are not ordained.  
> But you are… recognized.”

### Sense the Ledger [1]

You have an intuitive awareness of magical impropriety.

The GM may inform you when:

- magic is corrupted, unstable, or improperly performed
- souls are being tampered with
- a ritual breaks the rules
- a magical death has been mishandled

> “Something about this… is not filed correctly.”

### Mercy of Delay [1]

Once per scene, you may automatically stabilize a dying character without a roll.

> “This death is not yet entered into the record.”

### Sanctioned Access [1]

When using temple or restricted archives:

- +2 to Research or Thaumatology rolls
- time required may be reduced at GM discretion

### Rite-Safe Casting [1]

You are practiced in clean, properly bounded magic.

Gain +1 to resist critical failure or backlash when casting:

- Necromancy spells
- Summoning spells
- soul-affecting spells

### Corrective Touch [2]

Optional.

Once per scene, you may either:

- turn a failed death check into a success

or

- allow a character to ignore shock or penalties from a major wound for 1 minute

> “This damage is… not yet binding.”

## Norwin Stoneeye and the Ruby Magistrates

[[Norwin Stoneeye]] has been inducted into the Ruby Magistrates, though he does not fully realize the nature of that induction.

To Norwin, this may appear to be professional recognition: access to records, grudging respect from Jasite clergy, and an expectation that he will assist when magic and death intersect.

The truth is sharper.

Wee Jas has placed a literal finger upon him.

Her priestesses can see the mark.

It appears to Jasite senses as a ruby-dark impression upon his soul or magical presence, like the perfect trace of a fingertip pressed into wax. It does not make him a priest. It does not give him divine authority in the ordinary clerical sense.

It means he has been noticed.

Among the priestesses of Wee Jas, that is not symbolic.

That is jurisdiction.

### What the Mark Means

Possible Jasite readings of the mark include:

- Wee Jas has accepted Norwin as a lawful witness
- Norwin has crossed a threshold of death-magic knowledge and survived with his identity intact
- Norwin has been set aside for future service
- Norwin’s testimony may carry unusual weight in soul-law matters
- Norwin is not to be casually interfered with by lesser temple authorities
- Norwin may be claimed later, should the goddess require him

The priestesses will not necessarily explain this to him.

They may assume he knows.

They may enjoy discovering that he does not.

## Relationship with Arcane Practice

The priesthood of Wee Jas sits at the boundary between temple and tower.

Gorundian magical culture already recognizes that several churches maintain sanctioned magical practitioners, and Wee Jas is specifically associated with magical law and death rites. Temple scholars often serve as magical legal authorities, archivists of dangerous magical knowledge, and specialists against undead threats.

This gives the Jasite priesthood a respected role in regulating necromantic knowledge.

The core doctrine is:

**Knowledge is not the crime.**

**Misuse is.**

Studying necromantic theory may be lawful. Animating the dead, binding souls, or using death as fuel without sanction is generally not.

This makes Wee Jas one of the few religious powers willing to tolerate dangerous magical scholarship, provided it is recorded, bounded, and kept under competent supervision.

Or at least supervision that looks competent in formal robes.

## Relationship with Nobility

Wee Jas has quiet favor among certain Gorundian nobles.

This is especially true among:

- widows
- court mages
- aging beauties
- duelists
- inheritance lawyers
- houses with old funerary traditions
- noblewomen who understand that grief and power often share a wardrobe

Her appeal is not sentimental. A noble patron of Wee Jas may be drawn to her because she offers dignity without softness, beauty without frivolity, and law without public pleading.

For some noble houses, a Jasite priestess is preferred for deathbed witnesses, private memorials, magical inheritance questions, and funerary presentation. A Pelorite may comfort the family. A Heironean may praise the virtues of the dead. A Pholtan may verify the truth of a confession.

A Jasite makes sure the body, the will, the name, the jewels, the portrait, and the silence all say exactly what they are supposed to say.

This makes them useful.

Which, among nobles, is often mistaken for holy.

## Relationship with the Triad

The Triad does not fully embrace the Jasites, but it recognizes their necessity.

### Pelorites

Pelorites often dislike Jasite coldness, but they respect their role in protecting souls from abuse. Pelor comforts the dying. Wee Jas audits what happens after.

### Heironeans

Heironeans respect Jasite discipline and lawful conduct. They may find the priestesses unsettling, but Jasite verdicts carry weight when honor has been violated through corpse misuse or oath-breaking after death.

### Pholtans

Pholtans are the closest theological cousins and the most frequent rivals. Both value law, order, and truth. The difference is that Pholtus seeks the one truth beneath the light; Wee Jas records the final truth after the light leaves the eyes.

They cooperate beautifully.

They also irritate each other magnificently.

## Common Rituals

### The Ruby Witness

A ritual performed over a corpse whose cause of death, soul-status, or legal identity is disputed.

A ruby lens, flame, or polished gem is held above the brow while a priestess names the deceased and asks whether body, soul, and name remain in lawful accord.

### Sealing of the Last Name

A funerary rite in which the name of the dead is formally recorded and sealed, preventing casual magical impersonation, unlawful summoning, or false claims of inheritance.

### The Quiet Flame

A silent vigil kept beside bodies that cannot yet be buried.

The flame is not for comfort.

It is a warning: this death remains under jurisdiction.

### The Veil of the Last Reflection

A private Jasite rite favored by some noble houses, especially widows and aging courtiers.

A mirror, ruby lens, or polished silver plate is blessed before a funeral, deathbed vigil, or inheritance court. The subject is dressed, painted, veiled, or arranged so that their final public appearance is dignified and controlled.

The rite may be performed for the dead, the dying, or the bereaved.

Among nobles, it is sometimes said:

> “Let grief have the heart.  
> The face still belongs to law.”

### Censure of the Unquiet Art

A formal condemnation of unlawful necromancy, corpse-binding, soul theft, or magical desecration.

This can be read in court, at temple, or over the remains of the offender if everyone has already had a very long day.

## Tone and Presentation

Jasite priestesses in Gorundia should feel:

- formal
- precise
- elegant
- beautiful in a severe way
- legally terrifying
- calm around death
- offended by sloppiness
- deeply uninterested in excuses

They do not rant.

They do not posture.

They ask one question too many, and then someone important realizes the answer is fatal.

## Roleplaying Notes

A Jasite priestess should speak as if every sentence could be entered into a court record.

Common phrases:

> “That is not death. That is interference.”

> “The corpse is not yours to use.”

> “The soul has rights beyond your convenience.”

> “You mistake silence for consent.”

> “The ledger remains open.”

> “Magic explains the method. It does not excuse the offense.”

> “Do not confuse animation with life.”

> “A name is not a handle for fools to pull.”

> “Beauty is not vanity. Vanity begs to be seen. Beauty endures being witnessed.”

> “Grief may tremble. The hand signing the writ may not.”

## Use in Play

The priesthood of Wee Jas is useful when the campaign needs:

- legal horror without gore
- divine authority outside the Triad
- magical ethics with actual teeth
- consequences for necromancy
- experts on souls, revenants, ghosts, and corpse-law
- noble death customs that are both beautiful and terrifying
- people who can look at [[Norwin Stoneeye]] and know he has been marked before he does

The Ruby Magistrates provide a way to reward engagement with Wee Jas without requiring full priesthood, Power Investiture, or conversion.

They are ideal for characters who operate through:

- investigation
- magical law
- supernatural forensics
- occult research
- death-right disputes
- soul-bound artifacts
- undead and spirit cases

For [[Norwin Stoneeye]], this should feel like an optional prestige path, not a forced religious class change. He can remain a wizard while gaining access to the Jasite worldview: not more power, but more accountability.

Naturally, that may be worse.

They are not villains.

They are not comforting allies.

They are the people who arrive after everyone else has made a mess and say, very calmly, that death has rules.

Then they start naming who broke them.
