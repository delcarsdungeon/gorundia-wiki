# House Durathen

> **House Words:** _What Is Owed, Is Answered._

House Durathen is a proud, poor baronet house seated at [[Greyford Hall]], a small holding within the orbit of [[House DeSalle]]. They stand above ordinary landed knights, but only barely. They possess old blood, old grievances, old manners, and the sort of old furniture repaired so many times it is now mostly spite and woodworking.

They are not grand magnates. They are not court-fed parasites. They are workbench nobility: clean steel, mended cloaks, callused hands, careful ledgers, and a chapel full of names that are not spoken lightly.

House Durathen is defined by four things:

- Honor that survives poverty.    
- Labor without shame.    
- Vengeance without apology.    
- Loyalty that does not require stupidity.    

They are humble enough to work with their own hands and proud enough to remember every insult done to their blood.

## Overview

House Durathen holds a modest estate centered on [[Greyford Hall]], with two villages, an aging orchard, limited grazing rights, a small toll bridge, and older claims to charcoal and woodland rights complicated by DeSalle control.

Their lands produce little luxury and less comfort, but enough to keep the house alive.

For generations, House Durathen existed as a minor client house within the DeSalle sphere. It was a practical arrangement rather than a loving one. DeSalle held the baronial title, iron wealth, and formal power. Durathen held discipline, local respect, and the kind of stubborn martial competence larger houses only notice when they need someone reliable to die in the right place.

That relationship broke after the Thralen disaster.

It broke fully when [[Baron Remy DeSalle]] tried to survive his own shame.

## Character of the House

Durathens are not flashy. They do not waste coin pretending to be wealthy. Their best clothes are carefully maintained, their armor is repaired instead of replaced, and their servants often eat at the same long tables as their sworn folk when the season is lean.

Among richer nobles, Durathens are sometimes dismissed as provincial, stubborn, and overproud.

Among common folk, soldiers, and minor houses, they are respected because they do not pretend bread appears by magic or that peasants are scenery with taxes attached.

A Durathen lord may haul sacks during harvest.

A Durathen lady may account grain with ink-stained fingers.

A Durathen daughter may be beautiful enough to ruin a courtier’s judgment and clever enough to make it profitable.

A Durathen son or sworn blade is expected to stand the line when standing the line is a bad idea.

## The Durathen-Thralen Marriage

The central wound of the current generation came through [[House Thralen]].

[[Myreth Durathen]] was born into House Durathen in 1165 OR. She married [[Aledrik Thralen]], lord of the struggling Thralen line of Marsten, hoping to combine Durathen’s modest resources with Thralen’s dwindling inheritance. It was both alliance and gamble.

Aledrik Thralen was a good lord in the old Gorundian sense. He was no blazing zealot and no court peacock. He was a militant steward, a commander who drilled his levies, marched with them, kept his books, and understood that a house is not just a sword, but the hand that grips it, the roof that shelters it, and the name that outlives it.

Through Aledrik, Braga inherited more than land claims and grief.

She inherited the Thralen connection to the Church of Heironeous.

Aledrik’s siblings were not minor names. His sister, [[Cwenheld Thralen]], became the **Venerated Shield of Heironeous**. His brother, [[Patriarch-General Clytus]], became one of the great militant figures of the Heironean church.

This means Braga’s Thralen blood is not merely a family tragedy.

It is a church-adjacent political weapon.

The kind of detail people leave out of reports because once the Church of Heironeous enters the page, the page stops being private.

## Aledrik Thralen

[[Aledrik Thralen]] was born in 1170 OR and died in 1216 OR at the age of 46. He was the son of [[Haldrik Thralen]] and [[Ithildra Caldrith-Thralen]], and brother to Cwenheld and Clytus.

Where Cwenheld saw visions and Clytus marched crusades, Aledrik stood at home as a banner-lord. He was the steady one. The practical one. The lord who made fields defensible, levies disciplined, and duty bearable.

He served Heironeous in the quiet manner of a local lord: not through spectacle, but through watchfulness, order, and courage.

His watchwords were simple:

> “Hold the line.”

Aledrik’s death ended the stable heart of House Thralen. His daughter Braga remembered him with reverence. His wife Myreth carried his memory like a coal under ash.

## Garren Thralen and the Redwood Fen

[[Garren Thralen]], son of Aledrik and Myreth, was the last real hope for restoring the Thralen line.

He died because [[Baron Remy DeSalle]] wanted a glorious story.

Remy DeSalle planned a grand expedition into the [[Redwood Fen]], a perilous marshland rumored to hold ancient treasures, cursed remnants of old war, and enough danger to make fools mistake stupidity for courage.

There was no true military need to cross the Fen.

There was no urgent royal command.

There was only DeSalle ambition, courtly vanity, and the desperate hunger of a middling lord who wanted to return with a relic and a legend tied around his neck.

The expedition collapsed into fear, mist, and confusion. Remy imagined pursuit, overestimated threats in the wrong places, underestimated the Fen itself, and assigned a rearguard to protect his retreating caravan.

Sir Garren Thralen and his loyal men held that rearguard.

They died covering a retreat that should never have been necessary, against attackers drawn from the Fen’s madness and DeSalle’s incompetence. No great enemy had outmaneuvered them. No noble purpose demanded the sacrifice. Garren died because a baron wanted to look brave and then needed better men to cover the consequences.

The relic was lost.

The expedition failed.

Remy DeSalle survived with minor casualties and major excuses.

House Thralen did not survive.

## Braga’s Renunciation

After Garren’s death, [[Braga Kharne|Braga]] publicly broke from DeSalle service.

With the Thralen male line broken and the family’s legal position ruined, the Thralen lands reverted to Baron DeSalle. It was legal. It was orderly. It was the sort of clean legal cruelty that lets nobles commit robbery with excellent penmanship.

Braga renounced her position, released herself from the oath structure that had bound her, and departed as a Freeblade.

She did not become a courtly dependent.

She did not politely wait to be married off.

She became a knight-errant in all but name: wandering, fighting for peasants, taking small purses for hard work, and grinding survival from the tournament circuit.

She bore her brother’s memory on her armor.

She became known as Braga the Brazen.

The name was not given because she lacked shame.

It was given because she refused to accept that shame belonged to her.

## Myreth Durathen

[[Myreth Durathen]] is the true architect of House Durathen’s revenge.

Born in 1165 OR, she is now 65 years old. She is short, white-haired, blue-eyed, and carries the presence of a woman who has survived enough loss to become dangerous in rooms where louder people think themselves safe.

She was born Durathen, married Thralen, and later renounced the Thralen name in despair. That renunciation was never a true rejection of the dead. It was a refusal to let DeSalle define what remained.

Myreth lost her husband.

She lost her son.

She lost the Thralen lands.

She did not lose the account.

Her hatred of Remy DeSalle is not wild. It is not tavern rage. It is disciplined, strategic, and patient. She has spent years turning grief into plans, plans into obligations, and obligations into pressure.

Sir Sorek Redmarch once described her as having the eyes of a woman who lost everything twice and still sleeps with her boots on. That is about right.

Myreth’s grief did not make her helpless.

It made her accurate.

## The Revenge Plot

The plot against [[Baron Remy DeSalle]] did not begin as a lawful duel.

It began as murder.

Myreth Durathen and those loyal to the Durathen-Thralen grievance had reached the end of patience. They were prepared to kill Remy DeSalle outright and take whatever punishment followed. Exile, prison, disgrace, forfeiture, execution: all of it had been weighed against Garren Thralen’s death, the loss of the Thralen lands, and years of DeSalle cowardice hiding behind title.

Myreth did not need it clean.

She needed it done.

Old obligations were called in. Quiet ones. No single favor large enough to draw attention. No obvious hiring of blades. No open purchase of arms.

What was gathered was information.

Routes.

Habits.

Weak moments.

Blind angles.

Hours where DeSalle was technically attended, but functionally alone.

The guiding phrase repeated often enough to be noticed was:

> “We don’t need him weak. We need him finished.”

That was not metaphor.

The Durathens were prepared to whack him and take the lumps.

## Beringer’s Intervention

[[Beringer Amadis]] changed the story.

By recognizing the danger early and alerting Braga, Beringer prevented the Durathen-Thralen revenge from becoming a murder that would have destroyed the family politically, legally, and morally.

This won him enormous favor with Braga.

Not polite gratitude.

Real favor.

The kind that sits deep because he did not talk her family out of justice. He gave them a way to have justice without burning themselves alive to get it.

Beringer acted before the strike. He drew the matter into his authority, coordinated the response, and drafted [[Edwyn Durathen]] before the vengeance plot could become blood in a corridor.

This was the crucial turn.

House Durathen had been ready to spend itself.

Beringer found a way to make DeSalle spend himself instead.

## Myreth’s Acid Bomb

The danger was not theoretical.

During the Lord’s Muster, Myreth Durathen threw an acid bomb at Remy DeSalle from the galleries.

It was a raw moment of grief, rage, and intent made visible. Not politics. Not rhetoric. Not courtly positioning.

A mother tried to melt the man she blamed for her son’s death in front of the realm’s assembled power.

[[Norwin Stonyeye]] caught the acid bomb.

That act mattered more than most people in the room understood at first.

Norwin did not merely save DeSalle’s life. He saved Myreth from crossing a line that would have damned her cause. He saved House Durathen from becoming the family that murdered a baron during the Muster. He saved Braga from having to choose between blood loyalty and the law. He saved Edwyn from inheriting a revenge already spoiled.

The family was not denied vengeance.

They were preserved long enough for vengeance to become honorable.

## The Lawful Turn

After the failed gallery attack, Beringer and the Lord Marshal’s office moved the matter onto lawful ground.

Remy DeSalle was baited, pressed, and given every opportunity to reveal himself.

He obliged.

Like many mediocre nobles with more title than judgment, DeSalle confused being cornered with being insulted, then confused speaking loudly with speaking well.

He insulted the King.

He insulted the kingdom.

He insulted the honor of better men than himself.

By the time he finished, the matter was no longer only a Durathen grievance. It had become a public challenge to royal dignity, knightly order, and the moral foundations of Gorundian noble service.

The Lord Marshal appointed [[Edwyn Durathen]] as champion.

This was legal.

It was also engineered.

And that was the point.

House Durathen’s vengeance had been headed toward criminal bloodshed. Beringer, Norwin, and the Lord Marshal gave it witnesses, structure, and law.

They did not save DeSalle.

They saved the Durathens from having to become DeSalle to destroy him.

## The Duel Under Theron’s Statue

The revenge against [[Baron Remy DeSalle]] reached its public answer beneath the new statue of [[Theron Sylvanranth]] in the courtyard of Heironeous.

The duel was legal.

That does not mean it was clean.

The Lord Marshal did not stumble into the confrontation by accident. The ground had been prepared by crisis, intelligence, and intervention. Beringer Amadis had already recognized that House Durathen was on the edge of outright murder. Norwin had already stopped Myreth’s acid bomb from turning the Lord’s Muster into an assassination.

Everyone who mattered knew the truth:

If this was not handled immediately, House Durathen would finish DeSalle another way.

So DeSalle was given enough rope, enough audience, and enough pressure to become the author of his own destruction.

He insulted the King.

He insulted the kingdom.

He insulted the honor of the court.

That changed the shape of the matter.

The Lord Marshal appointed [[Edwyn Durathen]] as champion, taking the vengeance that Myreth had nearly made criminal and giving it lawful form. Edwyn stood not as an assassin, but as a sanctioned blade.

This was the difference between murder and judgment.

It was also the difference between House Durathen being ruined and House Durathen being vindicated.

Edwyn did not kill DeSalle in a corridor. He did not strike from hiding. He faced him under Theron’s gaze, before nobles, crown, court, and church.

When the duel ended, steel went quiet.

The old debt was paid in public.

For Myreth, it was the death she had demanded.

For Braga, it was vengeance without dishonor.

For Edwyn, it was glory sharpened by family obligation.

For Beringer, it was the moment he proved to Braga that he understood the difference between stopping vengeance and redeeming it.

For Norwin, gods help him, it meant he had caught an acid bomb and somehow made that the less insane version of events.

For the court, it was a warning:

A minor house had been wronged. A baron had sneered too loudly. The Lord Marshal chose the champion. And before the assembled nobility could look away, the system they all claimed to honor actually worked.

## Church Shadows

The Thralen connection to the Church of Heironeous gives the Durathen revenge plot a dangerous outer edge.

[[Cwenheld Thralen]], Braga’s aunt, is the **Venerated Shield of Heironeous**.

[[Patriarch-General Clytus]], Braga’s uncle, is a senior militant power within the Church of Heironeous: Oeridian, weathered, towering, gray-eyed, and very much the sort of man whose disapproval arrives wearing armor.

There is no proof that either Cwenheld or Clytus directly aided Myreth’s plot.

That distinction matters.

It also matters that everyone careful enough to understand politics knows the distinction may not save them.

If either of them were involved, the matter would not end quietly. If they were not involved, the mere possibility still changes how sane people behave around the Durathen-Thralen grievance.

This is not only family business.

It is family business with a holy warhorn sitting on the mantel.

## Edwyn Durathen

[[Edwyn Durathen]] is the young blade of House Durathen: charming, daring, ambitious, and far too handsome for the comfort of people who prefer their reckless men easier to dismiss.

He is 29 years old, dark-haired, blue-eyed, athletic, and dangerous both in the yard and in conversation. He has the quick wit of a court favorite, the instincts of a duelist, and the hunger of a man who knows he was not born first but intends to be remembered anyway.

Edwyn grew up close to Braga, his cousin. Their childhood was built on sparring, hunts, rivalry, and affection. Braga could best him with a blade often enough to keep him honest. Edwyn could win a room often enough to remain insufferable.

He is now sworn directly to the [[Lord Marshal]] as liaison to the minor houses. This gives him a formal role beyond family intrigue and places him where his charm, nerve, and knowledge of small-house politics can matter.

Edwyn’s position is also dangerous.

He was drawn into Myreth’s vendetta against Remy DeSalle, then drafted into Beringer’s lawful solution before the family’s revenge became criminal bloodshed. He admires Myreth’s mind, fears her manipulations, and understands that the story she built could have made him either glorious or damned.

The duel made him a champion.

But it did not erase the shadow of how close the family came to murder.

He wants justice.

He wants recognition.

He wants history to remember him kindly.

These desires do not always walk in the same direction.

## Myra Durathen

[[Myra Durathen]] is Edwyn’s sister and Braga’s cousin, formerly Braga’s lady-in-waiting and now effectively the seneschal and working mind behind much of the daily administration of Orin Kharne’s barony.

Myra is young, beautiful, intelligent, and ambitious.

She knows this.

She is not embarrassed by it.

Myra understands that beauty is a weapon, a key, a disguise, and a currency. She uses it with the same practical intelligence another noble might use a horse, a sword, or a seal-ring.

She once threw herself at [[Beringer Amadis]], fully aware that he was likely to become a duke. This was not girlish fantasy. It was strategy. When the matter of [[Dame Venom]] arose, Myra’s answer was brutally practical: Beringer could keep Venom as a side attachment, and Myra herself was unspoiled.

It was bold.

It was transactional.

It was socially dangerous.

It was also very Durathen.

Myra is not a villain. She is a young noblewoman from a poor house who understands that survival and advancement do not arrive politely. She has watched richer houses dress appetite as tradition for her entire life. She has simply decided not to be worse at the game than they are.

In Orin’s barony, Myra has become indispensable because she is willing to do the brainy, boring work that keeps a realm from collapsing into heroic nonsense. Ledgers, correspondence, household discipline, supply, staffing, reputation, social approaches, and quiet corrections all pass through her hands.

Orin may be the baron.

Braga may be the storm.

Myra makes sure the doors open, the grain moves, the letters arrive, and nobody important forgets who they owe.

## Braga Kharne

[[Braga Kharne]] carries both Thralen grief and Durathen legacy.

She honors her father’s name as Thralen, but the Durathen ethos lives in her bones: wrongs are remembered, debts are answered, and family blood is not decoration for heralds to paint on shields.

After Garren’s death and the reversion of the Thralen lands, Braga chose exile over submission. She became a Freeblade, fighting where she could, helping peasants for too little reward, and surviving through tournaments, road work, and raw defiance.

This shaped her into what she is now: a storm in armor.

Braga is not subtle by nature, but she is not stupid. She understands moral clarity better than most nobles because she paid for it in hunger, bruises, and the humiliation of standing outside rich halls waiting to see if anyone would hire her blade.

Her marriage to [[Orin Kharne]] did not erase Durathen or Thralen.

It expanded them.

Braga is Kharne by marriage, Thralen by honored blood, and Durathen by legacy. Anyone trying to split those loyalties is volunteering for an educational experience.

### Braga and Beringer

Beringer earned profound favor with Braga by intervening before House Durathen’s revenge became murder.

This was not because he protected Remy DeSalle.

Braga would never thank him for that.

He earned her loyalty because he protected her family from the consequences of their own grief. Myreth was ready to kill DeSalle and burn with the house if necessary. Edwyn was being drawn toward glory through blood. The Durathen name was close to becoming a cautionary tale.

Beringer found another road.

He alerted Braga, acted before the strike, drafted Edwyn as the proper blade, and helped turn vengeance into lawful judgment.

That matters to Braga.

He did not tell her to forgive.

He made sure her family did not have to become criminals to be answered.

## Greyford Hall

[[Greyford Hall]] is the ancestral seat of House Durathen. It is old stone, old timber, patched rooflines, cold corridors, and polished dignity. It is not grand, but it has endured worse than poverty and will happily endure snobs.

The hall contains:

- A small chapel honoring Durathen and Thralen dead.    
- A cramped but well-kept armory.    
- A long hall used for meals, councils, and winter work.    
- Old guest chambers kept ready out of pride more than practicality.    
- Records of debts, marriages, service, and grievances dating back generations.    

The chapel matters.

Aledrik and Garren are not abstractions to the family. They are names prayed over, spoken around, avoided at the wrong hour, and invoked when decisions become costly.

## Holdings

House Durathen’s lands are small but stubbornly intact.

### Villages

The house controls two small villages owing customary dues and labor. Durathen rule is not soft, but it is personal. The family knows their tenants by name, which is charming until one remembers that also means they know exactly who is late on wool, grain, or sons for levy.

### Orchard and Grazing

The aging orchard produces uneven harvests but has symbolic value. Grazing rights provide limited support for horses, sheep, and household cattle.

### Toll Bridge

A modest bridge crossing provides small but reliable income. The toll is not large, but for House Durathen even small, reliable income is treated with the reverence other nobles reserve for saints and blackmail.

### Woodland and Charcoal Claims

The house maintains older claims to woodland and charcoal rights now complicated by DeSalle control and baronial paperwork. These claims are legally murky, emotionally clear, and likely to become an issue whenever someone needs another excuse to hate DeSalle.

## Current Family

House Durathen is ruled by [[Roderic Durathen]], Baronet of Greyford Hall. He is a pragmatic and affable lord in his late 50s, respected less for battlefield glory than for steady judgment, careful negotiation, and the ability to keep a poor house from sliding into ruin.

Roderic is not a great warrior, and he knows it. His gift is management: marriages, leases, repairs, small revenues, sworn obligations, and the endless arithmetic of minor nobility. He maintains a small but capable knightly force, not enough to threaten a great house, but enough to make Greyford Hall costly to insult.

His weakness is caution. Roderic can see danger three turns ahead and opportunity only after it has politely knocked twice.

His wife, [[Elinora Durathen]], runs the household with quiet efficiency. Clever, resourceful, and socially sharper than she lets on, Elinora has spent years stretching limited resources without letting the family appear desperate. She is especially proud of her children’s achievements and has been central in arranging what advantageous connections the house can claim.

Elinora’s flaw is that she micromanages everything. This is irritating. It is also one of the reasons the house still has clean linens, solvent accounts, and guests who do not notice the roof has opinions about rain.

### Roderic and Elinora’s Children

[[Jorric Durathen]] is the heir to Greyford Hall. A dutiful knight in his early 30s, Jorric is brave, dependable, and deeply traditional. He lacks Edwyn’s charm and Myra’s cleverness, but he has the virtue every small house needs in an heir: he shows up, does the work, and does not mistake drama for leadership.

[[Evelyne Durathen]] is married to [[Lord Aldric Farwynd]], a minor lord and baronet whose holding is respectable but not grand. The marriage gave House Durathen a useful connection rather than a dramatic elevation. Evelyne remains humble, pragmatic, and dutiful, often serving as a quiet bridge between Greyford Hall and the broader web of minor houses. She is frequently caught between the interests of her natal house and her married one, which is apparently what noble marriage is for, because civilization hates women with free afternoons.

[[Edwyn Durathen]] is Roderic’s younger son, a knight of considerable charm, ambition, and skill. Once merely a younger son seeking renown in neighboring service, Edwyn has now become far more important after the DeSalle affair. Drafted as champion before House Durathen’s vengeance curdled into murder, he became the lawful blade by which Remy DeSalle was answered. He is now tied to the Lord Marshal’s work as liaison to the minor houses, a role that suits both his charm and his hunger for significance.

[[Myra Durathen]] is Roderic and Elinora’s youngest daughter, formerly the clever unmarried daughter at Greyford Hall who advised her father on estate matters whether anyone asked or not. She later served as Braga’s lady-in-waiting and has since become effectively the seneschal and administrative mind behind much of Orin Kharne’s barony. Myra is intelligent, beautiful, ambitious, and fully aware that every one of those traits can be sharpened.

[[Landric Durathen]] is the youngest son, still in training as a squire. Eager, dutiful, and determined, he looks up to Jorric and wants badly to prove he belongs in the same family stories as Braga and Edwyn. This is noble and also dangerous, because young men trying to become legends are one bad speech away from becoming casualties.

### Extended Family

[[Myreth Durathen]] is Roderic’s sister, widow of [[Aledrik Thralen]], and mother of [[Braga Kharne]] and [[Garren Thralen]]. She is the grief-struck architect of the DeSalle revenge and the living reminder that Durathen memory is not decorative.

[[Harlan Durathen]] is Roderic’s elder brother, a retired knight and respected local figure. Though elder by birth, Harlan set aside any claim to rule after long service and injury, leaving Roderic to manage the house. He remains at Greyford as advisor, trainer, and the man young knights fear disappointing more than their actual fathers.

[[Alden Durathen]] is a cousin of the main line and a merchant by trade. His modest wealth and trade connections provide quiet but essential support to the house. The family may pretend not to rely on merchant coin, because nobles enjoy lying to themselves with heraldry nearby, but Alden’s income has kept more than one Durathen plan from dying in the account book.

Through Myreth’s marriage into House Thralen, Braga is also tied by blood to [[Cwenheld Thralen]], the Venerated Shield of Heironeous, and [[Patriarch-General Clytus]], one of the great militant voices of the Heironean church. This connection gives the Durathen-Thralen grievance weight far beyond Greyford’s modest borders.
## Politics

House Durathen’s political position is delicate.

They are too small to dictate terms to the great houses.

They are too proud to be comfortably owned.

They are too connected to be safely dismissed.

Their former client relationship with House DeSalle has been permanently poisoned. DeSalle authority may still cast legal shadows over former Thralen matters, woodland rights, and old service obligations.

Edwyn’s direct service to the Lord Marshal gives House Durathen new relevance among the minor houses. He can speak for those too small to command a ducal audience but too important to be casually trampled.

Braga’s marriage to Orin Kharne gives Durathen blood an unexpected tie to rising military power.

Myra’s role as seneschal gives the house influence through competence rather than title.

The Thralen church connection gives Braga’s family history a dangerous moral legitimacy. Nobles can dismiss a poor house. They are much slower to dismiss a poor house whose dead were kin to the Venerated Shield and a Patriarch-General of Heironeous.

This is how House Durathen survives: not by pretending to be larger than it is, but by becoming very difficult to move without consequence.

## Reputation

### Among Minor Houses

House Durathen is respected. They are seen as proof that a poor noble house can retain dignity without crawling, and that small houses can still demand satisfaction when wronged.

Many minor nobles quietly admire the DeSalle matter, even if they would never say so near DeSalle ears.

### Among Great Houses

The great houses view Durathen as useful, prickly, and possibly contagious.

They are not dangerous in raw numbers, but they are dangerous in precedent. If every minor house began demanding lawful satisfaction from negligent superiors, the entire noble order would become much less convenient for the sort of people who enjoy being irresponsible from horseback.

### Among Common Folk

Commoners near Greyford Hall tend to speak well of House Durathen. The family is stern, but present. They work. They listen. They fight.

That buys more loyalty than velvet ever has.

### Among DeSalle Loyalists

Durathen is remembered as insolent, overproud, and ungrateful.

This is mostly because DeSalle loyalists prefer their victims quiet.

### Among Heironeans

The Thralen name carries weight. Aledrik was not a church prince, but he came from a family that gave the faith Cwenheld and Clytus. Braga’s bloodline therefore receives more careful attention from Heironean knights than a random minor-house grievance would.

Not automatic support.

Not immunity.

But attention.

And attention from Heironeous is rarely comfortable.

## Current Role in the Campaign

House Durathen now sits at the intersection of several active forces:

- The minor houses need representation and protection.    
- DeSalle power has been publicly checked.    
- Braga and Orin’s barony requires competent administration.    
- Myra is emerging as a serious political operator.    
- Edwyn’s role with the Lord Marshal makes him a key liaison.    
- Myreth remains a dangerous strategist shaped by grief.    
- The Thralen connection ties the family to major Heironean authority.    
- Beringer earned serious favor by saving their revenge from becoming criminal.    
- Norwin unexpectedly preserved the whole lawful shape of events by catching Myreth’s acid bomb.    
- The old Thralen wound remains a symbol of what happens when noble incompetence hides behind title.    

House Durathen should feel like a small house stepping into history not because it wanted glory, but because greater fools kept leaving debts unpaid.

## Themes

### Proud Poverty

Durathen poverty is not comic relief. It is pressure. It shapes their choices, limits their options, and hardens their contempt for waste.

### Vengeance Redeemed by Law

The house nearly chose murder.

They were saved from it.

That matters.

Their revenge against DeSalle became powerful because it was pulled back from assassination and remade as public judgment.

### Women as Operators

Myreth and Myra are not background decoration. Myreth planned vengeance. Myra runs machinery. Braga embodies consequence.

### Labor and Nobility

Durathens believe noble blood does not excuse idle hands. Work is not shameful. Failure to answer duty is.

### Memory as Inheritance

The house does not merely inherit land. It inherits obligations, grudges, debts, and names.

Especially names.

### Church Weight Without Church Proof

Cwenheld and Clytus do not need to be proven conspirators to matter. Their existence changes the gravity of every room where Braga’s grievance is discussed.

## Table Use

Use House Durathen when the campaign needs:

- A proud minor house with legitimate grievances.    
- A grounded noble family that contrasts with richer, more corrupt houses.    
- Political allies who are useful but not submissive.    
- A reminder that vengeance can be understandable, dishonorable, lawful, or redeemed depending on who catches the bomb.    
- A way to show Braga’s roots outside Orin and Kharne.    
- A vehicle for Myra’s rise as a dangerous administrative and social operator.  
- Minor-house politics with teeth.    
- Heironean church implications without immediate church intervention.    

## Voice and Manner

Durathens speak plainly for nobles. They can do courtly language when needed, but prefer words that land clean.

Common phrases:

> “Debt does not vanish because ink dries over it.”

> “A poor house may still keep clean steel.”

> “We do not forget. We decide when remembering becomes useful.”

> “Rank is not armor against consequence.”

> “The hand that works may still hold a blade.”

> “We do not need him weak. We need him finished.”

## Adventure Hooks

### The Charcoal Claim

An old Durathen woodland right, tangled in DeSalle records, becomes relevant to army supply. DeSalle agents insist the claim reverted with Thralen lands. Durathen records say otherwise.

### The Last Fen Survivor

A surviving retainer from the Redwood Fen expedition emerges with proof that Remy DeSalle knowingly fabricated the rearguard danger or abandoned Garren after realizing the mistake.

### Myra’s Ledger

Myra uncovers irregularities in baronial accounts that point back to DeSalle, Thralen land seizure, or someone quietly profiting from the dead.

### Minor Houses’ Petition

Edwyn gathers minor houses to bring grievances before the Lord Marshal. Some are legitimate. Some are traps. All are politically explosive.

### Clytus Notices

Patriarch-General Clytus learns more than anyone intended about the DeSalle matter. He does not act immediately. He asks for records. This is worse.

### Cwenheld’s Silence

Cwenheld offers no public comment on Braga’s grievance. Her silence becomes political weather.

### DeSalle’s Quiet Friends

Not everyone accepts that the matter is settled. A DeSalle loyalist faction seeks to shame, isolate, or provoke House Durathen into overreach.

### The Acid Bomb Becomes a Rumor

Someone leaks that Myreth threw the acid bomb. The official story becomes unstable. If the wrong people learn Norwin caught it, they may try to frame the intervention as a cover-up rather than mercy.

### Braga’s Name

Someone challenges Braga’s right to invoke Thralen honor after the reversion of their lands. This is an excellent way for someone to have a brief, educational afternoon.

## GM Notes

House Durathen should never feel helpless.

They are poor, not weak.

They lack money, not pride.

They lack troops, not courage.

Their danger lies in commitment. Once they decide a thing must be done, they will use every tool available: marriage, charm, law, household loyalty, old records, public shame, steel, beauty, church kinship, and patience.

They almost became murderers.

That should not be softened.

They were saved from that line by Beringer’s intervention and Norwin’s absurdly timely act of heroism. That should not be softened either.

The best version of House Durathen is not pure.

It is redeemed.

They do not forget.

They answer.
