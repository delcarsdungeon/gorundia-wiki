# House Vogel — History, Crimes, and Fall

> [!danger] GM Reference
> This article includes hidden genealogy, occult crimes, and succession claims not generally known in-world.
>
> Public knowledge, proven fact, archival suspicion, and GM-level truth are separated where the distinction matters.

## Current Status

House Vogel was one of Gorundia’s founding ducal lines and ruled the Duchy of Grevandar for centuries through military prestige, administrative discipline, fear, and a carefully maintained legend of necessary severity.

That rule ended on **16 Readying 1231 OR**.

Duke Wilhelm Vogel chose trial by combat after the collapse of his conspiracy against the Crown. During the confrontation he publicly revealed that Lord Marshal Beringer Amadis was another son of Galmyr Vogel. Beringer defeated and killed Wilhelm in the arena.

Wilhelm’s body was damaged beyond ordinary resurrection. He left no legitimate issue. The ducal dignity of Grevandar is unsettled, Castle Direhold is under Crown seizure and investigation, and the old Vogel coalition has fractured into surviving vassals, frightened functionaries, opportunists, and the Ashen Wing.

The Raven Queen’s promise that Wilhelm will return has become the central creed of the Ashen Wing. This promise persists despite the established impossibility of restoring him by ordinary resurrection. It may be a lie, a promise of transformation rather than resurrection, or simply the sort of technical distinction infernal and divine powers enjoy using to ruin everyone’s week.

# Heraldry and House Identity

## Ducal Arms

> **Sable, a manticore rampant Or armed and langued Gules, within a bordure embattled Argent.**

## Motto

> **The Shadow Takes Flight.**

## Colors and Associated Symbols

- Black, gold, silver, and deep crimson
- The golden manticore as the lawful ducal charge
- The embattled silver border as fortress, boundary, and claimed protection
- The raven as an unofficial and increasingly ominous household badge
- Black armor and crimson mantles among elite Vogel troops

The manticore projected predatory strength under discipline. The silver battlement claimed that Vogel cruelty was defensive necessity. The raven, whether adopted as omen, pun, or devotional leakage from Wilhelm’s later pacts, became harder to separate from the Raven Queen as his reign decayed.

## House Ethos

The governing premise of House Vogel was simple:

> **Weakness invites disorder. Order is mercy.**

In practice this produced:

- rigid social hierarchy
- serfdom and controlled movement
- heavy taxation
- narrow channels of lawful appeal
- severe punishment presented as prevention
- meticulous records
- military readiness
- habitual concealment of anything that weakened the family’s image

This system did provide safe roads, disciplined garrisons, reliable toll stations, and rapid punishment of banditry. It also trained generations of officials to mistake fear for consent and silence for order.

# The Vogel Blood Affliction

> [!secret] GM-Level Truth
> A hereditary affliction runs through portions of the Vogel and Ellysaran bloodlines. Its expression is inconsistent and shaped by upbringing, magic, and individual will.

Observed tendencies include:

- men drawn toward obsession, control, paranoia, and the belief that only they can hold the world together
- women drawn toward fury, predatory instinct, and in rare cases hereditary weretiger transformation
- heightened danger when the bloodline is combined with isolation, shame, or occult influence

Leena represents a controlled but genuinely cursed expression of this inheritance. Wilhelm represents an expression channeled into domination and covenant. Beringer carries the blood but was raised outside the Vogel machine, with Amadis duty and chosen obligations acting as restraints rather than excuses.

The blood explains pressure. It does not absolve choices.

# Rise of the Iron Dukes

## Maletryx’s Long Rule

Maletryx ruled for decades and transformed Grevandar into a militarized, tightly administered duchy. He expanded fortifications, regularized taxes, strengthened the house cavalry, and built the political image later generations inherited: the Vogel duke as the hard lord willing to do what softer nobles would not.

This prestige was never entirely fraudulent. Vogel troops were disciplined, roads under their direct protection were reliable, and the house answered national war with real blood.

The danger was that genuine service became a permanent argument against scrutiny.

## The Battle of Red Gorge, 1190 OR

Red Gorge ended the Dominion’s great westward offensive and secured Gorundian independence.

Count Alistair Amadis was the principal architect of the battle plan. House Moffet executed the dangerous feigned retreat on the right. Lady Nienor Allarson held the wooded left. House Vogel provided the violent center.

Maletryx rode with his son Maxen and eighteen-year-old grandson Galmyr beneath the golden manticore.

The Vogel charge shattered the Dominion center but met a brutal counterattack led by a half-orc warlord and Hextorian shock troops. Maletryx and Maxen were killed. Galmyr seized his father’s sword, rallied the survivors, and became one of the battle’s defining heroes.

The story later became cleaner than the battle:

- Maletryx and Maxen became sacrificial patriarchs.
- Galmyr became the Shield of Red Gorge.
- The Vogel charge became proof that their authority was earned in blood.
- The contributions of Amadis, Moffet, Allarson, and the allied forces were remembered, but Vogel pageantry ensured that the manticore dominated popular memory.

The legend was deserved. House Vogel’s later conclusion that deserved glory made them permanently unaccountable was not.

## Galmyr’s Rule, 1190–1210 OR

Galmyr inherited the duchy directly from the battlefield.

His rule rebuilt Grevandar, strengthened Direhold, secured mines and trade routes, and crushed banditry. He was an effective ruler and a severe one.

### The Redgrove Massacre

The ugliest crime associated with Galmyr’s rule is the suppression of Redgrove.

A peasant rising driven by famine, taxes, and the killing of a tax collector was answered with overwhelming force. Surviving testimony and local tradition hold that Galmyr’s troops killed not merely the rebels but families and children, then burned the settlement as an example.

Vogel records described a lawful suppression of armed revolt. The absence of complete official records is itself suspicious.

“Remember Redgrove” survived as graffiti, whispered resistance, and part of the curse traditions surrounding Wilhelm’s wives.

### Galmyr’s Death

Galmyr died in 1210 OR in an officially recorded hunting accident involving a fall and a boar. No later source has established murder, though the event has never been free of suspicion.

Wilhelm was still a child. Zanara became regent.

## Zanara’s Regency

Zanara stabilized Grevandar, prevented rival houses from exploiting Wilhelm’s minority, maintained revenue, and deepened the house bureaucracy.

She was intelligent, ambitious, and more politically capable than her magical reputation suggested. Her methods also reinforced the habits that made later conspiracy possible:

- controlled archives
- compartmentalized officials
- punishment for embarrassing truths
- the use of family necessity to excuse private horror

Zanara died in 1220 OR in a fire that also killed Wilhelm’s first wife.

Any source describing Zanara as alive during Wilhelm’s final reign is obsolete.

# Wilhelm Vogel

## Rule and Character

Wilhelm inherited a legend too large for him and spent his life trying to become harder than it.

He was:

- intelligent
- physically courageous
- administratively competent
- an effective military organizer
- intolerant of ridicule
- dependent upon fear
- incapable of allowing uncertainty to remain outside his control

He continued heavy taxation, fortified Direhold, expanded mines, and elevated the Iron Covenant.

His central failure was not simple cowardice or incompetence. It was his conviction that any act preserving his control became necessary by definition.

## The Iron Covenant

The Iron Covenant served as Vogel’s elite heavy corps.

Commonly reported characteristics included:

- companies organized around the number sixty-six
- black plate veined with magical script
- crimson mantles
- chain-linked formation tactics
- unusual endurance and silence
- an absence of ordinary camp disorder
- rumors of infernal reinforcement or alteration

Not every member was necessarily a knowing cultist. The organization as a whole was part military order, part political theater, and part occult machinery.

After Wilhelm’s death, surviving Covenant personnel fractured among surrender, Crown custody, flight, and Ashen Wing recruitment.

# The Six Wives

## Established Facts

Wilhelm married six women. All six died. None produced a surviving heir.

The first wife died in the same 1220 fire that killed Zanara. Later wives died through illness, accident, or circumstances that Vogel records treated as unrelated.

The Halls of the Dead later established that the dead wives’ souls had been bound within Wilhelm’s occult legacy. Beringer freed them.

This destroys the old official claim that the pattern was merely tragic coincidence. It does not provide a complete individual murder ledger for all six women.

## The Virtuous Maiden

One wife became remembered as the Virtuous Maiden, a revenant or restless sacred figure associated with vengeance and Pelorite justice.

The revenant tradition was treated for years as village superstition. The later discovery of the wives’ bound souls gave it far more credibility.

Any “widow” encountered at Direhold after Beringer freed the wives cannot be assumed to be one of the original souls. It may be an echo, counterfeit, construct, or trap.

## Full Archive Ledger of Earlier Theories

Before the fall of House Vogel, six main explanations circulated.

### The Curse Theory

The house was cursed by Redgrove, a betrayed woman, an ancestral crime, or the bloodline itself.

### The Stern Husband Theory

Wilhelm’s coldness and control destroyed the women emotionally and exposed them to neglect or despair.

### The Raven Queen’s Price

The marriages were offerings, installments, or failed attempts to satisfy a bargain.

### The Quiet Murder Theory

Wives who failed to produce heirs, learned too much, or became politically inconvenient were removed.

### The Fertility Theory

Miscarriages, failed heirs, and medical complications were concealed beneath cleaner official causes.

### The Political Theory

Each death dissolved an alliance that had begun to constrain Wilhelm or his mother.

The later evidence suggests these theories were not mutually exclusive. Human beings, having invented both bureaucracy and occult sacrifice, are fully capable of using several bad systems at once.

# Cornelia Bordella and the Marriage Crisis

Cornelia Bordella was originally betrothed to Bertram Moffet. The arrangement was broken so that she could be offered to Wilhelm.

Bertram and Cornelia consummated their relationship before the proposed Vogel marriage. Ponzio Bordella answered through violence and family discipline; Bertram was maimed and politically disowned, while Cornelia was confined to the Monastery of the Celestial Veil.

Wilhelm treated the affair as personal humiliation, dynastic theft, and proof that lesser houses had denied him what he believed he was owed.

The episode intensified:

- the Vogel–Moffet feud
- Ponzio’s cruelty and ambition
- Cornelia’s political radicalization
- Wilhelm’s obsession with constructing or obtaining an acceptable bride
- later theories involving altered bodies, soul substitution, and the Raven Queen’s contract

Cornelia never became Duchess Vogel.

After the Steel Purge she relinquished title and independent authority. She and Allec Bordella were placed under Bertram Moffet’s responsibility and later sent to Zara’s lands.

# The Regicide Conspiracy

## Objective

Wilhelm’s final conspiracy was intended to kill King Rimmand II, destroy Beringer politically, and seize control of the resulting succession crisis.

The assassination was not completed.

## The Bloodline Mechanism

The ritual exploited Vogel blood shared through several branches:

- Wilhelm’s own blood
- the royal line through Allynnora Vogel Ryomar
- Beringer’s descent from Galmyr
- blood oaths, true names, and royal relics

The plot used kinship as both weapon and forged evidence.

## Maszareth Wearing Ouro’s Face

Maszareth, an infernal contract devil, was to operate under the appearance of Ouro, a wizard once publicly bound by oath to Beringer.

This would make the magical and legal evidence point toward Beringer:

- Beringer’s blood
- Beringer’s former wizard
- Beringer’s oath
- Beringer’s supposed motive
- forged correspondence prepared to complete the story

The conspiracy was designed not merely to murder a king but to make the wrong man’s guilt appear metaphysically true.

## Krozen

The original plan called for removing or neutralizing Krozen, the King’s hound archon guardian.

That plan failed.

Zara blasted Krozen during the arena crisis, but he was not successfully removed as the conspirators intended. Heironeous returned him publicly in a bolt of lightning during the Steel Purge declaration.

The PCs know what Krozen is.

## Zara Vallytrex

Zara’s exact actions while corrupted cannot be reduced to the old claim that she was simply Wilhelm’s willing accomplice.

Current controlling facts are:

- she was corrupted and spiritually compromised
- she remained bound by feudal oaths within Wilhelm’s orbit
- she was later purified and restored
- she exposed herself fully within the dreamscape, expecting judgment rather than rescue
- the PCs saved her
- she reaffirmed her oaths before King Rimmand II in the Temple of Heironeous
- the King publicly cleared her and affirmed that her position beside Wilhelm arose from feudal obligation
- she is now attempting restitution
- her responsibility for the arena dead remains a personal moral wound

Earlier dossiers that state Zara unquestionably planted evidence, deliberately banished Krozen, or freely joined the regicide plot are superseded.

# Crimes and Findings

## Confirmed or Established

### Attempted Regicide

Wilhelm organized or directed an infernal bloodline conspiracy against King Rimmand II.

### Attempted Frame of Beringer

The plot deliberately used Beringer’s blood, former associations, and oaths to manufacture evidence of treason and regicide.

### Infernal and Raven Queen Compacts

Wilhelm’s government maintained occult relationships beyond lawful royal or ecclesiastical authority.

### Binding of the Wives

The souls of Wilhelm’s dead wives were incorporated into his occult legacy and later freed by Beringer.

### Dynastic Coercion

Women, including Cornelia, were treated as resources to be acquired, spent, altered, or sacrificed for succession.

### Abuse of Ducal Machinery

Archives, household offices, military forces, vassal obligations, and controlled testimony were used to hide private crimes and manufacture lawful appearances.

### Maintenance of the Iron Covenant

The Covenant operated as an elite force with evidence of unlawful magical or infernal alteration.

## Strongly Supported but Incompletely Adjudicated

### Redgrove

Galmyr’s massacre is supported by local memory, missing records, and the political culture surrounding the suppression. A complete royal judgment was never rendered.

### Deaths of Individual Wives

The overall occult exploitation is established. The exact killer, method, and purpose in each individual death remain partly unreconstructed.

### Direhold Experiments

Dungeons, hidden chambers, ritual sites, altered troops, and missing servants support extensive unlawful experimentation. The full inventory remains under investigation.

## Structural Complicity

House Vogel’s fall did not mean every vassal was equally guilty.

It did establish that the ducal system rewarded silence:

- Lynwenden Cron confessed complicity and was executed.
- Ponzio Bordella was convicted and executed.
- Blackvale was removed from House Bordella.
- Some vassals remained lawful and were confirmed rather than punished.
- Zara was purified and publicly cleared.
- House Moffet was not stripped.
- House Kendal remained in possession.
- House Terevon remained in possession.
- Nerys Cron retained Thunderhold after her husband’s death, subject to continuing review.

# Public Reputation Before the Fall

## The Protector Story

Many commoners sincerely respected Vogel rule.

Typical beliefs included:

- Vogel kept roads open.
- Vogel punished bandits.
- Vogel maintained granaries and disciplined troops.
- Vogel worked while softer nobles debated.
- Harshness was regrettable but necessary.

The Iron Covenant’s clean formations and lack of ordinary looting made the protector story credible.

## The Counter-Story

In Bard’s Gate and the frontier, the story began to fail.

Bards called the Iron Covenant the **Choir of Shovels** and mocked Wilhelm as:

- Wilhelm the Whisperless
- the Duke Who Dines Alone
- Lord of the Empty Table

The more Wilhelm tried to regulate satire under “truthful account and moral burden,” the more the Bardic Colleges treated censorship as free advertising.

Beringer’s money and the anti-Vogel narrative campaign did not need everyone to believe Wilhelm was a monster. It only needed them to stop agreeing that he was necessary.

## Regional Differences

### Grevandar Peasantry

Many older peasants remembered safe roads and effective punishment. Younger people increasingly saw arbitrary severity and inherited fear.

### Frontier Baronies

Vogel’s claim to protect the realm weakened when neighboring lands saw few Vogel troops against raids and local monsters.

### Merchants

Merchants valued predictable tolls and hated the scale of those tolls. They respected order while quietly preparing for the next administration.

### Clergy

Heironeans respected discipline but warned against pride. Pelorites increasingly saw the human cost. Pholtans were divided between admiration for order and alarm at manufactured truth.

### Nobility

Most nobles waited to see which story would win, the traditional spiritual vocation of the hereditary governing class.

# The Fall

## Collapse of the Plot

The assassination conspiracy was foiled completely. The King survived. The frame against Beringer failed. The evidence instead exposed the depth of Vogel’s occult and political crimes.

## Trial by Combat

Wilhelm chose trial by combat.

Before the duel he revealed Beringer’s descent from Galmyr Vogel, turning a hidden dynastic fact into public knowledge.

Beringer killed Wilhelm in the arena.

The death was final enough that even Zara later remarked upon its thoroughness. Ordinary resurrection is impossible.

## The Steel Purge

After Wilhelm’s death, the Crown turned from one man to the system that had protected him.

Major consequences included:

- Lynwenden Cron executed in the Hall of Steel
- Ponzio Bordella condemned and executed
- Cornelia Bordella abdicated title
- Cornelia and Allec placed under Bertram Moffet’s responsibility
- Dana Sylvanranth appointed Countess of Blackvale
- Count Vorias Serrin arrested and censured for failure of oversight
- Baron Aldren Veymar arrested for the illegal alchemical poison trade
- Baron Remy DeSalle’s earlier death followed by Jareth’s succession
- royal and inquisitorial entry into Grevandar
- seizure and inventory of Vogel archives, armories, treasury, ritual sites, and demesne property

## The Steel Crusade

The Crown declared that the purge would not end in the Hall of Steel. Paladins, Crown agents, and allied forces moved into Grevandar to expose hidden crimes, occupy dangerous sites, and prevent Vogel hardliners from reforming as a coherent army.

Vogel troops were dispersed in controlled groups rather than granted a glorious final battle. Knights were permitted to surrender with limited honor; ordinary troops were disarmed and sent away in fragments. The strategic purpose was explicit: they would leave as individuals, not remember themselves as an army.

# Confiscations and Current Disposition

## Ducal Dignity

No successor to Wilhelm has been recognized.

The title is not awarded to Beringer, Cron, Ryomar, Leena, or any other claimant by this article.

## Castle Direhold and Ducal Demesne

Direhold and the former direct Vogel lands are under Crown seizure, military supervision, and continuing religious and arcane investigation.

The long-term owner and ruler remain unsettled.

## Blackvale

The county was removed from House Bordella and granted to Dana Sylvanranth.

This was a refoundation, not a regency for the Bordella heirs.

## Wyrmlea

Zara Vallytrex retained Wyrmlea after purification, oath reaffirmation, and public royal clearance.

## Ironmere

House Moffet retained Ironmere.

## Thunderhold

Lynwenden Cron’s death did not automatically extinguish the barony. Nerys Cron remains Baroness of Thunderhold pending any later judgment.

## Sunhollow and Southmere

House Terevon and House Kendal remain in possession.

## Private Vogel Property

Moveable wealth, archives, military equipment, ritual materials, and estates proven to be part of the conspiracy are forfeit or held as evidence. Personal claims by innocent servants, tenants, creditors, and collateral kin remain subject to adjudication rather than blanket seizure.

# Surviving Claims to Vogel Blood and Grevandar

> [!warning] A blood claim is not the same as a lawful award.
> Proximity, legitimacy, attainder, royal prerogative, and political survival all matter.

## The Crown

House Ryomar descends from Allynnora Vogel, daughter of Maletryx.

The Crown also holds the sovereign right of escheat and forfeiture where a ducal line fails or is attainted.

This is the strongest institutional claim, though absorption of Grevandar into the Crown Demesne would alter the kingdom’s balance of power.

## Beringer Amadis

Beringer is Galmyr Vogel’s living natural son and Wilhelm’s half-brother.

**Strengths:**

- closest living publicly acknowledged descent from Galmyr
- Lord Marshal
- victor over Wilhelm
- strong royal trust
- broad military legitimacy

**Weaknesses:**

- illegitimate birth
- raised and identified as Amadis
- no stated desire to become Duke of Grevandar
- appointment could look like payment for killing his brother

He has a real claim. He has not pressed it.

## House Cron / Cranne / Crawne

> [!secret] Hidden Genealogical Truth

House Cron descends from the lost Cranne or Crawne cadet line of the old Vogel blood.

Evidence includes:

- variant spellings across charters
- heraldic similarities
- unusual Vogel preference toward Thunderhold
- erased or obscured marriages
- the half-elf Jedall Crawn carrying the archaic name
- Norwin Stoneyeye’s surviving connective analysis

Only a handful of archivists, genealogists, and old clerics suspect the truth.

The claim is weakened by Lynwenden Cron’s execution, the secrecy of the evidence, and the absence of a named heir under Nerys.

## Leena / Elissa Vogel

Leena is Elissa Vogel, living daughter of Maletryx and Ellysara.

Her children Kaelen and Mirelle continue that bloodline.

This may be the oldest living direct collateral Vogel branch. It is politically unusable without exposing:

- Leena’s identity
- the falsified family record
- her curse
- her intelligence network
- her children

Leena has shown more interest in protecting Beringer and her children than in sitting in Direhold.

## The Royal Maternal Branches

Glandyr Ryomar, Lydena Orenic, and their descendants also carry Vogel blood through Allynnora.

These are remote collateral claims and are politically subordinate to the Crown’s institutional authority.

## The Ashen Wing

The Ashen Wing claims loyalty to Wilhelm’s promised return.

It has no lawful claim.

It possesses:

- hardliners
- fugitive Vogel officers
- Raven Queen devotees
- surviving military contacts
- a story desperate men may prefer to legal reality

That is enough to start a war and not enough to inherit a duchy.

# What Survives of House Vogel

House Vogel survives in five forms.

## Blood

Beringer, Leena, the Ryomar descendants of Allynnora, the hidden Cron/Crawn line, and other remote branches carry Vogel ancestry.

## Law

Grevandar’s tax rolls, roads, garrisons, courts, and land customs cannot be erased simply because their architects became criminals.

## Victims

The wives, Redgrove, coerced tenants, altered soldiers, missing servants, and ruined families remain part of the house legacy.

## Myth

Red Gorge was real. Galmyr’s courage was real. Vogel discipline sometimes protected people. That truth makes the later crimes harder to dismiss as the work of cartoon villains and easier for hardliners to weaponize.

## Rebellion

The Ashen Wing offers people the comforting lie that the old order can return without its costs returning with it.
