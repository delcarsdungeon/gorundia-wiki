>Apocrypha.  Order of events changed, the mission was inserted later, Beringer did not witness the day of prayer.  Orders and tone otherwise the same.

Beringer is ushered to the door outside the royal chapel.

To his surprise, Cwenheld—the Venerated Shield of Heironeous—and Patriarch-General Clytus stand watch.

Other paladins line the hall beyond, silent and impassive.

Cwenheld turns at the sound of his approach—

not quite towards his face at first, but close enough.

Her chin lifts a fraction, listening rather than looking.

Before she can challenge, Clytus speaks.

“The King is waiting."

A beat.

“It will be quick.”

He says nothing else.

The doors open.

The king kneels at the altar.

His sword lies across it.

Beringer is brought in.
---
The king kneels at the altar.

His sword lies across it.

The king does not turn.

Does not rise.

For a long moment—

only the quiet murmur of prayer.

---

Then:

---

> “They have heard.”

No question in it.

---

> “Vogel is dead.”

> “Bordella follows.”

A breath.

---

> “They’ll have filled in the rest with rumor.”

---

> “It does not matter.”

---

A slight shift of his weight—steel faintly touching stone.

---

> “What matters is this—”

---

> “The men in that block have no master.”

> “No command.”

> “No future that resembles the one they marched here for.”

---

> “They know it.”

---

A pause.

---

> “Some of them will be waiting for orders that will never come.”

> “Some of them will be deciding what kind of men they are without them.”

---

> “That is the moment they are in.”

---

Now the turn—the handoff to Beringer:

---

> “You will attend to what comes next.”

> “They have run out of strong drink.”
> 
> “They are running out of food.”
> 
> “Their sergeants are dead.”
> 
> “Their officers… uncertain.”

A pause.

---

> “Good men break under less.”

---

> “We will not storm them.”
> 
> “We will not grant them the dignity of a final stand.”
> 
> “And we will not make martyrs of fools who followed orders they barely understood.”

---

> “You will break them.”

---

> “Not with steel.”
> 
> “With hunger.”
> 
> “With isolation.”
> 
> “With certainty.”

---

> “Knights—”

A slight shift in tone. Respect, but controlled.

---

> “—will come out in groups of no more than three.”

> “They will retain their swords.”

> “Their names will be taken.”

> “Their oaths will be witnessed by the priests of Pholtus.”

> “Their honor will be preserved.”

A beat.

---

> “Their army will not be.”

---

> “The soldiery—”

Flat. Practical.

---

> “—will come out in groups of ten.”

> “They will surrender their arms.”

> “They will be counted.”

> “And they will be sent on their way.”

---

> “On foot.”

---

> “If they linger—”

> “they will be encouraged.”

---

> “If they gather—”

> “they will be broken again.”

---

> “The Black Stag Lancers will see to that.”

---

> “And above them—”

A glance upward.

---

> “—they will be watched.”

---

> “They will not leave together.”

> “They will not march together.”

> “And when they are gone—”

---

> “They will not remember themselves as an army.”

---

A pause. Then, quieter:

---

> “Some of them will fall.”

> “Hunger does that.”

> “Fear does that.”

> “Bad men will become worse.”

---

> “But they will not do it here.”

---

> “See it done.”

---
As Beringer steps out—

the hall has not changed.

Paladins still stand. Silent. Watching.

---

But now—

there is one more man.

---

Small.

Old.

Bright-eyed.

---

He stands off to the side, waiting his turn like anyone else.

He gives Beringer a quick, respectful salute.

---

A satchel at his hip.

An apron, neatly folded over his arm.

---

His hands are steady.

Used to their work.

---

If asked, he answers without ceremony:

---

> “Royal barber, my lord.”

A shrug, almost apologetic.

---

> “His Majesty has a muster in the morning.”

---

He shifts the satchel slightly.

---

> “Likes to look the part.”
