## Summary

Zara Vallytrex continues to operate under constraint—bound by a geas that prevents direct interference or overt aid to the party. Despite this, she is actively probing the limits of that binding, offering assistance through indirect, socially acceptable, or plausibly deniable means. Each interaction reflects careful calibration: helpful enough to matter, subtle enough to avoid triggering the geas.

Her approach favors proximity, tone, and implication over action. She cannot warn, cannot act openly—but she *can* position herself, reinforce alliances, and ensure the right people are paying attention at the right moments. These efforts are not random; they are deliberate tests of the geas’ boundaries.

The visit to Cornelia and Bertram’s residence is one such maneuver. On its surface, it is nothing more than a polite social call—congratulations offered, courtesies exchanged. In context, it is a signal: Zara is watching, present, and choosing to be seen in controlled ways.

The strain is beginning to show. These “harmless” gestures are increasingly precise, increasingly intentional. She is not breaking the geas—but she is leaning on it hard enough to hear it creak.

## Scene — The Visit (As Reported by Cornelia)

Bertram barely gets the door shut before Cornelia’s hands are on him.

“Gods above,” she says, laughing as she kisses his cheek anyway, “you smell like you fought a brewery and lost.”

He grins, a little glassy, boots already coming off where he kicked them.

“You should see the brewery.”

She takes his cloak from him without comment, folds it over a chair like this is the most natural thing in the world, then pauses and wrinkles her nose.

“…All right,” she says. “You’re going in the bath before you sit on anything I care about.”

She steers him down the hall with a hand at his back, firm and fond. The water’s already heating—she thought ahead. Steam curls as she pours it in, sleeves rolled, utterly unbothered by the state he’s in.

He leans against the doorframe while she works, watching her like he always does when he’s finally home.

“You were gone all day,” she says, not accusing. Just stating. “Everyone was very loud about it.”

“Mm,” he manages. “That tracks.”

She laughs again, helps him out of his shirt, then presses a cloth into his hands.

“Wash,” she orders gently. “You look like the floor.”

He does, obediently. She helps with the rest—scrubbing road dust from his arms, rinsing ale out of his hair. Quiet care. Comfortable. Earned.

Halfway through, like it’s nothing at all:

“Oh—Zara stopped by today.”

He blinks water out of his eyes. “Zara?”

“Yes. Briefly.” Another bucket tips over him. “Very gracious. Quite distracted, though. I can’t blame her—her duke under scrutiny, half her peers locked down. Anyone would be rattled.”

She hands him a towel. Then another when he drops the first.

“She congratulated us,” Cornelia continues, matter-of-fact. “Spoke kindly of you. I didn’t keep her long.”

Bertram stands there, dripping. The steam feels thicker now.

“…She came here?”

Cornelia looks down at him, smiles, and kisses his forehead.

“Mm-hmm. Don’t worry. It was perfectly proper.”

A pause. Soft. Honest.

“I wanted you to hear it from me.”

She squeezes his hand, then nudges him back toward the towels.

“Now,” she says, “dry off before you soak the floor. You’re home. Try to look like it.”

She hums as she turns away, already reaching for clean clothes.

And Bertram is left standing there—clean, warm, and suddenly very aware—

That while he was drowning in ale and politics,

Zara had time to be polite in his house.

## Notes

- Zara’s visit is **technically harmless**, socially acceptable, and offers no actionable intelligence.
- It nonetheless establishes:
  - Awareness of Bertram’s position
  - Recognition of Cornelia’s status
  - A subtle presence inside their household sphere
- This likely represents a **low-risk geas-compliant action**:
  - No warning given
  - No instruction offered
  - No interference performed
- The precision of the visit suggests **intentional signaling**, not coincidence.
