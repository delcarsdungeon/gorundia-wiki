# The Admiral’s Office, Capital City

The room smells wrong.

Ink. Old paper. Lamp oil.  
No pitch. No salt. No wet rope.  

And worst of all—

The floor doesn’t move.

---

Stone walls. A narrow window over tiled roofs and spires. A desk so solid it might as well be part of the foundation.

A place for clerks.

Or prisoners.

Not sailors.

---

The Admiral stands when Thalrik is shown in. Not because the room demands it—

Because habit does.

---

> “Master Thalrik Vaultforge,”

His voice is smooth. Capital-polished.

> “By writ of the Crown and under temporary assignment to the capital, I formally acknowledge you as acting master of the Crown’s flying vessel—its crew, stores, and attached obligations.”

---

Behind him stand five naval officers—captains all.

Boots too clean. Cloaks brushed.

Men who belong on rolling decks and hate that they’re not.

---

> “You will not prey upon Gorundian shipping,” the Admiral continues.  
> “You will answer summons in time of war, incursion, or national embarrassment.”  
> “You will not bring trouble home.”

---

A pause. Precisely measured.

> “Any questions for the record?”

---

Silence.

Thick enough to lean on.

---

The Admiral nods once.

> “Captains. Out. All of you—except you. And you.”

---

Relief shows. Briefly.

The others file out.

The door closes.

The latch drops.

---

The Admiral exhales.

Long.

Slow.

Like a man finally setting down a pack he’s carried too far.

---

## The Two Who Remain

The first is **Captain Roderic Thane**.

Human. Late forties. Built like a pier post.  
Iron-gray beard. Broken nose set wrong. One ear half gone.

He stands wide-footed, balanced like the deck might roll at any moment.

He looks at Thalrik once.

Slow.

Deliberate.

A single nod.

*Seen worse. Lived. Respects that.*

---

The second is **Captain Jorven Hale**.

Younger. Lean. Sharp-eyed.

His uniform is neat—

But honestly worn. Salt-bleached at the cuffs in ways no valet could fake.

He watches Thalrik’s hands. His stance. The angle of his shoulders.

Taking notes.

Without writing anything down.

---

> “These two,” the Admiral says, not turning,
> “are the ones who don’t confuse orders with understanding.”

---

## Now We Talk Like Sailors

The Admiral nudges the desk aside with his hip.

Not violently.

Just enough to make his opinion of it clear.

---

A battered sea-chest comes open.

Out comes a squat ceramic bottle wrapped in sailcloth.

Three scarred cups.

---

He pours.

The smell hits immediately—

Proper grog.

Dark rum. Citrus peel. Something bitter enough to count as medicine.

---

A cup slides to Thalrik.

> “Now,” the Admiral says, sitting on the desk instead of behind it,  
> “we can talk like sailors instead of furniture.”

---

## Black Veil

> “Let’s get this straight from the start,” the Admiral says.  
> “Your ballywick should never put you anywhere near Black Veil territory.”

---

Thane’s jaw tightens.

Just a fraction.

Hale doesn’t react at all.

Which is its own kind of reaction.

---

> “They stay out of Gorundian waters because it’s not worth the fuss.  
> Too many patrols. Too many priests. Too many people who remember how to burn a ship until the bones stop screaming.”

---

A sip.

---

> “But they cause trouble. Drift close. Hit soft targets. Leave messes.”

---

Now he looks at Thalrik.

Not weighing him.

Just speaking plainly.

---

> “And I know you’ve dealt with worse than rumors.”

---

## Gratitude

> “I won’t pretend this is some great honor I’m bestowing,” the Admiral says.  
> “This is me being grateful.”

---

That lands.

---

> “That flying ship was a crown toy parked on my conscience.  
> If it went wrong under me, it wouldn’t be a naval failure—it’d be a political one.”

---

A dry smile.

---

> “You taking it means it finally has a helm held by someone who understands risk instead of symbolism.”

---

He lifts his cup.

> “Thank you.”

---

Thane inclines his head—deeper this time.

Hale’s eyes flick at the mention of the ship.

Interest.

Not suspicion.

---

## Official vs Real

> “Officially,” the Admiral says, rolling his eyes,  
> “I’m your superior.”

---

Thane snorts.

Hale doesn’t—

But the corner of his mouth twitches.

---

> “Unofficially?”  
> “If I ever need your very expensive, until-recently royal flying carriage, we’ll sort it out like adults.”

---

He leans forward.

Lower voice.

---

> “It’s still your helm. Your crew. Your call.”

---

A beat.

---

> “And if you’re smart—and I believe you are—  
> you’ll keep it far away from places where dead men still answer orders.”

---

## Departure

The Admiral drains his cup.

Stands.

The capital settles back onto him like a coat he doesn’t enjoy wearing.

---

> “Do your work. Stay out of my waters. Don’t make me explain you to priests or princes.”

---

Then, quieter.

Almost fond.

---

> “And if trouble finds you anyway… deal with it cleanly.”

---

A gesture toward the door.

---

Thane’s look says:

*I’d sail with you.*

Hale’s says:

*I’ll remember this conversation.*

---

The door opens.

The noise of the capital floods back in.

---

For just a moment—

before the desk is shoved back into place—

the Admiral looks like a man counting the hours until there’s a deck under his boots again.

---

## GM Note — The First Mate

**Captain Jorven Hale** did not remain a captain for long.

Following this meeting, he transferred from command of a **small coastal patrol vessel** to serve under Thalrik aboard [[The Second Draft]].

He now acts as:

- **First Mate**
- **Operational counterweight to Thalrik’s instincts**
- The man who notices problems before they exist

Where Thane would have followed—

Hale chose to *build something*.

And Thalrik was worth building it for.
