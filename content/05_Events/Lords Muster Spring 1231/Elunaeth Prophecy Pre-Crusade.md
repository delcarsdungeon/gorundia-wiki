The air cools.

Not cold—

but *elsewhere*.

Elunaeth’s gaze drifts past the room, as though the walls have politely stepped aside for something older.

When she speaks, it is quiet… but it lands like weight.

> “The king kneels.”

A faint pause.

> “The sky does not answer.”

Another beat.

> “It listens.”

Her fingers twitch, as though feeling rain that has not yet fallen.

> “Many hands on one scale.”

> “None of them gentle.”

A slow breath.

> “The metal learns its shape in fire.”

> “It does not ask what it will become.”

Her head tilts.

> “He does not either.”

Silence stretches.

Then—

> “Roots remember storms long after branches forget.”

> “The forest watches a hunter choose his arrow.”

A flicker of something—amusement, or sorrow.

> “It is not the shot that matters.”

> “It is what he calls it after.”

Her eyes unfocus slightly.

> “There is a crown that will not be worn.”

> “And because of that… many will burn to hold it.”

A faint exhale.

> “The sun finds a seed buried too long.”

> “It breaks.”

> “This is called growth.”

A pause.

> “It is also called pain.”

Her voice softens, almost fond:

> “The small thing becomes a shield.”

> “The shield learns it is also a blade.”

Then—

a shift.

Subtle. Dangerous.

> “A door is being built.”

> “Strong.”

> “Just.”

> “Necessary.”

Her fingers close slightly.

> “It will open very easily.”

Silence.

> “Not for him.”

A longer pause now. The room has that feeling like everyone forgot to breathe at the same time.

> “The banners will say *cleansing.*”

> “The earth will say *cutting.*”

> “The dead will not argue.”

Her head turns slightly, as if listening to someone standing just behind a listener’s shoulder.

> “There is a path where truth stands alone.”

> “There is a path where lies kneel and call it peace.”

A faint, almost invisible smile.

> “They look the same… for a while.”

Then—

quietly:

> “He has not chosen.”

A breath.

> “That is why the moment matters.”

Her eyes close briefly.

> “The storm is not the danger.”

> “The ground remembering the storm is.”

When she opens them again, she is suddenly *here*.

Fully.

Sharply.

> “Tell the oak—”

A pause.

> “—that iron forgets the forest.”

And then, softer:

> “And the forest does not forget iron.”

Silence.

# (Less confusing DM reference)

The air around Elunaeth stills.

Not silence—never silence.

The faint rustle of leaves, the pull of distant stars, the hush of tide against a shore no one here can see.

When she speaks, it is as though she is continuing a conversation that began three days ago, or three years from now.

> “He kneels in prayer… and the sky leans close.”

Her head tilts slightly, listening to something beyond the room.

> “Not one hand. Many.”

> “The sword-lord’s thunder.”  
> “The sun-father’s warmth.”  
> “The old wild root that remembers before crowns were crowns.”

A breath.

> “They are watching him.”

Her eyes shift—not quite focusing on anyone present.

> “And he is watching them back.”

She smiles then, but sadly.

> “That is the danger.”

A pause.

> “I have seen kings who sought power.”  
> “I have seen kings who fled duty.”  
> “This one does neither.”

> “He hardens.”

> “Not into cruelty.”  
> “Into purpose.”

> “His anger is not the anger of a spoiled lordling denied.”  
> “It is the anger of a just man who has finally seen how deep the rot goes.”

Her voice lowers.

> “Righteous fury is still fury.”

She turns slightly, as though seeing banners snapping in a wind no one else feels.

> “The hall will shake.”  
> “Old names will fall.”  
> “Houses will be broken open like rotten fruit.”  
> “And from the wound will come iron, prayer, and fire.”

> “A crusade, yes.”

> “Not born from madness—”  
> “born from certainty.”

Now her attention slides, suddenly, toward Thranduil—even if he is not present, as though some part of him stands in the room anyway.

> “And there—at the forest’s torn edge—”  
> “the arrow pauses.”

> “He stands where all old peoples stand, when the hour grows narrow.”

> “Between the beautiful lie…”  
> “and the lonely truth.”

A faint shiver.

> “The lie says: _submit, and be preserved._”  
> “The lie says: _Celene remembers you._”  
> “The lie says: _bend the knee, and call it wisdom._”

Then, sharper:

> “The truth says: _stand alone if you must._”  
> “The truth says: _what is not free is not saved._”  
> “The truth says: _a people can die from comfort as surely as from spears._”

She blinks, and for just a heartbeat looks tired beyond measure.

> “He has not chosen yet.”

Then, softer now—warmer.

> “And the prince…”

A tiny, strange smile.

> “The strong oak’s acorn lies long in shadow.”  
> “Pressed into earth.”  
> “Forgotten by those who only count branches.”

> “But under the warmth of the sun…”

She places a hand lightly against her own breastbone.

> “It splits.”  
> “It reaches.”  
> “It becomes what it was always meant to be.”

> “Not merely heir.”  
> “Not merely son.”

> “A shield with light behind it.”

A pause.

> “He will take fire into himself and call it mercy.”  
> “He will wear the sun not as ornament… but as burden.”

Now her expression changes again. Sadness. Warning. The part that makes people in the room suddenly wish they were elsewhere.

> “And this is where the road forks.”

> “Because good men, when given lawful tools, build lawful engines.”

> “Strong levers.”  
> “Clean chains.”  
> “Holy warrants.”  
> “A throne made harder to defy.”

She looks toward the king’s unseen place of prayer.

> “He may use them well.”

Then:

> “He may use them _righteously._”

Then, the knife:

> “But he is not the last man who will sit where he sits.”

Silence.

> “I have seen what comes after great reform.”

> “A gate built to keep wolves out…”  
> “remembering too late that it also keeps lambs in.”

> “A law made for traitors…”  
> “used later for critics.”

> “A righteous crown teaching a wicked one where the handles are.”

Her voice softens to almost a whisper.

> “He is not building a tyranny.”

> “He is building a kingdom that a tyrant could use.”

Then she closes her eyes.

> “This is the grief of good rulers.”

> “They must act.”  
> “They must cut.”  
> “They must leave scars.”

> “And every scar becomes a place the next knife may find.”

When her eyes open again, she seems suddenly, piercingly present.

> “So tell him this—”  
> “if he would harden, let him harden like oak, not iron.”

> “Oak shelters.”  
> “Oak bends.”  
> “Oak remembers it was once alive.”

> “Iron only remembers the forge.”

And then, almost gently:

> “The gods have placed their hands upon the scale.”

> “Now comes the mortal part.”
