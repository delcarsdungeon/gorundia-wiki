The skyship rests at dock, quiet, lines taut.
No one is boarding. No one is leaving.

At the foot of the gangplank stands a dwarf.

Not blocking it.
Not guarding it.

Waiting.

Three paces off to the side, a royal courier stands in visible distress, clutching a sealed scroll like a charm against misfortune. He keeps glancing at the dwarf, then at the ship, then at the city behind him, trying to calculate which direction contains the least amount of death.

Beringer approaches.

The dwarf looks up. Measures. Decides.

One step forward.

“Hrothik Stonevein,” he says.
“Clan-sergeant of the deep ranks. I speak for the stoneborn attached to your command.”

No bow. No salute. Just fact, laid flat.

He jerks his chin—brief, economical—toward the courier.

“He arrived first.”

The courier startles, straightens, and launches into motion like he’s been holding his breath since dawn.

“Y-yes—Sir—Lord—by word and seal of His Grace Vladek Orenic, Duke of Royonad, Lord of Royograd, Warden of the Storm Marches, Bearer of the Storm Lynx—”
He gulps, plows on.
“—you are formally invited to join him for breakfast this morning. Private table. No retinue. His Grace emphasized informal.”

He steps forward and offers the scroll with both hands.

Then freezes.

No one moves to take it.

The silence stretches. Ropes creak. Somewhere above, canvas snaps once and settles.

Beringer accepts the scroll.

The courier exhales like a man reprieved at the gallows, nods far too many times, mutters something that might be thanks or a prayer, and all but flees down the dock. His boots hit stone. He does not look back. He is gone.

Only then does Hrothik speak again.

“Stone waits,” he says calmly.
“Paper hurries.”

A beat.

“You are Sir Beringer of the West,” he continues, voice steady.
“Champion of Bard’s Gate.
Lord of Blackbriar.”

The titles are delivered without mockery. Without warmth either. Just… weighed.

At the end of the list, there is the slightest pause.

The faintest nod.

Approval—measured, reluctant, real.

“You give orders,” Hrothik says.
“I decide how many dwarves live long enough to carry them out.”

No threat. Just accounting.

“We will hold when told to hold. We will kill when killing ends the problem. We will not chase glory, banners, or elves—high or feral.”

That last lands like old doctrine, not opinion.

“If you’re wrong, I’ll correct you.”
“If you’re right, I’ll make you look clever.”

Another nod. This one purely professional.

“Knight.”

He steps back half a pace, plants himself at the foot of the gangplank again, and waits—patient as bedrock—while the city resumes breathing around them.
