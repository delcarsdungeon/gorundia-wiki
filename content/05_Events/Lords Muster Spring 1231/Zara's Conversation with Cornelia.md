## Zara’s Visit — What Cornelia Realizes Too Late

Zara does not waste time on pleasantries.
She is polite.
Perfectly so.
But she does not pretend.

> “I won’t stay long.”

Cornelia offers the expected courtesies.

Zara declines them with the ease of someone who has already decided how the conversation ends.

She studies Cornelia.
Not her dress.
Not her posture.
Her.

> “You’re holding too much.”

Cornelia stills.
Just slightly.

> “I beg your pardon?”

Zara’s expression doesn’t change.

> “Secrets.”

A beat.

> “Fear.”

Another.

> “Responsibility that doesn’t belong to you.”

Cornelia’s chin lifts a fraction.

> “I don’t know what you mean.”

Zara almost smiles.
Almost.

> “Of course you do.”

Silence stretches.
Zara lets it.
She’s very good at that.

Then, casually—

> “There are rooms in this Kingdom where silence is mistaken for loyalty.”

Her gaze drifts—past Cornelia.
Not looking at anything.
Remembering.

> “It isn’t.”

Cornelia doesn’t take the bait.
Smart.

> “What would you have me do?” she asks.

Careful. Neutral.
Zara looks back at her.
And for the first time—
there’s something sharper there.

> “I wouldn’t.”

A beat.

> “You don’t belong to me.”

That lands heavier than it should.
Then, softer—

> “But I have watched this… before.”

She steps closer.
Not intimate.
But no longer distant.

> “A young woman—clever enough to see what was coming…”
> “Too careful to act on it.”

Cornelia’s breath tightens.
She doesn’t interrupt.

> “She told herself she needed certainty.”
> “Proof.”
> “The right moment.”

A pause.

> “She told herself silence was safer.”

Zara tilts her head slightly.

> “It wasn’t.”

Cornelia swallows.

> “What happened to her?”

Zara doesn’t answer immediately.
When she does, it’s quiet.
Flat.

> “She survived.”

A beat.

> “Which was worse.”

That hangs there.
Heavy.
Zara straightens slightly.
Distance returning.
Mask settling back into place.

> “You’re not stupid.”

Not praise.
Assessment.

> “So I won’t insult you by pretending you don’t understand the shape of things.”

Cornelia’s voice is steadier than she feels.

> “And the shape is…?”

Zara’s gaze flicks to the door.
The hall beyond.
The world waiting.

> “Closing.”

No drama.
No flourish.
Just a statement.
A long silence follows.
Then—
Zara steps back.
Fully composed again.

> “Your husband is well-liked.”

A faint shift.
Something almost resembling warmth.

> “He reminds me of someone I was very fond of.”

There it is.
Personal.
Unavoidable.

> “Men like that…” she continues,

> “…tend to believe they can fix things by standing in front of them.”

A pause.

> “Sometimes they can.”
> “Sometimes they just get crushed.”

Cornelia’s voice is quieter now.

> “And me?”

Zara considers her.
Honestly, this time.

> “You’re the one who decides whether they’re standing alone.”

That’s the closest thing to a blade she gives her.
Then—
she moves to leave.
At the threshold, she stops.
Not turning.

> “When it matters…”

A beat.

> “Don’t wait to be certain.”

And then, almost as an afterthought—

> “Certainty is what gets you killed.”

She leaves.
No warning given.
No names spoken.
No laws broken.
## What Cornelia Understands Later

Too late.
Zara wasn’t asking her to choose a side.
She already knew Cornelia had one.

She was telling her:

- **You’re already in it.**
- **Silence won’t protect you.**
- **Hesitation is the only mistake left.**

And most of all—
Zara didn’t sound angry.
Not really.
Not outraged.
Just…
**tired of watching the same mistake happen again.**

## Scene — After the Storm (Cornelia & Bertram)

The room is quiet.

Not court quiet.  
Real quiet.

Cornelia sits across from him, turning her ring once along her finger.

Thinking.

Then—

> “I should have told him.”

No buildup.

She looks up.

> “If I’d gone to the King early… without trying to be clever…”

A faint, humorless smile.

> “I might have walked out of that hall with less blood on my name.”

A beat—then quickly, firm:

> “Not that I was treated unfairly.”

That matters to her.

> “He was measured. More than he had to be.”

A glance at Bertram.

> “Your King.”

Then it slips.

Back to it.

> “She knew.”

That lands heavier.

> “Zara.”

She holds his eyes now.

> “She didn’t say it outright. She couldn’t. But she knew I was about to make the worst version of that mistake.”

A breath.

> “And I made it anyway.”

No self-pity. Just fact.

> “She told me not to wait.”

That line sits there.

> “Not to be certain. That hesitation would be the thing that killed me.”

A small shake of her head.

> “And I understood her.”

A beat.

> “And I still didn’t act.”

Silence.

Then the crack in it—

> “So I don’t understand.”

She leans back slightly, frustration showing.

> “Why do that? Why come here, say just enough that I might have saved myself…”

Her hand opens, then closes again.

> “…and then burn everything down?”

She watches him.

> “That wasn’t careful. That wasn’t subtle.”

A quieter line:

> “That looked like rage.”

A beat.

> “I don’t know which version of her is real.”

She exhales, steadies.

Decision point.

> “I told you everything she said.”

No hedging.

> “The others don’t have that context.”

She studies him, precise now.

> “If I tell them, it makes her look like she was trying to help.”

A small tilt of her head.

> “Which… she was. In a way.”

Another beat.

> “It also makes me look worse. Because I understood her—and still failed to act.”

And finally:

> “It complicates her.”

A faint, bitter edge.

> “I’m not sure any of us benefit from that right now.”

She meets his eyes.

Not a noble. Not a courtier.

Just his wife.

> “Do I tell them?”

Softer:

> “Or do I let her stay the monster everyone thinks she is?”
