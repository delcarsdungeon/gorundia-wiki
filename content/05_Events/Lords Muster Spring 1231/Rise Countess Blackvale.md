The courtyard still smells of iron.

The block remains beneath the statue of Theron Sylvanranth, darkened now, the last of the blood tracing slow lines down its face. No one has moved to clean it.

The king stands before it.

“Let it be known,” he says, voice carrying clean across stone and steel, “that House Bordella is no more.”

No flourish. Just law.

“By treason, by complicity, and by the defilement of both crown and blood—its name is struck from standing.”

A beat.

“Its lands, titles, and rights are forfeit to the Crown.”

His gaze shifts.

“Blackvale does not fall with it.”

He turns.

“Dana Sylvanranth.”

A pause.

“Approach.”

---

She does not _walk_.

She advances.

Measured. Even. Parade-ground precise—each step placed, each movement controlled. Armor steady, posture unbroken.

She halts at regulation distance.

Comes to attention.

Does not kneel.

Does not bow.

Not yet.

The king watches her a moment—reading that.

Accepting it.

Good.

---

“By right of service. By strength proven. By the need of this realm—”

“I name you Countess of Blackvale.”

A beat.

Then, formally:

“Rise… and take what remains.”

---

She does not move.

Still at attention.

Because she never knelt.

There’s the faintest shift in the court—those who notice, _notice_.

The king does not correct her.

Instead—

He steps forward.

Closes the distance by a single pace.

Not towering over her.

Not looming.

Meeting her where she stands.

And with a simple, deliberate motion—

He places his hand briefly against her shoulder.

Not a strike.

Not a dubbing.

A weight.

Recognition.

Authority given. Burden shared.

Then he withdraws.

---

“Bring them forward.”

Four blades are carried out—flat across the palms of attendants.

They are laid out before her.

Steel, history, failure.

The king stands beside them.

“Count Lavinio’s blade.”  
“Ponzio Bordella’s.”  
“Allec Bordella’s.”

A pause.

Then—

“Cornelia Bordella’s.”

That one lingers.

Then:

“These swords once defended Blackvale.”

“They do so no longer.”

He turns to Dana.

“House Bordella is broken.”

“No hand remains to bear these in its name.”

A measured breath.

“And so they fall to you.”

He gestures.

“Not as spoils.”

“Not as trophies.”

“As burden.”

“These were the hands that failed their people.”

“These were the blades that did not rise when they should have.”

His eyes fix on hers.

“And now—”

“—they are yours.”

A beat.

“You may redeem them.”

“Or you may cast them aside.”

Then, quiet and sharp:

“From this moment forward, Blackvale is not unarmed.”

“It is re-armed.”

“With you.”

---

Dana steps forward.

Not to take them.

She turns.

“Baron Orin Kharne.”

“Baroness Braga Kharne.”

Her voice is command held just inside restraint.

“I stand alone.”

“No knights. No sworn blades. No house to carry this weight with me.”

A breath.

“I ask you—”

“—will you honor me, and bear this burden for a time at my side?”

---

They step forward.

Orin takes two blades.

Braga takes the other two.

No ceremony.

Just acceptance.

They fall in behind her.

---

Dana turns back.

And now—

She bows.

Deep.

Not to the king.

To Orin.

A warrior’s bow. Earned respect, given without hesitation.

“My lord,” she says, voice steady, grounded.

“Gorundia stands because men like you chose to stand when others did not.”

A beat.

“I will not forget it.”

---

Then—

**Beringer** steps forward.

No herald.

No announcement.

In his hands—

Her father’s sword.

He stops before her.

“Your father’s.”

---

She takes it.

And this time—

It hits.

Grip tightening. Breath catching—just once.

Then steel again.

“I take this not as inheritance—”

“—but as judgment.”

She turns slightly, the blade angling toward the block… toward what was just done.

“Whatever rot remains in Blackvale—”

Her grip firms.

“—I will cut it out.”
