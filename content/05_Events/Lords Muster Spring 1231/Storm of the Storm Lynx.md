#  **Vignette — The Walk**

They don’t stroll.

They move.

Fast enough to matter, not fast enough to look like panic.

Sorek sets the pace. Bertram keeps up easily—long stride, natural confidence, the kind that makes people assume he knows where he’s going even when he doesn’t.

Good.

That helps.

---

Sorek doesn’t look at him at first.

Just walks.

Then—

A folded letter appears in his hand.

He presses it into Bertram’s chest without breaking stride.

---

> “Read it later.”

A beat.

---

> “Or don’t.”

---

Bertram glances down at the seal.

Orenic.

That’s already a problem.

---

> “Duchess?”

---

> “Mm.”

---

Sorek exhales through his nose.

---

> “She sent one to Beringer too.”

A beat.

---

> “This one’s for me.”

---

That tells Bertram everything he needs to know.

This is _not_ just business.

---

They turn a corner.

Two guards snap to attention and flatten themselves out of the way.

Sorek doesn’t slow.

---

> “Here’s where we are.”

Finally—he looks at Bertram.

---

> “Lady Calira Meroven.”

> “Orenic blood.”

> “Got scooped up during the Bordella mess and nobody circled back.”

---

A short, irritated shake of his head.

---

> “Which is exactly the kind of thing that happens when the whole damn castle’s on fire…”

A beat.

---

> “…and exactly the kind of thing that gets people killed if you leave it sitting.”

---

There’s the edge.

---

> “Beringer dropped it.”

Flat.

Not angry.

Just stating it.

---

Then immediately—

---

> “—and he’s had about twelve things on his plate that would’ve killed a lesser man, so I’m not hanging him for it.”

---

That’s the balance.

Respect _and_ frustration.

---

They keep moving.

---

> “Problem is—”

A thumb taps the letter still in Bertram’s hand.

---

> “—she noticed.”

---

Another turn.

Closer now.

---

> “And when she notices, her husband stops thinking and starts _acting_.”

---

A glance sideways.

---

> “You saw him.”

---

Bertram definitely saw him.

---

Sorek continues:

---

> “So.”

A small inhale.

---

> “You’re the solution.”

---

That lands.

No ceremony.

No buildup.

Just—_you’re up_.

---

> “You go in.”

> “You check her.”

> “You get her on her feet and you walk her out like she was never anything but an honored guest who’s been… mildly inconvenienced.”

---

A beat.

---

> “You give her dignity.”

---

Then—

quieter:

---

> “Because right now that’s what’s actually at risk.”

---

They’re almost there.

The corridor ahead is already starting to clog with tension.

---

Sorek slows just a fraction.

Not enough to stop.

Just enough to _anchor_ the moment.

---

> “Vladek’s going to watch you.”

---

A glance.

---

> “That’s not a threat.”

---

A beat.

---

> “That’s an opportunity.”

---

Now he looks at him properly.

---

> “He already likes you.”

---

Dry as hell:

---

> “Don’t give him a reason to reconsider.”

---

They reach the final stretch.

Guards visible.

Locked up tight.

---

Sorek rolls his shoulders once.

---

> “You keep it clean, you keep it respectful, and you keep it _moving_.”

---

A pause.

Then, quieter—

just for Bertram:

---

> “And if she’s angry…”

A faint smirk.

---

> “…good.”

---

Bertram raises an eyebrow.

---

> “Means she’s still thinking like a noble.”

---

They stop just short of the guards.

The moment before everything starts.

---

Sorek leans in just slightly.

Last words.

---

> “This is the part people remember.”

A beat.

---

> “Make it a good story.”

---

Then he straightens.

Back to the old warhound.

---

> “Right.”

A clap once.

---

> “Let’s go fix your boss’s problem.”

---

# ⚔️ **Vignette — The Corner Turn**

They hit the corner—

—and walk straight into it.

---

**Vladek Orenic** is pacing.

Not strolling.

Not waiting.

**Pacing.**

A warhorse denied a charge.

---

His boots hit stone like punctuation.

Back and forth.

Back and forth.

---

He’s muttering under his breath—

half in Oeridian, half in that clipped Suel dialect—

sharp, ugly syllables that sound like they were designed to start fights.

---

> “…ne tak… _ne tak_… this is insult—”

A turn.

---

> “—hold my blood in box like dog—”

Another turn.

---

> “—I tell her we come here for honor—_honor_—”

---

He stops.

Sees them.

---

The corridor tightens.

Guards snap straight.

Onlookers suddenly discover they have places to be.

---

Vladek’s eyes lock on Sorek first.

Of course they do.

---

A beat.

That same old tension flashes—fast, hot, gone nowhere.

---

Then—

his gaze shifts.

---

To Bertram.

---

And just like that—

the storm changes direction.

---

> “Ah.”

A sharp nod.

---

> “Good.”

He steps forward.

Big presence. Too big for the hallway.

---

> “You bring right man.”

---

Sorek doesn’t take the bait.

Doesn’t step forward.

Doesn’t _engage_.

---

Instead—

he slows.

Just a fraction.

Then stops.

---

A deliberate half-step _behind_ Bertram.

---

Hands loose at his sides.

Neutral.

---

> “Your Grace.”

Calm.

Flat.

---

> “Sir Bertram’s got it.”

---

That’s it.

No speech.

No claim.

Just a clean pass of authority.

---

Vladek clocks it immediately.

Eyes flick back to Sorek.

---

There’s a moment there—

where he _could_ push it.

Make it about him.

Make it personal.

---

He almost does.

---

Then he exhales sharply through his nose.

Lets it go.

---

Because the problem is still the problem.

---

His attention snaps fully to Bertram.

---

> “Good.”

A jab of a finger toward the door.

---

> “Then fix.”

---

He steps aside—

but not far.

Never far.

---

> “I watch.”

---

Behind Bertram, Sorek murmurs just low enough:

---

> “Told you.”

---

Then, quieter—

almost amused:

---

> “Try not to impress him too much.”

---

A beat.

---

> “He’ll adopt you.”

---

Vladek, still staring at the door, mutters:

---

> “I hear that.”

---

Without looking back.

---

Sorek huffs a quiet laugh.

---

> “Of course you do.”

---

The guards are already moving.

Keys shaking just slightly.

Lock turning.

---

The door creaks.

---

And for just a second—

right before it opens—

the whole corridor holds its breath.

---
# The Door Opens

The lock turns.

Slow.

Loud in the quiet.

---

The door creaks open—

—and the smell hits first.

Not filth.

Not dungeon rot.

But **wrong**.

Heavy stew gone thick.

Stale linens.

Oil lamps burning too long.

---

Then—

**Lady Calira Meroven**.

---

She is standing.

Of course she is.

---

Not pacing.

Not slumped.

Standing near the far wall like she chose the position and dared the room to disagree.

---

Her dress is yesterday’s.

Still elegant—

but creased, slightly dulled, the hem no longer pristine.

Her hair has been redone by her own hand.

Carefully.

Not perfectly.

---

Her eyes snap to the door.

---

Relief flashes—

gone instantly.

Replaced by something far sharper.

---

> “Finally.”

---

Her gaze lands on Bertram.

Takes him in.

Assesses.

Ranks.

---

Then flicks past him—

to Vladek in the hall.

---

A breath.

A shift.

Now it’s personal.

---

> “Your Grace.”

A precise incline of her head.

---

> “I was beginning to wonder whether I had been forgotten…”

A beat.

---

> “…or simply _misplaced_.”

---

Behind Bertram, Vladek makes a low sound.

Dangerously close to agreement.

---

Calira continues—

voice tightening just slightly.

---

> “I have been confined for days.”

> “No word.”

> “No explanation.”

---

A small, controlled gesture toward the room.

---

> “The food is barely fit for servants.”

> “The linens are unchanged.”

> “And I am quite certain I have been _observed_ at irregular hours.”

---

The guards outside go rigid.

---

Sorek, behind Bertram, mutters just under his breath:

> “Oh, here we go…”

---

Calira’s eyes sharpen further.

---

> “And while I have been sitting here—”

A step forward.

Now she’s in motion.

---

> “—I have missed the Bannerhall.”

> “The Muster.”

> “Three private gatherings.”

> “And at least two conversations I _guarantee_ I now have to recover secondhand.”

---

That last one?

That’s the real wound.

---

She stops just short of Bertram.

---

> “So.”

Calm.

Cold.

---

> “You will explain to me—”

A slight tilt of her head.

---

> “—whether I am a guest, a prisoner…”

---

A beat.

---

> “…or an administrative oversight.”

---

Silence.

---

This is the moment.

# **Vignette — The Recalibration**

The door opens.

She delivers the opening volley—controlled, cutting, precise.

Then—

She really _looks_ at him.

---

A fraction of a pause.

Barely visible.

But it’s there.

---

Because this is not a clerk.

Not a functionary.

Not some forgettable knight sent to tidy a mistake.

---

This is **Sir Bertram Moffet.**

---

Clean posture.

Easy strength.

That disarming, stupidly natural confidence.

The kind that makes people _want_ to forgive him for things he hasn’t even done yet.

---

Calira’s eyes sharpen.

Reassessing.

Re-ranking.

---

> “My Lady—  
> You are neither prisoner nor suspect—”

She listens.

Fully now.

---

And somewhere in the back of her mind—

**click.**

---

_This one matters._

---

By the time he hits:

> “—before anyone else has the chance to get the story wrong—”

---

She’s already adjusted her angle.

---

The anger doesn’t disappear.

It _refines_.

Focuses.

---

She steps closer.

Slow.

Measured.

---

> “Mm.”

A small nod.

---

> “Yes… that would be advisable.”

---

But her tone has shifted.

Just a shade warmer.

A shade more… _engaged._

---

She lets her eyes linger on him a fraction longer than strictly necessary.

---

Then—

casual as anything:

---

> “Sir Bertram, is it not?”

---

She absolutely knows.

But this is court.

You make people say their own names.

---

Behind him, Sorek closes his eyes briefly like:

> _Oh no._

---

Bertram confirms.

---

> “It is.”

---

She inclines her head.

---

> “I had wondered if you would distinguish yourself this week.”

A beat.

---

> “It seems you have.”

---

That’s not flirtation.

Not yet.

---

That’s **claiming awareness**.

---

She steps past him—

then pauses.

Just enough to turn her head slightly back toward him.

---

> “You will walk with me.”

---

Not a question.

---

Then—softer, just for him:

---

> “You may begin by telling me what I’ve missed.”**

## Vignette — The Walk Back (She Hears Everything)

They step out of the cell.

The corridor breathes again.

Guards loosen their grip on steel.  
Voices return—low, careful.

Vladek falls in beside them, a looming shadow that has decided, for now, not to fall.

Calira moves as if nothing touched her.

Head high.  
Spine straight.  
Composed.

Bertram at her side.

Behind them—

Sorek.

“She’s already working you.”

No reaction.

“Try not to enjoy it too much.”

She tilts her head.

Listening.

Bertram—

“Don’t look at me.”

Calira stops.

Clean.

Controlled.

The corridor halts with her.

She turns.

Slowly.

Looks directly at Sorek.

“Sir Sorek Redmarch.”

A small incline of her head.

Perfect.

“I had wondered whether you were still in the habit of commenting on conversations you are not part of.”

Vladek makes a low sound—dangerous, restrained.

Sorek exhales.

“Only the interesting ones, my lady.”

Her eyes narrow—just a fraction.

“Then I shall endeavor to remain interesting.”

A beat.

Then—

something changes.

Not much.

But enough.

“I heard of your daughter.”

Quiet.

No performance in it.

No audience.

Just fact.

“Sarielle Redmarch.”

She says the name cleanly.

Correctly.

“She was… part of my circle, for a time.”

Not _mine_.

Not claimed.

Just… acknowledged.

“She should not have been there.”

A small breath.

Measured.

“And yet—”

Her gaze flicks, briefly, to his.

“I am told she chose her ground. And held it.”

Silence in the corridor.

“She bought lives with it.”

Another beat.

Softer now—

“Which is more than can be said of many who had the years and rank to know better.”

There it is.

Not sharp.

Not cruel.

But it lands.

Sorek doesn’t answer.

Not immediately.

Because he understands exactly what she’s done—

She honored his daughter.

And, in the same breath, reminded him what courage looks like when it isn’t _complicated._

Calira inclines her head again.

Smaller this time.

Almost private.

“She did your name credit.”

A pause.

Then—

the mask returns.

Seamless.

“Now—”

She turns back to Bertram as if the moment never happened.

“You were about to tell me what I have missed.”

And just like that—

they’re moving again.

But Sorek walks a step slower for half a heartbeat.

Because that—

was not politics.

That was a girl remembering another girl—

and choosing to say it out loud.
