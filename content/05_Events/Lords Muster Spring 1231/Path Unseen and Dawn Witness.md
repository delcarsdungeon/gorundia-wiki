## Vignette — The Path Unseen

The temple is quiet.
Too quiet.

---

The great brazier burns low.
Gold light, dim and tired.

---

Lyosha wakes.
Not from sleep—
But from stillness.

---

Something is... strange.

---

And then—

---

Light.

---

A shaft of sunlight cuts across the temple floor.
Midnight.
No dawn.  
No moon-glow.

---

Just a clean, golden beam—
Spilling through a high window that should not face the sun.

---

It does not illuminate the room.
It ignores everything else.

---

It exists only for him.

---

Dust hangs within it—
Perfectly still.

---

Then—

---

It moves.

---

Sliding across the stone.
Toward the great doors.

---

And stops.

---

Waiting.

---

No voice.
No vision.

---

But the meaning is unmistakable:
**Come.**

---

When Lyosha steps into the light—
Warmth.
Recognition.

---

Not comfort.  
Not peace.

---

**Purpose.**

---

The doors open.
No one touches them.

---

## The Walk

The light leads.

---

Out of the temple.
Into the streets.

---

Bard’s Gate sleeps.
Or pretends to.

---

The beam glides ahead—

Across cobblestone.  
Up steps.  
Around corners.

---

And no one reacts.

---

A drunk slumps in an alley—
Does not stir.

---

A watch patrol passes—
Eyes forward.

---

One guard looks straight through the light—
And sees nothing.

---

Not the beam.  
Not the impossible glow.  
Not Lyosha.

---

It is as if the world has been—

**Changed.**

---

Just enough to let this happen.

---

## The Gate

The castle looms ahead.

---

Torches burn.
Guards stand ready.

---

The light does not slow.

---

It passes between them—

---

And they do not move.
Do not challenge.
Do not even turn their heads.

---

Lyosha walks through the gate unannounced.
Unstopped.
Unseen.

---

## The Halls

The corridors are dim.
Silent.

---

The light continues—
Climbing stairs.  
Turning corners.

---

Always just ahead.
Never out of reach.

---

Doors open before him.
Or perhaps they were never closed.

---

No servants.
No guards.

---

Or if they are there—
They do not see.

---

## The Prince’s Door

The light stops.

---

At a door Lyosha knows well.
The prince’s chamber.

---

It lingers.

---

As if confirming:
**Here.**

---

## The Room

The door opens.

---

Sunlight floods the chamber.

---

Not a beam.
Not a shaft.

---

**Sunlight.**

---

Full.
Golden.

---

At midnight.

---

It fills the space—
Without source.  
Without shadow.

---

And at its center—

---

[[Porthos Ryomar]]

---

Standing still.
Awake.

---

Already in the light.
As if he has been for some time.

---

He looks at Lyosha—
Not surprised.
Not relieved.
Not even questioning.

---

Just certain.

---

A quiet breath.

---

Then:

> “You saw it too.”

---

A pause.

---

And then, softer—

---

> “I think… this isn’t about us choosing anything.”

---

His eyes flick once—around the room.
At the light.
Then back.

---

> “I think we’ve already been chosen.”

---

The light does not move.
It does not fade.

---

It simply remains—

---

**Bearing witness.**

---
## Vignette — _Dawn Witness_

Morning comes hard.

---

Not gently.
Not gradually.

---

The sun **breaks** over the city—
Bright.
Unforgiving.

---

Lyosha hasn’t slept.

---

The path of light is gone—
But its memory sits behind his eyes like a brand.

---

The temple is already awake.

---

Not with the usual rhythm.

---

With purpose.

---

Armor.
Footsteps.
Low voices.

---

And at the center—

---

**The courtyard.**

---

Open to the sky.

---

No shade.

---

No shadow to hide in.

---

They are all **Pelorites**.

---

No mixed orders.
No shared ceremony.

---

Only the Dawnfather’s knights.

---

Steel polished.
White and gold catching the morning sun.

---

Hard faces.

---

The kind that come when mercy has already been weighed—

---

And found insufficient.

---

At the center—

---

**Crown Prince Porthos Ryomar.**

---

Bareheaded.
Unarmed.

---

Standing in full sunlight.

---

No canopy.
No comfort.

---

Just the sun.

---

And it is **on him**.

---

Not brighter.
Not unnatural.

---

But _present_ in a way it is not for anyone else.

---

Lyosha feels it immediately.

---

That same pressure.

---

That same attention.

---

He is not the focus.

---

He is the **witness**.

---

A Pelorite knight steps forward.

---

Older.
Scarred.

---

A man who has seen what happens when light comes too late.

---

He speaks:

---

> “You stand in the light of the Dawnfather.”

---

> “Not as prince.”

---

> “Not as heir.”

---

> “But as a man to be judged.”

---

Silence.

---

> “Do you understand?”

---

Porthos:

> “I do.”

---

A long look.

---

Then:

---

> “Then speak.”

---

## The Oaths of the Dawnfather

Porthos does not rush.

---

> “I will stand in the light, and not turn from it.”

---

> “I will carry the sun into dark places, and not falter when it reveals what I would rather not see.”

---

> “I will give mercy where it can live—”

---

> “—and end what must not.”

---

> “I will not hide behind crown, title, or command.”

---

> “If judgment must be done, I will stand within its reach.”

---

> “I will not call upon the Dawnfather to spare me from the cost of what is right.”

---

That one lands.

---

> “And if I fail—”

---

> “—then let the light fall on me as it would any other man.”

---

Silence.

---

The knight steps forward.

---

Hand to shoulder.

---

Firm.

Testing.

---

And quietly:

---

> “The sun does not care who you are.”

---

A beat.

---

> “Only what you do.”

---

Then, louder:

---

> “Stand.”

---

> “Stand as a knight of the Dawnfather.”

---

The Pelorite paladins move as one.

---

They **salute**.

---

Fist to chest.

---

No flourish.
No pageantry.

---

Just recognition.

---

## ☀️ The Witness Named

Only then—

---

The knight turns.

---

To Lyosha.

---

For the first time.

---

> “You were called.”

---

A statement.

---

> “You have seen.”

---

A beat.

---

> “That is enough.”

---

Another.

---

> “The Dawnfather does not waste witnesses.”

---

And that’s it.

---

No blessing.
No praise.

---

Just acknowledgment.
