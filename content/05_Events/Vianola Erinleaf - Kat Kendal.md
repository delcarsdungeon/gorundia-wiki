## Vianola Erinleaf Speaks with Dame Katherine Kendal

Vianola does not know Dame Katherine Kendal.

But she knows **Blue’s Kat**.

That is dangerous.

She has read enough of Blue Sylvanranth’s journals to have met Katherine through Blue’s affection, guilt, admiration, irritation, longing, and catastrophic young-heart confusion.

Vianola is self-aware enough to know that version may be wrong.

So when Katherine arrives, Vianola’s first move is formal.

Not warm.

Formal.

Respect is safer than pity.

> “Dame Katherine Kendal.”

Vianola inclines her head.

Not deeply.

Enough.

> “Professor Erinleaf.”

Kat returns the courtesy properly, though her eyes flick once toward the papers.

She is polite.

Alert.

And a little confused.

That, at least, is sane.

Vianola gestures to the chair.

> “Sit, if you wish. Stand, if that helps you feel less cornered. Leave, if you decide this was a mistake.”

Kat studies her.

> “Is this an interrogation?”

> “No.”

A pause.

> “If it becomes one, you may leave.”

That does not exactly reassure her.

It does, however, sound true.

Vianola continues:

> “Lord Marshal Beringer gave me permission to ask for this conversation. That permission does not bind you.”

Another pause.

> “Your consent is your own.”

Kat’s confusion deepens slightly.

> “Then… what is this about?”

Vianola folds her hands.

> “Blue Sylvanranth.”

The name changes the room.

## Why Katherine

Vianola does not rush to fill the silence.

Kat’s face does several things she would probably prefer it did not.

Then the knight in her comes back.

> “What could I tell you?”

It is not refusal.

Not quite.

It is honest confusion wearing armor.

Vianola accepts the question as serious.

> “Possibly nothing.”

A beat.

> “Possibly the part no report contains.”

Kat waits.

Vianola continues:

> “I am conducting research into what was done to Blue. The larval influence. The alterations in temperament. The shaping of loyalty, fear, attachment, and obedience.”

She does not make it sound mystical.

She makes it sound worse.

Clinical.

> “Documents can tell me what was placed inside her. Witnesses can tell me what changed after.”

Kat looks down.

> “I do not know that I noticed anything.”

> “Good.”

Kat looks back up, startled.

Vianola’s voice remains even.

> “Then do not invent certainty to be helpful. Useful lies are still lies.”

A pause.

> “But you were close to her. Close enough that she tried to be seen by you. Close enough that she may have hidden things from you very carefully.”

Kat absorbs that.

Not comfortably.

> “So this is about what I saw?”

> “Partly.”

Vianola’s eyes remain on her.

> “And partly about what Blue believed you saw.”

## Blue’s Journals

Kat’s gaze returns to the papers.

> “Those are hers.”

> “Some of them.”

A pause.

> “I have read enough.”

Kat’s face tightens.

> “All of them?”

> “Enough.”

Vianola does not soften the answer.

But she does clarify it.

> “Not casually.”

That matters.

Vianola does not handle the journals like gossip, evidence, or treasure.

She handles them like remains.

> “A journal is not testimony, Dame Katherine.”

She taps one page lightly.

> “It is weather. Useful, vivid, and often unfair.”

Kat says nothing.

Vianola continues:

> “Blue’s entries about you tell me less about what you did than what you meant.”

That lands.

Harder than accusation would have.

> “There are people we know only through their absence. You, Dame Katherine, I know through someone trying very hard not to say how much she needed you.”

Kat’s face changes before she can stop it.

Vianola notices.

Of course she notices.

> “That is not an accusation.”

A beat.

> “Not yet.”

## Blue’s Kat

Vianola studies her.

Not cruelly.

Like a scholar checking whether a map has mistaken hill for mountain.

> “You are shorter than I expected.”

Kat blinks.

> “I… apologize?”

> “Do not. It is Blue’s fault.”

Kat freezes slightly.

> “She wrote height emotionally.”

A faint pause.

> “People often do, when someone feels larger than the room.”

Kat looks down.

Vianola lets her.

> “Blue made you into proof.”

Kat’s voice is quiet.

> “Proof of what?”

> “That goodness could be armed and still remain kind.”

Silence.

> “That a girl could carry a sword and still belong.”

Another pause.

> “That nobility did not have to mean cruelty. That discipline did not have to mean coldness. That someone could be dangerous and safe at the same time.”

Vianola’s eyes do not leave Kat.

> “You were not merely her friend. You were evidence.”

Kat swallows.

> “I didn’t know.”

> “No.”

Vianola’s answer is immediate.

> “You were young. She was wounded. The adults were busy mistaking motion for responsibility.”

A dry edge enters her voice.

> “A common administrative disease.”

## The Questions

Vianola does not ask, *Why did you fail her?*

She asks:

> “When did you first realize Blue was performing happiness?”

Kat may answer.

Kat may not.

Either is acceptable.

If Kat says she does not know, Vianola only nods.

> “Good. Do not invent certainty to make guilt feel useful.”

If Kat gives an answer, Vianola asks:

> “And why did you believe the performance?”

Not cruel.

Precise.

Kat may say:

> “Because I wanted to.”

Vianola nods once.

> “Yes.”

A pause.

> “Wanting is often the first accomplice.”

Kat flinches.

Vianola does not soften the words.

But she does soften the next one.

> “It is also human.”

## Guilt

Kat says, eventually:

> “I should have been better.”

Vianola does not lie.

> “Yes.”

The word hurts.

It is supposed to.

Then:

> “But not better than your age, your knowledge, your fear, your training, and every adult failure surrounding you.”

Kat looks up.

Vianola’s voice remains steady.

> “Guilt is greedy, Dame Katherine. It will try to eat things it did not earn.”

A pause.

> “Do not feed it Blue whole.”

## The Record

Vianola closes one journal.

> “I must keep reminding myself you are not a character in Blue’s sorrow.”

Kat says, very quietly:

> “Sometimes I feel like one.”

For the first time, Vianola’s expression shifts.

Not pity.

Recognition.

> “Then stop.”

Kat looks wounded by the bluntness.

Vianola continues:

> “She loved you. She did not author you.”

That is the difference.

The important one.

> “Blue’s journals are not a chain.”

A pause.

> “Do not live as though every page is a commandment.”

Kat says nothing.

Vianola lets the silence breathe.

Then, softer:

> “Remember her accurately enough to disappoint her ghost.”

## If Kat Breaks

Kat tries to stay composed.

For a while, she succeeds.

Then one breath does not become another.

The training remains in her spine.

The grief finds the cracks anyway.

> “I’m sorry.”

Vianola watches for one second.

Then the professor disappears.

Not completely.

Enough.

> “Katherine.”

Kat shakes her head.

> “I’m sorry. I’m sorry, I—”

> “No.”

Vianola steps closer.

Slowly.

Deliberately.

Giving Kat every chance to refuse.

Then she opens one arm.

Not sweet.

Not ornamental.

Available.

> “Come here.”

Kat stares at her, horrified by how badly she wants to.

> “I can’t.”

> “You can.”

A pause.

> “You may.”

That does it.

Kat steps into her, and the sob comes out like something dragged from under armor.

Vianola holds her firmly.

One arm around her shoulders.

One hand between her shoulder blades.

Steady.

Contained.

Protective.

> “There.”

Kat breaks harder.

Vianola lets her.

> “You are not on parade.”

A pause.

> “No one is grading this.”

Kat chokes:

> “I failed her.”

Vianola closes her eyes briefly.

> “Perhaps.”

Cruel word.

Honest word.

Then:

> “But not alone.”

Her hand tightens slightly.

> “Never alone.”

Kat clings harder.

Vianola does not pull away.

> “You loved her.”

A pause.

> “That is not the same thing as saving her.”

Another.

> “And it is not nothing.”

Kat sobs.

Vianola lowers her voice.

> “Breathe, child.”

> “Let it hurt.”

> “Do not make it useful yet.”

For a few minutes, Dame Katherine Kendal does not have to become stronger.

She only has to be held.

## Vianola’s Private Note

**Dame Katherine Kendal:** substantially real beneath Blue’s idealization. Blue used her as symbol of armed goodness, noble permission, and survivable belonging. Subject carries guilt but has not yet converted it into identity. Must be kept from becoming a monument to someone else’s wound. Strong candidate for growth if allowed failure without humiliation.

Addendum: I had expected resentment. Defensiveness. Perhaps polished knightly denial.

Instead, Dame Katherine apologized to someone who is not dead, which is a more complicated cruelty.

Grief has rules. Absence does not.

She does not know whether to mourn Blue, wait for Blue, prepare for Blue, or forgive herself before Blue has the chance to do it properly. This leaves her trapped in a corridor with every door half-open. No wonder she stands so straight. If she bent, she might see how much floor is missing.

She thanked me before leaving.

Not for the conclusions. Not for the questions. For not making Blue smaller.

I find this detail difficult to file.

She also straightened her armor before stepping outside, though the guard had heard her weep. Pride, not vanity. The old reflex: if one has fallen apart, assemble before witnesses return.

I have known queens with less dignity.

Further note: I should not become fond of her.

Further note, amended: that instruction has already failed.

## What Vianola Later Tells Beringer

> “Dame Katherine is not what Blue made of her.”

A pause.

> “That is not an insult.”

Another.

> “It is mercy.”

Vianola looks toward the tent-flap, still seeing the shadow of Dame Katherine composing herself before she stepped outside.

> “She believes in you.”

The words are plain. No accusation. No praise.

Somehow worse.

> “Not blindly. Not childishly. I would be less impressed if she did.”

A pause.

> “She has seen enough of men with swords to know that strength is not the same thing as safety.”

Her gaze returns to Beringer.

> “And still, when she speaks of you, she speaks as though your judgment may be trusted even when your temper should not be.”

A faint, dry edge.

> “A charitable distinction. I recommend earning it.”

Another pause.

> “Do not make her wrong.”

Another.

> “Not because she is fragile.”

Vianola’s voice lowers slightly.

> “Because she is not. And people like that do not break all at once.”

She folds her hands.

> “They keep standing until everyone mistakes the standing for proof that no harm was done.”

Vianola holds his gaze a moment longer than is comfortable.

Then, unexpectedly, she inclines her head.

Not a bow.

Not quite.

> “I should also thank you.”

A pause.

> “For allowing the conversation.”

Another.

> “And for not attempting to curate her answers before I heard them.”

It is almost an apology.

Almost.

Vianola’s hands fold again, precise as closing a book.

> “You should understand something, Sir Beringer. My caution is not personal.”

A faint, dry correction follows at once.

> “Not entirely personal.”

She lets that sit.

> “I have known good men. Brave men. Dutiful men. Men who loved their country, their friends, their gods, and their own idea of justice.”

Her voice does not harden.

That makes it worse.

> “Many of them were not monsters when they began.”

A pause.

> “They merely became certain.”

Another.

> “Certain that the danger was too great. Certain that delay was weakness. Certain that those who questioned them did not understand the stakes.”

A beat.

> “Some of them saved lives.”

Another.

> “Some of them saved cities.”

Then, quieter:

> “And some of them became the reason other people needed saving.”

She exhales once, controlled.

> “So no, Sir Beringer. I do not think you are Wilhelm Vogel.”

A pause.

> “But I have seen men walk toward terrible things while telling themselves they were walking away from worse ones.”

Another.

> “That is the road I watch for.”

A faint change passes over her expression.

Not anger.

Older than anger.

> “But do not mistake me.”

A pause.

> “I have no admiration for the opposite failure.”

Her mouth tightens slightly.

> “I live in Duke Allaric’s duchy. I have seen what comes of patient hands folded too neatly in the lap.”

Another pause.

> “The slow rot. The gracious delay. The elegant committee. The beautifully worded postponement.”

Her voice remains even.

> “My own people have made an art of watching the house burn while debating the historical significance of flame.”

A dry breath.

> “It is very refined. Very old. Very expensive.”

Her eyes return to him.

> “So no, Sir Beringer. I am not asking you to become harmless.”

A beat.

> “Harmless men have done their share of damage.”

Then, quieter:

> “I am asking you to remain answerable while you act.”

The edge leaves her voice, just slightly.

> “Dame Katherine’s trust does not settle my concerns.”

A pause.

> “But it does require me to leave room for the possibility that she has seen a better part of you than I have.”

Then, almost gently:

> “I would be pleased to be wrong.”

A faint, humorless flicker.

> “Do not make that more difficult than it already is.”

Only then do her eyes pause on Beringer’s ring.

> “That was made quickly.”

Beringer glances down.

> “Norwin.”

> “Obviously.”

A pause.

> “Tell him I have questions.”

Another.

> “No. Do not. I will have questions whether he is warned or not.”
