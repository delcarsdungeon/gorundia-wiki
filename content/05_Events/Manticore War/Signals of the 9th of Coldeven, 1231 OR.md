# Signals of the 9th of Coldeven, 1231 OR

On the **9th of Coldeven, 1231 OR**, two major military signals reached the Lord Marshal’s channel.

The first came from **Brindleford**, not Greyspur, confirming that **Greyspur Post had fallen** to Vogel-aligned forces and black-armored troops identified by survivors as the **Iron Covenant**.

The second came from **Blackbriar**, under the seal of **Count Alistair Amadis**, reporting organized orc probes along the **Blackbriar Thornline Watch**, judged likely to be Dominion-directed but not yet an open escalation.

Together, the messages made the strategic picture uglier in two directions at once: the west had been bloodied, and the east was being measured.

Because naturally the realm could not simply have one problem. That would be vulgar.

---

## Signal One: Dame Quinn Moffet from Brindleford

The signal comes through from **Brindleford**, not **Greyspur**.

That alone tells the room enough.

The transmitting voice is not **Magus Tavin Elrow**. He is dead. This mage is older, flatter, and careful in the way of someone reading words written by a commander who is very much alive and very much angry.

> “Lord Marshal’s channel. Brindleford field station.
>
> Transmitting for Dame Quinn Moffet.”

A pause.

> “Message follows.”

Then Quinn’s report begins.

---

### Message from Dame Quinn Moffet

> “To the Lord Marshal of the Crown, from Dame Quinn Moffet, commanding remnant forces withdrawn from Greyspur.”
>
> “Greyspur Post is lost.”
>
> “Enemy forces struck from Westfold under tattered Vogel banners. The assault included heavy infantry in sealed black iron, supported by lesser Westfold and Vogel-aligned troops.”
>
> “The outer gate was shattered by force. The first breach was contained. The second was not.”
>
> “Magus Tavin Elrow was killed at his station while maintaining field signal.”
>
> “I was badly wounded during the collapse of the gate defense and removed from the field by Sir Kersan Hes against my order.”
>
> “He was correct.”
>
> “This will not make him less insufferable.”

---

### Withdrawal to Brindleford

> “Surviving forces withdrew west in broken order to Brindleford, carrying wounded, surviving records, partial signal kit, and the Moffet standard.”
>
> “The post itself could not be held.”
>
> “Our present position is Brindleford. We are digging in. We will not counterattack without direct order.”

---

### Casualty Accounting

> “Initial strength at Greyspur stood at approximately three hundred eighty fighting personnel and attached staff.”
>
> “Current accounting gives one hundred thirty-two confirmed dead on the field.”
>
> “Forty-one are missing, presumed dead or taken.”
>
> “One hundred forty-six are wounded.”
>
> “Of the wounded, clerical care is expected to save most who would otherwise die. However, no fewer than forty are judged unlikely to return to combat duty soon, if ever, due to maiming, loss of limb, loss of sight, crushed joints, or similar wounds.”
>
> “Fewer than sixty remain immediately battle-ready.”

---

### Directed Action Against House Moffet

> “Sir Kersan Hes directly observed black-armored enemy troops moving among fallen and wounded after the breach.”
>
> “They took no prisoners from among Moffet personnel.”
>
> “Men attempting surrender were killed.”
>
> “Wounded bearing Moffet colors were finished where they lay.”
>
> “Stretcher parties were struck.”
>
> “Clerics and medics killed by the enemy whenever possible.”
>
> “This was not ordinary seizure of a post.”
>
> “This was a directed action against House Moffet.”

---

### Iron Covenant Assessment

> “The name ‘Iron Covenant’ has been repeated by several survivors and prisoners from earlier actions as a term for these black-armored Vogel elites.”
>
> “I cannot yet confirm whether this is a formal unit name, soldiers’ rumor, or enemy affectation.”
>
> “Whatever they are called, they fight above the weight of ordinary heavy infantry.”
>
> “They are disciplined, unusually difficult to bring down, and appear willing to continue the assault through losses that would break conventional troops.”

---

### Current Position and Recommendation

> “My father is mustering additional forces and calling in available household strength.”
>
> “Until reinforced or ordered otherwise, Brindleford will be held as the next western defense.”
>
> “I recommend all Moffet banners, detachments, messengers, and kin be treated as likely priority targets for remaining Vogel-aligned forces.”
>
> “Greyspur was not taken cleanly.”
>
> “It was broken.”
>
> “We are not.”
>
> “House Moffet remains in the field.”
>
> “I remain in command.”
>
> “Send orders.”

---

## Signal Two: Count Alistair Amadis from Blackbriar

The signal comes through cleanly from **Blackbriar**.

No urgency.

No raised voice.

No theatrical gravity.

Just the kind of calm that makes everyone in the room immediately less comfortable, because apparently the universe still has a sense of timing and it is a bastard.

The voice is **Aelindrë Lumaeril’s**: elegant, measured, elvish, and so composed that even the word “invasion” would sound like a matter of table placement.

> “Master of Signals, this is Aelindrë Lumaeril, sworn wizard of House Amadis, transmitting under the seal of Count Alistair Amadis of Blackbriar.”
>
> “This message is to be entered in duplicate for His Majesty the King and for the Lord Marshal of the Crown.”
>
> “Message follows.”

A pause.

Then Count Amadis’ words.

---

### Message from Count Alistair Amadis

> “To His Majesty King Priam Ryomar, and to Sir Beringer Amadis, Lord Marshal of the Crown.”
>
> “Reports from the Blackbriar Thornline Watch indicate increased movement among orc war-bands east and southeast of Griefwatch.”
>
> “These movements are organized enough to merit record. They are not yet sufficient to merit alarm.”
>
> “Small parties have tested watch roads, beacon distance, patrol intervals, and response speed between Griefwatch, Crookhorn, Dead Lance, and the lesser signal posts.”
>
> “Two parties withdrew before contact.”
>
> “One attempted to draw pursuit into broken ground.”
>
> “One was engaged and driven off with minor losses.”
>
> “No fort of the Blackbriar Thornline Watch has been threatened.”
>
> “No beacon has been forced.”
>
> “No settlement behind the line has been reached.”

Aelindrë’s voice remains perfectly even.

---

### Dominion Assessment

> “The orc forces observed do not appear to be acting as independent raiders.”
>
> “Their timing is too careful.”
>
> “Their withdrawals are too orderly.”
>
> “Their routes show knowledge of our roads, patrol habits, and visual signals.”
>
> “Their actions are consistent with forces being directed by an outside hand.”

Then colder:

> “I judge that hand to be Dominion.”
>
> “Not openly.”
>
> “Not under imperial banner.”
>
> “Not in a manner requiring formal answer by treaty or crown declaration.”
>
> “They are using deniable and expendable allied forces to test the eastern marches while Gorundia is engaged in internal violence.”

A brief pause.

No ornament. No anger. Just conclusion.

> “This is opportunism, not escalation.”
>
> “At present.”

Another pause.

> “They are testing whether civil disorder has reduced the readiness of the eastern defenses.”
>
> “It has not.”

There it is. Cold enough to keep fish fresh.

---

### No Alarm, No Reinforcement Request

> “This is not a request for reinforcement.”
>
> “This is not an alarm.”
>
> “Blackbriar is not under immediate threat.”
>
> “The Blackbriar Thornline Watch remains intact.”
>
> “Mercygate remains prepared to receive prisoners, deserters, refugees, or infiltrators as circumstances require.”

---

### Operational Orders

> “Local commanders have been instructed to avoid unnecessary pursuit.”
>
> “Signal discipline is to be preserved.”
>
> “Patrol overlap has been increased.”
>
> “Night watches have been doubled.”
>
> “Minor roads west of the Thornline are being inspected without public notice.”
>
> “Orc combatants bearing Dominion marks, Hextorite brands, courier knots, or evidence of organized direction are to be separated for examination.”
>
> “Irregular war-bands without such signs are to be driven back, not followed into ground chosen by another commander.”

Aelindrë lets the next silence land exactly where it should.

---

### Final Assessment

> “My present assessment is that the Dominion seeks measurement.”
>
> “They should receive none that is useful.”
>
> “I will send further report if probes increase, if handlers are confirmed, or if the Dominion hand becomes visible enough to name formally.”
>
> “Until then, Blackbriar holds its line.”
>
> “By my hand and seal,”
>
> “Alistair Amadis, Count of Blackbriar.”

A final pause.

> “End message.”

Then Aelindrë adds, in her own voice:

> “Receipt confirmation is requested. Count Amadis does not request discussion unless His Majesty or the Lord Marshal requires clarification.”

And that is it.

No flourish.

No plea.

No panic.

Just Blackbriar quietly informing the Crown that the east has been tested, the test was noticed, and the examiner has been marked.

---

## Strategic Read

These two messages are not the same kind of emergency.

**Greyspur** is a battlefield disaster and a targeted atrocity. The post is lost, House Moffet has been deliberately punished, and the **Iron Covenant** has emerged as a serious heavy infantry threat.

**Blackbriar** is a controlled warning. The line holds, the probes have failed to gain useful information, and Count Amadis is making sure both the King and the Lord Marshal know the Dominion is testing the realm’s seams.

Together, they show the wider war taking shape:

- Vogel-aligned remnants are striking hard in the west.
- The Iron Covenant is acting with discipline, cruelty, and political purpose.
- House Moffet is now a marked target.
- The Dominion is probing the east without crossing the line into open war.
- Blackbriar remains calm, prepared, and extremely unwilling to be baited.
- The Lord Marshal is being forced to read two fronts at once.

Because apparently one collapsing front was not sufficiently educational.
