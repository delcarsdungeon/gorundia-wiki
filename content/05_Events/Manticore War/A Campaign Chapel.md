# A Campaign Chapel

The chapel is packed.

Not grand.

Canvas walls.
Rough benches.
A simple altar.
Shields stacked beside the entrance.

The sort of place built because soldiers need somewhere to pray and the gods, unlike nobles, apparently do not require dressed stone.

Beringer sits in the first rank because someone has decided that this is where the Lord Marshal belongs.

He is already regretting this.

It is not that Beringer dislikes sermons.

Quite the opposite.

He believes.

Deeply.

He has prayed before battles, after battles, over dead friends, over decisions he was not certain he had the right to make.

He believes in Heironeous.

He believes strength exists for service.

He believes duty matters when nobody is watching.

Unfortunately, today he has been seated with three people who make **his** devotion look positively relaxed.

Elizavetta is on his arm.

Not ceremonially.

Comfortably.

Close enough that when his expression tightens, she feels it before she sees it.

The priest knows who she is.

More importantly, he knows enough to know there is something unusual about her.

Something in the way the divine has marked her life.

Something unfinished.

He has never asked.

A competent priest occasionally recognizes that the gods have already begun a conversation and perhaps do not require his assistance.

On Beringer's other side sits Radomir.

Straight-backed.

Attentive.

Entirely sincere.

A man who regards prayer, orders, and sharpening a sword as three variations of preparing properly for the day.

And beyond him—

Cwenheld.

The Venerated Shield of Heironeous.

The actual, living, motherfucking Shield.

Battered.
Scarred.
Older than most of the officers in the chapel.
Sitting quietly as though she is merely another old soldier attending service.

Nobody within thirty feet believes that.

Least of all the priest.

He is not intimidated by her presence.

If anything, it has simplified matters.

Should Beringer later attempt to explain that there is nothing particularly holy, symbolic, or divinely interesting about him, the priest has only to gesture toward the bench.

The Lord Marshal.

The strange woman clinging affectionately to his arm whose soul has plainly been through things priests write dissertations about.

His devout bodyguard.

And the Venerated Shield of Heironeous.

Apparently Providence has developed a sense of staging.

The priest is good.

That's the problem.

Old campaign Heironean.

Broken nose.
Missing two fingers.
Sword belt polished from decades of wear.

His voice reaches the back without ever becoming a shout.

He has spent twenty minutes talking about fear.

Not cowardice.

Fear.

The useful kind.

The tightening in the stomach before the charge.

The voice that tells a man he may die and thereby gives him the opportunity to decide what he is going to do about it.

Then:

> "Heironeous and Hextor are brothers."

Beringer's eyes close.

Just briefly.

Elizavetta feels his arm tighten.

She glances up at him.

Then toward the priest.

Then back to Beringer.

A tiny smile begins.

Beringer sees it.

> "Don't."

She says nothing.

Which is worse.

Radomir notices too.

He does not smile.

He inclines his head toward the priest.

Interested.

Cwenheld does not move at all.

She has the serene expression of a woman who has realized exactly where this sermon is going and considers the route perfectly acceptable.

Beringer looks down the bench.

Elizavetta.

Radomir.

Cwenheld.

Not one ally.

Great.

The priest continues.

> "One believed strength existed to defend what was right."

A pause.

> "The other believed strength made whatever he desired right."

Quiet murmurs move through the chapel.

Beringer stares directly ahead.

He knows where this is going.

Everyone knows where this is going.

Apparently they are going there anyway.

> "And not long ago, before half the realm, two other brothers stood upon the same ground."

There it is.

Beringer's jaw tightens.

Someone three benches behind him shifts slightly to get a better look.

Beringer can **feel it**.

Elizavetta gives his arm the faintest squeeze.

Not teasing now.

Support.

The priest continues.

> "One came armored in fear."

He begins pacing slowly before the altar.

> "Fear of losing his station."

> "Fear of judgment."

> "Fear that without obedience forced from others, he might discover there was nothing beneath him at all."

Wilhelm.

No name necessary.

Elizavetta's expression has gone still.

Whatever amusement there was is gone.

She knew men like that.

She knew the faith that made such men virtues of themselves.

Beside her, Beringer feels the change.

His hand settles briefly over hers.

The priest sees it.

He does not pause.

> "The other entered that ground carrying something heavier."

The priest stops.

The chapel waits.

Beringer becomes fascinated by a knot in the floorboards.

It is an extraordinary knot.

Possibly of historical significance.

> "Duty."

Elizavetta looks up at him.

Radomir gives one slow nod.

Cwenheld's eyes close briefly in approval.

Beringer mutters:

> "Oh, for fuck's sake."

Elizavetta bites the inside of her cheek.

Radomir doesn't even look at him.

> "Is true."

Beringer's head moves perhaps half an inch.

> "Not helping."

A tiny shrug from Radomir.

> "Was not trying."

From farther down the bench comes Cwenheld's quiet voice.

> "He's right."

Beringer turns.

Cwenheld shifts slightly.

> "Don't start."

He faces forward again.

Elizavetta is now shaking silently against his arm.

The priest continues.

> "And when the trial was finished—"

A pause.

> "—the righteous brother stood."

That gets them.

Fists touch breastplates.

Heads bow.

From around the chapel:

> "By the Invincible."

Radomir's is immediate.

Cwenheld's slower, older, no less certain.

Elizavetta does not make the gesture.

But her eyes remain fixed on the altar.

Beringer sees that too.

And because he is a true believer despite desperately wishing everyone would stop making that fact theatrically relevant—

his fist touches his breast with the others.

> "By the Invincible."

Quietly.

Sincerely.

The priest hears him.

Naturally.

Then he turns away from Wilhelm.

Toward the war.

> "And now Hextor's servants come against us."

The room changes.

Elizavetta becomes utterly still.

This isn't metaphor for her.

Not entirely.

Radomir's jaw sets.

Cwenheld looks up.

Beringer's embarrassment drains away.

The priest continues.

> "Some ask why."

He looks over the assembled soldiers.

> "Why now?"

> "Why when our fields have already drunk Gorundian blood?"

> "Why when treason wounded the Crown?"

> "Why when brother was forced to raise steel against brother?"

A pause.

> "Because they have seen the same thing we have."

The old priest raises one scarred hand.

> "Gorundia is standing up."

Silence.

> "The corrupt have been dragged into daylight."

> "Old lies have been named."

> "Old fears have been challenged."

> "And men once taught to endure injustice have remembered that endurance is not obedience."

Elizavetta's fingers tighten around Beringer's arm.

That one landed somewhere private.

Beringer glances down.

She does not look at him.

So he doesn't make her.

The priest continues.

> "The Dominion does not strike because we are weak."

Silence.

> "It strikes because we are shedding weakness."

Radomir's fist closes slowly against his knee.

Cwenheld nods once.

Hard.

The priest's eyes pass over Elizavetta.

Just briefly.

He knows enough.

And therefore says nothing directly to her.

> "Hextor does not fear the man already crawling."

> "The tyrant does not fear the broken man."

> "The tyrant fears the man who has put one knee beneath himself."

Radomir murmurs:

> "Good."

Cwenheld hears him.

> "Aye."

Beringer looks down the bench again.

Three near-zealots.

He is surrounded.

Worse, they're right.

> "Because the tyrant knows what comes next."

The priest reaches for the ceremonial sword laid across the altar.

He draws it.

No flourish.

Simply raises it.

> "He stands."

Radomir is on his feet immediately.

Fist hard against his breast.

Cwenheld rises more slowly.

Age and old wounds have opinions.

Nobody mistakes slowness for hesitation.

The **Venerated Shield of Heironeous stands**.

Half the chapel straightens merely because she has.

Elizavetta rises with Beringer, still holding his arm.

And the Lord Marshal of Gorundia discovers that remaining seated is no longer merely theologically awkward.

It would now require him to remain seated while:

- his devout bodyguard stands,
- Elizavetta stands,
- an entire campaign congregation stands,
- and the Venerated Shield of his own god is literally beside him.

There are battles with better odds.

Beringer stands.

The response rolls through the chapel:

> **"BY STEEL, WE STAND."**

Radomir says it like an oath.

> **"By steel, we stand."**

Cwenheld says it like something she has proven repeatedly.

> **"By steel, we stand."**

Elizavetta says it more quietly.

Carefully.

As though trying the words against old scars.

> "By steel, we stand."

Beringer hears her.

And whatever embarrassment remains simply dies.

His hand closes around hers.

Then:

> **"By steel, we stand."**

For perhaps ten seconds, Beringer forgets that the sermon is about him.

Which is probably why it works.

---

## Afterward

Outside, soldiers disperse slowly.

Some are talking.

Some aren't.

The old priest catches Beringer near the chapel entrance.

Elizavetta is still on his arm.

Radomir is close behind.

Cwenheld is taking her time because after a lifetime in Heironeous's service she has earned the right to make everyone else wait.

The priest bows.

> "My lord."

Beringer stops.

Because naturally there is more.

> "I hope I did no injustice to what happened."

Beringer looks at him.

Then:

> "Wilhelm was my brother."

The priest nods.

> "Yes."

> "He was a traitor."

> "Yes."

> "I killed him because he made that necessary."

Another nod.

> "Yes."

Beringer gestures back toward the chapel.

> "You make me sound like I descended from the fucking heavens."

The priest considers this.

His eyes flick briefly across the little group.

Elizavetta.

Radomir.

Then Cwenheld, finally emerging from the chapel behind them.

The priest looks back at Beringer.

> "My lord."

A pause.

> "You attended my sermon with the Venerated Shield of Heironeous sitting three feet from you."

Cwenheld stops.

Beringer's expression hardens.

The priest continues.

> "Your bodyguard muttered agreement with every third sentence."

Radomir frowns.

> "Not every third."

The priest gives him a glance.

> "Near enough."

Then his attention shifts to Elizavetta.

Much more carefully.

> "And Lady Elizavetta has experienced enough of the divine that I have deliberately chosen never to ask her questions without invitation."

Elizavetta raises an eyebrow.

The priest returns to Beringer.

> "You are entitled to argue that you are not holy."

A beat.

> "You are entitled to argue that you are not special."

Another.

> "But, respectfully, my lord—"

He gestures toward the three people around him.

> "—you have chosen a very poor audience for the argument."

Silence.

Cwenheld makes a noise.

Not quite a laugh.

Beringer looks at her.

> "Don't."

Cwenheld's eyebrows rise.

> "I said nothing."

> "You were about to."

> "I was."

Beringer looks back at the priest.

> "I am a soldier."

The priest nods.

> "Yes."

> "I did my duty."

> "Yes."

> "That's all."

The priest considers him for a moment.

Then:

> "My lord, with respect, that is the central argument of the sermon."

Beringer opens his mouth.

Stops.

Elizavetta makes a tiny choking noise.

Radomir looks toward the sky.

Cwenheld simply smiles.

Beringer points accusingly at the priest.

> "I don't like you."

> "That is not required."

> "...I hate clergy."

The priest raises an eyebrow.

> "You employ several, my lord."

> "I hate them too."

Radomir glances around.

Then quietly:

> "This is unwise thing to say outside chapel."

Beringer turns.

> "Are you correcting my theology?"

> "No."

A beat.

> "Only location."

Elizavetta finally loses the fight and laughs.

Beringer looks at her.

She presses closer against his arm.

> "Don't you start."

> "I said nothing."

From behind them, Cwenheld:

> "She didn't need to."

Beringer closes his eyes.

The priest, wisely, allows him several seconds.

Then:

> "There is one other matter."

Beringer's eyes remain closed.

> "No."

> "My lord—"

> "Whatever you're about to say, no."

> "The High Sword has requested a copy."

Beringer's eyes open.

He stares at the priest.

Radomir nods.

This is reasonable.

It is a Heironean sermon.

Cwenheld looks unsurprised.

The priest continues.

> "The Solar Prelate has requested one as well."

Beringer slowly turns his head.

> "Why?"

> "Courtesy."

Nobody speaks.

Even Elizavetta knows what that word means when senior clergy say it.

The priest continues:

> "And the First Magistrix of the White Court."

Beringer stares.

> "Also courtesy?"

> "Naturally."

Cwenheld actually laughs.

One short bark.

Beringer looks at her in betrayal.

> "You find this funny?"

> "Very."

Radomir attempts to remain neutral.

Fails.

Beringer notices.

> "You too?"

> "No."

A beat.

> "Little."

Elizavetta is openly smiling now.

Beringer looks from one to another.

> "I am surrounded by fanatics."

Radomir looks mildly offended.

> "You are believer."

> "I know."

> "Then four fanatics."

Cwenheld nods approvingly.

> "Better."

Beringer stares at all three of them.

Then simply walks away.

Elizavetta goes with him because she is still attached to his arm and has absolutely no intention of releasing the prisoner.

The priest watches them go.

Radomir remains for a moment.

Then:

> "Do not tell him when copies arrive."

The priest looks toward Beringer's retreating back.

> "I wasn't planning to."

> "Good."

Radomir considers.

> "He has enough enemies."

The priest raises an eyebrow.

Radomir gestures vaguely upward, encompassing the higher clergy of three faiths.

> "Does not need High Sword, Solar Prelate, First Magistrix discussing him."

The priest says:

> "I don't believe they're discussing him."

Radomir gives him a flat look.

The priest lasts two seconds.

> "...exclusively."

Radomir exhales.

> "Gods preserve him."

Behind them, Cwenheld says:

> "They seem to be trying."

Radomir looks at her.

Then toward Beringer.

Then back.

> "This may be problem."

Cwenheld's helmeted head tilted slightly.

> "For him?"

> "Yes."

A beat.

> "Very much."

# Sermon Notes — The Brothers' Lesson

The sermon is spreading.

Not by royal proclamation.

Not by coordinated instruction.

And certainly not because anyone thoughtfully concluded that the Lord Marshal needed the worst afternoon of his life turned into portable religious instruction.

The comparison is simply too obvious for the Heironean clergy to leave alone.

**Heironeous and Hextor.**

Brothers.

One teaches that strength exists to defend what is right.

The other teaches that strength makes whatever the strong desire right.

And now, within living memory of every soldier in Gorundia, two other brothers stood before the realm.

**Wilhelm Vogel and Beringer Amadis.**

One armed himself with conspiracy, infernal bargains, fear, and the machinery of a house that had forgotten the difference between obedience and loyalty.

The other walked into the arena and killed him.

Not easily in conscience.

But cleanly in fact.

And lawfully.

The emerging Heironean name is:

## **The Brothers' Lesson**

Other forms already circulating include:

- **The Lesson of the Arena**
- **The Worthy Brother's Triumph**
- **The Brother Who Stood**

Beringer despises the last two.

This has had no measurable effect on their popularity.

The second half of the sermon is becoming more important than the first.

The Dominion is attacking now.

Hextor's servants are coming harder precisely while Gorundia is tearing corruption out by the roots.

The central teaching:

> **The enemy does not strike because Gorundia has become weak.**
>
> **The enemy strikes because Gorundia has begun to remember what strength is.**

The Dominion does not smell weakness.

It smells recovery.

The corrupt have been exposed.

The Crown survived.

Old lies have been named.

Old systems have been challenged.

People taught to endure injustice are remembering that endurance and obedience are not the same thing.

So Hextor strikes harder.

Not because the righteous brother is losing.

Because he is getting back onto his feet.

Thus the current war becomes part of the parable:

> **Hextor does not fear the man already crawling.**
>
> **The tyrant fears the man who has put one knee beneath himself.**
>
> **Because he knows what comes next.**
>
> **He stands.**

Beringer cannot simply dismiss this as religious flattery.

He is himself a believer.

The central doctrine is doctrine he actually accepts.

His objection is personal:

He does not believe doing one's duty makes a man holy.

Unfortunately, this argument becomes considerably less persuasive when delivered while standing beside:

- **Cwenheld, Venerated Shield of Heironeous**
- **Radomir, whose faith borders pleasantly on militant zeal**
- **Elizavetta, whose relationship with the divine is complicated enough that experienced priests know better than to poke it with a stick**

Beringer's preferred explanation is:

> "I did my duty."

The clergy's increasingly irritating response is:

> **"Yes. That's the point."**

And Krozen's public restoration only makes the developing religious narrative harder to resist:

**Conspiracy exposed.**

**The corrupt brother cast down.**

**The Crown survives.**

**The celestial guardian returns.**

**Hextor's servants strike from beyond the frontier.**

At this point the gods have stopped providing metaphors and begun issuing fucking sermon notes.

Naturally, the **High Sword of Heironeous** wants copies circulated.

And, in the ancient spirit of perfectly sincere Triad cooperation, copies have also been requested by the **Solar Prelate of Pelor** and the **First Magistrix of the White Court**.

As a courtesy.

Naturally.

Because the three great churches of Gorundia would never use "courtesy" as a polite term for:

**We saw what your god is doing.**

**We have notes.**

**We'll be discussing them.**
