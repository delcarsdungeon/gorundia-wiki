# AFTER-ACTION WITNESS STATEMENTS

## INCIDENT ONE: ATTACK UPON THE FIELD SHRINE

**Location:** Shrine of Heironeous, western command quarter  
**Enemy:** One barbed devil; four Dominion temple soldiers  
**Primary Defender:** Dame Cwenheld Thralen, Venerated Shield of Heironeous  
**Witness:** Brother Edric Vale, junior shrine attendant  
**Statement taken by:** Shield-Commander Alwen Hart

---

**SHIELD-COMMANDER HART:** State what occurred.

**BROTHER EDRIC:** The iron vessel struck near the officers’ tents. The ground shook hard enough to knock the traveling lamps from their hooks.

The shrine bell began ringing.

No one was pulling it.

**HART:** Divine manifestation?

**EDRIC:** I do not know, Commander.

**HART:** Continue.

**EDRIC:** Four Dominion soldiers reached us first. Temple troops. Red gauntlets on their shields. The shrine guard met them outside.

Then the devil came through them.

Not behind them.

Through them.

They moved aside without looking, as though they had practiced it.

It was taller than the entrance frame. Black-red skin. Spines from its shoulders, arms, face, everywhere. Long tail. Smoke coming from its mouth.

It looked into the shrine and saw the statue.

Then it smiled.

**HART:** Did it attack you?

**EDRIC:** No.

That was the point.

It wanted us alive to watch.

It said:

> “See what becomes of a god who cannot defend his own image.”

Dame Cwenheld was kneeling before the altar.

She stood.

Slowly at first.

Her legs were stiff.

She had no helmet on. She had been praying without it.

The devil looked at her eyes and understood she could not see it clearly.

It laughed.

**HART:** Did Dame Cwenheld respond?

**EDRIC:** She asked me:

> “Is it between me and the door?”

I said yes.

She said:

> “Good.”

Then she told us to leave.

**HART:** Did you obey?

**EDRIC:** Not immediately.

**HART:** Why?

**EDRIC:** I thought she needed help.

**HART:** And then?

**EDRIC:** She said:

> “Brother Edric, if I must protect the shrine, the wounded, and your poor judgment, I shall become cross.”

We left through the rear canvas.

**HART:** Continue from what you observed outside.

**EDRIC:** The devil reached for the statue.

Dame Cwenheld stepped between them.

It struck her shield.

The sound was like a smith dropping an anvil.

She went backward into the altar rail. One of the posts broke. The statue rocked on its traveling base.

She caught it with her sword arm.

The devil pushed harder.

She held the shield with one hand and the statue with the other.

It called her an old ruin guarding another.

She said:

> “This one is consecrated.”

Then the light came.

Blue-white.

Through every seam in her armor.

Her back straightened.

Her bad leg stopped shaking.

She set the statue upright and took her sword properly.

The devil stopped laughing.

**HART:** Describe the engagement.

**EDRIC:** It was not like watching an old knight fight.

It was not like watching a young knight fight either.

She did not move quickly except when she had to.

She simply knew where it would be.

The devil tried to circle her.

She turned before it moved.

It struck high. She raised the shield.

It swept its tail low. She stepped over it.

It threw spines. She put the shield in front of the statue rather than herself.

One struck her shoulder.

Another went into her thigh.

She did not remove them.

**HART:** Did her impaired sight appear to hinder her?

**EDRIC:** She was not watching the devil.

She was listening to it.

Every scrape of its claws. Every movement of the canvas. Every breath.

When it tried to move quietly, the shrine bell rang once.

Just once.

She turned and struck it in the mouth.

**HART:** The bell rang without assistance?

**EDRIC:** Yes, Commander.

**HART:** Continue.

**EDRIC:** The Dominion soldiers attempted to enter behind her.

She kicked the altar bench across the doorway.

It struck the first two and blocked the others.

She never turned away from the devil.

It said:

> “Kneel.”

Dame Cwenheld said:

> “No.”

The command hit hard enough that everyone outside felt it. Three of the shrine guard dropped to one knee.

She did not.

The devil said it again.

> “Kneel.”

She planted her shield.

The lightning bolt upon it began to glow.

She answered:

> “You stand in his house.”

The devil charged.

She charged too.

**HART:** Dame Cwenheld charged inside the shrine?

**EDRIC:** Yes.

There were perhaps six yards between them.

She crossed it in two steps.

Her shield hit its chest.

The devil went through the front wall.

Not the entrance.

The wall.

Dame Cwenheld followed.

**HART:** Through the wall?

**EDRIC:** Yes, Commander.

**HART:** Naturally.

**EDRIC:** They landed among the guy ropes.

The devil caught her with its claws and drew her against the spines.

I saw blood run down the back of her armor.

She drove her forehead into its face.

Then she said:

> “You have mistaken endurance for helplessness.”

She struck it under the ribs.

The blade went in perhaps a hand’s breadth.

The light went all the way through.

Every barb on the creature lit blue.

The devil screamed.

It tried to pull away.

She caught one of its horns behind the rim of her shield and held it in place.

Then she struck it again.

And again.

The third strike broke the sword.

**HART:** Her sword broke?

**EDRIC:** At the middle of the blade.

The devil laughed again.

Dame Cwenheld looked down toward the sound.

Then she drove the broken sword into its throat.

**HART:** Result?

**EDRIC:** The devil began to come apart.

There were cracks in it, like iron in a forge. Light came through them. Then flame.

Dame Cwenheld turned her back upon it and raised her shield toward the shrine.

**HART:** She shielded the structure rather than withdrawing?

**EDRIC:** There were wounded men inside.

**HART:** Understood.

**EDRIC:** The devil exploded.

The shield stopped most of it.

Most.

Dame Cwenheld remained standing until the fire cleared.

Then she asked whether the statue was damaged.

**HART:** Her own condition?

**EDRIC:** She was burning.

**HART:** Brother Edric.

**EDRIC:** Her mantle and the wig attached to her helmet had caught fire. Her armor was black. The spines were still in her shoulder and leg.

She asked about the statue first.

The raised wooden sword had broken from its hand.

Dame Cwenheld found one of the devil’s larger barbs in the wreckage.

She pushed it through the wooden sword and into the statue’s fist, fixing it back into place.

Chaplain Herward asked whether the barb should be removed when repairs were made.

She said:

> “No.”

Then she touched the scorch marks on the statue.

> “Leave those as well.”

**HART:** Did she explain why?

**EDRIC:** She said:

> “It came here to leave a mark.”

Then she sat down because her leg would no longer hold her.

---

### FINDINGS

1. The shrine suffered major canvas and frame damage but remained operational.
    
2. The portable statue remains structurally sound.
    
3. The statue’s sword is presently secured by a barb recovered from the destroyed infernal entity.
    
4. Dame Cwenheld refused treatment until the shrine guards and attendants had been examined.
    
5. The infernal barb and scorch marks are not to be removed without her permission.
    
6. Brother Edric has been excused from shrine duty for one day due to smoke inhalation, minor burns, and an entirely reasonable need to sit somewhere quiet.

# INCIDENT TWO: ATTACK UPON THE SOUTHERN LABOR LINES

**Location:** Laundry tents, field kitchens, and southern supply wagons  
**Enemy:** Approximately twelve Dominion infantry; one temple officer  
**Primary Defender:** Aisling, household servant attached to Lady Leena’s party  
**Witness:** Mistress Hella Dorn, civilian laundress  
**Statement taken by:** Lieutenant Garran Vale, Quartermaster Corps  
**Distribution:** Restricted

---

**LIEUTENANT VALE:** Begin with the landing.

**HELLA DORN:** One of the iron coffins came down beside the grain wagons.

It did not land properly.

It rolled.

Took the wheels off two carts and scattered burning coals through the laundry lines.

Everyone ran.

Well.

Almost everyone.

**VALE:** Aisling did not?

**HELLA:** She was walking in the opposite direction.

**VALE:** Toward the enemy?

**HELLA:** Toward us.

That woman did not care where the enemy was.

She was counting workers.

**VALE:** Explain.

**HELLA:** She moved from tent to tent, pulling people up, checking underneath wagons, sending them north in groups.

Not shouting.

Not panicking.

She would touch someone on the shoulder and say:

> “Go to the blue wagon.”

Or:

> “Take these two with you.”

And people did it.

Even soldiers.

**VALE:** Was she using magic?

**HELLA:** At that point I thought she was simply frightening.

**VALE:** Continue.

**HELLA:** The Dominion soldiers came out of the coffin.

Full armor. Short swords. Shields.

One of them saw the workers running and ordered his men to cut us off from the center of camp.

Aisling heard him.

She tied a knot in a piece of black thread around her finger.

**VALE:** Black thread.

**HELLA:** Yes.

The officer gave the order again.

No sound came out.

He kept shouting.

Nothing.

His own men began looking at him.

Aisling said:

> “There. That is quieter.”

Then she kept moving people.

**VALE:** What happened next?

**HELLA:** Three soldiers advanced on her.

The first raised his sword.

Aisling looked at him and said:

> “You have forgotten which hand is yours.”

He struck the man beside him.

Not deliberately.

His sword arm moved wrongly, like someone had pulled the string on a puppet.

The second soldier tried to stab her.

His feet would not move.

His upper body kept going.

He fell face-first into the mud.

The third reached her.

**VALE:** He injured her?

**HELLA:** He put a short sword through her stomach.

All the way through.

**VALE:** You are certain?

**HELLA:** I saw the point come out of her back.

**VALE:** Yet Aisling remains alive.

**HELLA:** That concerned him too.

She looked down at the sword.

Then at him.

She said:

> “You have put that in the wrong woman.”

She stepped closer.

The sword went farther through her.

She put one hand behind his neck and whispered something into his ear.

**VALE:** What?

**HELLA:** I did not hear.

He began screaming.

Not from anything she did with her hands.

She was barely touching him.

He dropped the sword and ran.

He ran directly into a burning wagon.

**VALE:** Did Aisling pursue him?

**HELLA:** No.

She pulled the sword out.

Handed it to a cook.

Then pressed both sides of the wound together.

By the time she reached the next tent, it had stopped bleeding.

**VALE:** Stopped bleeding or closed?

**HELLA:** Closed.

Mostly.

**VALE:** Mistress Dorn—

**HELLA:** You asked.

**VALE:** Continue.

**HELLA:** The temple officer saw it.

He pointed at her and said she was a witch.

Aisling answered:

> “Your education has not been entirely wasted.”

He ordered the soldiers to take her alive.

That is when the laundry lines became confusing.

**VALE:** Explain “confusing.”

**HELLA:** There were suddenly more tents.

Rows of them.

White canvas everywhere.

Workers running in six directions.

Wagons where no wagons had been.

I followed Aisling because she was the only thing that remained in the same place twice.

The Dominion soldiers did not follow her.

They kept attacking laundry.

One hacked apart a sheet for perhaps half a minute before realizing there was no one behind it.

Another ran directly between two wagons that were not actually far enough apart for him.

He became stuck.

**VALE:** Illusion magic.

**HELLA:** Likely.

The temple officer kept seeing through it.

Or enough of it.

He followed her.

He struck her across the back with something that burned black.

She fell.

I thought she was dead.

She told me:

> “Keep walking.”

**VALE:** While lying on the ground?

**HELLA:** Yes.

I kept walking.

When I looked back, she was standing again.

Her dress had split open across the back. The flesh underneath was burned down to the ribs.

Then it began growing back.

**VALE:** Rapidly?

**HELLA:** I would prefer another word.

There is not one.

**VALE:** Did Aisling transform?

**HELLA:** Into what?

**VALE:** Any visible alteration of shape. Animal characteristics. Increased size. Claws.

**HELLA:** No.

She remained a woman.

An increasingly irritated woman.

**VALE:** Continue.

**HELLA:** She led the temple officer between the grain wagons.

He knew she was doing it.

He followed anyway.

He said Hextor would hang her skin from his altar.

She said:

> “Men have promised me worse.”

Then they disappeared behind the wagons.

**VALE:** You followed?

**HELLA:** Not closely.

I had acquired judgment by then.

**VALE:** What did you hear?

**HELLA:** He laughed.

Then she said something I could not understand.

Then there was a sound like a large rug being beaten.

The officer screamed once.

After that I heard dragging.

**VALE:** Dragging in which direction?

**HELLA:** Away from the camp.

Toward the drainage ditch and the trees.

**VALE:** Did you see who was dragging whom?

**HELLA:** No.

**VALE:** Did you hear an animal?

**HELLA:** I heard breathing.

Large breathing.

But the smoke was thick, and I had been struck on the head.

**VALE:** Aisling returned afterward?

**HELLA:** Five minutes later.

**VALE:** In what condition?

**HELLA:** Human.

Same dress, though considerably less of it.

No visible wounds.

She was carrying the temple officer’s red-gauntlet insignia.

**VALE:** Did she explain what happened to him?

**HELLA:** She said:

> “He left the field.”

**VALE:** Did you ask for clarification?

**HELLA:** Lieutenant, I wash officers’ underclothes for money.

I am not paid enough to interrogate witches who recover from being disemboweled.

**VALE:** Fair.

**HELLA:** She returned the insignia to me and asked that it be given to whoever counted the dead.

Then she started organizing the bucket line.

**VALE:** She assisted with the fires?

**HELLA:** She ran it.

Half the soldiers obeyed her without knowing who she was.

When Sergeant Mallon asked under whose authority she was giving orders, she said:

> “Presently, mine.”

He accepted that.

**VALE:** Sergeant Mallon has confirmed this.

**HELLA:** Sensible man.

### ADDITIONAL WITNESS OBSERVATIONS

- Three workers separately reported seeing Dominion soldiers attack empty spaces or nonexistent tents.
    
- Two soldiers were found alive but unable to speak. Both recovered within one hour.
    
- One Dominion soldier was discovered trapped beneath a laundry frame he insisted was “a stone gate.”
    
- Aisling received at least four apparently serious injuries during the engagement:
    
    - penetrating abdominal wound;
        
    - severe burn across the back;
        
    - crossbow bolt through the neck;
        
    - deep cut to the left forearm.
        
- No injury remained visible at the time she was examined after the battle.
    
- Aisling denied being wounded.
    

When shown the bloodstained and burned remains of her dress, she stated:

> “Laundry is rarely kind to good fabric.”

---

### STATUS OF ENEMY TEMPLE OFFICER

The enemy temple officer has not been recovered.

Searchers found:

- his shield;
    
- one boot;
    
- blood;
    
- a broken length of black thread;
    
- drag marks continuing for approximately forty yards before ending abruptly.
    

No animal tracks were found.

This detail is not to be circulated among the general soldiery.

---

### EXAMINING OFFICER’S NOTE

Mistress Dorn appears sober, coherent, and irritated at being questioned repeatedly.

Her account is supported in part by seventeen workers, six soldiers, Sergeant Mallon, and the physical condition of the southern labor lines.

Aisling was asked to provide a statement.

She declined on the grounds that:

> “The quartermaster’s people have enough to do.”

No further questioning is recommended without authorization from the Lord Marshal, Maester Norwin, or someone personally prepared to explain why the answer is needed.

---

## DISPOSITION

The southern labor lines remained functional.

Seventy-three workers were evacuated from the immediate assault area.

Twenty-one wounded personnel were recovered from burning or collapsed tents.

Enemy losses are estimated at seven dead, four captured, and one missing.

Aisling resumed ordinary household duties before the fires were fully extinguished.

At evening meal, she was observed correcting the placement of Lady Leena’s cup.
