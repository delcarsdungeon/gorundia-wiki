# Sword-Chaplain Marek Valen Calls on Lyosha

It is late.

Late enough that the camp has settled into that false quiet which comes after battle.

Not peace.

Exhaustion.

Armor being unbuckled.  
Wounded men finally sleeping.  
Low voices around cookfires.  
Somewhere, someone is still hammering something back into a shape vaguely resembling military equipment.

Sword-Chaplain **Marek Valen** finds Lyosha with a folded bundle of rough pages tucked beneath one arm and a bottle of respectable red wine in the other.

No entourage.

No vestments beyond what he fought and prayed in today.

His surcoat still has dust on it.

He knocks against the doorframe with the bottle.

> "Dawnfather."

A small lift of the wine.

> "I bring tribute."

Then:

> "And congratulations."

Lyosha looks at the bottle.

Then at him.

Valen's mouth twitches.

> "I am told one of Hextor's devil-servants ceased being a theological problem tonight."

He makes a small gesture with one hand.

Not quite an explosion.

More an acknowledgment that **vaporized** is difficult to mime without looking like an idiot.

> "Very thoroughly."

He offers the bottle.

> "Good work."

No rivalry.

No edge.

He means it.

A priest of Heironeous is entirely comfortable recognizing excellent work performed upon a devil.

Once the wine changes hands, Valen pulls the papers from beneath his arm.

They are not a finished manuscript.

Not yet.

Different inks.  
Lines scratched out.  
Whole sentences jammed sideways into margins.  
One page has clearly been rewritten during a meal.

At the top:

## _The Brothers' Lesson_

Valen looks down at it.

There is some pride there.

But mostly concentration.

A craftsman who thinks he may finally have the shape right and wants another craftsman to look before he puts it in front of an army.

> "I am giving this tomorrow morning."

Lyosha looks at the title again.

Valen nods.

> "Been turning in my head since the arena."

A pause.

> "Tonight it finally found the rest."

He taps the pages.

> "The Dominion came because they think this is when we break."

His expression hardens.

> "I think they came because they are afraid we won't."

Then he looks directly at Lyosha.

> "That's the sermon."

A beat.

> "Or I hope it is."

He hands over the notes.

> "Before I preach it, I want another pair of eyes."

Then, deliberately:

> "Pelorite eyes."

That matters.

Valen takes the bottle back long enough to uncork it.

> "The brotherhood part is ours."

A little shrug.

> "Heironeous. Hextor. Beringer. Wilhelm."

Then he taps farther down the manuscript.

> "But this part isn't."

Lyosha reads.

> **The Dominion does not strike because we are weak.**
> 
> **It strikes because we are shedding weakness.**

Valen waits.

> "That's bigger than us."

He pours.

One cup for Lyosha.

One for himself.

> "Your people will hear hope in it."

A small nod toward Lyosha.

> "Pholtus will hear truth coming back into daylight."

Then, almost amused:

> "We will hear somebody getting back on his feet and reaching for a sword."

A sip.

> "Triad remains remarkably predictable."

He lets Lyosha read another few lines.

Then:

> "If I have spoken past mercy, tell me."

A pause.

> "If I have mistaken vengeance for courage, definitely tell me."

That one is utterly sincere.

No joke.

Because Marek Valen has been a priest too long and a soldier too long to confuse **feeling righteous** with **being right**.

Then the humor returns.

Just slightly.

> "If it is merely badly written, be gentle."

A beat.

> "I have been hit in head many times."

Lyosha looks up.

Valen realizes what he just said.

> "No. That's Radomir."

He sighs.

> "Long day."

He settles into a chair.

> "I preach at first assembly."

He raises his cup.

> "So."

A tiny gesture toward the manuscript.

> "Save me from embarrassing Heironeous before breakfast."

# The Brothers' Lesson

### Rough Homily of Sword-Chaplain Marek Valen

Heironeous and Hextor are brothers.

This we are taught from childhood.

But do not make the mistake of thinking their quarrel belongs only to the gods.

It is ours.

It is fought whenever strength must decide what purpose it serves.

Heironeous teaches that strength is given so that what is right may be defended.

Hextor teaches that strength itself determines what is right.

One raises the shield.

The other raises the fist.

And not long ago, before the eyes of this realm, two mortal brothers stood upon the same ground.

One came armored in fear.

Fear of losing station.

Fear of judgment.

Fear of a world in which obedience could no longer be forced from those beneath him.

The other carried something heavier.

**Duty.**

He did not enter because he hated his brother.

He did not enter because victory would bring him joy.

He entered because there comes a moment when refusing judgment becomes another form of cowardice.

And when the trial was finished, the righteous brother stood.

Do not mistake the lesson.

The lesson is not that righteous men always win.

The lesson is that righteous men must **stand**.

And now Hextor's servants have come against us.

Today they came in steel.

In sorcery.

In machines dragged through the sky.

They came while our own blood is still wet upon our fields.

And some will ask:

Why now?

Why when treason has wounded the Crown?

Why when brother has been forced to raise steel against brother?

Why when our houses are divided and our dead are not yet buried?

Because our enemies see what we are only beginning to see.

**Gorundia is standing up.**

The corrupt have been dragged into daylight.

Old lies have been named.

Old fears have been challenged.

Men and women taught to endure injustice are remembering that endurance is not obedience.

The frightened are finding courage.

The wounded are returning to their feet.

The faithful are remembering why faith was given to them.

The Dominion does not strike because we are weak.

**It strikes because we are shedding weakness.**

Hextor does not fear the man already crawling.

The tyrant does not fear the broken man.

He fears the man who has put one knee beneath himself.

Because the tyrant knows what comes next.

**He stands.**

And so must we.

Not because victory is promised.

Not because suffering is finished.

Not because the gods owe us an easy road.

We stand because strength is still ours to choose.

We stand because duty has not ceased merely because it has become costly.

We stand because there remain people behind us who cannot.

And if Hextor's servants have looked upon Gorundia and decided they must strike before we rise fully—

then let their fear be instructive.

They have seen what we are becoming.

Perhaps it is time we saw it too.

**By steel, we stand.**

## What Happens After Morning Service

If the homily lands the way Valen thinks it will, **then** it begins moving.

Copies first among the Heironean chaplains.

Then upward.

And when the High Sword sees it, _that_ is when the message comes back:

> **The High Sword himself has asked for copies.**

And, with all the perfectly innocent ecclesiastical courtesy three allied churches can muster, he intends to provide them to the **Solar Prelate of Pelor** and the **First Magistrix of the White Court**.

Not because Marek Valen has suddenly become impressed with himself.

Though he'll admit the sermon came out well.

What satisfies him is simpler.

Men walked into morning service tired, frightened, and wondering whether yesterday meant the war was getting worse.

They walked out understanding something different.

**The enemy hit them because they were worth stopping.**

For a campaign priest, that's a day's work done properly.
