# Vignette — The Trouble with Looking Elsewhere

Sophia nearly walks into a spear rack before breakfast.

Not quite.

Her hand catches the upright at the last moment. Six spears rattle together.

Every soldier nearby looks over.

Sophia releases the post, straightens her sleeve, and continues as though she had merely inspected it.

Radomir watches her go.

Then the rack.

Then Sophia again.

Kat Kendal frowns.

> “She nearly walked into that.”

Radomir nods once.

> “Da.”

Sophia passes the tent she plainly meant to enter.

Stops.

Looks at the wrong flap.

Turns back.

Kat’s frown deepens.

> “Is she ill?”

> “Maybe.”

> “What illness makes someone forget where a tent is?”

Radomir glances down at her.

> “Common one.”

> “I have never had it.”

> “No.”

A beat.

> “You walk directly into problems.”

Kat accepts that as reasonable.

---

By midday, Sophia has allowed tea to go cold, missed a question from a quartermaster, and nearly caught her boot under a tent rope.

Sophia Amadis does not miss questions.

She usually answers before lesser minds finish arranging them.

Now she stands with a folded letter in one hand, staring toward the western road.

Kat marches over.

> “Sophia.”

No response.

> “Sophia.”

Sophia turns too quickly.

> “Yes?”

Kat points down.

Sophia’s boot is resting against the rope.

> “That.”

Sophia looks at it.

> “I had seen it.”

> “You were about to fall.”

> “I had stopped.”

> “After walking into it.”

Radomir arrives carrying the peaceful expression of a man who has discovered entertainment at no personal cost.

> “Lady Sophia.”

Sophia narrows her eyes.

> “Radomir.”

> “You are distracted.”

> “I am not.”

Kat points back toward the crooked spear rack.

> “You attacked army equipment with your face.”

> “My face did not touch it.”

> “Hand saved face.”

Radomir nods gravely.

> “Good tactics.”

Sophia folds the letter again.

Far too carefully.

Radomir notices.

Kat notices him noticing.

> “What is that?”

> “A letter.”

> “I know what a letter is.”

> “Then the matter is settled.”

Radomir looks at the page.

> “Interesting letter.”

> “All correspondence may be interesting in context.”

> “This is yes wearing armor.”

Kat looks between them.

> “Why would a letter make her unable to walk?”

Radomir turns toward her.

> “Little kitten.”

Kat stiffens.

> “Do not.”

> “There are forces in world stronger than discipline.”

> “Magic?”

> “Close.”

Sophia closes her eyes.

> “You are enjoying this.”

> “Da.”

Kat points at the letter.

> “Who wrote it?”

Sophia says nothing.

Radomir’s mouth twitches.

> “Man.”

Kat looks immediately concerned.

> “Is he threatening you?”

> “No.”

> “Does he want something?”

Sophia hesitates.

> “Possibly supper.”

Kat waits.

> “That is all?”

> “At present.”

Kat turns to Radomir.

> “Why is that making her stupid?”

Sophia’s head snaps around.

> “I am not stupid.”

> “You nearly walked into six spears.”

> “Temporary distraction is not stupidity.”

Radomir gestures toward the letter.

> “Important distraction.”

Sophia gives him a look intended to strip bark from trees.

He remains unmoved.

---

Elizavetta finds Sophia later beneath the lee of a supply wagon.

Sophia is not hiding.

She has merely chosen somewhere no one presently requires her judgment.

The letter is open again.

> “You have read that before.”

Sophia looks up.

> “I was considering the phrasing.”

> “Six times?”

> “Were you counting?”

> “Radomir was.”

Sophia sighs and hands her the letter.

Elizavetta reads carefully.

At the final lines, her expression softens.

> “He answered you first.”

> “Yes.”

> “Before his father.”

> “So he says.”

> “Before approaching your family.”

> “Yes.”

Elizavetta returns the page.

> “He understood.”

Sophia’s thumb rests over Porthos’s name.

> “He is trying to.”

> “Better sign.”

Sophia looks toward the camp.

> “Katherine thinks the letter has made me stupid.”

> “Katherine does not understand romance.”

> “She has a terrible crush on Beringer.”

> “Also true.”

Sophia looks at her.

> “How can both things be true?”

> “Very easily, when no one explains anything to her.”

Sophia laughs despite herself.

Elizavetta smiles.

> “You are pleased.”

> “I am considering being pleased.”

> “You walked past your own tent.”

Sophia’s smile vanishes.

> “Radomir?”

> “Radomir.”

---

That evening Kat corners him beside the cook fires.

> “You told people.”

> “Told no one.”

> “They were laughing.”

> “Soldiers laugh.”

> “At me.”

> “Also common.”

Kat folds her arms.

> “You called me little kitten while explaining romance.”

> “I call you this often.”

> “Not while discussing madness.”

> “Not madness.”

> “Sophia gets letter. Sophia cannot walk.”

> “Temporary.”

> “Beringer writes me letters.”

Radomir’s face becomes very still.

> “Does he.”

> “Many.”

> “And afterward you walk into spears?”

> “No.”

> “Then letters lack something.”

Kat stares.

> “They contain orders.”

> “Ah.”

> “Patrol routes.”

> “Very passionate.”

> “Armor inspections.”

> “Scandalous.”

Kat realizes the trap.

Too late.

> “I do not have a crush on Beringer.”

> “Naturally.”

> “I admire him.”

> “Deeply.”

> “As knight.”

> “Most inconvenient part.”

> “And commander.”

> “Da.”

> “And person.”

Radomir leans slightly closer.

> “You understand this part very well, little kitten.”

Kat goes scarlet.

Then punches him in the arm.

Hard.

Radomir accepts the price of scholarship.

---

## Quiet Hours

Beringer notices Elizavetta before she comes to him.

A pale figure beside Sophia’s pavilion.

And Sophia, distracted all day.

He waits until the last reports are finished.

Elizavetta appears beside his tent.

Beringer does not look up immediately.

> “You’ve been floating around Sophia.”

> “Floating?”

> “Near enough.”

He sets the report aside.

> “Radomir saw you.”

> “Radomir sees too much.”

> “Kat saw Sophia nearly walk into a spear rack.”

Elizavetta smiles.

That confirms it.

> “What happened?”

She sits beside him.

> “Sophia received a letter.”

> “From Lyosha?”

> “No.”

A pause.

> “From Porthos.”

Beringer goes still.

> “The prince.”

> “Yes.”

He considers Sophia’s cold tea, missed tent, and distant expression.

> “Ah.”

> “Very restrained.”

> “I’m deciding whether I approve.”

> “You do.”

> “What did he write?”

> “He answered her directly. Before his father. Before approaching her family.”

Beringer’s expression shifts.

> “Good.”

> “He wants to see her again.”

> “Properly?”

> “Very.”

> “Better.”

> “He made clear that caution is not lack of interest.”

Beringer looks sideways.

> “How clear?”

Elizavetta’s mouth curves.

> “Clear enough to endanger camp furniture.”

A quiet breath escapes him. Nearly laughter.

> “She’s pleased.”

> “Very.”

That settles something.

Then his mind begins working.

Porthos.

Blackvale.

Ponzio’s former boon companion.

A stabilizing royal presence.

Elizavetta watches the calculations move across his face.

> “No.”

> “I said nothing.”

> “You began arranging Blackvale.”

> “He could help stabilize it.”

> “There.”

> “That is not interference.”

> “You lasted twelve seconds.”

> “I’m Lord Marshal.”

> “You are her brother.”

That stops him.

House Amadis had given him a place before asking what use he might be.

Sophia had never treated him as anything less than hers.

He nods.

> “Fair.”

Elizavetta rests a cool hand over his.

> “Let her have this before the kingdom finds a use for it.”

> “I won’t move anything.”

> “Good.”

> “Unless there is a security concern.”

> “Beringer.”

> “He is Crown Prince.”

> “He wrote a letter.”

He holds the argument another moment.

Then yields.

> “I’ll leave it alone.”

A pause.

> “For now.”

Across the camp, Kat’s voice rises:

> “I DO NOT HAVE A CRUSH ON HIM.”

Radomir answers:

> “Naturally, little kitten.”

Something metallic crashes.

Beringer closes his eyes.

> “What is happening to my army?”

Elizavetta leans against him, almost weightless.

> “Romance.”

> “Worse than cavalry.”
