# Vignette — The Second Draft Resupplies the Badger

The **Second Draft** came in low over the road with the calm arrogance of a thing that had no business floating and knew everyone below was too impressed to file a complaint.

Her shadow passed over wagons, tents, picket lines, stacked crates, and finally the crouched iron bulk of **the Badger**.

Every gnome on the ground looked up.

Every gnome aboard looked down.

For one clean, blessed heartbeat, discipline survived.

Then someone on the ground whispered:

> “Is that the revised aft-anchor bracing pattern?”

And someone aboard whispered back:

> “Is that a real Teenkdiwar pressure-balance drive casing?”

A third voice, somewhere near the Badger’s left foreleg, said:

> “Do you think they’d let us look at the condenser?”

A fourth, from the Second Draft’s forward rail:

> “Do you think they’d notice if we measured the suspension?”

The answer to both questions arrived at the same time.

From above:

> “Back from the rail.”

Captain **Jorven Hale** did not shout.

He did not stamp.

He did not perform naval theater for the benefit of clever men with insufficient survival instincts.

He simply stood at the top of the gangplank, one gloved hand resting on the rail, and looked along his engineering deck.

That was enough.

The nearest engineer went pale.

A measuring cord disappeared.

A sketchbook closed.

One gnome, already halfway into a climbing harness, slowly lowered both hands as if surrendering to a hostile god.

Jorven’s voice stayed mild.

> “No one leaves the ship until I give the order.”

A beat.

> “No one leans over the rail.”

Another beat.

> “No one drops a tool, a note, a probe, a question, or themselves onto Dame Venom’s camp.”

The engineers stiffened.

Jorven’s eyes moved once.

Slowly.

> “And if anyone says the words ‘purely observational inspection,’ I will personally assign them to bilge-valve inventory until they achieve enlightenment.”

Silence.

Beautiful, grieving silence.

Beside him, **Kelwin**, Norwin’s brother and senior engineer of the Second Draft, did not look up from the slate tucked under one arm.

> “Captain, with respect, enlightenment is unlikely.”

Jorven glanced aside.

Kelwin continued, dry as sawdust:

> “Bilge-valve inventory may produce humility, despair, or improved handwriting. But enlightenment feels optimistic.”

Jorven said:

> “Then we will settle for despair.”

> “A practical naval standard.”

Below, **Dame Venom** was doing the same work by different means.

She stood beside the Badger, one hand resting lightly on her sword, dark hair stirred by the downdraft. Her expression was serene.

That was the problem.

The senior sparkwright had been talking quickly.

Very quickly.

Very, very quickly.

Venom let him reach the phrase:

> “counter-rotational stabilizing clankshaft harmonics”

Then she looked at him.

Not sharply.

Not angrily.

Just looked.

The sparkwright stopped breathing.

Venom said:

> “Master Tindle.”

> “Yes, Dame Venom.”

> “If any member of your department touches that airship without permission, I will be disappointed.”

Master Tindle swallowed.

Somehow, this was worse than a threat.

> “Yes, Dame Venom.”

> “If any member of that airship’s crew touches the Badger without permission, you will not demonstrate excitement.”

> “No, Dame Venom.”

> “You will demonstrate restraint.”

> “Yes, Dame Venom.”

> “You may write questions.”

> “Thank you, Dame Venom.”

> “You may not ask them in groups larger than two.”

His face fell.

> “Yes, Dame Venom.”

From the gangplank, Kelwin watched this with professional interest.

> “Efficient.”

Jorven said:

> “Dame Venom?”

> “The threat architecture.”

Kelwin made a note.

> “Minimal language. Maximum compliance. Strong field applicability.”

Jorven’s mouth twitched.

> “Do not turn her into a procedure.”

> “Too late. Initial draft already forming.”

The gangplank dropped fully.

Jorven descended first.

Kelwin followed a few paces behind with the manifest slate, a tool case, and the weary expression of a gnome cursed by competence. His eyes moved across the Badger with visible hunger, but his boots stayed exactly where discipline put them.

A tragic thing, restraint.

Civilization had a lot to answer for.

Jorven was handsome in the irritating way of men who knew their boots were clean, their coat sat properly, and their jawline had been issued by some corrupt quartermaster with a taste for drama.

He moved like a naval officer: balanced, composed, certain. His eyes checked the camp without appearing to check it. Weapons. Hands. Routes. Wind. Ground. Gnome density.

Competent.

Annoyingly competent.

He reached Venom, removed one glove, and bowed with exact courtesy.

> “Dame Venom. Captain Jorven Hale, commanding the Second Draft under Captain Vaultforge’s authority.”

His voice was warm, confident, practiced.

> “We carry fuel, preserved rations, copper-thread braid, alchemical sealant, replacement couplings, two sealed crates of calibrated sparkwright parts, and three packages labeled in handwriting I choose to believe is legal.”

Venom offered her hand.

> “Captain Hale.”

He took it.

That was when the problem occurred.

Jorven Hale, captain in the Gorundian navy, second in command to Thalrik Vaultforge, currently responsible for an armed royal airship full of gnomes, refugees, volatile cargo, and at least three people whose inventions should probably be tried for conspiracy...

forgot the next sentence.

Not for long.

Not enough for anyone untrained to notice.

But Venom noticed.

Of course she noticed.

His eyes held on her face half a breath too long.

His confidence did not vanish.

It tripped.

Only slightly.

Like a duelist catching a heel on a carpet and pretending the floor had challenged him.

> “We are, ah—”

Kelwin looked up from his slate.

Venom remained perfectly polite.

Jorven recovered.

> “We are prepared to transfer supplies at your command. With your permission, my quartermaster and Master Kelwin will coordinate directly with your detachment’s stores officer.”

Venom gave him mercy by pretending nothing had happened.

> “Permission granted.”

A small pause.

> “Your restraint with your engineers is appreciated.”

Jorven’s expression steadied.

> “Their enthusiasm is a useful thing when pointed at an enemy engine.”

He glanced back toward the ship, where five engineers were visibly pretending not to stare at the Badger.

> “Less useful when pointed at allied property.”

Kelwin murmured:

> “A principle learned only after the invention of allied property.”

Venom’s eyes moved to him.

> “Master Kelwin.”

He inclined his head.

> “Dame Venom.”

> “Norwin’s brother.”

> “An accusation with supporting evidence.”

Jorven said:

> “Master Kelwin is senior engineer aboard the Second Draft.”

Kelwin added:

> “Which means I am responsible for ensuring the ship does not explode, descend, catch fire, invert, argue with gravity, or become an educational opportunity.”

Venom’s gaze slid briefly toward the ship.

> “A demanding post.”

> “The ship is manageable.”

Kelwin looked toward the engineering crew, then down at the sparkwrights gathered near the Badger.

> “The people are the hostile environment.”

Venom almost smiled.

Almost.

Jorven noticed that too.

Damn him.

There it was again.

A fraction.

Not flirtation.

Not yet.

Just surprise.

Venom knew the shape of it well enough: the sudden recalculation of a confident man who had expected a capable knight and found something sharper, calmer, and far more dangerous to his concentration.

It was flattering.

In another life, perhaps it might even have been amusing.

But her heart was already spoken for.

And that truth sat in her like a drawn blade.

So she gave him professionalism.

Clean, polite, merciful.

> “Captain Hale, your supplies are welcome. Your ship is under your authority. The Badger is under mine.”

> “Understood.”

> “Your people may observe from ten paces.”

> “Generous.”

> “They may ask questions through Master Kelwin.”

Kelwin shut his eyes.

> “Punished for excellence. Again.”

Venom continued:

> “They may not climb, sketch internal housings, take rubbings, test resonance, tap armor plates, measure stress seams, or say the word ‘quickly’ while holding tools.”

Jorven gave a small nod.

> “Reasonable.”

Kelwin muttered:

> “Cruel, but accurate.”

Jorven turned slightly.

> “Mister Harrow.”

The engineering chief snapped straight.

> “Captain.”

> “Ten paces.”

> “Aye, Captain.”

> “Hands visible.”

> “Aye, Captain.”

> “Questions through Master Kelwin.”

The engineering chief’s face fell with the expression of a man condemned to bureaucracy.

> “Aye, Captain.”

Kelwin lifted one finger.

> “I accept bribes in tea, batteries, and silence.”

Venom said:

> “No bribes.”

Kelwin lowered the finger.

> “I accept moral disappointment in bulk.”

The work began.

Ropes came down.

Crates followed.

Ground crews moved in careful lanes. Airship crewmen carried loads with naval rhythm, while Venom’s sparkwrights received them under the tense, holy suffering of people forbidden to ask forty-seven questions at once.

Kelwin positioned himself between the two gnomish populations like a tired customs officer at the border of Bad Ideas.

A sparkwright tried to edge past him.

Kelwin did not look up.

> “No.”

> “I was only—”

> “No.”

> “But the venting profile—”

> “Still no.”

> “You do not even know what I was going to ask.”

Kelwin finally looked at him.

> “You were going to ask whether the Second Draft’s auxiliary lift envelope uses a hot-cycle bleed or a cold ether vent.”

The sparkwright stared.

Kelwin tapped his slate.

> “It does.”

> “It does which?”

> “Yes.”

The sparkwright’s face went through awe, fury, grief, and professional respect in under three seconds.

Kelwin nodded once.

> “Good. Now carry that crate.”

Nearby, Jorven remained beside Venom, watching the transfer.

After a while, he said:

> “Your people are disciplined.”

Venom watched one young gnome physically turn another away from the Second Draft’s exposed intake vent.

> “They are motivated.”

> “By duty?”

> “By duty.”

A pause.

> “And fear.”

Jorven smiled despite himself.

> “Efficient combination.”

> “Gorundian standard.”

That drew a quiet laugh from him.

Then he looked at her again.

Too directly.

Not improper.

Just honest before he had meant to be.

> “Admiral Vaultforge described you.”

Venom’s expression remained still.

> “That was unwise of him.”

> “He undersold you.”

There it was.

Kelwin, who had absolutely been listening while pretending to count sealant tins, shut his eyes briefly.

> “And now we begin taking on water.”

Jorven’s jaw tightened.

Not anger.

Embarrassment.

Venom spared him again.

> “I believe Admiral Vaultforge may undersell everyone who is not Admiral Vaultforge.”

Jorven caught the rope she offered.

Gratefully.

> “That is true.”

> “And oversells anything he has personally touched.”

Kelwin added:

> “Including ideas, ships, dice, drinks, excuses, and once, I am told, a goat.”

Jorven looked at him.

> “The goat was a misunderstanding.”

Kelwin’s eyes narrowed.

> “No one ever says that when it was truly a misunderstanding.”

Venom’s gaze moved across the transfer lines.

The supplies were nearly clear.

Order had held.

Barely.

Aboard the Second Draft, the engineers were vibrating in place like sealed kettles.

On the ground, the sparkwrights were doing the same thing, but shorter.

Human civilization, such as it was, had survived another hour.

Jorven drew himself back into full command.

Whatever had passed through him when he first took Venom’s hand, he locked it down properly now.

A good officer.

A disciplined man.

That made the stumble kinder, somehow.

He bowed again.

> “Dame Venom. We will remain aloft within signal range until your quartermaster confirms the count.”

> “Thank you, Captain Hale.”

> “If you require further support, the Second Draft will answer.”

Venom inclined her head.

> “I will remember.”

A softer beat followed.

Not romantic.

Not cold either.

Just the narrow place where courtesy had to carry what honesty could not.

Jorven understood enough not to press.

Perhaps he had heard the stories.

Perhaps he had simply looked at her and realized there was already someone standing in the room with her, even absent.

He put his glove back on.

> “Then I will leave your camp intact.”

Kelwin looked toward the two groups of gnomes.

> “Ambitious.”

Jorven turned toward the gangplank.

> “Mister Harrow.”

> “Captain?”

> “Anyone still staring when I reach the deck loses evening privileges.”

Every engineer aboard the Second Draft instantly became fascinated by rope, cloud, horizon, bootlaces, and the abstract concept of obedience.

Venom looked to Master Tindle.

> “Master Tindle.”

The sparkwright straightened.

> “Dame Venom?”

> “Anyone still staring when Captain Hale reaches the deck gets assigned to inventory washers by diameter.”

Every sparkwright on the ground suddenly discovered urgent logistical duties.

Kelwin made another note.

Venom looked at him.

> “What?”

> “Washer discipline. Useful phrase.”

> “Do not credit me.”

> “Too late. I use citations.”

The two commanders exchanged a final look.

Quiet.

Calm.

Mutually approving.

Then Jorven Hale went back up the gangplank with his confidence restored.

Mostly.

Kelwin followed him halfway, then paused beside Venom.

Without looking at her, he said:

> “Well.”

Venom’s eyes stayed on the ship.

> “Do not.”

> “I said nothing.”

> “Continue.”

Kelwin clasped his slate to his chest.

> “I merely observe that Captain Hale appears to have survived first contact with Dame Venom.”

A beat.

> “Though I recommend checking him later for structural fatigue.”

Venom finally looked at him.

Kelwin immediately became very interested in the supply manifest.

> “Inventory washers by diameter,” he said.

> “On it.”

Above them, the Second Draft rose.

Below, the Badger crouched in the dust.

And between the two marvels of gnomish engineering, order somehow held.

Not because anyone was less curious.

That would be absurd.

But because Dame Venom and Captain Jorven Hale had both made it very clear that curiosity was welcome...

and consequences were scheduled.
