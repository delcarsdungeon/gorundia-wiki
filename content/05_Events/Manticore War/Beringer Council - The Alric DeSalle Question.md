# Beringer Council - The Alric DeSalle Question

In-game date: 10 Coldeven, 1231 OR

Rain ticked against the canvas.

Inside Beringer’s command tent, the map table filled most of the space. Knives held the corners flat. A single lantern swung from the ridge pole, throwing gold over wet cloaks, mail, and tired faces.

Beringer sat at the head.

Baron Jareth DeSalle sat stiff-backed to his right, gloved hands folded before him.

Baron Kaelric Vorthal leaned forward, already watching the roads on the map as if one of them might stand up and challenge him.

Baron Stanislav Vorokov sat opposite Jareth, immaculate despite the weather, one hand around an untouched cup of wine. He had the mild, polished expression of a man who could insult three bloodlines before breakfast and leave everyone thanking him for the lesson.

Behind him stood Radovan Belik.

Helmet tucked under one arm. Long pale hair tied back. Scarred face still. He did not sit. He did not fidget. He looked like cavalry doctrine had learned to stand upright.

Radomir Stonehand stood near the tent wall, silent, broad-shouldered, and watchful. When Belik entered, Radomir had gone a little straighter. Not much. Enough.

Sophia Amadis sat near the side table with ink, sand, and a clean stack of paper. She was taking notes with the grave restraint of a woman choosing, moment by moment, not to improve everyone in the room by force.

Beringer let the tent settle.

Then he looked to Jareth.

“Your brother.”

Jareth’s jaw tightened.

“Sir Alric.”

“Your brother,” Beringer said again.

Jareth held his gaze, then nodded.

“Yes.”

“I need to know what he does when this closes around him.”

Jareth looked down at the map.

“My brother is proud.”

No one interrupted.

“Too proud, sometimes. He always was. Alric never learned the trick of bending before the branch cracks.”

“Will he crack?” Beringer asked.

“No.”

That came too quickly to be anything but true.

Then Jareth added:

“Not unless someone makes him choose between honor and survival.”

Beringer watched him.

“And if someone does?”

“Then he chooses honor.”

Kaelric’s expression sharpened slightly.

Vorokov noticed. His mouth curved.

Beringer stayed on Jareth.

“Is he loyal to Vogel?”

“He is loyal to his oath.”

“That was not my question.”

“It is the answer.”

Sophia’s pen paused.

“Hm.”

Barely a sound.

Beringer heard it anyway.

The rain filled the pause.

Jareth’s voice lowered.

“Alric took Vogel’s service because it was offered under law, under seal, and under the authority our father told us all our lives was sacred.”

A thin bitterness entered his voice.

“Our father wore virtue the way some men wear scent. Strong enough to enter a room before him. Thin enough to wash off when inconvenient.”

Vorokov’s smile faded a shade.

Jareth continued.

“Alric and I both wanted to be the kind of men Remy DeSalle pretended to be.”

That cost him.

Not visibly much.

Enough.

“My brother took service under Duke Vogel because he had sworn himself to it. He wore the manticore because it was his lord’s badge, not because it was his soul. His blood is DeSalle. The serpent is not so easily mistaken.”

A pause.

“He is a man of oaths. Not a fool.”

Beringer’s eyes narrowed.

“Then what is he?”

“A knight.”

Jareth said it plainly.

“A stubborn one. A proud one. One who will not throw down his sword because a stronger man tells him history has shifted beneath his boots.”

Vorokov glanced toward Kaelric.

“Careful, Ravenster-boy. You begin to look hungry.”

Kaelric did not look away from Jareth.

“Will he fight?” Beringer asked.

“If cornered.”

“Will he surrender?”

“No.”

Then Jareth lifted one hand slightly.

“But he may yield.”

Kaelric looked up.

Vorokov leaned back.

Belik did not move.

Beringer asked, “Explain the difference.”

“Surrender tells him he was wrong.”

“And yielding?”

“Yielding lets him admit the field has changed.”

Jareth drew a breath.

“If you send men at him under accusation, he will meet them as enemies. If you offer lawful custody under noble witness, with his men’s lives preserved and his honor held pending judgment, he may accept it.”

“May.”

“Yes.”

Jareth’s eyes hardened.

“He will not white-flag like a frightened merchant. He will not order his men to die proving a point, either. Alric is proud. Not wasteful.”

Vorokov turned his head slightly.

“Belik.”

Belik’s eyes moved.

“Proud. Not wasteful,” Vorokov said. “Your thought?”

Belik answered without changing posture.

“Good.”

A beat.

“Smart proud man can be frightened. Stupid proud man must be buried.”

Radomir’s chin lifted a fraction.

Not agreement.

Recognition.

Kaelric looked back at Belik.

Belik met his eye.

“Is true.”

Beringer ignored the exchange.

“If Vogel ordered him to hold at all cost?”

Jareth answered at once.

“Then he holds until the order becomes unlawful, impossible, or dishonorable.”

“That leaves blood between here and there.”

“Yes.”

“Then we shorten the road.”

Jareth nodded once.

“That is why you asked.”

Vorokov touched the edge of the map with one elegant finger.

“If you want no blood, then yes. Terms. Witnesses. Careful words.”

His smile returned.

“If you want resistance broken, I have brought more direct instrument.”

Belik stepped closer only after Vorokov gave the faintest nod.

He pointed to the road.

“They hold here?”

Jareth looked at the map.

“Yes.”

Belik nodded.

“Front is obvious. So they watch front.”

His finger moved.

“One troop wide through low ground. No banner. No trumpet. Cut messenger path. Loose horses. Noise behind them.”

He tapped the main road.

“Second troop shows front. Slow. Lances down. Not charge. Let them think.”

Another tap, sharper.

“Then flank. Break shield edge. Take officers alive if smart. Quiet if not.”

Kaelric’s brows rose.

“That is not a duel.”

Belik looked at him.

“No.”

A pause.

“Is victory.”

Vorokov laughed softly.

Jareth’s voice stayed controlled.

“And every Vogel man watching will learn that yielding to us means humiliation before horsemen.”

Belik shrugged.

“Only if done badly.”

“Crushing men already willing to hear terms is doing it badly.”

“Maybe.”

Belik’s eyes stayed flat.

“Or maybe they want time.”

Jareth had no answer for that.

Sophia wrote that down.

Slowly.

Kaelric leaned both forearms on the table.

“There is another way.”

Vorokov sipped his wine at last.

“Now Ravenster-boy comes to altar.”

Kaelric ignored him.

“Jareth is right. His brother needs a way to refuse before he yields. A public shape. A form men understand. Not surrender. Not rout.”

He looked to Beringer.

“A duel answers that.”

Jareth’s mouth tightened, but he did not object.

Kaelric continued.

“Terms declared before witnesses. If Sir Alric stands, crosses steel, and yields after honorable exchange, his men can lay down arms without shame.”

Belik said, “And if he kills your champion?”

Kaelric smiled.

“Then choose the champion carefully.”

Vorokov’s eyes brightened.

“There it is. I knew you would find way to volunteer without using word.”

“I am serious,” Kaelric said.

“So am I,” Vorokov said. “It is what makes this charming.”

Kaelric kept his voice even.

“This is what a duel is for. Settlement. Pressure released through one wound instead of a hundred.”

Sophia made a small sound.

“Hmph.”

Kaelric looked briefly toward her.

Sophia did not look up from her notes.

Beringer did not smile.

Jareth studied Kaelric.

“You would face Alric?”

“If asked.”

“He is no fool with a sword.”

“Good.”

Jareth’s eyes narrowed.

Kaelric’s tone softened.

“If he were a fool, this would not work. A fool turns a duel into theater. A proper knight knows when steel has spoken enough.”

Belik muttered:

“Proper knights. Many rules.”

Kaelric looked back.

“That is the point.”

Belik’s mouth twitched.

“Tragic.”

Beringer lifted one hand.

The tent quieted.

He looked back to Jareth.

“Could you bring him in?”

Jareth held still.

“Yes.”

No hesitation.

Then:

“If you ask it of me.”

“But?”

“It may complicate things.”

Vorokov set his cup down.

“My brother will hear me,” Jareth said. “He may even trust me. That is the useful part.”

“And the other part?”

“I am his elder brother. His baron. Our father’s heir. The man who now bears the serpent while he stands beneath the manticore.”

He paused.

“That is a great many chains to drag into one conversation.”

Beringer waited.

“If I ride to him and tell him to yield, he may do it. But every man watching will see Alric DeSalle brought to heel by his brother.”

His mouth tightened.

“And Alric will know they saw it.”

“So he refuses.”

“Not necessarily.”

Jareth met his eyes.

“He may need a way to refuse first.”

Kaelric said, “A duel.”

Jareth nodded.

“A challenge. A token fight. Formal resistance witnessed by men who understand what they are seeing.”

Vorokov looked amused.

“Blood for pride?”

Jareth shook his head.

“Blood for order. Pride is cheaper.”

Belik gave a small nod.

Jareth continued.

“If Alric is given terms, lawful custody, protection for his men, judgment reserved to proper authority, he can accept that. But not if acceptance looks like fear. Not if it looks like I came to fetch him home by the ear.”

Beringer asked, “And if I send someone else?”

“Then he can stand against that man without making it a family shame.”

“Someone he respects?”

“Someone he cannot dismiss.”

Kaelric looked at the map.

Jareth did not look at him.

“Someone who will not sneer.”

Vorokov glanced over his shoulder.

Belik said, “I am out.”

Kaelric almost smiled.

Belik remained solemn.

“I know myself.”

Vorokov murmured, “For once, tragedy is self-aware.”

Jareth pressed on.

“Alric will not throw his men away. But he will spend his own blood if that is the price of keeping their respect.”

“So we give him a price he can pay,” Beringer said.

“Yes.”

Kaelric spoke carefully.

“Terms before the bout. Publicly. His men hear them. Our men hear them. No trap.”

Jareth nodded.

“If he yields after honorable exchange, his men stand down under protection. No stripping of arms in mockery. No chains unless law later requires them. His officers retain dignity pending judgment. The common men are not punished for obeying their knight.”

Sophia’s pen stopped again.

“Hm.”

This time, approval.

Probably.

Vorokov tapped the table once.

“And if they do not stand down?”

Beringer looked at him.

Vorokov said, “Then Belik becomes useful.”

Belik’s eyes warmed a fraction.

Jareth said, “After the offer is refused. Not before.”

Vorokov inclined his head.

“First hand open.”

Belik said, “Then fist.”

Kaelric looked to Beringer.

“If you mean to set the duel, set the field cleanly. Boundaries. Witnesses. Physicians. Cleric if one can be spared. No missile weapons. No second entering unless terms are broken.”

Belik said, “Many rules.”

Kaelric answered, “That is why it is not murder.”

“Murder is simpler.”

“Yes.”

Vorokov smiled.

“I do enjoy when everyone is correct and unhappy.”

Beringer let the rain fill one breath.

“No crushing unless terms fail.”

Vorokov nodded.

“As you command.”

Belik stood straighter.

“And the duel?” Kaelric asked.

Beringer looked from Jareth to Kaelric, then to Vorokov and Belik.

“We offer terms first.”

Jareth nodded.

“We offer a duel to give those terms a shape.”

Kaelric’s expression settled into calm readiness.

Belik said, “Dinner bell.”

Kaelric ignored him.

“Jareth carries the terms,” Beringer said. “He does not command his brother. He does not beg him. He stands witness.”

Jareth bowed his head once.

“Kaelric prepares the field.”

“My lord.”

“Vorokov holds the riders ready.”

Vorokov nodded.

“And Belik prepares the answer if Alric refuses.”

Belik’s grin appeared and vanished.

“As ordered.”

Vorokov added, “This means he is pleased.”

Belik said, “Very pleased.”

“You hide it beautifully.”

“Practice.”

Beringer’s voice hardened.

“No one mocks Alric. No one mocks his men. No one calls it surrender before them. If Alric yields, he yields under protection. If he refuses after lawful offer and honorable satisfaction, every man watching will know what follows was chosen.”

The tent settled.

Jareth bowed his head.

“My lord, give him a lawful road to walk. If he chooses blood after that, let it be his choice.”

Then, quieter:

“But I do not think he will spend his men merely to prove he has a spine.”

A bitter smile touched his mouth.

“Father would have.”

Beringer said nothing.

Jareth’s smile faded.

“Alric won’t.”

Kaelric stood.

“I will see to the ground.”

Vorokov rose more slowly, smoothing one sleeve.

“I will see that riders are ready and disappointed.”

Belik said, “Not disappointed.”

Vorokov looked back.

“Deferred, then.”

Belik considered.

“Better.”

Radomir remained silent.

But when Belik spoke, his posture held that same extra measure of attention.

Belik noticed.

He gave Radomir the smallest nod.

Radomir returned it, deeper.

No one commented.

Sophia wrote anyway.

Beringer looked to Jareth.

“You will carry the words.”

“Yes.”

Jareth paused.

“One more thing.”

Beringer waited.

“Do not call him mistaken.”

The rain ticked against the canvas.

“Call him bound.”

Kaelric nodded once.

Vorokov’s expression lost its amusement.

Belik went very still.

Sophia did not write for several seconds.

Beringer said, “Bound men can be released.”

Jareth bowed.

“If the hand on the knot is steady.”

Belik spoke softly.

“And if not?”

Jareth looked at him.

“Then we learn which of us came here for honor, and which came only for victory.”

Belik held his gaze.

Then nodded once.

“Good.”

Sophia’s pen moved again.

Carefully.

As if this part mattered.
