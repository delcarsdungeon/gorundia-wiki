# AFTER-ACTION WITNESS STATEMENT

## INCIDENT: ATTACK UPON LADY SOPHIA’S HOUSEHOLD AND THE SOUTHERN LABOR LINES

**Location:** Southern supply quarter, adjoining Lady Sophia’s field household
**Enemy:** Approximately twelve Dominion infantry; one Hextorian temple officer
**Primary Defender:** Aisling, presently serving within Lady Sophia’s household
**Household Guard:** Dame Koriel Targost, Shieldmaiden of House Amadis and veteran of Red Gorge
**Witness:** Marta Eselin, laundress and wife of Corporal Jannik Eselin, Fourth Spear Company
**Statement taken by:** Truthseeker Halvern, Order of Pholtus
**Status:** Restricted pending determination of magical agency

---

**TRUTHSEEKER HALVERN:** State your name.

**MARTA ESELIN:** Marta Eselin. My husband is Corporal Jannik Eselin, Fourth Spear. I work the laundry and help in Lady Sophia’s tents when they need hands.

**HALVERN:** You were attached to Lady Sophia’s household?

**MARTA:** No, sir. Not properly.

Aisling was.

Or had made herself so.

**HALVERN:** By whose appointment?

**MARTA:** I do not know.

I do not think anyone appointed her.

She simply began helping Lady Sophia, and after the first day it seemed she had always been there.

**HALVERN:** Lady Sophia accepted this?

**MARTA:** Lady Sophia accepts very little quietly.

But she accepted Aisling.

Dame Koriel did too.

**HALVERN:** Dame Koriel Targost?

**MARTA:** Yes, sir. Lady Sophia’s Shieldmaiden.

**HALVERN:** A veteran of Red Gorge.

**MARTA:** She carries herself like one.

She is not young, but I never saw her move as though she had forgotten how to fight.

Only as though she knew exactly how much of herself each fight deserved.

**HALVERN:** How did she regard Aisling’s presence?

**MARTA:** With relief, I think.

Aisling knew where Lady Sophia had left things. She remembered which messages had been answered, which meals she had refused, and when she had slipped away from her escort.

Dame Koriel would ask whether Lady Sophia was where she claimed to be.

Aisling would tell her where she actually was.

Neither seemed surprised by the other.

They worked well together.

**HALVERN:** Continue with the attack.

**MARTA:** The iron vessel came down beside the grain wagons.

It struck the earth badly and rolled. It tore the wheels from two carts and scattered burning coals through the laundry tents.

People began running.

Aisling was carrying folded linens toward Lady Sophia’s pavilion.

She put them down.

Very neatly.

Then she began counting us.

**HALVERN:** Counting?

**MARTA:** The workers.

She moved from tent to tent and called each person by name. Even those she had only met that morning.

She found a kitchen girl beneath a wagon. She lifted an old groom who had broken his ankle. She sent the children toward the blue-painted water cart and told me to keep them together.

She was not shouting.

But everyone heard her.

Dame Koriel came from the pavilion with her shield raised and her sword drawn.

She asked:

> “Where is she?”

Aisling answered:

> “With the surgeons. She will remain there if you keep the road open.”

Dame Koriel looked once toward the enemy, then toward the workers.

> “You have them?”

> “I have them.”

Dame Koriel nodded.

Then she went to hold the road.

They did not need to discuss it further.

**HALVERN:** The enemy emerged from the vessel at this time?

**MARTA:** Yes.

Full armor. Red gauntlets. Short swords and shields.

One of their officers saw us trying to reach the center of camp. He ordered his men to cut us off.

Aisling tied a piece of black thread around her finger.

**HALVERN:** You saw the thread clearly?

**MARTA:** Yes, sir.

She pulled the knot tight.

The officer gave another order.

No sound came from him.

**HALVERN:** A spell of silence.

**MARTA:** Perhaps.

He was shouting. I could see that.

His men could not hear him.

Aisling said:

> “Keep moving. Do not look at them.”

So we kept moving.

**HALVERN:** You believe Aisling caused the silence?

**MARTA:** I believe she saved us.

**HALVERN:** That was not the question.

**MARTA:** No, sir.

But it is the truth.

**HALVERN:** Continue.

**MARTA:** Three soldiers came toward her.

The first raised his sword.

Aisling looked at his hand.

His arm twisted aside and struck the shield of the man beside him.

The second tried to run at her. His feet caught as though roots had closed around his boots. He fell hard enough to lose his sword.

The third reached her.

He stabbed her.

**HALVERN:** Describe the wound.

**MARTA:** The sword went through her stomach.

I saw the blade come out behind her.

**HALVERN:** Could armor have stopped it?

**MARTA:** She wore no armor.

She wore a servant’s dress.

**HALVERN:** What happened then?

**MARTA:** She looked down at the blade.

Then she looked at the soldier.

She did not seem angry.

That frightened him more than anger would have.

She put her hand over his wrist and said something quietly.

I could not hear it.

The soldier released his weapon.

He backed away from her.

Then he turned and ran.

**HALVERN:** Under magical compulsion?

**MARTA:** I do not know.

He looked as though he had remembered something terrible.

**HALVERN:** Aisling still had the blade through her?

**MARTA:** Yes.

She pulled it free herself.

There was blood down the front of her dress.

She put one hand against the wound and told us to stay together.

**HALVERN:** Did she cast a healing prayer?

**MARTA:** Not that I heard.

**HALVERN:** Did she invoke a god?

**MARTA:** No.

**HALVERN:** Make a sign of faith?

**MARTA:** No, sir.

She simply kept walking.

By the time she reached the next wagon, the blood had slowed.

By the time she lifted the wounded groom again, it had stopped.

**HALVERN:** The wound had closed?

**MARTA:** Yes.

**HALVERN:** Entirely?

**MARTA:** I saw torn cloth and unbroken skin beneath it.

**HALVERN:** You understand why that raises questions of wizardry.

**MARTA:** I understand that she should have died.

**HALVERN:** Witchcraft, then.

**MARTA:** No.

**HALVERN:** You answer quickly.

**MARTA:** Because I was there.

Witches in stories curse children and sour milk.

Aisling carried our children through fire.

**HALVERN:** Stories are not doctrine, Mistress Eselin.

**MARTA:** Neither is calling every mercy witchcraft because it frightened you.

**HALVERN:** Take care.

**MARTA:** I am trying to, sir.

**HALVERN:** Continue your statement.

**MARTA:** More soldiers entered the laundry lines.

Then the tents began changing.

Not truly changing, perhaps.

There were more rows than there should have been. More wagons. Paths bent back upon themselves.

The Dominion soldiers chased people who were not there.

One hacked at an empty sheet as if a man stood behind it.

Another tried to pass between two wagons and struck something solid where there was only air.

But the workers could still see Aisling.

She stood at the end of each safe path.

When we reached her, she pointed us toward the next.

**HALVERN:** A controlled illusion affecting selected subjects.

**MARTA:** Is that wizardry?

**HALVERN:** Most likely.

**MARTA:** Then wizardry led us out.

**HALVERN:** Magic has no moral character of its own.

**MARTA:** The person using it does.

**HALVERN:** Continue.

**MARTA:** The temple officer saw through enough of it to follow her.

He carried a black iron staff. When he struck her across the back, the light from it burned through her dress.

She fell.

I saw bone.

**HALVERN:** You are certain?

**MARTA:** Her ribs were bare.

She turned her head toward me and said:

> “Marta. Take the children to the water cart.”

She knew my name.

I had never told it to her.

**HALVERN:** Had another member of the household used it?

**MARTA:** Perhaps.

I took the children.

When I looked back, she was standing again.

**HALVERN:** How much time had passed?

**MARTA:** A few breaths.

The burned flesh was closing.

Not all at once.

Slowly enough that I could see it becoming whole.

**HALVERN:** Did light accompany the healing?

**MARTA:** No.

**HALVERN:** Warmth? Prayer? A sacred symbol?

**MARTA:** Nothing I could name.

But the little ones stopped crying when they saw her rise.

One of them said Heironeous had sent her.

**HALVERN:** Aisling does not bear the mark of Heironeous.

**MARTA:** A child does not know heraldry.

She knew someone had stood back up for her.

**HALVERN:** And what did you believe?

**MARTA:** I thought perhaps the gods had remembered that servants were in the camp too.

**HALVERN:** That is not evidence of divine intervention.

**MARTA:** No, sir.

It is only what I thought.

**HALVERN:** Where was Dame Koriel during this?

**MARTA:** At the road.

The Dominion soldiers tried three times to break through.

She stopped them each time.

She fought behind her shield and gave ground only when the workers had cleared the space behind her.

There was no shouting from her.

Only orders.

> “Back.”

> “Close.”

> “Move the wounded.”

Once, I saw a younger soldier rush past her toward the children.

Dame Koriel caught him with the edge of her shield and put him on the ground.

She did not look down to see whether he rose.

She already knew he would not.

**HALVERN:** Was she wounded?

**MARTA:** Several times.

She had a cut over one eye and blood beneath her armor.

It did not seem to change anything.

She had survived Red Gorge.

I think the Dominion men understood that before I did.

**HALVERN:** What happened to the temple officer?

**MARTA:** Aisling drew him away from us.

**HALVERN:** Deliberately?

**MARTA:** Yes.

She let him see her between the grain wagons.

He followed.

He called her an abomination.

He said Hextor would reveal what she truly was.

Aisling said:

> “You have seen enough.”

Then she stepped behind the wagons.

The officer followed her.

**HALVERN:** Did you observe the confrontation?

**MARTA:** No.

Dame Koriel reached us then.

She had blood on her sword and a cut across her face.

She asked where Aisling had gone.

I told her.

She looked toward the grain wagons for a long moment.

**HALVERN:** Did she pursue?

**MARTA:** No.

She said:

> “She knows her work.”

Then she turned back to the wounded.

**HALVERN:** You heard nothing further?

**MARTA:** Fighting.

The wagons shook once.

Then there was silence.

**HALVERN:** Aisling returned?

**MARTA:** Later.

I cannot say how long.

The fires made time difficult.

Her dress was torn and burned. There was blood on her sleeves, but I could find no wound on her.

She carried the temple officer’s red-gauntlet chain.

**HALVERN:** Did she state where he was?

**MARTA:** She gave the chain to Dame Koriel.

Dame Koriel asked:

> “Dead?”

Aisling answered:

> “Gone.”

Dame Koriel looked at her for a moment.

Then she closed her hand around the chain and said:

> “Good.”

**HALVERN:** No further explanation?

**MARTA:** No.

**HALVERN:** And afterward?

**MARTA:** Aisling returned to the workers.

She organized the bucket line. She showed the cooks how to smother the grain fire. She knelt beside a boy whose mother was missing and held his hands until she was found.

Dame Koriel remained beside her for a little while.

She leaned on her shield when she thought no one was looking.

Aisling noticed.

She brought her water and said:

> “Sit.”

Dame Koriel said:

> “Sophia.”

Aisling answered:

> “Is with the surgeons, exactly where we left her.”

Dame Koriel sat.

Only then.

When Lady Sophia finally arrived, she demanded to know why Aisling had left the pavilion and why Dame Koriel had allowed her near the fighting.

Dame Koriel looked at Aisling.

Aisling looked at Dame Koriel.

Neither answered immediately.

Lady Sophia said:

> “I am speaking to both of you.”

Dame Koriel finally said:

> “My lady, the camp was attacked.”

Lady Sophia replied:

> “I had noticed.”

Aisling handed her a cup of water.

Lady Sophia took it without thinking.

Then the three of them began helping with the wounded.

**HALVERN:** Aisling resumed the role of servant?

**MARTA:** I do not think she had ever stopped.

## TRUTHSEEKER’S FINDINGS

The witness appears sincere. Her account is supported in whole or in part by:

* Dame Koriel Targost;
* thirteen camp workers;
* four soldiers of the southern reserve;
* three surviving Dominion prisoners;
* physical evidence of illusion or spatial misdirection among the laundry lines;
* extensive blood upon Aisling’s clothing inconsistent with her unwounded condition after the battle.

The observed effects potentially include:

1. **Silencing magic**
2. **Compulsion or disturbance of bodily control**
3. **Illusion affecting multiple hostile subjects**
4. **Unidentified accelerated healing**
5. **Possible foreknowledge or extraordinary perception**

No clear divine invocation was observed.

No standard wizardly components were identified beyond black thread and spoken words too quiet for witnesses to reproduce.

The distinction between innate faculty, formal sorcery, divine favor, and other supernatural agency remains unresolved.

---

## STATEMENT OF DAME KORIEL TARGOST

Dame Koriel provided only the following clarification:

> “Aisling took responsibility for the workers. I took responsibility for the road.”

When asked whether she had previously witnessed Aisling employ supernatural abilities, Dame Koriel answered:

> “I have served House Amadis for longer than you have worn that badge.”

When advised that this did not answer the question, she replied:

> “Then you have understood me.”

No further statement was obtained.

---

## SUPPLEMENTAL EXCHANGE

**HALVERN:** One final question. Do you believe Aisling is holy?

**MARTA:** I am a soldier’s wife, sir.

I know what armor looks like. I know what priests look like. I know that holy people usually have cleaner clothes and more witnesses.

I do not know what Aisling is.

I know she was wounded where we could see it.

I know she rose.

I know she could have gone anywhere in the camp.

She came to us.

**HALVERN:** That is not the same as holiness.

**MARTA:** Perhaps not.

But when the sword went through her, she did not look toward the knights.

She looked toward our children.

Write whatever name your order has for that.

---

## DISPOSITION

Seventy-three noncombatants were removed from the immediate assault area.

Twenty-one wounded personnel were recovered from burning or collapsed shelters.

Enemy losses in the southern quarter are estimated at:

* seven dead;
* four captured;
* one missing, presumed dead or removed from the battlefield.

Aisling declined formal examination.

Lady Sophia objected to further questioning of her household and stated that any future inquiry should be addressed to her directly.

Dame Koriel Targost supported this request.

The matter remains open in the Truthseekers’ ledger.

No accusation has been entered.
