# The Locket from Orenic

## The Command Tent

The command tent remains active despite the late hour.

Canvas walls breathe softly with the wind. Lamps hang from the center poles, their flames bending whenever the night air finds a gap. Maps cover the campaign table, held flat beneath daggers, stones, and an empty cup no one has remembered to remove.

Radomir stands near the entrance.

Not blocking it.

Watching it.

The tent flap lifts.

Elizavetta enters alone.

Radomir acknowledges her with a slight movement of his head.

His eyes go first to her face.

Then to the letter in her hand.

Cream paper.

Travel-stained.

Lady Nienor’s personal seal.

Elizavetta allows the canvas to fall behind her.

> “This came with the southern courier.”

Beringer straightens over the table.

> “From Orenic?”

Elizavetta nods.

She does not offer him the letter immediately.

Outside the tent, armor shifts.

A familiar voice quietly answers one of the sentries.

Kat.

Close enough to hear if anyone raises their voice.

Radomir’s eyes move toward the canvas.

Then back to Elizavetta.

Beringer notices.

> “What is it?”

Elizavetta crosses the short distance between them and places the letter upon the map.

> “Sir Edrick Halvarson.”

For a moment, the name does not settle.

Then Beringer remembers.

A crowded hall.

Music.

Katherine Kendal trying to appear unimpressed by everything around her.

A young knight standing before her with more courage than polish, asking her to dance before someone older, wealthier, or better connected could occupy the space.

> “The young knight.”

Elizavetta’s gaze drops briefly to the sealed letter.

> “The one who danced with Kat.”

Radomir goes still.

Not the watchful stillness he wears every day.

Something heavier.

> “Little Kitten’s knight.”

There is no teasing in it.

Beringer looks toward the tent flap.

> “She is outside.”

> “She is my guard,” Elizavetta says.

A pause.

> “She is almost always outside.”

Radomir turns his face slightly away.

Only slightly.

It is the nearest he comes to recoiling.

Beringer picks up the letter.

## Lady Nienor’s Message

> **To Sir Beringer Amadis,**
>
> I regret that this account has taken so long to reach you. The southern roads remain uncertain, and I would not place what follows upon an ordinary casualty roll.
>
> Sir Edrick Halvarson fell during the withdrawal from the southern road.
>
> His horse was killed beneath him during the first assault. He continued on foot with the rear guard while the wounded were carried clear.
>
> He was ordered to withdraw.
>
> Instead, he returned for a wounded man who had been left beside the road.
>
> He brought that man back.
>
> He then returned for another.
>
> Sir Edrick was struck before reaching him.
>
> The line held long enough for both wounded men to be recovered. They remain alive as of this writing.
>
> Sir Edrick did not regain sufficient awareness to speak before his death.
>
> I tended him personally.
>
> Time did not permit the full rites before the army moved. I washed his face and hands, recorded his wounds, and prepared him for burial with the others who fell there.
>
> Beneath his armor, secured close against his chest, I found a small locket.
>
> It contained a commissioned miniature of Dame Katherine Kendal.
>
> The painting is recent and of fine quality. The locket had been worn often.
>
> Among his personal effects was also an unfinished letter addressed to her.
>
> I read only enough to identify its intended recipient.
>
> The locket and the letter remain sealed together under my protection. I will not place either in the common post or release them to a clerk unfamiliar with their significance.
>
> I do not know what understanding existed between Sir Edrick and Dame Katherine.
>
> I know only that he carried her likeness over his heart into battle and had begun writing words meant for her.
>
> He was young.
>
> He was brave.
>
> He died attempting to bring wounded men home.
>
> Whatever else is later said of him, let this much be remembered correctly.
>
> I have washed his face.
>
> I have washed his hands.
>
> I will remember him.
>
> I believed you should know before Dame Katherine is told.
>
> **Nienor**

## After the Reading

Beringer reads the final lines without moving.

Outside the tent, Kat speaks quietly to someone passing through the camp.

There is no strain in her voice.

No grief.

Not yet.

Elizavetta stands opposite Beringer, one hand resting against the campaign table.

Radomir remains near the entrance.

He has not moved since Nienor’s account of the death.

> “He went back?”

His voice is low.

> “Twice,” Elizavetta says.

Radomir nods once.

A soldier’s judgment.

> “Good man.”

His eyes settle upon the canvas behind which Kat is standing.

> “Good death.”

The old words come automatically.

Then his jaw tightens.

> “Bad loss.”

Beringer folds the letter along its existing crease.

Precisely.

Once.

Then again.

> “Did Kat know?”

> “About the painting?” Elizavetta asks.

> “About any of it.”

Elizavetta shakes her head.

> “I do not think so.”

Radomir shuts his eyes.

Briefly.

> “Little Kitten.”

The nickname has always been a needle.

A way of testing her temper, making her snap back, making certain she remains sharp.

Now it is only affection.

And helplessness.

The answer makes the tent feel smaller.

This was not an affair interrupted.

Not a courtship cut short after promises and declarations.

It was only a beginning.

Perhaps not even that.

A young man had danced with Kat Kendal, commissioned her likeness, carried it into battle, and begun a letter whose ending he would never choose.

> “Nienor kept the letter sealed,” Beringer says.

> “She would,” Elizavetta answers.

> “And the locket.”

> “Until someone she trusts can carry them.”

Beringer places Lady Nienor’s note inside his dispatch case.

> “The unfinished letter belongs to Kat.”

> “Yes.”

> “No one else reads it.”

> “No one will.”

Radomir looks at Beringer.

> “Tell deed first.”

Beringer understands.

Before the locket.

Before the unfinished letter.

Before grief turns Edrick into nothing more than a boy who admired her.

Kat must hear that he held the road.

That he went back.

That wounded men lived because of him.

> “First,” Beringer agrees.

## At the Tent Flap

A shadow passes across the lamplight shining through the canvas.

Kat approaches the entrance.

Her boots stop just outside.

> “My lady?”

Elizavetta’s face changes.

The grief is not gone.

It is placed somewhere private.

Somewhere disciplined.

The expression of a noblewoman settles over it.

> “Yes, Kat?”

> “Will you want your heavier cloak when you come out? The wind has turned.”

Elizavetta closes her eyes.

Only for an instant.

Radomir looks down.

Beringer’s hand tightens over the dispatch case.

Kat waits outside, concerned that Elizavetta might be cold.

> “Yes,” Elizavetta says.

Her voice remains steady.

> “Please bring it.”

> “Right away, my lady.”

Kat’s footsteps retreat across the frozen ground.

Elizavetta remains where she is until they fade into the surrounding camp.

Then Radomir speaks.

> “I tell her, Lady. If wish.”

Elizavetta looks at him.

The offer costs him.

It is written plainly across his scarred face, despite every year spent learning how to reveal nothing.

> “No.”

He gives one short nod.

No argument.

> “She stands outside my tent while I sleep,” Elizavetta says. “She checks my food. She watches every stranger who approaches me. She walks into danger first and behaves as though it is merely part of her position.”

Her eyes remain on the canvas.

> “I will not repay that service by sending someone else to tell her.”

Radomir nods again.

> “As you wish.”

Beringer rests one hand over Lady Nienor’s seal.

> “Not while she is on duty.”

> “No,” Elizavetta says.

> “But soon.”

Elizavetta draws a slow breath.

> “Before the camp tells her for us.”

That is the final cruelty of armies.

News rarely waits for dignity.

A name passes from clerk to groom, from groom to cook, from cook to sentry, until grief arrives as overheard conversation.

Beringer looks toward Elizavetta.

> “Tell her the truth.”

Elizavetta turns toward Radomir.

He answers before she can ask.

> “Start with road.”

A beat.

> “Tell her he went back.”

Elizavetta nods.

Radomir turns his face toward the tent wall.

Toward the place where Kat’s shadow had been.

> “She be proud.”

His voice nearly fails him.

Nearly.

> “Makes worse.”

No one contradicts him.

Outside, Kat returns carrying Elizavetta’s heavier cloak.

Attentive.

Loyal.

Still untouched by the news.

Lady Nienor remembers the dead.

Now Elizavetta must face the living.

## Overview

A delayed message from the fighting in Orenic lands reaches [[Sir Beringer Amadis]] through [[Elizavetta Golitsyn]].

The dead knight is [[Sir Edrick Halvarson]], the young man who boldly asked [[Katherine Kendal]] to dance.

[[Lady Nienor]] tended his body personally. Beneath his armor, she found a locket containing a commissioned miniature of Kat. Among his effects was an unfinished letter addressed to her.

Elizavetta brings the message to Beringer herself.

Kat is not distant, missing, or beyond reach.

She is immediately outside the command tent, serving as Elizavetta’s guard and attendant.

[[Radomir Stonehand]] is inside the tent with Beringer. As an Orenic soldier, he understands exactly what Edrick did and takes fierce pride in it.

As someone deeply fond of Kat, he also understands exactly whom the news will wound.

## Lady Nienor and the Dead

Lady Nienor considers every soldier who falls beneath her command to be one of **her dead**.

Whenever time and circumstance permit, she assists personally with the funerary work:

- identifying the fallen
- washing and preparing their bodies
- recovering and recording their personal effects
- assisting priests, relatives, and comrades with the proper rites
- ensuring names are spoken and possessions returned

She does not reserve this care for officers, knights, or nobles.

When the army must move quickly and the dead must be buried upon the field, Nienor still kneels beside each body she can reach.

She washes the face.

She washes the hands.

She learns the name.

Then she remembers.

Lady Nienor always remembers.
