# The Empty Vessels

## Beringer’s Camp

The army has reduced the field to mud.

Not ordinary mud.

Campaign mud.

Mud churned by wagons, cavalry, marching companies, tent stakes, cookfires, and several thousand men who all somehow believe the next person should have chosen a better path.

Katherine Kendal stands outside the Lord Marshal’s command pavilion, boots sunk nearly to the ankle, waiting for Beringer to finish with his officers.

Elunaeth waits beside her.

Kat knows Elunaeth.

This does not help.

The archdruid is barefoot in the mud, looking entirely untroubled by it. Her attention drifts between the command pavilion, the sky, and places that are not currently visible to anyone burdened with experiencing time in the approved order.

Kat glances toward the pavilion.

> “He is still meeting with the captains.”

Elunaeth nods.

> “He was.”

> “He is.”

> “Yes.”

Kat lets that one go.

Experience has taught her that arguing tenses with Elunaeth is like fencing fog.

Elunaeth studies the pavilion.

> “He never liked command tents.”

Kat follows her gaze.

> “The Lord Marshal has spent most of his life in command tents.”

> “Not after.”

Kat looks back at her.

> “After what?”

> “The funeral.”

Elunaeth says it absently, as though discussing a reception they both attended.

> “It rained the night before. The field was dreadful. Worse than this.”

She lifts one muddy foot and considers it.

> “But the morning was clear.”

Her expression warms.

> “Lovely service.”

Kat’s stomach tightens.

> “Whose funeral?”

Elunaeth looks mildly surprised by the question.

> “Beringer’s.”

A pause.

> “I was there.”

Then, kindly:

> “Sorry you missed it.”

Kat stares at her.

Beringer’s voice can still be heard from inside the pavilion, clipped and irritable, correcting someone’s estimate of wagon capacity.

> “He is alive.”

> “Yes.”

> “You just said you attended his funeral.”

> “I did.”

> “That has not happened.”

Elunaeth tilts her head.

> “Not here.”

Kat takes a careful breath.

> “Why did I miss it?”

Elunaeth’s expression changes.

The distraction falls away.

For one terrible moment, she is completely present.

And completely heartbroken.

> “You had already done the brave thing.”

Kat says nothing.

Elunaeth looks past her, into another field beneath another sky.

> “You carried his banner after the pole broke.”

Her voice is soft.

> “You wrapped it around your arm because your hand would not close anymore.”

Kat feels the mud beneath her boots, the weight of her cloak, the living noise of the camp.

Elunaeth sees none of it.

> “You were shouting his name.”

A faint smile touches her face.

> “Not because you thought he would hear you.”

> “Because the others did.”

Kat’s mouth has gone dry.

> “Who were the others?”

> “The frightened ones.”

Elunaeth’s smile deepens, proud and mournful together.

> “They stood when you shouted.”

A beat.

> “The men who killed you were very angry about that.”

Kat goes still.

Elunaeth reaches out and brushes a streak of mud from Kat’s sleeve.

> “You were magnificent.”

> “Was?”

Elunaeth blinks.

The army camp returns to her eyes.

Kat is alive again.

Standing in front of her.

Young.

Unbloodied.

> “Oh.”

A small wince.

> “Wrong tense.”

> “That is not the part I object to.”

> “There is another where you live to eighty-three.”

Kat waits.

Elunaeth considers the alternative future.

> “You become unbearable about horse blankets.”

> “That is not reassuring.”

> “It reassures the horses.”

A voice comes from behind them.

> “What are we reassuring horses about?”

Sophia approaches along the edge of the churned path, cloak pinned up and boots placed with the precise care of someone refusing to become part of the terrain.

Kat looks at her with naked relief.

> “Elunaeth was waiting to see the Lord Marshal.”

Sophia glances toward the pavilion.

> “He is in council.”

> “He finished,” Elunaeth says.

The voices inside continue.

Sophia gives Kat a questioning look.

Kat shakes her head slightly.

Do not touch the tense.

Sophia looks back to Elunaeth.

> “Is the matter urgent?”

> “You asked me that tomorrow.”

Sophia absorbs this.

> “And what did you tell me?”

> “To fetch him.”

Sophia turns toward the two guards standing near the pavilion entrance.

> “You heard her.”

The guards exchange the look of men who have discovered that military service contains threats omitted from the recruiting broadside.

One clears his throat.

> “The Lord Marshal ordered that he not be disturbed unless the southern line broke, my lady.”

Kat says:

> “She has just described his funeral.”

The guard starts toward the pavilion.

Elunaeth suddenly inhales.

Her eyes widen.

Not with pain.

Recognition.

Her lips part.

For a moment she appears to be listening to someone very far away.

Then she smiles.

> “She woke.”

Sophia steps closer.

> “Who woke?”

Elunaeth turns toward her.

> “Me.”

And collapses.

There is no stumble.

No cry.

No attempt to catch herself.

One moment she is standing.

The next, every muscle releases.

Sophia catches her beneath one arm.

Kat gets the other.

They slow the fall but cannot stop it.

All three go down into the mud.

Elunaeth lies between them, silver hair spread across the black ground.

Motionless.

Sophia moves first.

Two fingers beneath the jaw.

Then at the wrist.

She leans close to Elunaeth’s mouth.

Kat watches her face.

> “Well?”

Sophia checks again.

More carefully.

> “There is no pulse.”

Kat presses a hand against Elunaeth’s chest.

Nothing.

> “She is not breathing.”

> “I know.”

> “You said that very calmly.”

> “Someone has to.”

Kat looks toward the guards.

> “Fetch a healer.”

Neither moves immediately.

Sophia does not raise her voice.

> “Now.”

They both leave.

Apparently each has concluded that remaining alone with the senior officers and the dead prophet would exceed his oath.

Sophia rolls Elunaeth carefully onto her back.

No wound.

No blood.

No constriction at the throat.

No sign that anything happened at all.

Kat kneels in the mud, one hand supporting Elunaeth’s head.

> “She was speaking.”

> “Yes.”

> “She was insulting my future knowledge of horse blankets.”

> “Yes.”

> “She cannot simply die between those two subjects.”

Sophia begins chest compressions.

> “The body is under no obligation to respect conversational structure.”

Kat looks at her.

> “That may be the worst thing you have ever said.”

> “It was not intended to comfort you.”

Sophia continues.

Nothing changes.

Elunaeth remains pale, still, and strangely peaceful.

Not dead-looking.

Just absent.

Boots splash through the mud.

Beringer emerges from the pavilion, followed by officers, one of the guards, and a healer attempting to fasten his robes while running.

Beringer sees Elunaeth on the ground.

The command irritation leaves his face.

> “What happened?”

Kat answers too quickly.

> “She was waiting for you. She attended your funeral, apparently. I missed it because I had already died carrying your banner. Then she said she woke and collapsed.”

Beringer looks at her.

> “My funeral.”

> “It was lovely.”

Sophia does not stop working.

> “No pulse. No respiration. No visible wound.”

The healer drops to his knees beside Elunaeth.

He places his hands over her sternum and begins a prayer.

Warm light gathers beneath his palms.

It passes through Elunaeth.

Nothing happens.

The healer frowns.

He tries again.

A stronger working.

The light enters her chest, spreads through her limbs, and fades without resistance.

Beringer watches him.

> “What?”

> “There is nothing to heal.”

Kat gestures at Elunaeth.

> “She is not breathing.”

> “That is not an injury.”

> “It seems like one.”

The healer checks for poison.

Disease.

Paralysis.

Curse.

Each working returns nothing.

He sits back.

Sophia wipes mud from one hand.

> “Can you determine whether she is dead?”

The healer hesitates.

That hesitation is worse than an answer.

Beringer’s voice lowers.

> “Do it.”

The healer places two fingers against Elunaeth’s brow and begins an aura reading.

For several breaths, nothing moves.

Then his face loses color.

Sophia sees it.

> “What did you find?”

He does not answer.

Beringer leans closer.

> “Speak.”

The healer looks down at Elunaeth.

> “There is no soul within this body.”

Kat’s grip tightens beneath Elunaeth’s head.

> “Then she is dead.”

> “No.”

> “She has no pulse, no breath, and no soul.”

> “A corpse bears the mark of departure.”

The healer searches for the words.

> “The tearing where life separated from flesh. The wound death leaves behind.”

He touches Elunaeth’s brow again.

> “There is no severance.”

Sophia looks at him steadily.

> “Then what is she?”

The healer swallows.

> “An empty vessel.”

Silence settles around them.

One of the guards takes a quiet step backward.

His companion follows.

Kat looks down at Elunaeth.

> “Empty.”

> “Not abandoned,” the healer says quickly.

He does not sound convinced enough for anyone’s comfort.

> “There is still a connection. Faint. Beyond my reach.”

Beringer asks:

> “Where is her soul?”

> “I cannot tell.”

> “Can it be called back?”

> “Resurrection would fail. The soul is not dead.”

The healer looks toward Beringer.

> “It is elsewhere.”

Sophia sits back on her heels.

> “For how long?”

> “I have no way to know.”

Hours later, the healer will still have no answer.

Neither will the second healer.

Nor the druid summoned from a nearby unit.

Nor the sigil-mage, whose reading confirms the same impossible fact:

The body is intact.

The body is claimed.

The body is empty.

## Orin’s Camp

At nearly the same moment, miles away, another Elunaeth stops speaking.

She has been sitting beside Orin’s campaign table, staring at a map as though it contains several wars nobody has started yet.

Then she smiles.

> “She found the bed.”

Orin looks up.

> “What bed?”

> “The one I am already sleeping in.”

Mara Moffet glances toward him.

> “Do you understand her?”

> “No.”

> “Good.”

Elunaeth looks at Orin.

For one lucid moment, her expression is gentle.

> “You look good.”

Orin waits.

> “Not burning.”

A beat.

> “Not screaming.”

> “There it is.”

Then Elunaeth’s eyes lose focus.

> “She needs both hands.”

Her body folds sideways.

Orin catches her before she strikes the table.

He lowers her to the ground.

No breath.

No pulse.

Mara kneels beside them, checks once, then again, then looks at Orin with rapidly widening eyes.

> “She is dead.”

> “No.”

> “She has no heartbeat.”

> “She does that.”

> “Does she?”

Orin does not answer.

Mara calls for the camp healer and reaches for the focus used to carry magical messages.

Her first message to Beringer is composed with the discipline expected of House Moffet.

It remains composed for nearly six words.

> **Lord Marshal, Elunaeth has collapsed in Orin’s camp. No pulse, no breath, no response to healing. Orin says she may not be dead, but he is holding what appears to be her corpse, so his confidence is not presently useful. Please respond immediately.**

The camp healer begins his examination.

His expression soon becomes identical to the healer’s in Beringer’s camp.

There is no soul.

No death-mark.

Nothing to heal.

Mara sends a second message.

> **Correction. Her body has no soul. The healer called it an empty vessel. I require clarification on whether that phrase means something less catastrophic than it sounds.**

Orin looks down at Elunaeth.

> “She said she was sleeping somewhere.”

Mara stares at him.

> “You left that out of the first message?”

> “You were talking.”

> “I was reporting a death.”

> “She isn’t dead.”

> “You keep saying that while she remains aggressively corpse-shaped.”

## The Lord Marshal’s Reply

Beringer’s sigil-mage kneels beside the campaign table.

Silver lines burn across a square of prepared parchment, writing Beringer’s words in steady strokes at Orin’s camp.

The reply is much calmer than Mara’s.

This does not make it more comforting.

> **We have another manifestation here. Same condition. No pulse, no breath, no soul, no mark of death. Do not attempt resurrection. Do not permit anyone to destroy, bury, burn, consecrate, or possess the body. Keep it clean, guarded, and warded.**
>
> **She is absent. We do not yet know for how long.**
>
> **Tell Orin he was right. Do not tell him I said that with any enthusiasm.**

Mara reads the message twice.

Then hands it to Orin.

He reads it.

> “Beringer has one too.”

> “I gathered that.”

> “He says I was right.”

> “He specifically instructed me not to encourage you.”

Orin looks down at Elunaeth.

Her face is peaceful.

Too peaceful.

As though this body has never known Dungeonland, prophecy, love, terror, or any of the thousand impossible futures she carries.

An empty house.

Still waiting for its owner.

Mara folds the message carefully.

> “How long do we wait?”

Orin looks toward the moonless sky.

> “As long as it takes.”

## The Waiting

The two manifestations do not wake that hour.

Or the next.

They are washed, wrapped in clean linen, and placed upon guarded cots.

Wards are drawn around them.

Healers check them repeatedly.

There is no decay.

No stiffness.

No aura.

No soul.

Only a faint sense, detectable by the strongest magic, that these bodies still belong to someone.

At Beringer’s camp, Kat refuses to leave.

Sophia eventually brings her food and receives a glare as though eating beside a soulless prophet would constitute poor discipline.

At Orin’s camp, Mara writes down every change.

There are none.

By nightfall, rumors have already spread through both armies.

The Moon-Druid has died twice.

The Lord Marshal keeps one of her bodies.

Baron Kharne has the other.

The bodies are waiting for moonrise.

Anyone who touches one will lose his soul.

The guards assigned to the cots do not believe that last part.

They are simply careful never to touch them.
