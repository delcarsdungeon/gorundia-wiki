## Sermon — “Let Every Face Stand in the Light”

Delivered by Father Oswin Marth, priest of the Triad, at the Chapel of Saint Aldren’s Field, Blackvale

Brothers and sisters of Blackvale,

Stand easy.

No man or woman is called here today to sharpen a knife.

No son of this county is called here to raise a hand against his neighbor.

No daughter of this soil is called here to curse the Crown, the Countess, or the soldiers placed among us.

If that is what you came to hear, then you have walked into the wrong house of prayer.

This is not a hall for rage.

This is not a barn for plotting.

This is the house of the Triad, and before the Triad every heart is weighed.

Pelor sees the hungry.

Heironeous sees the oath.

Pholtus sees the truth.

And none of Them are fooled by our excuses.

We have lived through judgment.

Let no one pretend otherwise.

A lord of Blackvale has died beneath royal sentence. His house is broken, his kin scattered, his name now spoken in lowered voices, as though even the rafters fear to remember him too clearly.

Some say justice was done.

Some say vengeance wore a crown.

Some say both things can be true, and if that thought troubles you, good.

A troubled conscience is not sin.

A dead conscience is.

So pray for the soul of Ponzio Bordella.

Yes. Pray for him.

Not because he was blameless.

Not because rank washes blood from the hands.

Not because old banners must be kissed after they have dragged through filth.

Pray for him because the mercy of Pelor is not a coin we spend only on men we liked.

Pray also for those he harmed.

Pray for those who waited long years for judgment and now find that judgment, when it comes, does not give back what was taken.

Pray for the widow.

Pray for the orphan.

Pray for the prisoner who walked free and did not know where to sleep.

Pray for the servant who watched too much and spoke too little.

Pray for Blackvale.

Because this county has been cut open, and no surgeon worth his salt calls the bleeding a cure.

Now hear me.

The Crown has appointed a new countess.

Dame Dana Sylvanranth sits in Blackvale by writ and seal. By law, she is owed obedience in her office. By faith, she is owed the charity due to any soul placed beneath a burden not of her own choosing.

Do not make easy lies about her.

Do not call her beast, witch, elf-spawn, foreign knife, or any of the other filth that cowards mutter in alehouses when they lack the courage to speak plainly before witnesses.

A half-elven soul is not half a soul.

Let that be said in this chapel, under this roof, before sun, sword, and law.

Pelor gives no lesser dawn to mixed blood.

Heironeous does not measure valor by the shape of an ear.

Pholtus does not make truth dimmer because it passes through a half-elven mouth.

Those who say otherwise do not defend Blackvale.

They shame it.

But obedience is not blindness.

And peace is not silence.

A countess may be lawfully seated and still need to be seen.

A soldier may be honestly sworn and still need to be answerable.

A warrant may bear a royal seal and still need a witness.

The Triad does not fear witness.

The just ruler does not fear witness.

The lawful sword does not fear witness.

So I say this not in rebellion, but in reverence:

Let every face stand in the light.

If a man commands in the name of the Crown, let Blackvale know who commands.

If a hand takes grain from a granary, let the village know whose hand has taken it, under what writ, and for whose need.

If a door is opened in the night, let the warrant be read in the day.

If a prisoner is seized, let the charge be written.

If a helm is worn for war, let it be raised for judgment.

For Pelor’s mercy cannot comfort a child who sees only iron.

Heironeous’ valor is not honored when brave men look like shadows.

Pholtus’ law is not strengthened when the accused cannot name his accuser.

Some men hide their faces because they are ashamed.

Some hide them because they are afraid.

Some hide them because they have given up old names and taken new ones in service.

Perhaps that is noble.

Perhaps that is necessary.

Perhaps some among these helmed soldiers have sinned, repented, and found in discipline the road back to honor.

If so, praise the gods.

There is no soul so stained that the dawn cannot find it.

But repentance that cannot be seen cannot teach.

Service that cannot be named cannot reassure.

And power that cannot be answered will be feared, even when it means to protect.

That fear is not treason.

Fear becomes treason only when wicked men feed it until it grows teeth.

So do not feed it.

Do not take up cudgel or torch.

Do not spit at the Countess’ riders.

Do not whisper Dominion lies and call them county pride.

Do not paint hatred on granary doors and tell yourself you have defended your fathers.

You have not.

You have only made your mother weep.

But neither should those who govern mistake quiet for consent.

Blackvale is old.

Older than this crisis.

Older than this sentence.

Older than the grief of one house or the ambition of another.

The fields remember the names of those who sowed them.

The mills remember the hands that turned them.

The roads remember whose sons died keeping them clear in winter.

A county is not a chair.

It cannot simply be occupied.

It must be held in trust.

It must be learned.

It must be served.

So let the Countess serve.

Let her be obeyed where she rules lawfully.

Let her be corrected where she is poorly advised.

Let her be witnessed where she commands.

Let her be defended from slander.

Let her be restrained from haste.

Let her be welcomed when she walks openly among us.

And let those who stand beside her remember this:

Blackvale has seen masked evil before.

If you are not that, then do not resemble it more than duty requires.

Raise your visors in the market.

Give your names to the reeves.

Let complaints be written.

Let wrongs be answered.

Let the widow know who took her son.

Let the miller know who counted his grain.

Let the village know whether its bread goes to soldiers, refugees, or graves.

This is not weakness.

This is law with a clean face.

This is valor without arrogance.

This is mercy that does not ask the frightened to be grateful before they understand.

Brothers and sisters, the Dominion watches.

Do not doubt it.

The tyrant beyond the border laughs when free folk mistake each other for enemies.

Hextor’s priests would love nothing more than for Blackvale to tear itself open while calling the wound justice.

Do not give them that feast.

Hold your anger.

Demand witness.

Keep the peace.

Feed the hungry.

Name the dead.

Count the grain.

Open the books.

Raise the helms.

And let every face stand in the light.

By Pelor’s mercy.

By Heironeous’ courage.

By Pholtus’ truth.

Let Blackvale be healed without being blinded.

Let Blackvale be governed without being conquered.

Let Blackvale remember itself without becoming cruel.

And let no man here mistake patience for surrender.

Amen.
