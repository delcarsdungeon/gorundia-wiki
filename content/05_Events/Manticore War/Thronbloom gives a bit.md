## Scene — Thornbloom at the Road Camp

The camp has mostly gone quiet.

Not asleep.

A moving army never really sleeps. It just changes which men are miserable.

Wagons sit in uneven rows under canvas. Horses stamp and snort in the dark. Somewhere, a cook-fire gutters low. A wheelwright curses softly at a cracked pin. Farther off, sentries pass murmured challenges through the night.

Near the Lord Marshal’s command wagon, **Radomir** stands guard.

Still as a post.

Not decorative. Not bored. Watching everything the way a man watches a tree line after hearing wolves.

A heavy shape approaches through the dark.

Broad shoulders. Slow steps. One hand carrying a dark bottle. The other holding two battered cups by the rims.

Radomir shifts slightly.

Not blocking.

Just becoming more present.

> “State your business.”

**Sir Darien Thornbloom** stops.

Looks at him.

Then down at the bottle.

Then back up.

> “Medicine.”

Radomir does not smile.

Darien lifts the bottle a little.

> “For the Lord Marshal.”

A beat.

> “And maybe for whatever part of him is still awake and making poor decisions.”

Radomir studies him.

Darien lets him.

No offense taken. No puffed-up knightly bullshit. Just stands there like a barn with boots.

Finally Radomir steps aside.

> “He’s still working.”

Darien grunts.

> “That’s why I brought medicine.”

Radomir pulls the flap aside.

> “Don’t keep him too long.”

Darien passes him, then pauses just inside the light.

> “That advice for me?”

Radomir’s face does not change.

> “For him.”

Darien gives the smallest nod.

Respect.

> “Fair.”

Then he ducks into the command wagon.

## Inside the Command Wagon

It is cramped, lamplit, and moving even while still.

Maps pinned to boards. Dispatches stacked in weighted piles. A travel shrine wedged into one corner. Mud on the floorboards. Armor straps hanging from a peg. The smell of wax, damp wool, horse leather, and too many decisions.

[[Beringer]] is there with papers in front of him.

Of course he is.

Darien looks around.

> “Gods. It’s a crime scene with wheels.”

He sets one cup down near Beringer.

Pours.

Does not ask.

The liquor is amber, sharp, and probably older than some squires.

Darien fills his own cup after, then lowers himself onto a reinforced travel stool that complains under him but survives.

> “Drink.”

A beat.

> “Or hold it. Either way, looks better than bleeding over maps.”

He takes a swallow.

Winces slightly.

> “Hells. That’s got opinions.”

For a moment, the wagon creaks around them.

Outside, a horse stamps.

Then Darien says:

> “Heard Elderbrook’s name again.”

The air changes.

Darien does not soften it.

> “Funny thing about old fires. People remember who held the torch. Forget who stacked the wood.”

He looks at Beringer over the cup.

> “Theron burned it. No arguing that.”

A pause.

> “But he didn’t build the place dry.”

Darien leans forward, forearms on his knees.

> “You want a couple names? Men who let it rot. Men who fed well while it did.”

## Lord Magistrate Edran Vors

Darien says the name like it tastes stale.

> “Edran Vors. Lord Magistrate. Fine robes. Clean hands. Filthy ledger.”

Vors was supposed to keep Elderbrook’s mixed wards under lawful protection. Halfblood testimony. Property claims. Guild disputes. Watch complaints. All the pretty words Araisal likes putting in charters.

Darien snorts.

> “Half-orc mason’s shop burns? Vors needs witness confirmation.”
>
> “Half-elven widow loses her lease? Vors needs seal verification.”
>
> “Old family wants three mixed-ward blocks declared unstable? Seal’s found before the ink dries.”

He drinks.

> “Man didn’t start the riot. Worse. He made sure the law arrived late and dressed nice.”

**What Vors got out of it:**  
Old property families owed him. Disputed buildings changed hands. Liens, fees, “temporary custodianship,” all that clean little theft with wax on it.

> “He helped make theft look like paperwork. That takes education.”

## Bailiff Marric Dane

Darien’s voice goes lower.

> “Marric Dane. Ward bailiff. That one, I’d hang twice.”

Dane commanded local sworn men in one of the mixed wards. He was meant to keep the peace.

Instead, he armed “protective patrols.”

Darien’s mouth tightens.

> “You give clubs to men who already hate their neighbors, you’re not keeping order. You’re picking tomorrow’s corpses.”

Dane’s patrols blocked halfblood families from leaving certain streets while letting mobs through others. Later, when records were demanded, half the ward books had burned.

Darien raises his brows.

> “Convenient fire. Very tidy. Burned the right shelves.”

**What Dane got out of it:**  
Abandoned properties. Rents. Patronage from old ward families. A reputation as a hard man in difficult times.

> “Men like Dane always say difficult times. Never say profitable ones.”
> “Edran Vors. Marric Dane.”

He taps the table once.

“That kind of rat doesn’t die with the old banner. He sniffs out the new supply wagon.”

A pause.

“You’re about to move a lot of banners, Lord Marshal.”

“Expect rats.”

## Darien on Dana Freeblade

Darien pours a little more.

Not much.

This part needs less liquor, not more.

> “Also heard men muttering about [[Dana Freeblade]].”

He glances toward the canvas, toward the camp beyond.

> “Countess Blackvale now. That’s got some old Cron men nervous.”

A pause.

> “Not the ones who served with her. They know better.”

He looks back to Beringer.

> “Men who fought beside her know she’s cold because she keeps the fire banked. Not because there’s nothing in the hearth.”

Darien rolls the cup between both hands.

> “But men who only know the name Sylvanranth?”

A shrug.

> “They hear Theron’s daughter got a county and start wondering where the torches are stored.”

Outside, Radomir’s shadow passes briefly across the wagon canvas.

Darien notices. Says nothing.

> “She doesn’t help herself.”

A beat.

> “Walks like a verdict. Talks like she’s already read your confession. Fine traits in a commander. Poor traits when frightened men are deciding whether you’re your father with better posture.”

He takes another drink.

> “Anyone who served with her knows her character. Anyone who didn’t sees the tree and assumes the apple fell straight down.”

Then, dry:

> “And she’s so approachable you’d have better luck asking a siege tower for a dance.”

## Darien’s Advice

Darien sets the cup down carefully, because the wagon is cramped and wasting liquor would be immoral.

> “You want Blackvale steady? Don’t make men love her. Won’t happen quick.”

He taps one thick finger against the map board.

> “Make the right men talk.”

Another tap.

> “Not courtiers. Not poets. Not some silk-bellied herald saying words he polished in a mirror.”

He leans in.

> “Old soldiers.”

A third tap.

> “Men who saw her hold formation. Men who saw her spare prisoners when cruelty would’ve been easier. Men who heard her spit on Theron’s sins before it was safe.”

Darien sits back.

> “Battlefield gossip. That’s what soldiers trust.”

A pause.

> “Mostly because it’s too ugly to be court propaganda.”

## If Beringer Asks If Darien Trusts Her

Darien takes his time answering.

> “I trust what people do when no one rewards them for it.”

He looks into the cup.

> “Dana walked away from a powerful father because staying would’ve been easier.”

A beat.

> “That’ll do for a start.”

## Final Beat

Darien stands with a grunt.

The wagon shifts slightly under him.

He leaves the bottle on a flat crate beside the maps.

> “Vors. Dane.”

He points once at the papers.

> “Old rot. Might be dressed fresh now. Might have new titles. Might have friends who’ll tell you Elderbrook was one man’s sin.”

He looks at Beringer.

> “Don’t buy that.”

A pause.

> “Theron lit the fire.”

Then, quieter:

> “But plenty of bastards warmed their hands before he got there.”

Darien turns for the flap.

Radomir’s silhouette is still outside.

Before leaving, Darien glances back.

> “Drink before it evaporates. Cost more than my first horse.”

Then he ducks out into the night.

Outside, Radomir lets the canvas fall closed.

For a moment, Darien and Radomir stand together in the dark.

Radomir looks at the bottle still inside.

> “Good medicine?”

Darien exhales through his nose.

> “Terrible.”

A beat.

> “That’s how you know it works.”

Then Darien walks back into the camp, heavy steps fading between wagons, while inside the command wagon two names sit on the map like old blood under fresh ink.
