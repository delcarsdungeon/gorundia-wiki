# Breakfast at the Blood & Sand — The Tailor

Fenby had put eggs, thick bacon, fried potatoes, and fresh bread on the table.

For several minutes, the Blood & Sand achieved peace.

Then Toots said:

> “Tailor made me strip.”

Gravel stopped with half an egg on his fork.

Grenda looked up.

Slab kept chewing, but slower.

Fenby did not turn from the stove.

> “For the fitting,” he said.

> “Know that.”

Toots pointed her fork at him.

> “Still made me strip.”

> “Most tailors do.”

> “Most tailors don’t have six naked women wandering around.”

Gravel lowered the egg.

> “Six?”

> “Might’ve been five. One kept moving.”

Grenda leaned forward.

> “What sort?”

> “Women.”

> “I gathered.”

Toots shrugged.

> “Young one from the Gilded Lily. Merchant woman with a backside like a cart horse. Skinny elf. Little old halfling lady.”

Slab finally looked up.

> “Naked?”

> “Hat.”

> “What?”

> “Halfling kept her hat on.”

Fenby nodded toward the pan.

> “Standards.”

> “And maybe a noblewoman.”

Grenda asked:

> “How could you tell?”

> “Complained about the mirrors.”

Grenda nodded.

> “Noblewoman.”

Gravel was still working through the numbers.

> “All naked.”

> “That’s what I said.”

> “Same room.”

> “Still what I said.”

Toots buttered her bread.

> “Silk everywhere. Assistants running about. Women turning around on little platforms. Somebody shouting for blue ribbon. Somebody else shouting that blue ribbon was dead to him.”

> “Who?”

> “Tailor.”

> “Why?”

> “Didn’t ask. Seemed personal.”

She took a bite.

> “One girl walked past carrying three dresses over her head. Another came through with nothing on but stockings and a necklace. Music playing somewhere.”

Grenda blinked.

> “Music?”

> “Fiddle and drum. Fast.”

> “In a tailor’s?”

> “Apparently clothes fit better if everybody moves like they’re being chased.”

Fenby glanced over his shoulder.

> “Sound practice.”

> “Then this half-elf comes sweeping through the curtains.”

Toots made a broad, disdainful gesture.

> “Silk shirt. Rings. Measuring cord around his neck. Pins in his mouth. Didn’t walk anywhere. Arrived.”

Grenda was already smiling.

> “Master Ellisar.”

> “That him.”

> “He’s good.”

> “He’s irritating.”

> “Also that.”

Toots imitated Ellisar with unsettling accuracy, lifting her chin and narrowing her eyes.

> “‘Which one belongs to Thalrik Vaultforge?’”

She returned to herself.

> “I said I don’t belong to anybody.”

Back to Ellisar:

> “‘Excellent. Pride complicates the shoulders, but improves the posture.’”

Grenda snorted.

> “Then he looked at the chit.”

Toots adopted Ellisar’s voice again.

> “‘Reward for loyal service. Evening attire. Full account to the Vice Admiral.’”

She scowled.

> “Told him I didn’t want charity.”

> “‘Nor do I. Charity pays poorly and expects gratitude.’”

Fenby gave a small approving grunt.

> “Then he pointed at a platform and said, ‘Off.’”

Gravel frowned.

> “Off what?”

Everyone looked at him.

> “Clothes,” Toots said.

> “All?”

> “I asked that.”

She became Ellisar again.

> “‘Darling, unless you intend to wear the new dress over the old one, yes. All.’”

> “What’d you do?” Slab asked.

> “Told him I’d knock his teeth out.”

> “And?”

> “He said not until after he matched them to the buttons.”

Fenby coughed into one hand.

Slab stared very seriously at his plate.

Grenda’s shoulders had begun to shake.

> “So I stripped.”

Gravel’s fork stopped again.

Toots saw it.

> “Don’t make this strange.”

> “Wasn’t.”

> “Your egg hasn’t moved in a minute.”

Gravel ate the egg whole.

Toots continued.

> “Ellisar didn’t stare. Didn’t smirk. Didn’t act scared.”

That part came out differently.

Quieter.

Nobody interrupted.

> “Walked around me twice. Poked my shoulder. Lifted my arm. Turned me by the hips. Told me to stand straight.”

Grenda asked:

> “And did you?”

> “Was standing straight.”

Toots shifted back into the tailor’s voice.

> “‘No, darling. You were preparing to repel boarders. Different posture entirely.’”

Fenby nodded.

> “Accurate.”

Toots glared at him.

> “Then he pulled my shoulders back, stepped around in front of me, and looked straight at my chest.”

Gravel’s fork stopped for the third time.

> “And said…”

Toots took a drink.

> “‘Magnificent tits.’”

Grenda made a strangled sound.

Fenby turned back to the stove, but his shoulders shook once.

Slab stared down at his bacon as though it had betrayed him.

> “That’s what he said.”

Grenda managed:

> “What did you say?”

> “Asked if he wanted to keep his eyes.”

> “Reasonable.”

> “Then he said, ‘I have no personal interest in them whatsoever. Professionally, however, they are magnificent and are about to make my afternoon extremely difficult.’”

Grenda covered her face.

Toots pointed at her.

> “Wasn’t funny then.”

> “And now?”

Toots considered.

> “Little funny now.”

She took another bite.

> “Called them architectural.”

Fenby said:

> “That sounds expensive.”

> “Everything in there was expensive.”

> “What did he mean, architectural?” Slab asked.

Toots shrugged.

> “Started talking about balance, line, support, and whether silk could be trusted with responsibility.”

Fenby frowned.

> “Can’t.”

> “That’s what he said.”

Grenda was losing the fight now.

> “Then what?”

> “Then the madness started.”

Toots spread both hands.

> “He clapped once. Three assistants appeared.”

> “From where?” Gravel asked.

> “Curtains.”

> “Three.”

> “Could’ve been four. They moved fast.”

She pointed toward imaginary corners of the room.

> “One had cloth. One had cord. One had wine.”

Grenda asked:

> “For you?”

> “Tailor.”

> “Naturally.”

> “He drank it without looking.”

Toots mimed Ellisar flinging out an arm.

> “‘Red! No, not that red. That red apologizes for itself. Bring me the red that has killed a husband.’”

Slab slowly turned toward Grenda.

> “There’s different reds?”

> “Apparently.”

> “Then somebody threw cloth across the room.”

> “Threw it?”

> “Ellisar caught it.”

> “Without looking?”

> “Course without looking.”

Grenda laughed openly.

> “Then there was a blue dress.”

Toots’s expression soured.

> “Hated it.”

> “Why?”

> “Looked like a curtain.”

> “What sort of curtain?”

> “Rich person’s.”

> “Ah.”

> “Couldn’t move my arms properly. Told him.”

She shifted into Ellisar again.

> “‘Then we shall not dress you as upholstered furniture.’”

Fenby nodded solemnly.

> “Correct.”

> “He tore one sleeve off.”

Gravel looked up.

> “Off the dress?”

> “Right there.”

> “Why?”

> “Said it had offended him.”

> “What happened to the other sleeve?”

> “Assistant took care of it.”

> “How?”

> “Scissors.”

> “Quick?”

> “Very.”

Toots stabbed a potato.

> “Then came green silk. Gold cord. Black velvet. White lace.”

Her disgust deepened with each item.

> “At one point I had feathers.”

Grenda’s eyes widened.

> “Where?”

> “Don’t matter. They were removed.”

> “By you?”

> “Eventually.”

Slab asked:

> “Were they offended too?”

> “I was.”

She kept going.

> “Ellisar would put something on me, stare for three breaths, then shout a word or two.”

> “What words?”

> “‘Widow.’”

Grenda laughed.

> “‘Milkmaid.’”

Fenby shook his head.

> “‘Ambitious aunt.’”

Slab looked uncertain.

> “‘Provincial duchess.’”

Gravel frowned.

> “Bad?”

Everyone turned toward him again.

> “Very,” Grenda said.

Toots continued:

> “‘Second wife.’”

That one stopped the table.

Fenby slowly nodded.

> “Cruel.”

> “Dress deserved it.”

> “Then somebody tightened the back too far.”

Toots set down her fork.

> “Couldn’t breathe.”

Her imitation of Ellisar became suddenly sharp.

> “‘Stop.’”

No camp in that one.

No joke.

> “Whole room stopped.”

She looked briefly surprised even remembering it.

> “He loosened it himself. Asked who’d pulled the cord. Girl raised her hand.”

Toots became Ellisar again.

> “‘We dress women. We do not punish them for possessing ribs.’”

Grenda’s smile softened.

> “Then he threw that dress away too.”

> “Away?” Gravel asked.

> “Onto a noblewoman.”

> “Naked one?”

> “One of them.”

> “Which?”

> “Complained about the mirrors.”

> “Noblewoman.”

> “She liked it.”

Toots spread her hands.

> “Whole room was spinning by then. Cloth flying. Women changing places. Ellisar shouting. Assistants crawling around my feet with pins. Somebody brought more wine.”

> “For you?” Slab asked.

> “Still tailor.”

> “Naturally.”

> “Then he stopped.”

Her voice settled.

> “Just stopped in the middle of it.”

She looked down at her plate.

> “Asked what I hated.”

Grenda leaned forward.

> “What did you say?”

> “Dresses.”

> “Helpful.”

> “He said so too.”

Toots assumed Ellisar’s expression.

> “‘A profound contribution. We shall embroider it on the shop sign.’”

> “So I told him.”

She counted on her fingers.

> “No bare back. Don’t like people behind me. Nothing tight around the throat. Nothing I can’t walk fast in. Nothing that makes me look helpless.”

The room had gone quieter.

> “What did he say?” Fenby asked.

> “‘Then those garments were stupid.’”

Toots shrugged, but the shrug did not quite hide it.

> “Just like that.”

No questions.

No pity.

The garments were stupid.

> “Then he pulled out one more.”

Grenda asked:

> “What color?”

> “Dark red.”

> “The husband-killing red?”

> “Apparently.”

> “Good.”

> “Long skirt, but split and folded somehow so I could move. High back. Sleeves fitted here.”

She touched her upper arms.

> “Not tight. Strong stitching. Little bit of black around the collar and waist.”

> “Neckline?” Grenda asked.

Toots gave her a look.

> “Had one.”

> “How much of one?”

> “Enough for the architecture.”

Grenda broke again.

> “Didn’t hang loose. Didn’t squeeze. Didn’t make me look like somebody’s maid or somebody’s whore.”

A pause.

> “Looked like me.”

That silenced the table for half a heartbeat.

Toots immediately defended herself from the moment.

> “Me with cleaner hair.”

Fenby resumed cooking.

> “Naturally.”

> “What’d he say?” Grenda asked.

> “‘There. At last.’”

Toots’s imitation was quieter now.

> “‘The dress has stopped arguing with you.’”

Grenda smiled.

> “You liked it.”

> “Didn’t hate it.”

> “You liked it.”

> “Could kick someone in it.”

> “That is not a denial.”

> “He made me try.”

Slab looked up.

> “Kick someone?”

> “Assistant held a cushion.”

> “How’d it go?”

> “Cushion lost.”

Fenby turned from the stove.

> “Good dress.”

> “That’s what I said.”

She reached for more bread.

> “Then Ellisar stood behind me at the mirror and started adjusting things. Hair. Collar. Shoulders.”

> “Did he tell you that you were beautiful?” Grenda asked.

Toots snorted.

> “No.”

> “What did he say?”

> “‘Never let a garment make you smaller so it can feel important.’”

That landed.

Even Gravel seemed to understand that one.

Toots added quickly:

> “Still an irritating bastard.”

> “You like him,” Grenda said.

> “Don’t.”

> “You do.”

> “He stuck pins in my arse.”

> “Professionally.”

> “Enjoyed it.”

> “Professionally?”

Toots thought.

> “Probably.”

Gravel had been staring between them, brow knotted under the terrible burden of delayed comprehension.

At last:

> “Wait.”

Everyone looked at him.

> “The tailor’s a sissy?”

Silence.

Not offended silence.

The exhausted silence reserved for a man who had been standing beside the road while the entire caravan passed.

Slab slowly set down his fork.

Fenby turned from the stove.

Grenda stared at Gravel.

Toots blinked twice.

> “Gravel.”

> “What?”

> “He had six naked women in the room.”

> “Five,” Grenda corrected.

> “One kept moving.”

Fenby added:

> “And he did not care.”

Slab gestured with his toast.

> “Complimented her tits like he was inspecting a bridge.”

Toots pointed at Gravel.

> “Ellisar said one woman’s arse had ruined two marriages and he still told her to move because she was blocking the light.”

Grenda spread her hands.

> “What part did you miss?”

Gravel looked around the table.

> “Could’ve been polite.”

Fenby stared at him.

> “For two hours?”

> “Very polite.”

> “While holding pins in his mouth?”

> “Dedicated.”

Grenda’s eyes narrowed.

Something had changed.

A thin sheen had appeared across Gravel’s forehead.

His shoulders had gone rigid.

He reached for his cup without looking at anyone.

Drank.

Then drank again.

Grenda watched him.

> “Gravel.”

> “What.”

> “Why are you sweating?”

> “Nothing.”

Far too quickly.

Every face turned toward him.

Grenda’s mouth twitched.

> “What is it, Gravel?”

> “Nothing.”

Still too quick.

Fenby glanced toward the cold hearth beside the table.

> “Not hot.”

> “Eggs.”

> “Eggs don’t make people sweat.”

> “These do.”

Fenby looked at the pan.

> “No.”

Gravel wiped his forehead.

> “Spice.”

> “There is no spice.”

> “Hidden spice.”

> “I cooked them.”

Grenda started laughing.

Quietly.

She bent over her plate, trying to swallow it.

Her shoulders betrayed her immediately.

Gravel’s expression darkened.

> “What.”

> “Nothing,” Grenda managed.

> “What.”

> “Nothing.”

Toots looked from Grenda to Gravel.

Then toward the peg beside the door.

Gravel’s coat hung there.

Long.

Black.

Richly embroidered at the collar and cuffs in the flamboyant Galibanos style Gravel insisted was merely proper.

Toots slowly looked back at him.

> “Where’d you get that coat?”

> “Tailor.”

Grenda made a choking noise.

> “Which tailor?”

Gravel stared into his cup.

> “One in Hideaway.”

Slab turned toward the hanging coat.

Fenby leaned sideways to inspect it.

Toots smiled.

Slowly.

Mercilessly.

> “Master Ellisar?”

Gravel said nothing.

Grenda slapped the table.

> “I knew it.”

> “Shut up.”

> “He fits Gravel.”

> “Shut up.”

> “Personally.”

> “Coat needed fitting.”

> “Naturally.”

> “Normal tailoring.”

> “Completely ordinary.”

Gravel pointed at Grenda.

> “Don’t.”

She was laughing openly now.

> “Tell them.”

> “No.”

> “Tell us what?” Toots asked.

Grenda wiped at her eyes.

> “Ellisar tried to talk him out of the long coat.”

Toots glanced toward it.

> “Why?”

Gravel’s face had taken on a dangerous grey-green cast.

Grenda fought for breath.

> “Said covering that arse…”

She broke again.

Gravel closed his eyes.

> “…was a crime.”

Silence.

Then every head turned.

Slowly.

Toward Gravel.

Gravel remained seated for two seconds.

Then stood.

Everyone’s eyes followed.

He immediately sat back down.

Slab examined him with fresh professional interest.

> “Could be.”

> “Eat.”

Fenby put another helping of eggs onto Gravel’s plate.

> “You should be proud.”

> “Eat.”

> “Architecturally significant.”

Toots’s eyes dropped lower.

To the black leather trousers.

Tight enough to make several private facts public.

Her smile widened.

> “Those too?”

Gravel went still.

> “What.”

> “Ellisar.”

> “Leather’s leather.”

Grenda folded over the table.

> “He told him the coat was a crime because it covered the evidence.”

Slab looked from Gravel’s face to Gravel’s trousers.

> “Strong case.”

Gravel’s hand tightened around his fork.

Toots nodded thoughtfully.

> “Professional opinion.”

Fenby set down the pan.

> “Architectural.”

> “Eat,” Gravel said.

Grenda could barely breathe.

> “Did he make you turn around too?”

Gravel stood.

Every head followed.

He sat back down.

> “Nobody speaks.”

He picked up his fork.

> “Anyone speaks again…”

Toots leaned back, pleased with the world for perhaps the first time that morning.

> “Magnificent arse.”

Gravel stabbed an egg hard enough to break the plate.
