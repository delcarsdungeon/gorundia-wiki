# Bellwether Row South-Cut Incident

## Overview

The Bellwether Row South-Cut Incident is the first clear sign that [[House Sylvanranth]] has an active, organized, and very serious underground operation beneath [[Bard's Gate]].

The incident began when [[Crake]] and several of [[Crake's Boys]] cut through a black-wax Sylvanranth seal in the south-cut tunnel under [[Bellwether Row]], assuming it marked old property, dead claims, or abandoned elven arrogance.

It did not.

Beyond the seal was not an abandoned tunnel, forgotten guild vault, or stale noble cache. It was an active worksite: freshly braced, numbered, surveyed, guarded, and being processed by dwarves, cutters, clerks, and gray-coated operatives.

Crake's boys stumbled into a live containment and evidence recovery operation.

They were lucky only in the limited and humiliating sense that some of them survived.

## Short Version

Crake's boys broke into a sealed Sylvanranth underground work zone.

They found evidence crates, tagged bodies, dwarf bracework, survey marks, and a gray-coated response team that had procedures ready for intruders.

The response was fast, quiet, professional, and intentionally non-public.

Afterward, a warning was delivered to [[Erik Shineapple]]:

> Your organization is not the target.  
> Do not make it necessary to revise this.

Erik's conclusion was simple:

No retaliation. No rescue. No tunnel work near black wax.

## Known Facts

### The Seal

The breached seal was black wax on proper linen wrap, stamped clean across the arch.

This was not street theater, old noble decoration, or some forgotten ward from two generations ago. It was a working seal, placed deliberately to mark an active controlled zone.

The seal was Sylvanranth.

The breach was ugly. Crake's boys did not peel or inspect it. They hacked through it with a brass thieves' pick and snapped the pick in the wax line, because apparently the gods sometimes send warnings and men answer with burglary tools.

### The Tunnel

Twenty yards beyond the seal, the tunnel changed.

Features reported by Erik:

- Fresh bracework
- Dwarf pins in the sidewalls
- Slate tags every ten paces
- Air marks
- Survey numbers
- Active side chambers
- Tagged bodies
- Packed evidence
- Numbered rooms
- Cleared rooms
- Still-sealed rooms

This was not exploration.

This was a controlled excavation and investigation.

### The Worksite

The active work zone contained:

- Dead snake folk
- Men with filed teeth
- A woman with scaling at the neck
- Three ordinary-looking men with cult cuts
- Evidence crates
- Tools
- Covered boxes
- Dwarven measuring and recording staff

At least one dwarf was seen with a writing board, calling out measurements like timber pricing.

That detail matters.

It means the work was not merely martial. It was administrative, forensic, architectural, and probably legal. Someone is building a record.

## The Gray Coats

The gray-coated operatives who responded to the breach were calm, prepared, and trained for close tunnel work.

Observed response team:

| Role | Equipment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Crossbowman | Crossbow | Disabled one intruder with a shoulder shot |
| Hookman | Hook and cord | Took an intruder by the ankle and slammed him into the wall |
| Shieldman | Small blackened shield | Broke a nose and knee in one motion |
| Empty-Handed Operative | No visible weapon | Controlled two attackers using one as a shield, disarmed another, cut only enough to end the fight |

The empty-handed operative was, in Erik's assessment, the worst of them.

This strongly suggests the [[Black Seal Detail]] uses specialists trained for capture, containment, and quiet force rather than ordinary guards or hired blades.

They did not shout warnings.

They did not posture.

They simply ended the intrusion.

## The Second Team

Crake ran.

He did not get far.

There was another team behind them.

This is one of the most important details in the report. The gray coats did not merely react from inside the worksite. They had the breach route covered from behind.

Erik's conclusion:

The tunnel had one usable entrance that the Shineapple organization knew about.

The gray coats knew at least one more.

Or they had already mapped the Shineapple-side approach and allowed Erik to learn just enough to become useful.

Either interpretation is bad for anyone planning another clever little sewer expedition, because clever little sewer expeditions are how men become cautionary stains.

## Casualties and Prisoners

Known outcome:

| Person / Group | Status |
|---|---|
| [[Crake]] | Alive, captured or controlled |
| Three of Crake's boys | Taken |
| Two of Crake's boys | Dead |
| One injured boy | Broke both legs in a brace pit, then was splinted by a dwarf who called him an idiot |
| Little runner | Found hiding; given water by a gray-coated woman |
| [[Erik Shineapple]] | Allowed to leave |

The fact that an injured intruder was treated and a young runner was given water matters.

The gray coats are not butchers.

They are also not bluffing.

This is disciplined violence under rules. The rules are not public, not civic, and not negotiable from a street gang's position of ignorance.

## The Warning Note

Twenty minutes after Erik returned, a folded slip of gray paper was found under the back step.

No seal.

No address.

One word outside:

> SHINEAPPLE

Inside:

> Your organization is not the target.  
> Do not make it necessary to revise this.

Erik added his own line afterward:

> Loose things get people killed.

The warning was not a threat in the normal street sense. It was a boundary notice.

The [[Shineapple Crew]] is not currently considered an enemy, but that status is conditional. If Shineapple muscle, runners, cutters, or affiliates interfere with sealed Sylvanranth work again, the gray coats may revise the category.

That is a very polite way of saying the next lesson may not include medical care.

## The Children's Evidence Crate

The most disturbing reported detail is the opened crate.

Erik saw enough before the blue-white lamps changed:

- Old cloth
- Little shoes
- A doll with no head
- Children's things

Not loot.

Evidence.

This suggests the operation is not just clearing tunnels from the recent [[Serpent Cult]] or [[Urdlen Cult]] mess.

It may be tracing older crimes.

Possibilities include:

- Child sacrifice sites
- Cult recruitment or disposal routes
- Old trafficking tunnels
- Pre-purge victims
- Evidence from crimes buried before the current city leadership knew to look
- Links between older thieves' guild runs and serpent-cult infrastructure
- Hidden disposal chambers under civic neighborhoods

The children’s things make this more than a jurisdictional problem.

They make it a ledger problem.

Someone is collecting names.

## Working Interpretation

The Black Seal operation appears to be doing three things at once:

1. Clearing old undercity spaces.
2. Recording evidence of cult activity and earlier crimes.
3. Establishing control over dangerous tunnels without requesting permission from the city.

This is not a normal noble cleanup effort.

This is a silent occupation of the undercity by a private power with enough money, discipline, specialist labor, and political confidence to ignore ordinary civic ownership.

That power is almost certainly tied to [[House Sylvanranth]].

Whether the active chain of command runs through [[Dana Sylvanranth]], [[Theron Sylvanranth]], another Sylvanranth agent, or a sealed household office remains unknown.

## The Ash Yard Connection

The [[Ash Yard]] is a likely surface logistics link for the operation.

Not because anyone has been stupid enough to label a cart "secret elf evidence crimes, please ignore," though give the city time.

Likely reasons the Ash Yard matters:

- It can receive debris from underground clearing without attracting much attention.
- Ash, lime, spoiled cloth, bone fragments, and ruined timber are ordinary there.
- Cutters and laborers can move in and out without looking like noble retainers.
- Smoke, stink, and grit conceal chemical smells.
- Old furnace ash may connect to heat-vents under the city.
- Bodies, cult refuse, contaminated stone, and sealed crates can be staged nearby before being moved elsewhere.
- Orenic or hired cutters may be easier to hide among ash-haulers and rough labor crews.

The Ash Yard should not be treated as "the secret base" unless discovered in play.

It is better used as a pressure point, transfer node, rumor sink, labor screen, or trailhead.

The PCs can find the Ash Yard connection through:

- Cart traffic at odd hours
- Slate tags or survey chalk on discarded bracewood
- Ash-haulers paid in clean coin
- Dwarf pins showing up in refuse
- Blackened linen scraps
- Cult-marked stone chips
- A cutter with a fresh wound and too much money
- A priest quietly blessing waste pits before dawn
- An ash foreman who knows exactly when not to ask questions

## Relation to the Undercity

This incident confirms that several undercity networks overlap:

### Old Thieves' Guild Runs

The south-cut tunnel and nearby cuts were already known to thieves, smugglers, and salvage crews.

Crake's boys were using them to poke around for claimable goods before more powerful people noticed.

They noticed too late.

### Cult Infrastructure

The bodies found in the worksite suggest connection to serpent-cult activity, filed teeth, scaling, and marked human collaborators.

This may tie into the remains of the [[Serpent Cult]] and the deeper rot under the old thieves' guild.

### Dwarven Survey Work

Fresh bracework and dwarf pins mean someone competent is making the tunnels safe enough to process.

That is expensive.

It also means the operation is meant to last.

### Sylvanranth Control

The seal, the warning note, and the disciplined gray-coated team all point toward Sylvanranth control or sponsorship.

This is not a rumor hunt. It is an operation.

## Who Knows What

### Erik Shineapple

Erik knows enough to be scared.

He has seen the seal, the worksite, the operatives, the warning note, and the children's evidence. He also understands the difference between a street threat and a professional boundary.

His order is absolute:

No retaliation.

No rescue.

No tunnel work near black wax.

### Guaco Moe Lee

If informed by Erik, [[Guaco Moe Lee]] likely understands the political problem immediately.

The [[Shineapple Crew]] can survive being embarrassed.

They may not survive becoming an obstacle to a Sylvanranth operation tied to dead children and cult evidence.

Guaco's likely response is to cut losses, forbid freelancing, and start quietly finding out which of his people are too stupid to live.

### Crake

Crake is alive.

That is not necessarily good news for Crake.

He may be:

- Held for questioning
- Released with a message
- Turned into an informant
- Used to map Shineapple-side access
- Quietly handed back damaged but breathing
- Vanished into a ledger

Crake's value depends on what he saw, who he answers to, and whether the gray coats think he is more useful alive.

### The City

The city probably does not officially know.

Or, if someone in the city knows, that knowledge is compartmented tightly enough that street crews, thieves, and ordinary ward authorities have not been warned.

This may become a jurisdictional problem if [[Wonderful]] learns that an armed private force is clearing sealed tunnels under Bard's Gate without civic permission.

It may become a moral problem if she learns what they are finding.

Naturally, it can be both, because civilization is apparently a stack of bad choices wearing a hat.

## GM Notes

### Tone

This should feel like a crime scene under a city, not a dungeon crawl invitation.

The gray coats are not monsters in the tunnel.

They are worse for reckless PCs: competent adults with paperwork.

Their presence should imply:

- Procedure
- Discipline
- Money
- Legal confidence
- Moral gravity
- Hidden violence
- A chain of command that does not need to explain itself

### What Makes It Scary

The scary part is not that the gray coats can fight.

The scary part is that they expected this.

They had breach procedures. They had multiple teams. They had routes covered. They had medics or at least trained personnel. They had evidence discipline. They knew Erik's name, or knew enough to address the note correctly.

This is an organization that has already planned for fools.

That is terrible news, because fools are the city's most renewable resource.

### What Makes It Complicated

The Sylvanranth operation may be doing necessary work.

If they are recovering evidence of murdered children, cult crimes, and undercity atrocities, stopping them may protect civic pride while hurting justice.

But if they are operating without the city, controlling tunnels, detaining criminals, and deciding who gets to know what, they are also creating a private jurisdiction beneath Bard's Gate.

This is exactly the kind of problem where everyone can be partly right and still make things worse.

## Possible PC Entry Points

### Shineapple Warning

The PCs hear Erik's report, see the black linen, or are asked by Guaco to judge whether this is a political problem, a criminal problem, or a "leave it alone before someone loses a hand" problem.

### Ash Yard Lead

The PCs track odd cart traffic to the [[Ash Yard]] and discover laborers moving contaminated stone, sealed refuse, or tagged bracewood.

They may not find the Black Seal base.

They may find the edge of the logistics chain.

### Missing Crake

Someone wants Crake back.

This person is probably stupid, desperate, lying, or all three.

A rescue attempt risks turning the Shineapple organization into a target.

### Children's Evidence

A priest, mother, old watchman, or survivor recognizes an item from the crate.

This turns the operation from criminal politics into a moral knife fight.

### City Jurisdiction

[[Wonderful]] learns that a private Sylvanranth detail is working under city streets without open civic authority.

She must decide whether to confront them, quietly coordinate, or pretend not to know until pretending becomes impossible.

### Dwarven Pins

A dwarf mason recognizes the pins as belonging to a specific crew or contracted standard.

This can lead to [[Dwarven Dwell]], the [[Ash Yard]], or a front company.

### Black Wax Repeat

Another black-wax seal is found somewhere the PCs care about.

This time, they know better than to cut it.

Hopefully.

## Suggested Scenes

### Erik's Report

Erik arrives through the back, coat dusted with gray mortar, blood on one cuff, cap in hand.

His face is calm.

Too calm.

He lays out three things:

- Broken brass thieves' pick
- Black linen with wax
- Pale chip of clean-cut stone

Then he says:

> The south-cut tunnel under Bellwether Row is no longer quiet.

### The Ash Yard at Dawn

Ash carts creak under gray morning light.

A priest murmurs over a wagon bed while two cutters pretend not to hear.

A dwarf checks slate tags against a board.

A half-asleep laborer tosses blackened linen into the wrong pile and gets slapped hard enough to remember procedure.

The PCs smell lime, wet ash, old smoke, and something that should have stayed buried.

### The Black Wax Seal

The PCs find a clean black-wax seal across a tunnel arch.

No guard.

No visible ward.

No sound beyond it.

Just black linen, wax, and the quiet knowledge that Crake already tried being clever for everyone.

### The Gray Coat Conversation

A gray-coated woman steps from a side passage and speaks like someone reading from a policy manual.

> You are not the target.  
> Please do not become a correction.

She is polite.

That does not help.

## Consequences

### If the PCs Respect the Seal

They may gain a chance at controlled contact with the Black Seal operation.

Possible outcomes:

- A warning interview
- A supervised look at selected evidence
- A request to keep street crews away
- A chance to coordinate through a third party
- A quiet favor owed by someone who will never admit owing it

### If the PCs Break the Seal

The response should be immediate and professional.

Not a boss fight.

A containment event.

Possible responses:

- Blue-white lamp activation
- Silent flanking team
- Bolts into limbs, not hearts
- Hook and cord takedowns
- Shield work in cramped tunnels
- Capture over killing unless lethal force is required
- Evidence secured before prisoners questioned
- Names taken
- Gear catalogued
- Wounds treated just well enough to prove a point

### If the PCs Go Through the Ash Yard

They may learn more without directly breaching the operation.

Possible discoveries:

- Slate tags
- Fresh dwarf pins
- Black linen fragments
- Cart routes
- Paid cutters
- Clerks using coded ledgers
- Refuse that is too carefully sorted
- Someone pretending ash is just ash

### If the City Gets Involved

This becomes political.

Questions raised:

- Who owns the tunnels?
- Who has authority below old guild cuts?
- Can private forces detain criminals underground?
- Who controls evidence from cult atrocities?
- Can Sylvanranth keep operating if the findings implicate city figures?
- Does public exposure help justice or destroy the investigation?

## Canon Handle

The Bellwether Row South-Cut Incident is the first street-level proof that the Black Seal operation is real, active, and close enough to ordinary Bard's Gate crime networks to create accidental contact.

It links the [[Shineapple Crew]], [[Bellwether Row]], the [[Undercity]], [[House Sylvanranth]], and the [[Ash Yard]] into one developing thread.

It should not immediately expose the whole operation.

It should establish the rule:

Black wax means someone serious is already working.

And if someone serious is already working, the stupidest possible move is to bring a brass pick.
