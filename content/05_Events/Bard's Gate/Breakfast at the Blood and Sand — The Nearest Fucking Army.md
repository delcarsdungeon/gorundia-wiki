# Breakfast at the Blood and Sand

By morning, the story had achieved what stories in the Dregs always achieved.

It had become worse, funnier, less accurate, and available from six reliable witnesses who had each been in different buildings.

Fenby stood over the stove.

Caddoc chopped onions.

Nesta worked dough.

Harl stirred something thick enough to resist.

Grenda sat at the kitchen table with a cup.

Toots leaned against the flour bin, arms folded.

Gravel came through the curtain carrying the expression of a man burdened by intelligence.

Fenby did not look around.

> “If that face is about the books, eat first.”

> “It ain’t about the books.”

> “Then eat before gossip.”

> “It’s not gossip.”

Toots glanced at him.

> “You talked to Grukk.”

Gravel looked offended.

> “That can still be intelligence.”

> “Can it?”

> “All intelligence is relative.”

Grenda pushed a plate toward him.

Gravel sat.

> “Rukk heard the whole thing.”

> “Grukk,” Nesta said.

> “That’s what I said.”

> “No, you didn’t.”

> “I shortened it.”

> “You removed a letter.”

> “He doesn’t use most of them.”

Fenby placed fried potatoes onto Gravel’s plate.

> “What did the eloquent bastard say?”

Gravel picked up a fork.

> “Said Morg went in to ask Wonderful a woman thing.”

The kitchen stopped.

Grenda stared.

> “A woman thing.”

> “That’s how Grukk put it.”

Toots frowned.

> “What does that mean?”

> “I asked.”

> “And?”

> “He moved his hands.”

Caddoc looked up from the onions.

> “How?”

Gravel made two vague, helpless motions in front of his chest.

The kitchen stared at him.

Gravel lowered his hands.

> “It was clearer when he did it.”

> “No,” said Fenby. “You understood because your mind lives in the same ditch.”

Gravel pointed the fork at him.

> “Street communication.”

> “Ditch.”

> “Anyway. Morg tells her he’s served her **indigently**.”
> 
> Fenby stopped moving.
> 
> Grenda looked up.
> 
> “He served her poor?”
> 
> Gravel frowned.
> 
> “No. Means loyal. Thorough. One of them useful words.”
> 
> Fenby made a strangled noise and turned toward the stove.
> 
> His shoulders began shaking.
> 
> Gravel stared at his back.
> 
> “What?”
> 
> Fenby gripped the edge of the counter.
> 
> “Nothing.”
> 
> “That ain’t nothing.”
> 
> “Continue.”
> 
> “You’re laughing.”
> 
> Fenby finally turned, eyes watering.
> 
> “You have just accused Morg of serving the Lord Mayor in a state of destitution.”
> 
> Gravel considered this.
> 
> “He didn’t have much money.”
> 
> Fenby folded over against the counter.
> 
> Grenda covered her mouth with her cup.
> 
> Toots looked away.
> 
> Gravel pointed around the kitchen.
> 
> “Fuck all of you. Rukk said an **I-word**.”
> 
> From the stove, Fenby managed:
> 
> “He certainly did.”

> “Fine. He served her **industrially**.”

Fenby hit the counter again.

Gravel waited for him to finish.

He did not.

> “You understood what I meant.”

Fenby raised one hand without turning around.

> “That's making it worse.”

Gravel stabbed a potato with unnecessary force.

> “Anyway. Morg told her he’d been there. Done the work. Stood his post. Said he knew what she needed.”

The laughter faded.

Grenda’s expression changed.

Not surprise.

Recognition.

> “Oh, Morg.”

Toots grimaced.

> “He actually said that?”

> “Near enough.”

> “And Wonderful?”

> “Said he knew what the ward needed. Not what she wanted.”

Fenby straightened at last, wiping his eyes on his sleeve.

> “Good answer.”

Gravel ate the potato.

> “Said he never asked without already deciding.”

> “Better answer,” said Nesta.

> “Morg asks what’s wrong with him.”

Grenda looked into her cup.

> “That’s never the question they want answered.”

> “Nope,” Toots said. “They want you to say nothing.”

Harl scraped the spoon around the pot.

> “And if you say nothing?”

> “Then they hear someday.”

Gravel nodded.

> “Wonderful said no.”

> “That should’ve been the end,” Grenda said.

> “It was not the end.”

Fenby sighed.

> “Men built civilization because none of them could leave a sentence alone.”

Gravel took another bite.

> “Morg asked whether the King had put a chastity belt on her.”

The kitchen went dead silent.

> “And,” Gravel continued, because bad news was already awake, “whether she was saving herself for the Lord Marshal.”

The back door opened.

Druuna stepped into the kitchen carrying an empty basket.

> “Who is saving what for the Lord Marshal?”

Gravel closed his eyes.

Toots looked at the ceiling.

Grenda drank.

Fenby, without missing a beat, pointed at an empty chair.

> “A fortress.”

Druuna looked at him.

Then at Gravel.

Then at Grenda.

She set the basket down.

> “Morg mistook guarding the gate for having a claim on the keep.”

Gravel frowned.

> “That’s not exactly—”

Fenby kicked him under the table.

Gravel’s knee struck the underside hard enough to rattle the plates.

Druuna sat.

> “Wonderful is the keep.”

> “Yes,” Grenda said.

> “Morg guarded the gate diligently.”

> “For a long time,” said Toots.

Druuna nodded.

> “And when she would not let him inside, he decided another man must have locked the door.”

Fenby pointed a wooden spoon at her.

> “There.”

Druuna looked at him.

> “There what?”

> “Sermon over.”

> “I wasn’t preaching.”

> “That’s when you’re best at it.”

Fenby put a bowl in front of her.

Grenda leaned back in her chair.

> “Morg thought if he didn’t have the key, some other man must.”

Toots nodded.

> “Couldn’t just be that Wonderful didn’t want him upstairs.”

> “Or wanted no one upstairs,” Druuna said. “Or wanted someone once and never again. It is her keep.”

> “Exactly,” Grenda said.

Gravel looked between them.

> “Are we still talking about the fortress?”

> “Yes,” said Fenby.

> “No,” said Toots.

> “Mostly,” said Grenda.

Druuna buttered a piece of bread.

> “Morg was not really asking about locks.”

Gravel looked relieved.

> “Thank you.”

> “He was asking which man had beaten him.”

Gravel’s relief disappeared.

> “There wasn’t a fight.”

> “That was Wonderful’s point.”

Gravel frowned.

> “I lost the keep when the belt became masonry.”

Fenby pushed the potatoes closer to him.

> “Eat.”

> “I am eating.”

> “You’re talking.”

> “I can do both.”

> “Evidence says otherwise.”

Grenda watched Gravel for a moment.

> “You used to look at Wonderful.”

Gravel stopped chewing.

Everyone became intensely interested in him.

He swallowed carefully.

> “I look at fires.”

Grenda waited.

> “Don’t mean I climb in 'em.”

A small smile touched her mouth.

> “Good answer.”

> “I know.”

> “Practiced?”

> “Survival is practice.”

Fenby nodded.

> “Better than Morg’s answer.”

The humor ebbed.

Not entirely.

Just enough to let the wound underneath breathe.

Toots looked toward the back door.

> “He thanked everyone.”

> “House,” Gravel said. “He thanked the house.”

> “Same thing.”

> “No.”

Gravel stared at his plate.

> “House’ll still be here when he comes back.”

Grenda looked at him.

> “You think he will?”

> “If Kharne gives him something real to do.”

> “And if he doesn’t?”

Gravel shrugged.

> “Then Morg finds another army. Or a mercenary band. Or somebody else willing to tell him being angry makes him important.”

Fenby turned down the stove.

> “War is a poor cure for a bruised heart.”

> “Dregs ain’t known for its doctors.”

Druuna looked around the table.

> “He has us.”

Gravel’s jaw tightened.

> “Had.”

> “Still has.”

He looked at her.

Druuna met his eyes evenly.

> “He left angry. He did not stop being ours.”

Nobody mocked her.

That was the trouble with Druuna.

She kept saying simple things that left harder people nowhere comfortable to stand.

Gravel finally looked down and resumed eating.

After a moment, he said:

> “Grukk says Wonderful told Morg she’d been with Beringer because she wanted to be.”

Grenda nodded once.

> “Good.”

Gravel looked at her.

> “Good?”

> “She made it hers.”

Toots understood immediately.

Not Beringer’s conquest.

Not Morg’s humiliation.

Not a prize awarded to whichever man had accumulated enough loyalty, scars, or proximity.

**Hers.**

Gravel understood too.

He just preferred understanding things that could be settled with a ledger or a chair leg.

> “Morg didn’t hear it that way.”

> “No,” Fenby said. “He heard another man winning.”

Druuna shook her head.

> “Because losing to a man hurts less than being refused by a woman.”

The kitchen went quiet again.

Gravel looked at her.

> “You been saving that?”

> “No.”

She took a bite of bread.

> “It is not complicated.”

Fenby looked at Gravel.

> “For some people.”

Harl barked a laugh over the pot.

Caddoc nearly cut his thumb.

Nesta turned away to hide a grin.

Even Grenda laughed into her cup.

Gravel shook his head.

> “Nearest fucking army.”

Fenby returned to the stove.

> “Most wars start with worse reasons.”

The kitchen went back to work.

One man gone east with a pack, a loaf of bread, and a wound he had given himself.

The Blood and Sand did what it always did with its missing.

Cursed them.

Fed them when possible.

Kept their place without admitting it was being kept.
