# To Count Blackheart

My Lord,

I was told to stay when the King sent the Countess out.

He made a point of it.

Didn’t dress it up either. Just looked at me like he knew exactly what I was for.

So I stayed.

And I watched.

---

## Short of It

This wasn’t a court.

Wasn’t even politics, not the way we use the word.

This was a man deciding the line had been crossed… and then drawing a new one where everyone could see it.

---

## The Signs

You’ll hear stories. They won’t be wrong.

I’ll keep it simple.

Something answered him.

- A black-armored knight just *appeared* at his side. No warning. No ritual. Just there.
- A white fox walked into the hall like it owned the place, dropped a ring with a druid’s voice in it, and left as leaves.
- His son walked in under sunlight that didn’t belong indoors and didn’t leave him.

No one argued.

Not the mages.
Not the priests.
Not the elves.

That part matters.

---

## The King

You would’ve liked him.

Not because he’s pleasant.

He isn’t.

Because he’s *controlled*.

Didn’t shout. Didn’t threaten. Didn’t rush.

Everything he did felt like it had already been decided before he walked in.

That’s either discipline…

or he’s already made peace with what he’s becoming.

---

## Why I Was There

He didn’t hide anything.

Sent my lady out—proper, respectful.

Kept me.

Said nothing about it, but the meaning was clear enough:

> “You’re going to tell this story. Tell it right.”

So I am.

---

## What He Did

He started naming people.

Not wildly. Not fishing.

Like a man reading from a list he trusts.

- One baron lost his head on the spot. No delay.
- A handful of lesser nobles got dragged out for things everyone pretends not to notice.
- A count—mage, strong one—got called out for *watching* instead of acting. Went quietly. That part stuck with me.

Then the printers.

Those he didn’t even bother with in the room.

They got the rope outside while he kept talking.

You could hear it.

He didn’t stop for it.

---

## The Rule He’s Setting

He didn’t say it once. He said it a dozen ways.

I’ll say it plain:

> If you knew and did nothing, you’re guilty.

Doesn’t matter if you swung the knife or just stepped out of the way.

That’s new.

That’s going to hurt a lot of people.

---

## The Clever One

The mage—Serrin.

Worth noting.

He could’ve left. Everyone there knew it.

Didn’t.

Walked forward like he was curious how it ends.

That’s not a man I’d ever turn my back on.

---

## The Crowd

Here’s the part you’ll care about.

They didn’t break.

They adjusted.

One fool tried to run. Drew steel.

Didn’t get far.

Other nobles grabbed him before the paladins even got there.

That told me everything.

They’re already learning the new rules.

---

## What He Built

He set up a priest—Pholtus type.

“Confessor of the Blinding Truth.”

Sounds like a bad time, and I expect it will be.

Man’s job is to hear what people confess… or pull it out of them.

King made a point though:

Said the priest finds truth.

The Crown judges it.

We’ll see how long that line holds.

---

## What I Think

This isn’t a man losing control.

It’s a man taking it.

Clean.

Public.

Backed by enough miracles that arguing looks stupid.

---

## The Problem

Things like this don’t slow down on their own.

They pick up speed.

Especially when they start working.

---

## Last Thing

He looked at me like he knew exactly what I’d write.

Didn’t try to stop it.

Didn’t try to shape it.

Just… let it happen.

Men like that don’t fear being seen.

---

You asked me to watch.

I watched.

---

If we’re smart, we decide early:

Are we standing with him—

or making plans for what comes after?

---

Reigar
