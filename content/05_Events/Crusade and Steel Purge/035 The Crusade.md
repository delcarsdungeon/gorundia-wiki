
# THE KING’S DECLARATION — LAST DAY OF THE LORD’S MUSTER

The hall is already strained to breaking.

Too many trials.  
Too many revelations.  
Too many nobles suddenly remembering things they hoped would stay buried.

---

The King rises.

His holy sword leaves the scabbard with a sound like a storm breaking across stone.

Silence falls.

---

# She's YOUR fucking problem

He looks to Bertram, not her.

> “Sir Bertram Moffet.”

> “You stood when others did not.”  
> “You bled when others hesitated.”  
> “You have done more for this Crown… than many born to it.”

> “You once claimed this woman.”

> “You knew her… before title.”  
> “Before ambition.”  
> “Before failure.”

> “Do you claim her still?”

---

> “Then hear the terms.”

He looks at **Bertram**.

> “She will hold no title.”  
> “No independent authority.”  
> “No privilege of this court.”

> “What remains of her… is hers to rebuild.”

> “And she does not come to you alone.”

> “Her brother Allec Bordella goes with her.”

> “He is too young to be trusted, too proud to be loosed, and too dangerous to be left to drift between households nursing old grudges.”

> “You will keep him.”  
> “You will answer for him.”  
> “If he learns honor, the credit is yours.”  
> “If he disgraces himself, the shame is yours.”

> “You once claimed this woman.”  
> “Now you claim what comes with her.”

> “Both of them are your responsibility, Sir Bertram.”

> “See to it they become something worth the Crown’s trouble.”

---

# If Bertram Hesitates

Bertram hesitates.

> “Good.”

> “Now you understand.”

> “This is what it costs.”

> “Say it anyway.”

---

# Invoking the Dragon

A voice from the gallery:

> “…this feels like the Dragon’s hand.”

---

> “Yes.”

> “It does.”

---

> “Many of you…”  
> “were relieved… when you heard of Theron Sylvanranth’s death.”

> “You called him stern.”  
> “Efficient.”  
> “Practical.”  
> “A man who understood… necessity.”

---

> “You are not wrong.”

> “He did understand necessity.”

---

> “He understood what I chose… not to be.”

> “Where I offered mercy… he enforced consequence.”  
> “Where I sought balance… he bore the weight of it.”  
> “Where I compromised…”

> “He stood.”

---

> “You did not fear him because he was cruel.”

> “You feared him… because he was certain.”

> “Because when he spoke—”  
> “things ended.”

---

> “Theron Sylvanranth was no hound loosed at my command.”

> “He was the shield… that allowed you to mistake restraint for weakness.”

> “He was the line… that allowed you to pretend there was always time.”

> “He was the man… who carried the cost you refused to see.”

---

> “And you called him _necessary_…”

> “…so you would never have to be.”

---

> “That shield is gone.”

> “So now…”

> “…you will meet the Crown.”

---

## Dana Named

> “Dame Dana Freeblade.”

No rise in volume. No ceremony.

But it cuts the room clean in half.

---

He finds her.

Locks.

---

> “Stand.”

---

She does. Immediate. Precise.

---

There is a moment—small enough most miss it.

A single breath held a fraction too long.  
The slightest tightening at the jaw.

Not fear. Not hesitation.

Recognition.

---

Then it’s gone.

Filed away. Locked down.

Only those who know her—_really_ know her—see it.

---

> “A knight of this realm.”  
> “A Freeblade.”  
> “What she is—she has earned.”

The room understands what that means:

No one here can take that from her.

---

> “What she has not—”  
> “—is what you hide behind.”

---

> “Name.”  
> “House.”  
> “Excuse.”

---

> “You invoke Theron Sylvanranth’s name.”

> “You speak of necessity.”

---

> “Good.”

> “Then we will test that understanding.”

---

> “His daughter stands among you.”

> “The County of Bordella stands broken.”

> “And still—you waited for someone else to claim the burden.”

> “None of you stepped forward to mend it.”

---

> “So I have chosen someone who will.”

> “He cannot.”

> “She can.”

---

> “You will govern it.”

---

> “Yes, Your Majesty.”

---

> “You asked for certainty.”

> “You will have it.”

---

## Opening Strike

> “You have all heard the charges.”

> “You have all seen the rot.”

---

> “Do not mistake me.”  
> “This is not chaos.”  
> “This is judgment.”

---

> “Too many of you knew _pieces_.”  
> “Too many of you said nothing.”  
> “Too many of you decided it was not your place to act.”

> “You were wrong.”

---

## Kylesstra Called Forward

> “Duchess Kylesstra.”

> “Step forward.”

---

The shift in the room is immediate.

This is not a minor house.

This is **Ravenster**.

---

She steps forward—measured, controlled.

Still every inch the duchess.

---

> “Let us speak plainly.”

> “You received favors.”  
> “Trade concessions.”  
> “Advantage.”

> “You transferred a prisoner.”

> “To House Vogel.”

---

> “You knew his reputation.”  
> “You knew what he was.”

> “And still—”

> “You handed a woman over to that monster.”

---

## Ravenster Moves — And Is Stopped

A chair scrapes.

Glandyr rises—just enough to matter.

---

> “Sit.”

---

Flat. Immediate.

---

Two Heironeous knights step in behind him.

Close enough.

---

> “Sit.”  
> “If he rises again—”  
> “You will seat him.”  
> “If he resists—”  
> “Remove him.”

---

Glandyr holds for a heartbeat.

Then sits.

---

No one mistakes what just happened.

---

> “For what?”  
> “Better tariffs?”  
> “Smoother roads?”

> “Tell me, Duchess—”  
> “Are we merchants?”  
> “Or are we warriors?”

---

# Kylesstra Responds

> “Your Majesty.”

> “Yes.”

> “I made that decision.”

> “I made it with the information I had… at the time it was required.”

---

> “Trade concessions.”  
> “Roads.”  
> “Supply lines.”

> “You call them trivial.”

> “My people call them survival.”

---

> “You ask if we are merchants or warriors.”

> “We are rulers.”

> “And rulers make decisions that are… imperfect.”

---

> “If that is now a crime—”

> “I suspect I do not stand alone.”

---

## The King’s Answer

> “You are correct.”

> “Rulers make imperfect decisions.”

> “We weigh lives against time.”  
> “Risk against stability.”  
> “One cost… against another.”

> “That is the burden of the crown.”

---

> “Do not pretend that is what this was.”

> “You did not choose between two evils you could not avoid.”

> “You chose the one that was… convenient.”

> “You chose the one that did not threaten you.”

---

> “You speak of roads.”  
> “Trade.”  
> “Survival.”  
> “All worthy concerns.”

> “All beneath the cost you paid.”

---

> “You did not spend your life.”  
> “You did not spend your blood.”  
> “You spent hers.”

> “And you called it governance.”

> “That is the difference.”

---

> “And yes.”

> “Others in this hall have made similar bargains.”

> “We will speak of them.”

---

> “But today—”

> “You stand before me.”

---

> “Not for ruling imperfectly.”

> “But for deciding—”

> “that someone else would bear the cost of your perfection.”

---

> “Do not dress that in necessity.”

> “It was a choice.”

---

> “And now—”

> “it will be judged as one.”

---

## ⚡ THE KROZEN RETURNS

**Thunder detonates through the hall.**

Light tears across the air—

and then—

he is there.

---

The Krozen kneels.

Head bowed. Blade grounded.

---

Then rises—

and takes his place at the King’s shoulder.

---

No one in the hall misunderstands:

something has just chosen a side.

---

## The Interruption

Something enters.

Unannounced.

---

“Kylesstra.”

> “Sit.”

> “This is not over.”

---

“To our guest.”

> “We will not keep a spirit waiting.”

---

## The Fox

A white fox crosses the floor.

It walks to [[Jinwing]].

A ring hangs from living grass.

---

## The Voice

> “The roots have been stirred.”

> “The old paths open again.”

> “You walk now in the sight of more than kings.”

> “Choose well where you stand.”

> “The wild remembers.”

---

The ring settles into Jinwing’s hand.

The fox dissolves into drifting leaves.

---

No one knows where to look.

The Krozen does not move.  
The King does not bow.

---

And for the first time—

the court understands:

there are powers in this room that answer to neither.

---

## The King Claims the Moment

> “Good.”

> “Then there will be no doubt.”

---

> “You have seen it.”

> “Not rumor.”  
> “Not accusation.”

---

> “You have been **witnessed**.”

> “Not by crown.”  
> “Not by court.”

> “But by powers that do not ask our leave.”

---

> “And now—”

> “The wild itself has spoken.”

---

> “This is no longer a matter of crown and court.”

> “This is a matter of **mandate**.”

> “Divine.”

---

## The Furyondy Delegation

> “My brothers and sisters in the faith…”

> “You arrive at a moment… chosen for us.”

> “You will hear what is said in this hall.”

> “And you will carry it back to your king.”

---

> “My lady.”

> “What follows concerns the failings of my realm.”

> “You are free to depart.”

---

There is a pause.

Then the Countess inclines her head.

Graceful. Controlled.

---

She turns.

Silk shifts. Boots follow.

Her escort closes around her—deliberate, not hurried.

---

And she leaves.

---

Every noble in the hall watches her go.

---

Because now they understand:

she was given a choice.

They were not.

---

## The Pivot

> “You have all heard the charges.”  
> “You have all seen the rot.”

---

> “Not with villains.”

> “With good men and women—making reasonable decisions.”

---

## The Expansion

> “This is not one house.”  
> “Not one crime.”

> “This is a pattern.”

---

> “As I speak—”

> “Agents of the Crown move.”

---

His gaze sweeps the hall.

Not dramatic.

Accounting.

---

> “They carry writ.”

> “They carry authority.”

> “They will be obeyed.”

---

A slight shift in tone:

---

> “At my request—”

> “The Inquisition has agreed.”

---

That lands harder than anything shouted.

---

> “They now move into the Duchy of Grevandar.”

---

No flourish.

No apology.

---

> “They will find what we have missed.”

> “They will expose what has been hidden.”

---

Then—

just slightly—

he turns his head.

A flick of the eyes.

---

> “And while you have stood here…”

---

A gesture.

Small.

Deliberate.

Toward a space in the hall.

---

A space that is—

**empty.**

---

> “Others have already begun the work.”

---

> “Thranduil Stormarrow has been sent ahead.”

> “He does not stalk alone.”

> “He never has.”

A few in the room understand.  
Most do not.  
The ones who do… do not look reassured.

---

> “He prepares Blackvale.”

---

That lands differently.

Not investigation.

**Occupation.**

---

> “Not for comfort.”

> “For correction.”

---

Now back to the room:

---

> “We will root it out.”

> “We will drag it into the light.”

> “We will burn it out where it festers.”
---

## The Declaration

> “This is not a war between houses.”

> “This is not a settling of grudges.”

---

> “This is a reckoning.”

> “A cleansing.”

> “A crusade.”

---

## The Paladins

The doors do not creak.

They do not open slowly.

---

They are **thrown wide**.

---

Boots strike stone.

In unison.

---

Steel enters the hall.

---

They do not rush.

They do not spread.

They **advance**.

Measured. Relentless. Perfectly in step.

---

First—

The **Paladins of Heironeous**.

Blue and steel.

Cloaks like storm banners.

Helms forward.

They carry war the way a priest carries scripture—

with certainty.

---

Then—

The **Paladins of Pelor**.

Not the gentle priests the court remembers.

These are the **hard ones**.

Sun-bleached armor.

Burn scars not hidden.

Eyes that have seen what mercy costs when given too freely.

They do not smile.

They have come because mercy is over.

---

Then—

The **Paladins of Pholtus**.

White and gold.

Rigid.

Severe.

Helmets like judgment given form.

They do not look left or right.

They do not see people.

They see **deviation**.

---

Then—

The **Paladins of Stern Alia**.

Fewer.

Heavier.

Shields scarred from holding lines that should have broken.

They move like a wall given legs.

No ornament.

No softness.

Just endurance.

---

And among them—

Knights bearing lesser but still lawful lights of Gorundia.

Fewer.

No less resolved.

---

They do not fan out.

They do not address the nobles.

They do not kneel.

---

They **split**.

---

Half to the left.

Half to the right.

---

Forming a corridor of steel along the great carpet—

From the doors…

All the way to the King.

---

The galleries are cut off.

Minor nobles boxed in.

---

No easy exits.

No slipping away.

---

The meaning settles, cold and immediate:

---

**No one leaves.**

---

Stillness follows.

Not absence of motion—

**containment.**

---

## Porthos

He walks the length of the hall.

In Pelor’s colors.

Sunlight follows him.

---

The paladins salute.

Not to a prince.

To a brother.

---

He stops beside the King.

---

The hall understands.

---

**The Sun has chosen.**

---

## The Room

No one speaks.

They don’t dare.

---

But they **move**.

Not bodies.

Eyes.

Hands.

Breath.

---

---

---

Not awe.

Calculation.

---

---

He’s already adjusting.

---

---

> “…hah.”

---

A shake of the head.

Almost amused.

Almost.

---

> “Now _that_… is inconvenient.”

---

But he does not look away.

Not once.

---

For all his bluster—

he recognizes power when it walks in.

---

---

**Glandyr Ravenster** goes still.

Completely.

---

Not anger.

Not this time.

---

Something worse.

---

Understanding.

---

His eyes flick—  
once—  
to Kylesstra.

---

This changes her position.

His.

Everything.

---

He says nothing.

Because there is nothing safe to say.

---
## Kylesstra

She does not look at Porthos immediately.

---

That would be a mistake.

---

Instead—

she watches the **room reacting to him**.

---

The paladins.  
The King.  
The nobles shifting.

---

Only then—

does she look.

---

And in that moment—

the mask slips.

---

Just a fraction.

---

Because she understands faster than most:

---

This is no longer a negotiation.

---

This is alignment.

---

And she has just been placed on the wrong side of it.

---

---

## The Clergy

---

They bow their heads.

---

Not to the King.

---

To the light.

---

The Heironeans stand straighter.

Hands tighten on hilts.

---

The Pholtans do not move at all.

---

But one of them—

very slightly—

nods.

---

As if something has been… confirmed.

---

---

## The Lesser Nobles

This is where it breaks.

---

Some go pale.

---

Some look relieved.

---

Some look like men who just realized they backed the wrong horse—

and the race is already over.

---

A few begin to kneel—

then stop.

---

Because no one told them to.

---

And no one wants to be first.

---

---

## The Truth

No herald names it.

No priest declares it.

---

But it spreads anyway.

Fast.

Silent.

Unavoidable.

---

The thought passes from one mind to the next:

---

> _“We don’t get to decide this.”_

---

> _“It’s already decided.”_
