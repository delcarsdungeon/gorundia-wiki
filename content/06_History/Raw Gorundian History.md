**OR 645 = CY 1.**  
So for ancient negative CY dates, use **OR = CY + 645**. For later positive CY dates, use **CY = OR - 644**. Because calendars needed a no-year-zero trap, because apparently linear time wasn’t already annoying enough. The Keoland entry is a good sanity check: **303 OR = -342 CY**.

## Immediate canon fit

The clean structure is:

1. **Before c. 180 OR / -465 CY**  
    Gorundian Oeridians are already in place as old plains clans.
2. **c. 180-187 OR / c. -465 to -458 CY**  
    The “big migration” happens elsewhere. Many Oeridians head east into the Flanaess. Gorundian ancestors mostly stay put. This makes them culturally archaic Oeridian, not backwash migrants.
3. **223 OR / -422 CY**  
    Twin Cataclysms. Suel power breaks. Refugees and hard military survivors start moving. Your Rain file frames this as the disaster that scatters Suel survivors and eventually feeds the Dominion’s origin.
4. **226 OR / -419 CY**  
    Dominion is born from Rhola-Kesh survivors after the Hellfurnace crossing.
5. **Post-226 OR**  
    Dominion consolidates in the Khorovišk Valley, then begins pushing toward the western plains through trade, envoys, Hextorite priests, and fortified outposts.
6. **Later early kingdom era**  
    Gorundian clans settle, consolidate, form confederations, and eventually become a kingdom. Settlement creates towns, granaries, roads, confederations, and proto-noble clans.
7. **DeSalle placement**  
    House DeSalle’s serpent badge belongs after the first Gorundian kings start absorbing hill-clans, but before the noble hierarchy fully hardens. The snippet says the Serpent Men of Darkstone guarded iron roads and mine-mouths, then bent the knee while keeping the serpent, with the Crown adding the gold border as lawful arms.

So, yes: **the griffin story for Ryomar/Ravenster probably belongs in the same “clan badge becomes lawful arms” era**, not late chivalric heraldry. It should be older than the duchies, older than tidy Bannerhall etiquette, and tied to some chief-level myth where the griffin meant _sky-watch, royal omen, and war-leadership_ before it meant “fancy embroidered murder-bird on a noble shield.”
