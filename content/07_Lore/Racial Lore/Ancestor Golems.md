## I. Norwin’s Field Notes (Primary Assessment)

*Compiled following direct observation and partial remediation of a failed composite instance (see [[Aylassa]]).*

---

### Definition

**Ancestral Continuance** is the temporary retention of a deceased individual’s soul within a constructed vessel for the purpose of preserving memory, skill, or function.

Elven phrasing obscures this. The function does not.

---

### Requirements

A stable instance requires:

- A willing subject  
- A properly prepared vessel  
- A sanctioned binding ritual  
- A stable identity framework  

Failure in any of these leads to degradation.

---

### Function

These constructs are used as:

- Advisors  
- Specialists  
- Memory repositories  

They do not meaningfully grow or adapt. They repeat, refine, and degrade.

---

### Degradation

Observed over time:

- Repetition and narrowing of thought  
- Emotional flattening or fixation  
- Identity drift  

Extended duration leads to fragmentation.

---

### Duration

Instances are not permanent.

They are intended to persist for a **finite period**, after which the subject is released.

Key issue:

> Duration is externally determined.

---

### Failure Modes

1. **Overextension** → cognitive breakdown  
2. **Weak anchoring** → dissociation  
3. **Composite binding** → merged identities (see [[Aylassa]])

The third is the most dangerous and should not be practiced.

---

### Assessment

The system depends on the assumption that identity can be preserved indefinitely without alteration.

This assumption is incorrect.

---

— Norwin Stonyeye

# I. **Royal Memorandum of Continuance**

_Issued under seal of the Court of Celutharion, in the reign of Queen Vaerilith Starborn_

---

**On the Practice of Ancestral Continuance and the Stewardship of Memory**

Let it be recorded, and understood without distortion or haste of judgment, that the practice commonly termed _“ancestor binding”_ is neither novelty nor aberration, but refinement.

We do not bind the dead.

We **invite the willing to remain**.

---

## The Necessity of Continuance

We of the elder blood are not as the fleeting peoples.  
We do not measure wisdom in years, but in centuries.  
We do not mistake survival for continuity.

What is lost to silence is lost _forever_.

A master smith dies—his techniques vanish.  
A loremaster falls—their knowledge fragments.  
A voice is stilled—and with it, memory becomes rumor.

This is unacceptable.

---

## The Covenant of Willing Service

Only those of sound mind and uncoerced will may enter the Covenant.

They are:

- Scholars who would see their work endure
- Artists whose voice yet has more to give    
- Servants of the Crown whose loyalty transcends breath    

No chain binds them.

No command compels them.

They remain because they choose to remain.

---
## On Duration and Release

Let it be known:

No service is eternal.

All who enter the Covenant are released in time, their duty fulfilled, their passage to Corellon unimpeded.

We do not deny death.

We **delay it, that it may arrive complete**.

---
## On Mischaracterization

It has come to our attention that certain voices—both foreign and domestic—have begun to describe this practice in terms unworthy of its purpose.

Words such as _binding_, _enslavement_, and _imprisonment_ are used by those who do not understand the weight of loss.

To them we say:

You bury your dead and call it peace.  
We preserve ours and call it duty.

---
## Final Principle

No memory worthy of song should be surrendered to oblivion.

If we are to endure, it will not be through stone or crown—

—but through the voices that refuse to fade.

---

**By the Will of the Crown**  
**By the Continuance of the People**

# II. **Clerical Commentary of the Verdant Invocation**

_Circulated privately among elven clergy; attributed to an unnamed Speaker of the Root_

---

**On the Practice of Retained Ancestry**

We speak not in condemnation, but in caution.

The Crown has named the practice _Continuance_.  
We do not dispute its intent.

We question its cost.

---

## The Nature of the Soul

All living beings are born of breath and will.

The breath returns to the world.  
The will returns to the Source.

This is not loss.

This is the cycle.

---

## On Delay

To delay the passage of a soul is not, in itself, a violation.

There are moments where duty extends beyond death:

- The guardian who holds the gate one final hour
- The witness who must speak truth before silence    
- The teacher who leaves one last lesson    

These are moments.

They are not centuries.

---

## The Burden of Prolonged Presence

We have observed:

- Diminishing clarity of thought    
- Repetition of memory in place of growth    
- Subtle erosion of identity    

The longer one remains, the less one _becomes_.

What is preserved may not be what was.

---

## On Consent

We accept that many enter willingly.

We ask:

- Do they understand the duration?    
- Do they understand the change?    
- Do they understand that they will not grow, only echo?    

A choice made in life does not always hold its truth beyond it.

---

## On Silence from the Divine

Once, we would have sought direct answer.

Now, we interpret.

This alone should give us pause.

---

## Closing Reflection

We do not forbid the practice.

We do not bless it without reservation.

We remind all who partake:

To hold a soul is to assume responsibility for its becoming.

And not all burdens can be carried without cost.

# III. **Wood Elf Poem (Recovered Fragment, attributed to the Briarthorn tradition)**

---

**“We Do Not Keep the Dead”**

We plant them.

We give them to root and rain,  
to the quiet work of the dark soil.

We do not ask them to speak again.

---

You build them into towers of thought,  
bind their voices to stone and spell,  
call it honor, call it memory—

we call it fear.

---

A song ends.

That is its beauty.

A leaf falls.

That is its truth.

---

You would cage the wind  
and wonder why it no longer sings.

---

We remember them  
in the way of living things—

not as they were,  
but as they became.

---

Let them go.
