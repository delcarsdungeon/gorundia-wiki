# On the Halfbloods

## An Inquiry Concerning a People Mistaken for a Mixture

_Circulated without attribution. The earliest surviving copies appear to date from the third decade of the present century._

> _A thing incorrectly named is not thereby changed. Only our understanding of it is made worse._

### A Note Concerning Method

There are better libraries than those available to me.

There are scholars with access to royal genealogies, old elven histories, complete military rolls, and the sort of monastery collections in which a man may discover what three dead philosophers believed about a people none of them ever met.

I have had less.

I have consulted such histories and rolls as I could obtain. I have read military accounts of the western wars, several sermons concerning the mingling of peoples, two physicians upon inheritance, one of whom contradicts himself within six pages, and a truly dreadful travel account whose author appears to have learned everything he knew of orcs from a frightened mule.

I have therefore relied principally upon living witnesses.

Over many years I have recorded conversations with Halfblood laborers, caravan guards, soldiers, washerwomen, cooks, prostitutes, craftsmen, thieves, two men under sentence, three priests, several persons of uncertain occupation, and a considerable number who did not think their occupation was any of my business.

I have also spoken with humans, halflings and dwarves who have lived or worked among them.

I have not always taken names.

In several cases I was specifically instructed not to.

One should respect evidence even when the evidence has a knife.

This is not a census. I make no claim that a few streets, camps and border towns contain every variety of Halfblood life.

A lantern carried along one road does not illuminate the kingdom.

It does, however, illuminate the road.

---

# I. On the Word _Halfblood_

Among scholars and officials, the word _half-orc_ remains common.

Among the people themselves, **Halfblood** is increasingly preferred, and in some communities is nearly universal.

The distinction is not decorative.

The learned term begins with the assumption that such a person is principally the meeting-point of two other peoples. Half human. Half orc. A fraction before he is a man.

The common term has acquired another meaning.

A Halfblood is not understood merely as a person with two ancestries.

He is understood as belonging to **a third people**.

This claim will strike some readers as improper. Surely, they will say, blood is blood. One parent is human, the other orc, and arithmetic remains arithmetic however passionately the child objects.

But arithmetic is poor history.

A mule is not explained by separate essays upon horses and donkeys.

More importantly, a people is not created by blood alone.

A people is created by common circumstance, common habits, shared dangers, marriage, food, jokes, burial customs, grievances, neighborhoods, names, stories, and the expectation that another person will understand something before it must be explained.

By these measures, Halfbloods have become a people.

Possibly they became one some time ago, and the rest of us simply neglected to notice.

---

# II. Concerning Orcs, and a Common Human Error

The most persistent error made concerning Halfbloods is also the simplest.

Humans see orcish features and assume sympathy with orcs.

Tusks imply kinship. Grey skin implies kinship. Strength implies kinship.

This reasoning is understandable.

It is also very often wrong.

Among the Halfbloods with whom I have spoken, I have encountered **far more fear, hatred and disgust toward full-blooded orcs than affection for them**.

This fact surprises humans chiefly because humans have the luxury of regarding orcs as foreigners.

Halfbloods very often do not.

They remember them as raiders.

Overseers.

Mercenaries.

Camp masters.

Slave-takers.

The creatures on the other side of a locked door.

The soldiers who were permitted to do what regular soldiers were not supposed to do.

A teamster of approximately forty years gave me the clearest explanation:

> “You see one and think he looks like me.
> 
> I see one and wonder who he owns.”

Another, a woman from the western roads, laughed when I asked whether she felt kinship with orcs.

> “Do sheep feel kinship with wolves because both have four legs?”

I objected that she was not a sheep.

She replied:

> “Good. You are learning.”

There are exceptions.

There are Halfbloods who have traded peacefully with orcs, fought beside them, married among them, or retained affection for individual members of an orc tribe.

But the sentimental notion that Halfbloods naturally regard orcs as lost cousins appears to be largely a human invention.

Indeed, casually comparing a Halfblood to an orc may be considerably more insulting than the speaker understands.

The human believes he has remarked upon ancestry.

The Halfblood may hear that he has been compared to an ancestral enemy.

---

# III. Those Raised Among Orcs

There is a particular class of Halfblood that deserves separate consideration.

Among several communities they are simply described as **orc-raised**.

The phrase is rarely spoken admiringly.

Traditional accounts sometimes describe the Halfblood raised among orcs as an unusually cunning intermediary: weaker than a full orc but cleverer, surviving through deception, negotiation and careful manipulation of stronger tribesmen.

This description is not entirely false.

It merely mistakes an injury for a talent.

A Halfblood child in an orc camp is commonly smaller than those who threaten him and insufficiently orcish to enjoy whatever protections custom grants a full member of the tribe.

Strength cannot reliably protect him.

Open defiance cannot reliably protect him.

He therefore becomes skilled at something else.

He learns who is angry before the blow falls.

He learns which chief may be flattered.

Which warrior may be shamed only in private.

Which lie will be believed.

Which truth is more useful than a lie.

Who envies whom.

Who fears whom.

Who may safely be blamed.

Where food is hidden.

When an apology is required.

When an apology is dangerous.

Such a child may indeed grow into an extraordinarily perceptive adult.

It would be an obscenity to call this good fortune.

One Halfblood who had spent his childhood in such a camp told me:

> “I could tell who would win an argument before they spoke.”
> 
> I asked how.
> 
> “Because I had to know who to stand near when it ended.”

He was eleven when he escaped.

Halfblood communities often regard orc-raised adults with a complicated mixture of pity, suspicion and respect.

They are not considered “more truly orc.”

Quite the opposite.

They are understood to have learned habits required in a place where ordinary trust was unsafe.

An old dockworker put it with characteristic economy:

> “He was orc-raised.”
> 
> Seeing I did not understand, he added:
> 
> “Means he learned people wrong.”

This is perhaps unfair.

It is also perceptive.

A person taught from childhood that every relationship is a contest of power does not cease believing it merely because the room becomes kinder.

---

# IV. Names

Many Halfbloods choose their own names.

This practice has been dismissed as evidence of rootlessness.

I believe it is almost the reverse.

Birth records are frequently poor among the urban and frontier poor generally, and particularly poor among Halfbloods.

Some were given human names.

Some orcish names.

Some nicknames.

Some insults which became names because nobody bothered to provide another.

Some were recorded under whatever word a clerk believed he heard.

A surprising number reach adolescence and simply select something else.

The resulting names may appear ridiculous to outsiders.

Knuckles. Sunday. Little Ox. Nail. Pretty. Crooked Tom. Mother Hen.  Wonderful.  Brick.  Gutters.

The mistake is assuming the bearer does not know how the name sounds.

Often that is precisely why it was chosen.

A dangerous man may choose a foolish name because he enjoys forcing strangers to say it respectfully.

An ugly woman may choose a pretty name because nobody else ever called her pretty.

A large man may remain Little something because the name belongs to the child he was and to the people who knew him then.

Names among Halfbloods are frequently less concerned with ancestry than possession.

Not _Who were your fathers?_

But:

**What do you answer to now?**

There is a severe kind of dignity in that.

---

# V. Household, Kinship, and the Word _Ours_

Halfblood family structure is difficult to describe using noble genealogies, because Halfbloods have had the discourtesy to construct households according to survival rather than heraldry.

Blood relation matters.

It simply does not matter alone.

Children are frequently raised by mothers, older siblings, unrelated adults, surviving friends of dead parents, tavern households, work crews, mercenary companies, and whoever happened to remain when others did not.

This has created a very strong distinction between **belonging** and **ownership**.

A Halfblood may say:

> “She’s ours.”

To an outsider, this can sound possessive.

Usually it means almost the opposite.

It means:

_She eats here._

_She sleeps here._

_If she is sick, someone sits up._

_If she disappears, someone looks._

_If she is struck, someone asks why._

_If the answer is poor, several more people become interested._

One older woman corrected me sharply when I called two children in her house adopted.

> “Didn't adopt them.”
> 
> “Then whose children are they?”
> 
> “Ours.”

I attempted a more exact question concerning their legal parentage.

She told me to get out of her kitchen.

I have preserved the exchange because it explains the matter better than my question did.

---

# VI. Strength, Labor, and the Trap of Usefulness

Humans frequently praise Halfblood strength.

Halfbloods themselves are often suspicious of the compliment.

This deserves explanation.

A person valued principally for strength soon discovers that every opportunity offered to him requires lifting something, guarding something, frightening someone, breaking someone, or standing where arrows are likely to arrive.

A strong human boy may be told he would make a fine knight.

A strong Halfblood boy is more often told somebody needs a bouncer.

Or a pit fighter.

Or a caravan guard.

Or an infantryman whose principal tactical duty is to remain standing while better-born men decide what happens next.

These are honest occupations.

The insult lies not in the work.

It lies in assuming the worker is incapable of anything else.

A Halfblood wizard is treated as astonishing.

A Halfblood accountant is treated as amusing.

A Halfblood theologian would, I suspect, be treated as evidence that the gods possess a peculiar sense of humor.

Yet I have met Halfbloods who keep complicated gambling books entirely in their heads, remember debts across several years, identify lies from changes in posture, organize kitchens serving scores of people, and calculate the precise moment at which five drunk men will become six injured men.

The absence of schooling is not the absence of intelligence.

This ought not require argument.

Apparently it does.

---

# VII. Halfblood Women

Much nonsense has been written about Halfblood women.

I will contribute as little additional nonsense as possible.

They are commonly judged twice by appearance.

First, as Halfbloods: large, coarse, threatening, unfeminine.

Second, as women: available precisely because respectable society has already declared them improper.

These judgments combine unpleasantly.

A woman considered too ugly to protect may nevertheless be considered sufficiently desirable to purchase.

Those who work in taverns, fighting houses or brothels are especially vulnerable to the belief that visible flesh constitutes universal permission.

Halfblood communities consequently develop very clear practical distinctions between **looking, asking, paying, touching, and taking**.

Visitors sometimes mistake these rules for crudity.

They are generally clearer than the elaborate manners by which respectable men accomplish the same negotiations while pretending not to.

One woman summarized the difference:

> “You can think whatever you like.”
> 
> “Hands belong to you until you put them on me.”

I found no theological objection to this reasoning.

---

# VIII. On Humans

Halfblood attitudes toward humans are not easily summarized, partly because Halfbloods live among humans and therefore possess the unfortunate advantage of knowing them individually.

There is resentment.

There is also affection.

Halfbloods marry humans, work beside them, drink with them, worship beside them, rob them, protect them, fight them and become them in all the ways that matter socially.

What is notably uncommon is the desire to **become accepted as merely human**.

Younger Halfbloods especially seem less interested in proving that they are harmless versions of humans.

They wish instead for the right to be Halfbloods without that fact deciding the case before evidence is heard.

One man said:

> “Human fellow tells me I've got orc in me.”
> 
> “Well. He's got human in him. Never held it against him.”

Another:

> “Orcs gave us strength. Humans gave us sense.”
> 
> He considered this.
> 
> “Some humans.”
> 
> Then:
> 
> “Mostly we raised ourselves.”

That last remark is unfair to many decent human parents.

It is also difficult to dismiss after hearing it often enough.

---

# IX. The Dominion and the Inheritance of Fear

No account of Halfblood attitudes toward orcs can ignore the Dominion.

Dominion armies have long made use of peoples whom their officers regard as particularly suitable for violent work. Orcs and Halfbloods alike have served as auxiliaries, shock troops, camp enforcers and hired muscle under Hextorian banners.

The distinction between them matters enormously to Halfbloods even where Dominion officers found it convenient to ignore.

Several older witnesses told me stories of **orc overseers placed above mixed labor camps**, especially in frontier works and military supply operations.

The stories share common details despite having been collected far apart:

food withheld as discipline;

beatings conducted publicly;

Halfblood boys recruited as intermediaries because they could understand both orders and threats;

women moved between camps as though they were supplies;

and clever Halfblood subordinates promoted into petty authority so long as they kept others obedient.

I cannot independently prove every account.

I would be dishonest if I pretended their agreement meant nothing.

The Dominion also employed Halfblood troops in war.

This has created an ugly legacy.

Gorundians who fought them remember Hextorian Halfbloods as brutal enemy soldiers.

Halfblood civilians remember that those soldiers sometimes enjoyed rank, regular food, equipment, and legal standing unavailable to them elsewhere.

Thus a uniform hated by one Halfblood could represent the first institution that ever paid another on time.

People contain contradictions.

So do peoples.

Truth is not improved by removing inconvenient parts.

---

# X. Faith

I expected Halfbloods to be less religious than their neighbors.

This was foolish.

They are often less **institutional**.

That is different.

A person repeatedly turned away from respectable doors develops little affection for doors.

The gods themselves are another matter.

The Triad offers three ideas with obvious appeal to a people accustomed to being judged before speaking.

**Mercy**, because suffering does not become deserved merely because the sufferer is unpleasant to look upon.

**Courage**, because strength governed by duty is something nobler than strength governed by appetite.

And **Truth**, because truth, properly understood, has no face.

This last deserves more attention than it normally receives.

If testimony is true, the tusks of the witness do not make it false.

If a contract was made, skin does not unmake it.

If a man stole, the ancestry of the victim does not diminish the theft.

If a woman was struck without cause, the reputation of her street does not alter the bruise.

A law which first asks _what are you?_ before asking _what happened?_ is not examining the matter in the proper order.

I was once told by a learned man that this view was naive.

Perhaps.

But I have noticed that those who call equal law naive generally expect unequal law to favor them.

Halfbloods who find real religious instruction often take to it with startling seriousness.

Not all, certainly.

But for a person raised where every rule depended upon who was strongest in the room, there is enormous power in discovering a rule that claims to bind the strong man too.

Whether our institutions always fulfill that promise is another question.

They should.

A failed truth remains true.

It is we who have failed it.

---

# XI. Are Halfbloods Half Orc?

Obviously.

This is the sort of answer that satisfies people who ask the wrong question carefully.

Their ancestry is not disputed.

Their identity is.

To say Halfbloods possess orc ancestry tells us something about inheritance.

It tells us very little about allegiance, custom, morality, faith, or affection.

The Halfblood does not generally deny what is in his blood.

He denies that blood has completed the argument.

One young woman gave me what may be the simplest formulation:

> “We're not orcs.”
> 
> “We're not humans.”
> 
> “We're us.”
> 
> I asked what, exactly, _us_ meant.
> 
> She shrugged.
> 
> “Means if you have to ask, you're not.”

This answer annoyed me considerably.

After several years I have concluded that it was also correct.

---

# XII. Conclusion

Halfbloods are frequently described as a people between worlds.

I believe this description should be retired.

It suggests they stand forever upon a bridge, waiting to decide which shore deserves them.

Most are not waiting.

They have built houses on the bridge.

They have hung washing from it.

They have opened taverns.

Raised children.

Buried friends.

Started fights.

Ended others.

Prayed.

Worked.

Stolen.

Served.

Loved badly and well.

They have made customs from necessity and dignity from what others discarded.

This does not make them naturally virtuous.

No people is improved by being turned into a moral lesson for another.

There are cruel Halfbloods.

Cowards.

Bullies.

Murderers.

Fools.

There are also honest ones.

Patient ones.

Funny ones.

Men who protect children who are not theirs.

Women who feed strangers and pretend not to care whether they return.

Soldiers who hold.

Priests who pray.

Workers who save half a lifetime for one soft shirt simply because it belongs to them and no one can take it away.

This is not remarkable.

That is precisely the point.

A Halfblood is not an orc who has been partly civilized.

Nor is he a human spoiled by orcish blood.

He is not proof that two enemies can reconcile.

He is not proof that blood determines destiny.

He is not a symbol at all unless we insist upon making him one.

He is a person belonging to a people.

We should begin there.

For once, it would be useful to begin with the truth.
