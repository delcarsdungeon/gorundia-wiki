## **What Was Not Said**

No dwarf present needed the introduction.

Not really.

“Clan-sergeant of the deep ranks” was spoken for the humans.  
A courtesy. A simplification.

Among the stoneborn, the words meant something else entirely.

And every dwarf within earshot heard it clearly.

---

Hrothik Stonevein did not give his lineage.

He did not name his hold.

He did not list campaigns.

Because if those things needed saying—

he would not be the one speaking for them.

---

There are ranks among dwarves that are written.

Then there are ranks that are **carried**.

Hrothik carries his.

---

### **Among the Stoneborn**

A young shieldbearer, watching from the line behind the ballista, leans just slightly toward the veteran beside him.

> “That him?”

The older dwarf doesn’t look.

Doesn’t need to.

> “Aye.”

A pause.

> “Then stand up straighter.”

---

Hrothik is not the senior officer.

He is something far more dangerous:

The man the officers **listen to when no one else is around**.

---

There are oaths sworn to kings.

There are oaths sworn to clan.

And then there are the unspoken ones—

the kind that pass from shield to shield without words.

Hrothik is one of the ones who decides if those still hold.

---

### **Why He Speaks for Them**

Not because he was chosen.

Because no one else _argued it_.

---

In the deep ranks, authority does not come from appointment.

It comes from survival, judgment, and memory.

- He remembers which captains got men killed for pride
- Which holds stood when they should have broken    
- Which alliances held under pressure—and which cracked

And more importantly—

he remembers **why**

---

When Hrothik says:

> “I speak for the stoneborn attached to your command.”

What the dwarves hear is:

> “If you break them, you answer to me.”

---

### **The Pause After the Titles**

That moment—

that slight hesitation before the nod—

that was not uncertainty.

That was **weighing**.

---

Dwarves do not respect titles.

They respect _outcomes_.

And in that pause, Hrothik did the math:

- Bard’s Gate — survived
- Blackbriar — held    
- Champion — witnessed, not claimed
Conclusion:

> “Acceptable. For now.”

---

### **The Real Meaning of His Terms**

> “You give orders.  
> I decide how many dwarves live long enough to carry them out.”

No dwarf heard that as defiance.

They heard it as reassurance.

---

It means:

- He will not waste them    
- He will not let them be wasted    
- And if the command structure fails—  
    **he will step in before it becomes a graveyard**
    

---

### **Why No One Objected**

Not the courier.

Not the dwarves.

Not even the air around him.

---

Because men like Hrothik are the reason dwarven formations don’t break.

Not because they _can’t_.

Because they **decide not to**.

---

### **After the Scene**

Later—

quietly—

one of the dwarven engineers working the ballista mutters to another:

> “He’s attached to the command, then.”

The other grunts.

> “Aye.”

A beat.

> “That’s the king taking an interest.”

---

Because you don’t send a dwarf like Hrothik to observe.

You send him when:

- The stakes matter    
- The outcome matters    
- And you want someone there who can say, afterward—    

> “This was done right.”

—or—

> “This wasn’t. And here’s why.”

---

### **The Truth the Humans Won’t Get Yet**

To them, he’s a veteran sergeant.

Gruff. Competent. Maybe a little too comfortable talking back.

---

To the dwarves?

He’s the line between:

> “We followed orders.”

and

> “We held.”

---

And if he ever stops speaking—

That’s when the officers should start worrying.
