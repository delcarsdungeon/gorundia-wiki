# The Elven Point of View

Elves, though similar in outward form to humans, differ profoundly in how they perceive the world. Slighter of build and averaging near five feet in height, they are not so different physically as to create distance—but in thought and temperament, the divide is vast.

Elves, like most demi-humans, regard humanity with a mixture of suspicion and reluctant admiration. Humans advance quickly, achieve much in short spans, and burn brightly. Elves do not envy this. They simply do not understand the urgency.

## Time and the Elven Mind

The greatest divide between elves and other races lies in their perception of time.

An elf may live 1,200 years or more. In such a span, entire human civilizations may rise, flourish, and collapse. To an elf, this is not theory—it is memory.

Time, therefore, has little meaning to them in the urgent sense.

- There is no need to hurry  
- Few things demand immediate completion  
- Most efforts are measured in decades, not days  

Where shorter-lived races race against death, elves simply… do not.

## Death Without Fear

Elves possess a deep, ingrained acceptance of death as a natural part of existence. They do not seek it, but neither do they fear it.

This manifests mechanically and culturally:

- Immunity to effects rooted in fear of death (such as certain magical influences)  
- Resistance to panic in the face of mortality  
- A philosophical acceptance of life’s cycle  

Some traditions hold that elves are reborn after death, returning again to life in time. Whether true or not, the belief reinforces their lack of fear.

## Caution Over Bravado

Elves are brave—but rarely reckless.

Where humans may charge headlong into danger, elves weigh:

- What is lost if they fail  
- What time they still have ahead  
- Whether the risk is worth centuries of consequence  

They have too much life to waste it cheaply.

## The Long View

Elven decisions are guided by long-term outcomes rather than short-term gains.

Actions are taken not for immediate reward, but for:

- Future harmony  
- Lasting impact  
- Personal fulfillment across centuries  

Even their songs and dances serve this purpose—not idle distraction, but a means of sustaining joy over long lives.

Without such practices, immortality would become unbearable.

## Misunderstood by Other Races

To outsiders, elves often appear:

- Frivolous  
- Distracted  
- Wasteful of time  

This is a misunderstanding.

Elves understand time intimately—they simply refuse to be ruled by it.

## Empathy and Detachment

Elves possess a strong empathy for living things, particularly the natural world.

They feel a sense of unity with:

- Forests  
- Animals  
- The flow of life itself  

This leads to an unusual moral stance:

- They rarely hate other races outright  
- Even enemies are often viewed as temporary problems  

Orcs, for example, are not hated—only opposed.

There is one exception.

## The Drow Exception

Among all mortal races, only the drow inspire true hatred in elvenkind.

They are seen not merely as enemies, but as a betrayal of elven nature itself.

Against them:

- No mercy is given  
- No quarter is offered  
- No reconciliation is considered  

This hatred is ancient and absolute.

## Relationships and Isolation

Despite their empathy, elves form few deep bonds with shorter-lived races.

The reason is simple:

Everyone they befriend will die.

As a result:

- Friendships are rare, but meaningful  
- Many elves prefer the company of their own kind  
- Emotional distance is often a quiet defense  

## Curiosity and Secrets

Elves are drawn to knowledge, especially hidden knowledge.

They:

- Seek out secrets for the joy of discovery  
- Possess keen perception (both physical and emotional)  
- Value privacy deeply, even among themselves  

This duality makes them both insightful and inscrutable.

## Material Detachment

Elves place little value on material wealth.

They appreciate beauty and craftsmanship, but ownership itself is of minor concern.

Even elven thieves are rarely motivated by profit:

- They seek experience, not wealth  
- They value artistry over coin  
- They may even view theft as a lesson to the victim  

Nothing lasts forever—elves simply take that truth more seriously than others.

## Individualism and Authority

Elves are intensely self-willed.

They do not recognize rigid hierarchies in the same way other races do:

- Leaders are followed by choice, not obligation  
- Authority is respected only when it proves competence  
- Orders may be ignored if they seem unreasonable  

To outsiders, this appears chaotic. In practice, elven society remains stable through shared identity rather than imposed structure.

## Magic and the Elven Mind

Magic fascinates elves more than any other discipline.

They see it as:

- A source of endless variation  
- A means of expression  
- A tool for shaping long-lived existence  

They are among the most naturally gifted magic-users, though their minds differ from humans in ways that limit ultimate advancement.

## The Dreaming Memory

Elves do not sleep as other races do.

Instead, they enter a waking dream:

- Reliving past experiences  
- Exploring memory as a second reality  
- Remaining partially aware of their surroundings  

This explains:

- Their resistance to magical sleep  
- Their unusual alertness during rest  
- Their “distant” demeanor at times  

## Subtle Expression

Elven speech is soft and musical, layered with subtle emotional cues.

Most other races:

- Miss these nuances entirely  
- Misinterpret their meaning  

Much of elven communication is intentionally opaque to outsiders—another reflection of their love of secrets.

## Faith and Equality

Elven religion reflects their worldview.

They do not see their gods as distant rulers, but as kin.

- Corellon Larethian is regarded as progenitor, not master  
- Elven deities exist in cooperation, not hierarchy  
- Spiritual life mirrors their social structure: fluid, equal, and interconnected  

## War and Skill

Elves favor precision over brute force.

Their mastery of bows and swords reflects:

- Long-term practice  
- Foresight and perception  
- Cultural alignment with their deity’s favored weapons  

Combat, like all things, is approached with awareness rather than aggression.

## Population and Rarity

Elves reproduce slowly.

Their long lives naturally limit their numbers, keeping their populations stable but relatively small.

## Evil Among Elves

When elves turn to evil, it manifests differently than in other races.

They pursue:

- Destruction of life  
- Subversion of natural order  
- Silent, deliberate harm  

Such individuals are rare—and deeply feared.

## The Symbol of the Moon

The crescent moon stands as the perfect symbol of elven existence:

- Ever-present, yet always changing  
- Constant across centuries  
- Watching as others rise and fall  

Where humans measure time, elves endure it.
