# Lore of the Druids: The Root-Gnomes Before the Long Sleep

Among the elder druids of Nilanthalas, the forest gnomes were not counted as servants, subjects, or curiosities.

They were counted as root-kin.

They were small folk, quick of hand and bright of eye, with laughter close to the surface and caution never far beneath it. They were not mighty in the manner of princes, hunters, or warleaders, but the druids knew better than to mistake smallness for weakness. The forest has always kept some of its strongest things hidden under leaf, bark, and soil.

In the years before Jinwing’s long sleep, forest gnomes lived throughout the Great Forest in ways that left little mark upon the land. Some dwelt in permanent tunnel-nests beneath old hills and tangled rootworks. Others moved in seasonal camps, following springs, berry-thickets, animal paths, old druidic roads, and signs only their elders knew how to read.

Their homes were rarely seen by outsiders. A forest gnome tunnel-nest might lie beneath a foxglove rise, a beech grove, a bramble hill, or the roots of a fallen oak that had not truly fallen at all. The chambers below were warm, dry, and cleverly braced with root, clay, stone, and woven hazel. Smoke was carried away through hollow logs or old burrow vents. Doors were hidden beneath root mats, bark plates, thorn screens, or simple illusions that turned the eye aside.

The forest gnomes called such places nests.

Elves called them burrows.

Humans usually failed to find them.

The root-gnomes, as some druids named them, served the old forest in humble but necessary ways. They knew the narrow paths where a wounded child could be carried unseen. They knew which streams ran clean after battle and which pools held old sickness. They knew where the badgers nested, where the owls watched, and where goblins had once camped long enough to leave fear in the soil.

They spoke easily with small beasts, especially foxes, badgers, hares, owls, crows, and burrowing things. Their magic was not the formal spellcraft of towers and written circles. It was charm, glamour, misdirection, animal speech, hedge-rite, luck-knot, song-memory, and woodland craft woven together until only a patient mind could tell one thread from another.

The druids respected this.

Forest gnome magic did not command the forest. It persuaded it.

A root-gnome illusion was not merely a false image. It was a suggestion offered to shadow, leaf, mist, and fear. A trail might seem to bend. A hollow might appear empty. A campfire’s smoke might smell like damp bark. A band of children might pass under the eyes of enemies because the enemies suddenly found something else more important to notice.

The forest gnomes were also keepers of small memory.

They remembered the tree beneath which a vow had been sworn. They remembered which hill held the bones of an oath-breaker. They remembered where a druid had hidden seeds after a fire. They remembered minor names that did not enter songs, though those names had once saved lives.

To the old druids, this made them precious.

Great halls remember kings.

Burrows remember who survived.

When Jinwing went to sleep, the forest gnomes were still part of the living skin of Nilanthalas. They were scouts, charm-workers, herbalists, animal-speakers, seed-keepers, hidden guides, and quiet allies of the groves. They were not often named in the high songs of elven war, for such songs favored bright spears, crowned hunters, and tragic banners.

But the druids remembered.

When the forest had to hide something precious, it often trusted the small hands first.

## Names Used by the Druids

**Root-Gnomes**  
The most common druidic term. Respectful and plain. It marks them as people of hidden ways, burrowed memory, and living earth.

**Moss-Kin**  
An affectionate name used by grove druids and older woodland elves. It suggests closeness, softness, and stubborn survival.

**The Small Keepers**  
Used in rites where forest gnomes guarded seeds, children, messages, or sacred tools during danger.

**Burrow-Wise**  
A compliment, meaning one who understands the strength of what survives beneath the visible world.

**Foxglove Folk**  
A lighter name, sometimes teasing, sometimes fond. Forest gnomes usually accept it from friends and remember it from everyone else.

## After the Clay Rising

The Clay Rising broke the old pattern.

When Nilanthalas fell, many forest gnome camps were burned, scattered, or driven underground. Some tunnel-nests sealed their doors and never opened again. Others became refugee holes, spirit places, or warning sites where no gnome willingly sleeps. Many rootways were lost. Many animal paths were poisoned by fear. Many elders died carrying names that were never written.

The surviving forest gnomes endured by hiding, misleading, fleeing, and remembering.

Some remained in the Great Forest in hidden nests and moving camps. Some fled toward Araisal. Some followed the coast. Some vanished into druidic groves and returned only as rumor.

This is why modern forest gnomes are fewer than they were.

This is why they distrust maps.

This is why they answer old druidic signs before they answer noble summons.

And this is why Jinwing’s return matters.

To them, she is not merely an old druid awakened.

She is a witness from before the wound.
