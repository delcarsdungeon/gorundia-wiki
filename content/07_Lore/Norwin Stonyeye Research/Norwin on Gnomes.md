# Report to the Gorundian Mages’ Guild

## On the Gnomish Peoples: Rock, Forest, and the Rumored Deep

### Submitted by Norwin Stonyeye, Wizard of Teenkwidar Descent, Acting Consultant in Applied Arcane Systems, Field Thaumaturgical Irregularities, and Related Sensible Matters

To the Honored Examiners, Adjunct Reviewers, Licensed Practitioners, Seal-Carriers, Quill-Counters, and Other Persons of Apparent Authority within the Gorundian Mages’ Guild,

I submit the following report at the request of several parties who, after meeting one gnome wizard, apparently decided that my entire people could be reduced to “short, clever, probably owns too many tools.” This is not inaccurate, strictly speaking, but it is imprecise, and imprecision is how apprentices lose eyebrows, towers lose roofs, and humans write laws.

The renewed opening of Teenkwidar and its associated enclaves makes this subject no longer merely academic. It is now commercial, diplomatic, logistical, magical, and, gods preserve us, social.

Business will be conducted. Contracts will be signed. Trade will expand. Arcane exchanges will occur. Humans will attempt to simplify everything. Dwarves will pretend they are not interested. Elves will smile as though they invented courtesy and charge three generations of condescension for the privilege.

Therefore, some clarity is required.

There are, broadly speaking, three gnomish peoples relevant to Gorundian scholarship:

   
- Rock Gnomes, by far the most common among Gorundian gnomes and the primary people of Teenkwidar.   
- Forest Gnomes, confirmed kin, fey-touched, somewhat nomadic, magically infuriating, and fond of behaving as though a mushroom ring constitutes a laboratory.   
- Deep Gnomes, presently unconfirmed in any reliable Gorundian record and known chiefly through rumor, dubious dwarven campfire accounts, and stories told by miners who have been underground too long and should be fed fruit.   
 

I shall address each in turn.

## I. General Gnomish Taxonomy and Kinship

Rock and forest gnomes are not separate species, despite the claims of certain elven naturalists who become visibly excited whenever they get to divide people into categories while pretending it is compassion.

We are kin.

Rock gnomes and forest gnomes can intermarry freely. Such unions are viable, socially recognized, and often produce children who clearly favor one parent’s lineage over the other’s. This would be orderly enough, were that the end of it.

It is not.

Mixed rock-forest families have a surprisingly high incidence of twins. More maddeningly, it is not uncommon for one twin to show predominantly rock gnome traits while the other shows predominantly forest gnome traits. This has been documented often enough that even I cannot dismiss it as sentimental village exaggeration, though I confess the phenomenon offends my sense of inherited pattern.

To be clear: this is not magical “choice,” not a blessing, not a curse, and certainly not “the family spirit deciding which child belongs to root and which to stone,” as one forest gnome elder informed me while standing barefoot in a stream and calling that an interview.

It is hereditary expression. Mostly.

Probably.

Further research is required, preferably indoors.

## II. Rock Gnomes

Rock gnomes are the most common gnomes in Gorundia and the dominant people of Teenkwidar. We are, for lack of a better phrase, the normal ones.

This has caused some offense when said aloud. I stand by it.

### Appearance

Rock gnomes are typically shorter and sturdier than forest gnomes, usually around three feet in height, though individual variation exists. We tend toward compact builds, strong hands, large eyes, and prominent noses, the latter of which should be understood as an attractive and distinguished trait among civilized persons.

A well-formed rock gnome nose is not merely decorative. It is expressive, sensitive, socially meaningful, and useful when detecting smoke, bad solder, sour wine, or academic fraud.

Rock gnomes dress practically but not plainly. A human sees “too many pockets.” A rock gnome sees “nearly enough pockets.” Our clothing often includes tool loops, hidden clasps, reinforced stitching, watch charms, measurement cords, coded guild pins, and at least one compartment nobody else is supposed to know about.

### Temperament

Rock gnomes are grounded, technical, meticulous, verbal, exacting, and inclined toward systems. We appreciate mechanisms, ledgers, proof, repeatability, proper measurements, and written safety procedures.

Some outsiders interpret this as fussiness.

Those outsiders are welcome to continue living in buildings assembled by “feel.”

Rock gnomes prefer to know why something works. If it works only once, under moonlight, while a blue moth is present and a druid hums in Gnomish-accented Sylvan, then it does not “work.” It has happened. There is a difference.

### Magic

Rock gnomish magic tends toward:

   
- Enchantment   
- Illusion by design rather than whim   
- Alchemy   
- Optics   
- Measurement and calibration spells   
- Construct theory   
- Applied wards   
- Engineered spell matrices   
- Communication devices   
- Magical tools, keys, lenses, locks, traps, and self-correcting mechanisms that only occasionally become self-aware   
 

This is not because rock gnomes are biologically incapable of nature magic or hedgecraft. We are not. It is cultural. We train toward structure because structure allows knowledge to be shared, repeated, improved, audited, and billed.

A rock gnome wizard is happiest when a spell does exactly what it is written to do, at the expected cost, with predictable side effects and a clear maintenance schedule.

Naturally, this means life keeps happening to us.

### Teenkwidar and the Guild System

Teenkwidar’s magical institutions are famously elaborate. The central guild structure includes licensed spellwrights, instrument makers, ward inspectors, lenswrights, theorem auditors, rune solicitors, alchemical assessors, and at least seven categories of apprentice that nobody outside Teenkwidar can properly distinguish without a chart.

This is good.

Mostly.

It prevents dangerous incompetence, preserves standards, records magical discoveries, regulates trade, and ensures that no apprentice can accidentally sell a self-heating tea kettle that interprets “warm” as “molten.”

It also creates forms.

Many forms.

Forms for approving spell trials. Forms for revising approved spell trials. Forms requesting permission to reclassify a failed spell trial as an unexpected theoretical success. Forms to determine whether the prior form was submitted in the correct ink. Forms that, by tradition, cannot be filed until the reviewing clerk returns from lunch, even if the reviewing clerk died forty years ago and his department has since become a storeroom.

This is also good.

In principle.

I will concede, with reluctance, that certain minds of exceptional flexibility may find the system restrictive. I have occasionally been accused of being such a mind. This accusation is insulting, because it is both true and beside the point.

Now that Teenkwidar is opening again, the Guild should prepare for negotiations with gnomish institutions that are precise, cautious, contractual, proud, and very interested in trade. Expect requests for reciprocal licensing, patent recognition, component standards, spell safety classifications, and anti-plagiarism clauses enforced by people who consider a three-page footnote to be a warning shot.

Do not mistake bureaucracy for weakness.

A rock gnome who asks you to sign six copies of a document has already considered what happens if you refuse.

## III. Forest Gnomes

Forest gnomes are confirmed kin to rock gnomes, despite their determined effort to behave like a footnote written by a butterfly.

They are generally taller than rock gnomes, commonly around three and a half feet, with lighter builds, larger eyes, finer features, and a more overtly fey quality to their faces and movements. They often possess a nimbleness that looks elegant until one realizes it is being used to avoid helping carry equipment.

They are somewhat nomadic. Reliable communities are known or strongly suspected in the following regions:

- At least two good-sized enclaves within Araisal lands.
- A likely, though poorly documented, presence somewhere in or near the old woodland reaches of Nilanthalas.
- Several wandering or semi-settled groups along the Axe Coast.

I have not collected full details on these communities because, despite what some people assume, I do have work.

Also, forest gnomes are exceptionally difficult to inventory. Ask one where his village is and he may answer, “where the foxgloves remember us.” Ask for a road and he may give you a song. Ask for a map and he may look wounded, as though you have insulted his grandmother’s moss.

### Temperament

Forest gnomes are clever, perceptive, socially nimble, and often annoyingly competent. They also display many traits commonly associated with elves: grace, evasiveness, poetic ambiguity, an unhealthy confidence in old trees, and the ability to make a simple answer feel like a seasonal event.

Unlike elves, however, forest gnomes usually mean well.

This distinction is important.

Elves tend to patronize everyone, but they are genuinely fond of forest gnomes. This is similar to the elven treatment of tallfellow halflings: affectionate, protective, lightly insulting, and draped in enough graceful superiority to make any sensible person consider arson.

Tallfellow halflings, for their part, have developed the remarkable ability to let elven condescension slide off them like rain off waxed canvas, provided the food is good and nobody mentions height twice in one conversation. Forest gnomes respond differently. They often accept elven affection, steal elven idioms, learn elven songs, and then quietly make fun of elven solemnity behind their backs with alarming accuracy.

This is one of their better qualities.

### Magic

Forest gnome magic is the part of this report where I must restrain myself from using language unsuitable for guild archives.

They do not, as a culture, maintain proper divisions between arcane theory, hedge-wizardry, druidic ritual, fey compact, folk charm, illusion craft, herbal practice, and superstition. They blend these things together into a messy soup and then have the gall to produce results.

A forest gnome may cast an illusion using a rhyme learned from an aunt, a gesture copied from an elf, a focus made of beetle shell, three berries, and what he insists is “the mood of the creek.”

Then the illusion works.

Not always. Not cleanly. Not in a form any responsible examiner would certify without several evenings of drinking.

But it works.

This is the sort of thing that makes proper wizards look unreasonable when we object.

Forest gnomes are especially associated with:

- Illusion   
- Glamour   
- Animal speech   
- Concealment   
- Minor weather-charms   
- Nature magic   
- Luck-working   
- Fey bargains and warding customs   
- Spells that are technically not spells until they become inconveniently effective   
 

A troubling number are self-taught. Worse, many are competent.

Their instinctive grasp of light, sound, scent, fear, attention, and misdirection is remarkable. A trained forest gnome illusionist can vanish a wagon, silence a camp, redirect a patrol, or convince a hostile officer that he has urgent business in the wrong direction. A self-taught one may do the same accidentally while trying to avoid a conversation.

Let the Guild note this clearly: forest gnome spellcraft is not primitive. It is undisciplined.

There is a difference.

Primitive magic cannot survive serious examination.

Undisciplined magic can survive examination, embarrass the examiner, and leave berries in his shoes.

### Religion and Fey Association

Forest gnomes frequently honor the gnomish pantheon, as do rock gnomes. They also commonly show reverence toward Ehlonna, especially in Araisal and woodland communities. Many respect the Gorundian Triad: Pelor, Heironeous, and Pholtus. Their respect, however, is practical rather than exclusive.

A forest gnome may light a candle to Pelor at dawn, leave bread for a fox spirit at noon, invoke Ehlonna before entering old woods, swear by Garl Glittergold over dinner, and then hang a silver bell over the door “because the little moon-things dislike the sound.”

When challenged, they will often say, “It cannot hurt.”

This is false.

Many things can hurt.

That said, it often works, and that is the most irritating part.

### The “Color-Drained” Myth

Among some forest gnomes there exists a myth that rock gnomes were once brighter, freer, and more fey-touched, but lost this color when we turned away from root and moon, choosing stone, tools, ink, and regulation.

In this telling, rock gnomes are “what happens” when gnomes forget the forest.

This is theological slander with poor sample size.

Rock gnomes are not faded forest gnomes. We are not failed fey. We are not tragic little clerks of the under-root. We are a distinct and thriving gnomish culture whose achievements include durable enchantment, precision optics, multi-stage warding gears, licensed alchemical pressure valves, and bridges that do not collapse because someone sang to them in spring.

It is true that rock gnome coloration, clothing, and magical expression tend to be more subdued than the brighter, more variable traditions of some forest communities.

This is called taste.

## IV. Deep Gnomes

No confirmed contact exists between Gorundian gnomes and so-called deep gnomes.

None.

Let that word remain visible, because humans have a habit of converting “unconfirmed” into “basically true” and then building three policies and a tavern song around it.

Deep gnomes are a rumor. Possibly a memory. Possibly an underground cousin branch. Possibly a dwarven joke that became ethnography through repetition, which is how half of human scholarship seems to operate.

The stories vary wildly.

Some claim deep gnomes live beneath ancient mountain roots, below even dwarf-worked halls. Others say they inhabit caverns where no torch has ever burned. Some say they farm fungus, speak to crystals, and hear movement through stone. Several miners insist they have seen small figures vanish into walls. One Orenic tunnel crew claimed deep gnomes have no noses, which is so clearly false, insulting, and physiologically offensive that I nearly refused to record it.

No gnome people could properly develop without noses.

That is not bias. That is civilization.

### Common Rumors

The most persistent claims include:

   
- Deep gnomes can see in perfect darkness.   
- They speak very little, or communicate through tapping, stone-sense, or mineral resonance.   
- They distrust surface folk, including other gnomes.   
- They carve memories into stone because spoken words are considered too fragile.   
- They are resistant to illusion, perhaps because underground life makes visual glamour less dominant.   
- They can hear lies spoken through stone floors.   
- They know the old names of mountains from before the dwarves shaped them.   
- They can walk through unworked stone during eclipses.   
 

Most of this is nonsense.

Some of it is interesting nonsense.

The claim that deep gnomes hear lies through stone floors is probably metaphor, though I cannot entirely dismiss the possibility of a specialized tremor-reading discipline combined with magical lie detection. A concealed listener under a chamber could, in theory, identify changes in stance, pulse, and stress through floor vibration, though this would require absurd training, excellent hearing, and a floor of suitable composition.

So, naturally, I hate that it is plausible.

The claim that deep gnomes know mountain names older than dwarven settlement is mythic phrasing, but not useless. If an isolated gnomish population existed underground prior to or alongside early dwarven expansion, they may preserve place-names or geological lore otherwise lost. This possibility should interest the Guild, the dwarves, and anyone who enjoys watching academic conferences become knife fights.

The eclipse-stone-walking claim is almost certainly magical exaggeration.

Almost.

There are known spells that allow passage through stone. There are old earth rites, dwarven and otherwise, tied to moon and shadow cycles. If a subterranean gnomish people exists and developed earth-aspected magic independently, a restricted form of stone passage under rare celestial conditions is not impossible.

It is absurd.

But absurd is not impossible. I work with adventurers. I have had to lower my standards for reality.

### Recommended Guild Position

Until confirmed contact occurs, the Guild should treat deep gnomes as a possible hidden people rather than established fact.

Do not send heavily armed expeditions into dwarven depths looking for them. This will irritate the dwarves, alarm any deep gnomes who do exist, and likely result in a diplomatic incident described afterward as “unfortunate” by whichever idiot survives.

Instead:

   
- Collect mining accounts from dwarven enclaves and deep road crews.   
- Compare old stone-marks, especially those too small or too delicate for dwarven hands.   
- Investigate recurring legends from separate regions for shared details.   
- Do not publish speculation as fact.   
- Do not ask whether they have noses.

## V. Relations with Other Peoples

### Humans

Humans are energetic, adaptable, and dangerously comfortable with temporary structures. They are also excellent trading partners because they need everything quickly and rarely understand the long-term value of what they are buying until after a gnome has priced it correctly.

Human mages often divide spellcraft into neat institutional categories. This is respectable. They then ignore those categories whenever a war, prince, demon, prophecy, inheritance dispute, or emotionally unstable noble requires immediate improvisation. This is less respectable, though admittedly familiar.

Rock gnomes work well with humans when contracts are clear and deadlines are not invented by shouting.

Forest gnomes work well with humans when the humans stop asking where the forest gnome “really lives.”

### Dwarves

Dwarves are excellent craftsmen, reliable allies, and constitutionally incapable of admitting when a lighter solution would work.

Rock gnomes and dwarves often cooperate productively in mining, stonework, gear assembly, metal fitting, defensive engineering, and drinking after technical disagreements. Dwarves respect competence and endurance. Gnomes respect precision and cleverness. Both sides respect tools.

The difficulty is philosophical.

A dwarf sees a mountain and asks how to make it stronger.

A rock gnome sees a mountain and asks what can be hidden inside it, automated beneath it, measured through it, or made to open only when the correct sequence of pressure plates is triggered.

Both approaches have merit.

The gnome approach is more interesting.

### Elves

Elves are graceful, ancient, learned, dangerous, and in need of interruption.

Their fondness for forest gnomes is genuine, but it carries the usual elven habit of treating shorter-lived peoples as charming seasonal phenomena. This is not malice. It is worse: manners.

Forest gnomes often benefit from elven goodwill, especially in Araisal lands and near old woodland enclaves. They adopt elven songs, courtly phrases, herbal knowledge, and magical aesthetics. In return, elves enjoy having small, clever woodland kin who validate their belief that trees are better company than most people.

Rock gnomes have a more complicated relationship with elves. We admire their arcane preservation, craftsmanship, and long memory. We are less impressed by their tendency to take two centuries to decide whether a hinge should be silver or moon-silver.

### Halflings

Halflings are sensible until food is involved, at which point they become religious.

Gnomes and halflings share certain social advantages: underestimated stature, strong community networks, quick hands, and the ability to hear large folk say stupid things directly over our heads. Rock gnomes admire halfling logistics more than halflings realize. A halfling kitchen during a feast has more disciplined workflow than many human military camps.

Tallfellow halflings deserve special mention because elves often treat them much as they treat forest gnomes: fondly, patronizingly, and with the faint implication that proximity to elven grace has improved them. Tallfellows usually endure this with a smile that means, “You are lucky I am polite and currently holding a pie knife.”

Forest gnomes and tallfellows often get along well, though their conversations are unbearable to overhear. Too many metaphors. Too much laughing at things not yet explained.

### Half-Orcs

Gnomish relations with half-orcs vary widely by region and circumstance. Rock gnomes tend to judge half-orcs as we judge everyone: by competence, reliability, and whether they break equipment they were specifically told not to touch.

Forest gnomes are more likely to react to mood, scent, posture, and perceived intent. This can make them either more accepting or more evasive, depending on the half-orc in question.

The wise gnome remembers that being underestimated is unpleasant.

The wiser gnome remembers not to do it to someone with arms larger than his torso.

## VI. Practical Notes for Guild Mages

The following recommendations are offered for Guild personnel likely to interact with gnomish communities.

### When Dealing with Rock Gnomes

   
- Bring written proposals.   
- Define terms precisely.   
- Do not say “basically the same.”   
- Do not touch tools without permission.   
- Assume any device has at least one function you have not noticed.   
- If a rock gnome asks whether you are “certain,” reconsider your last sentence.   
- If a rock gnome asks whether you are “absolutely certain,” leave the room.   
 

### When Dealing with Forest Gnomes

   
- Do not demand a direct answer immediately unless lives depend on it.   
- If lives do depend on it, say so plainly. Most forest gnomes are flighty, not cruel.   
- Do not mock charms, bells, knots, offerings, or “little customs.” Some are nonsense. Some are not.   
- Expect magic that does not fit Guild categories.   
- Do not assume lack of formal training means lack of ability.   
- If invited to follow music into the woods, ask where the path returns before stepping onto it.   
- If they laugh and say, “Usually here,” do not go.   
 

### When Investigating Deep Gnome Reports

   
- Record the account before correcting the witness.   
- Compare accounts geographically.   
- Ask dwarves carefully and respectfully.   
- Avoid armed intrusion.   
- Treat stone markings as possible writing until disproven.   
- Do not lick glowing minerals.   
- I should not have to write that last instruction, but I have met apprentices.

## VII. On Gnomish Religion

Both rock and forest gnomes commonly retain reverence for the gnomish pantheon. This is true even among communities that publicly honor the Gorundian Triad.

Respect for Pelor, Heironeous, and Pholtus is common and sincere, especially in Gorundian-settled regions. Rock gnomes appreciate Pelor’s honest labor, Heironeous’ disciplined courage, and Pholtus’ devotion to order, even if Pholtine officials sometimes behave as though law was invented to make rooms less pleasant.

Forest gnomes are more likely to add Ehlonna to their household rites, particularly in woodland and Araisal-adjacent enclaves. This reflects both their nature magic and their social closeness with elven and half-elven communities.

A typical rock gnome household might keep a proper shrine, recorded feast days, ancestor tools, and a small ledger of vows made and fulfilled.

A typical forest gnome household might keep a shrine, a hanging charm, a bowl of milk for something nobody agrees exists, a carved fox, three saint-candles, a pressed flower, and a story about why the west window must never be latched during the first rain of spring.

Both arrangements function.

One is simply easier to audit.

Deep gnome religion is unknown. Claims range from ancestral stone reverence to worship of forgotten earth gods to absolute silence on divine matters. No responsible conclusion can be drawn at this time.

## VIII. Final Assessment

The Guild should abandon any attempt to treat “gnome magic” as a single subject.

Rock gnomes and forest gnomes share ancestry, language roots, kinship, and certain social instincts, but their magical cultures diverge sharply.

Rock gnomish magic is structured, engineered, documented, and commercially mature. It is suitable for formal exchange, licensing, and guild partnership, provided human institutions can restrain their habit of making everything worse by rushing.

Forest gnomish magic is informal, hybridized, intuitive, and entangled with fey and druidic practice. It is not safe to dismiss. It is not safe to fully trust without examination. It is especially not safe to let forest gnome charm-workers demonstrate “a harmless little glamour” inside a room containing mirrors, cats, or junior nobility.

Deep gnomes remain unconfirmed, but repeated subterranean legends justify cautious archival investigation.

Teenkwidar’s reopening should be treated as a major opportunity. Rock gnome enclaves are prepared to do business, exchange magical knowledge, license devices, establish component standards, and negotiate durable trade arrangements. The Guild would be wise to approach with respect, patience, and competent clerks.

Not friendly clerks.

Competent ones.

There is a difference, and gnomes will notice.
