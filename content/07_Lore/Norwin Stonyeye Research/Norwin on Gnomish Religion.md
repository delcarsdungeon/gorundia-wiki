# Norwin Stoneyeye on the Gnome Pantheon

> [!quote] Norwin Stoneyeye  
> I included a brief religious section in my prior cultural survey because leaving it out would have been irresponsible. Apparently including it was also irresponsible, because several readers then asked whether gnomes have gods. Yes. We do. We did not spring fully formed from a pantry wall with spectacles, opinions, and a working knowledge of compound pulleys.

## Preliminary Complaint

Since my earlier notes were evidently read with the care normally reserved for tavern wall graffiti, I will clarify the obvious.

Gnomes possess an old and varied religious tradition. It is not uniform. It is not tidy. It is not reducible to “they like tricks and gems,” despite the heroic efforts of certain human scribes to reduce every people they meet into one marketable tavern joke.

The gnome pantheon includes gods of wit, protection, illusion, metalwork, travel, earth, invention, burrows, memory, and, unfortunately, the thing under the floorboards that respectable families do not discuss at dinner.

In Gorundia, most gnomes honor the old gods privately, while publicly navigating the dominant religious structure of the kingdom: the Triad of Law and Light. This is not hypocrisy. This is adaptation. The difference is apparently subtle enough to injure scholars.

## The Gnome Pantheon in Brief

The following summary is necessarily compressed. A proper treatment would require three volumes, twelve genealogical charts, a comparative liturgical table, and at least one locked cabinet for the portions involving Urdlen. I have spared you this because I am merciful, despite evidence to the contrary.

## Garl Glittergold

Garl Glittergold is generally regarded as the chief figure of the gnome pantheon: patron of wit, cleverness, community, gems, laughter, protection through intelligence, and the sacred art of making a larger person regret underestimating you.

He is not merely a prankster, though that is the version outsiders prefer because it lets them imagine gnomes as decorative nuisances. Garl’s laughter is not idle. It is laughter as resistance, correction, survival, and social pressure. When a village elder tells a story where the arrogant lord slips into a dung cart, the point is not the dung cart. The point is that pride has weight, wheels have motion, and the clever survive by knowing when to step aside.

Among Gorundian gnomes, Garl is most often invoked at births, apprenticeships, negotiations, and public embarrassments that become “teachable moments.” These are, in practice, sometimes indistinguishable.

## Baervan Wildwanderer

Baervan is the patron of travel, forests, roads, hospitality, hidden paths, and the sort of wandering that looks irresponsible until one returns with three useful maps, a treaty, and a cheese no one asked for but everyone appreciates.

He is especially favored among forest gnomes and traveling tinkers. His worship blends easily with road shrines, woodland customs, and offerings left beneath old trees. He is also one reason some forest gnome rites are nearly impossible to distinguish from rustic druidry, fey etiquette, and whatever the local foxes appear to be doing.

This is not a criticism. It is an observation.

Mostly.

## Baravar Cloakshadow

Baravar is the god of illusion, concealment, protective deception, magical misdirection, and the survival value of not being where the axe lands.

Many human writers treat Baravar’s worship as morally suspicious, because humans have built entire legal systems on saying one thing while meaning another and then become offended when someone else does it with style.

Baravar is not the god of lies for their own sake. He is the god of masks that protect the vulnerable, shadows that hide refugees, false trails that save villages, and illusions that turn a massacre into an empty road.

Gorundian gnome illusionists often respect Baravar even when they do not worship him openly. In regions where Pholtine influence is strong, his shrines tend to be discreet. This is not because Baravar is evil. It is because Pholtans react to nuance the way cats react to bathwater.

## Flandal Steelskin

Flandal is patron of mining, smithing, metalcraft, heat, endurance, and skilled labor that leaves burns on the hands and useful things in the world.

He is popular among rock gnomes, especially those tied to mining, foundries, jewelry, clockwork, and the kind of careful metalwork humans call “delicate” right before they break it.

Flandal’s worship is practical. Prayers are spoken over tools, bellows, solder, gears, gem settings, and forge doors. The faithful tend to be less interested in sermons than in whether the hinge holds, the alloy sings true, and the apprentice still has all ten fingers.

Gorundian Flandal worship often overlaps with guild practice, particularly among metalworkers and engineers. This has occasionally led outsiders to mistake his faith for merely a trade cult. This is like mistaking a paladin’s oath for a sword-polishing schedule.

## Gaerdal Ironhand

Gaerdal is the patron of vigilance, discipline, defense, martial readiness, and the difficult virtue of standing watch while everyone else is asleep and dreaming of pastries.

He is not the most beloved gnomish god, because discipline is rarely beloved by sensible people. But he is respected. Every burrow gate, caravan watch, militia drill, and emergency tunnel plan owes something to Gaerdal’s influence.

Gorundian gnomes who serve in city watches, engineering corps, military logistics, fortified settlements, or noble household defenses often keep a token of Gaerdal. They may also honor Heironeous, particularly if they have served alongside Gorundian soldiers long enough to appreciate the distinction between bravery and theatrical shouting.

Gaerdal’s followers are not humorless. They simply believe the joke is better after the perimeter is secure.

## Segojan Earthcaller

Segojan is god of earth, burrows, the dead beneath the soil, deep memory, roots, tunnels, gems, and the proper relationship between living communities and the ground that shelters them.

His rites are among the oldest and least public. He is invoked at burials, mine openings, new burrow foundations, cave-ins, family memorials, and moments when the earth is asked to hold something safely.

In Gorundia, Segojan’s worship rarely conflicts with Wee Jas or Pelorite funerary customs, because gnomes are entirely capable of acknowledging that the soul has one road, the body another, and the family still needs somewhere to stand during the service.

Segojan’s priests are often quiet, patient, and extremely difficult to move once they have decided a place matters. Imagine a rock with opinions and family records.

## Nebelun

Nebelun is patron of invention, risk, creative madness, experimentation, sudden insight, and the small smoking crater that teaches valuable lessons about pressure containment.

Not every gnomish inventor worships Nebelun, but nearly every gnomish inventor has at some point said, “I think I understand what happened,” in a tone that counts as prayer.

Nebelun’s worship is strongest among artificers, experimental alchemists, arcane engineers, lens-makers, tinkerers, and those dangerous persons who hear the phrase “that cannot be done” as a formal invitation.

In Gorundia, Nebelun’s faithful are tolerated because their failures are usually localized and their successes are profitable. This is, admittedly, the same moral framework used for half the noble houses.

## Rill Cleverthrush

Rill is sometimes treated as minor, which is how one knows the writer has no understanding of gnomes. She is associated with speech, wit, music, satire, storytelling, verbal craft, and the ability to make a tyrant look smaller without drawing a sword.

Her influence survives in songs, clan histories, insult-poems, children’s rhymes, coded jokes, and public recitations where everyone laughs at the obvious meaning while the intended target turns red for the hidden one.

In Gorundia, Rill’s worship is often informal. A traveling bard, guild clerk, tutor, or grandmother with a terrifying memory may do more for her cult in one evening than a temple could in a year.

## Callarduran Smoothhands

Callarduran is most often associated with deep places, stone, patience, hidden communities, and what are usually described as deep gnomes.

And here we arrive at the part where someone will inevitably ask whether deep gnomes are real.

As I stated before, and as I will now restate because literacy is apparently an optional field of study: we do not know.

There are rumors. There are trade signs. There are old tunnel marks in places no surface gnome claims to have dug. There are stories of pale-eyed, stone-colored gnomes who move beneath the roots of mountains and speak only when necessary, which already makes them difficult to believe as gnomes.

Some miners claim that deep gnomes honor Callarduran as a guardian of hidden halls and buried communities. Others insist they worship Segojan under another name, or a stern underworld form of Garl stripped of laughter. A few claim they have no gods at all, only ancestral echoes and stone-oaths.

These accounts are contradictory, poorly sourced, and frequently delivered by men who were trapped underground for four days licking condensation from slate.

So, officially: deep gnomes remain unconfirmed.

Unofficially: if they do exist, they appear to be secretive, subterranean, cautious, religiously conservative, and unlikely to appreciate being summarized by a surface gnome wizard writing under protest.

## The Problem of Forest Gnome Worship

Forest gnomes are real. That is already inconvenient enough.

Their religion is also real, though one may be forgiven for mistaking parts of it for hedge-wizardry, druidic rite, fey bargain, seasonal superstition, animal etiquette, and elven cultural seepage collected in a mossy bowl.

Many forest gnomes honor Baervan, Segojan, Baravar, and Garl, but not always in clean temple forms. Their rites are tied to groves, springs, fox dens, mushrooms, old stumps, moonlit clearings, and other places where a sensible person checks whether the shadows are watching back.

In Gorundia and the older elven-influenced lands, some forest gnomes also honor Ehlonna.

I will not pretend to fully understand this.

Ehlonna’s worship among forest gnomes seems to emphasize woodland shelter, gentle beasts, flowers, young life, safe paths, and the protection of beautiful places from ugly people. This is entirely admirable. It is also frequently expressed through wreaths, antlers, whispered prayers to deer, and the belief that a rabbit appearing during a naming ceremony is “answering.”

It may be.

That is the maddening part.

Forest gnomes do not appear to find contradiction between honoring Baervan as the wanderer of hidden paths and Ehlonna as the lady of the green places those paths pass through. They will leave bread for Baervan, flowers for Ehlonna, a polished pebble for Segojan, and a carved joke-token for Garl, all at the same shrine, then look at you oddly if you ask which god the shrine belongs to.

This is why formal theology gives me headaches.

## Gnome Worship in Gorundia

Gorundia is bound, officially and culturally, to the Triad of Law and Light: Pelor, Heironeous, and Pholtus.

Gnomes living inside Gorundian towns, guild systems, noble domains, and chartered cities do not ignore this. That would be socially foolish, legally inconvenient, and in some districts likely to result in a stern conversation with a priest who owns both a lantern and a list.

Most Gorundian gnomes therefore practice layered worship.

The old gnomish gods remain household, clan, craft, and burrow powers. The Triad governs public oath, law, healing, civic duty, royal order, and military obligation.

This is not unusual. Humans do something similar whenever they swear by Heironeous before a battle, pray to Pelor over a sick child, and then ask Pholtus to make the neighbor stop moving boundary stones. They simply call it “proper religion” when they do it.

## The Triad Among Gnomes

### Pelor

Pelor is the easiest of the Triad for gnome communities to embrace. Healing, honest labor, mercy, warmth, and protection of the common folk are hardly offensive concepts, even when humans insist on making them into processions.

Gnome households often respect Pelorite healers, especially in mixed towns. Pelor’s priests tend to be useful, kind, and less likely than others to ask whether a burrow shrine is “authorized.”

Pelorite charity also fits naturally beside gnomish communal obligation. Feed the sick. Warm the cold. Mend the roof. Share what you can. Try not to make a theological crisis out of soup.

### Heironeous

Heironeous is honored by gnomes who serve in the military, town defenses, engineering corps, knightly households, and fortified caravans.

He is less common as a primary household god among gnomes, not because gnomes lack courage, but because gnomish courage is usually less interested in shining armor and more interested in whether the escape tunnel has been trapped correctly.

Still, Heironeous commands respect. Gnomes who have seen true paladins at work tend to distinguish them from ordinary armored loudness.

Gaerdal and Heironeous coexist well enough. Gaerdal watches the wall. Heironeous leads the charge. Sensible people arrange not to need the charge.

### Pholtus

Pholtus is complicated.

Gnomes appreciate law, contracts, recordkeeping, sworn testimony, and civic order. We do not appreciate being told that the universe contains exactly one proper shape and that all other angles are moral failures.

Gnomish merchants, clerks, barristers, guild advocates, and civic engineers often honor Pholtus publicly, especially in courts and charters. His priests are useful where contracts are concerned, and their intolerance for sloppy wording is, admittedly, admirable.

But Pholtine certainty grates against gnomish habits of layered meaning, protective secrecy, and tactical ambiguity. A Pholtan sees a hidden door and asks why it was concealed. A gnome asks whether it opens inward, outward, or onto someone’s kneecaps.

Both questions have merit.

Only one is fun at parties.

## Triad Worship in Gorundian Gnome Communities

In practice, a Gorundian gnome settlement may have no grand temple to the gnome gods. Instead, it may have:

- a Pelorite healing room near the public square;
    
- a legal shrine or lantern alcove used for Pholtine oath-taking;
    
- a small Heironean blade or shield mounted near the militia hall;
    
- household icons to Garl, Segojan, Flandal, or Baervan;
    
- workshop prayers spoken over tools;
    
- burrow-stones marked with ancestral names;
    
- one elderly relative who insists the fox behind the mill is sacred and must not be insulted.
    

This is not disorder. It is a functioning religious ecosystem.

The old gods govern identity. The Triad governs citizenship.

That distinction will save you a great deal of confusion if you allow it to.

## Zilchus and the Merchants’ Guild

Before anyone attempts to make this a “gnome merchant religion” issue, let us establish something clearly.

The Merchants’ Guild is not an exclusively gnomish operation.

It never was.

It contains humans, dwarves, halflings, half-elves, gnomes, and whatever sort of person looks at a crisis and wonders whether freight rates will improve. This is not a race. This is a profession. A spiritual illness, perhaps, but not a race.

Zilchus, patron of commerce, wealth, trade, negotiation, contracts, and the movement of coin through hands that insist they are clean, is therefore not a gnome god. His worship within the Merchants’ Guild reflects the guild’s trade culture, not gnomish ethnic practice.

That said, gnomes in mercantile circles often find Zilchus comprehensible. Trade is not inherently wicked. Fair exchange, clear accounts, reliable weights, enforceable contracts, and productive risk are the bones of civilization. Without them, we are left with banditry, noble privilege, and people saying “trust me” while reaching for your purse.

A gnome merchant may honor Zilchus for trade, Garl for wit, Flandal for craft, Pholtus for contract witness, and Rill for the ability to explain an unfavorable clause so sweetly that the other party thanks them.

This is not confusion. This is specialization.

Priests of Zilchus in guild districts tend to concern themselves with ledgers, debt, credit, trade roads, arbitration, tariffs, inheritance of commercial shares, and the ritual blessing of scales. Their temples are usually clean, well-funded, and filled with people pretending not to calculate the resale value of the candlesticks.

## The Merchants’ Guild and Moral Drift

A healthy guild understands that trade requires trust.

A sick guild discovers that fear also produces payment.

This is where Zilchus worship can curdle, not because Zilchus is some hidden monster, but because commerce attracts the same kind of ambitious scavenger that carrion attracts flies. A contract can protect the weak. It can also bind them until they bleed. Debt can finance a shop. It can also become a collar.

When a merchant begins speaking of “necessary pressure,” “collections irregularities,” “protective tariffs,” and “corrective enforcement,” check whether he has recently hired men with clubs.

If he has, the gods are no longer the problem. Men are.

It is almost always men.

## Urdlen

And now, because the world apparently requires balance, we must discuss Urdlen.

Urdlen is the dark stain in gnomish religion: the Crawler Below, the blood-hunger under the burrow, the blind thing that gnaws. Its cult is associated with greed stripped of craft, hunger stripped of need, secrecy stripped of purpose, murder, envy, spite, and the destruction of community from beneath.

I do not use the word “evil” lightly.

Here, it is insufficient.

Urdlen is not a god of honest darkness. Segojan has darkness. Mines have darkness. Winter cellars have darkness. Night roads have darkness. Darkness shelters. Darkness preserves. Darkness lets a frightened child hide until danger passes.

Urdlen is not that.

Urdlen is the darkness that chews through the support beams.

The cult of Urdlen is mercifully rare, but it appears in the places where gnomish virtues rot into their opposites. Cleverness becomes sadism. Privacy becomes conspiracy. Craft becomes trap-making for its own sake. Love of treasure becomes hoarding. Clan loyalty becomes blood purity and paranoia. Burrow-defense becomes kidnapping strangers in tunnels and calling it “security.”

There are accounts of Urdlen cults forming under mining communities, ruined burrows, collapsed guildhouses, and districts where debt, shame, and resentment are allowed to ferment without light or law.

No respectable gnome community tolerates them.

No healthy temple shelters them.

No sane elder says, “Perhaps they have a point.”

They do not have a point.

They have teeth.

## Urdlen and the Merchants’ Guild

Here is where the matter becomes unpleasant.

A corrupt merchant guild does not automatically become an Urdlen cult. Most corruption is depressingly ordinary. Bribes, ledgers, intimidation, rigged weights, predatory loans, false scarcity, protection rackets. The usual parade of human achievement, only with better ink.

However, Urdlen cults can thrive in the shadow of mercantile corruption because both enjoy secrecy, debt, resentment, and people disappearing into rooms no one discusses.

Where Zilchus becomes the excuse for ruthless accumulation, Urdlen waits underneath as the appetite that no longer needs an excuse.

One says, “Profit must be protected.”

The other says, “Take. Hide. Gnaw. Kill.”

If you cannot hear the difference, resign from public life immediately and take up mushroom sorting under supervision.

## Warning Signs of Urdlenite Influence

A community should be concerned when it sees several of the following together:

- repeated disappearances near cellars, mines, sewer routes, or old burrow works;
    
- bloodless thefts escalating into mutilation or body concealment;
    
- children warned not merely away from tunnels, but away from certain relatives;
    
- clan records altered to erase marriages, debts, or births;
    
- hoards of coin, gems, teeth, bone, or personal tokens hidden without trade purpose;
    
- traps designed not to defend, but to torment;
    
- religious icons defaced from below, especially by clawing or gnawing marks;
    
- merchants or guild factors using debt as justification for confinement;
    
- whispers of “the white mole,” “the hungry one,” “the thing beneath,” or “the old hunger.”
    

Naturally, one sign alone proves nothing. A gnome cellar full of odd noises is usually plumbing, bad storage, or someone’s uncle testing a kettle-valve.

Three signs deserve investigation.

Five signs deserve armed investigation.

Seven signs deserve fire, priests, and someone with the sense to block the exits.

## Final Clarification Regarding Deep Gnomes

Because I can already hear the question forming in the skull of some reader who believes persistence is the same as scholarship:

No, I am not claiming deep gnomes worship Urdlen.

No, I am not claiming deep gnomes worship Callarduran.

No, I am not claiming deep gnomes do not exist.

I am claiming the evidence is insufficient, contradictory, and heavily contaminated by miner folklore, elven rumor, dwarven silence, goblin nonsense, and the testimony of people who spent too long underground breathing bad air.

If deep gnomes exist, they may have their own old gods, their own names for our gods, or no patience whatsoever for surface classification. Given the quality of surface classification, I would not blame them.

## Final Clarification Regarding Forest Gnomes

Forest gnomes do worship the gnome gods.

They also honor forest powers, local spirits, fey-touched customs, elven rites, and, in some areas, Ehlonna.

I do not understand all of it.

This does not make it false.

It does make it irritating.

There is a difference.

## Concluding Note

Gnomish religion is not a single temple with a single altar and a single priest telling everyone where to stand. It is a layered structure of clan, craft, road, burrow, household, settlement, guild, and kingdom.

A gnome may pray to Garl before a negotiation, Flandal before soldering a hinge, Segojan at a burial, Pelor over a sick child, Pholtus when signing a contract, Zilchus when opening a shop, Baervan before a journey, Baravar when hiding refugees, and Gaerdal when locking the gate.

This is not inconsistency.

This is having more than one problem.

Perhaps humans should try it sometime.
