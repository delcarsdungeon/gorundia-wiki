# A Concordance for the Noble Reader Concerning the Gnomish Peoples

## Practical Notes Upon Master Stonyeye’s Report and Lord Moonshade’s Commentaries

> [!quote] Sophia Amadis, Lady Blackbriar
> Master Stonyeye teaches the noble reader not to underestimate gnomes. Lord Moonshade teaches the noble reader not to understand them too quickly.

## Purpose of This Concordance

The recent exchange between Master Norwin Stonyeye and Lord Faelar Moonshade concerning the gnomish peoples has produced a body of commentary both useful and, in places, unnecessarily armed.

This is not unusual in scholarship.

It is, however, inconvenient for noble readers who may require guidance without first mastering every ancestral grievance, archival habit, theological irritation, and species-level insult carried into the discussion by its participants.

I therefore offer this concordance not as a correction of either scholar, still less as an attempt to rival their expertise, but as a practical guide for noble houses, court officers, magistrates, guild patrons, military administrators, and other readers whose duties may eventually require dealings with gnomish communities.

Master Stonyeye gives us excellent field caution.

Lord Moonshade gives us interpretive discipline.

The noble reader requires both, provided neither is mistaken for policy in its raw form.

## On Reading Master Stonyeye

Master Stonyeye is at his strongest when he observes.

His account of gnomish kinship, practical magical distinctions, rock gnome institutional habits, forest gnome unpredictability, and deep gnome uncertainty is valuable precisely because he writes from within gnomish experience. He knows the habits of his people as a man knows the floorboards of his childhood home: which ones creak, which ones hold weight, and which ones conceal something no visitor should touch.

That intimacy is a strength.

It is also a danger.

Master Stonyeye often mistakes familiarity for neutrality. His fondness for rock gnome order and suspicion of forest gnome ambiguity lead him to describe one mode of life as normal and another as frustrating. He does not always ask whether the frustration belongs to the subject or the observer.

This does not make his observations useless.

It makes them observations in need of framing.

The noble reader should trust Master Stonyeye most when he describes practical conduct, magical habits, institutional requirements, and likely risks. He should be read more cautiously when he explains what those facts mean.

Master Stonyeye sees mechanisms clearly.

He sometimes grows impatient with meanings that do not behave like mechanisms.

## On Reading Lord Moonshade

Lord Faelar Moonshade is at his strongest when he interprets.

Where Master Stonyeye separates, Lord Moonshade relates. Where Master Stonyeye classifies, Lord Moonshade asks what older pattern or inherited memory may lie beneath the classification. His commentaries are particularly valuable in reminding the reader that a people’s customs are not always confused merely because they resist administrative categories.

This is a necessary correction.

It is not a final answer.

Lord Moonshade is often the more elegant reader, though elegance must not be mistaken for finality. He is rightly alert to history, symbolic meaning, ancient source material, and the dangers of dismissing non-academic knowledge. He is also, by habit and inheritance, inclined to find meaning where a practical officer may only need procedure.

The noble reader should trust Lord Moonshade most when he explains relation, context, historical depth, and interpretive error. He should be read more cautiously when the question before the court is immediate administration.

Lord Moonshade sees the canopy.

He occasionally forgets that magistrates must still repair the road beneath it.

## Points of Agreement Usually Hidden by Tone

It is worth noting that the two scholars agree more often than their styles suggest.

Both accept that rock and forest gnomes are kin rather than separate peoples in any hard species sense.

Both accept that rock gnomes are the most visible and institutionally significant gnomes in Gorundian civic and guild contexts.

Both accept that forest gnomes are not primitive, foolish, or magically inept.

Both accept that forest gnome magic deserves caution rather than dismissal.

Both accept that deep gnomes are unconfirmed.

Both accept that armed expeditions into dwarven depths in pursuit of rumor would be unwise.

Both accept that elven records, gnomish testimony, and practical observation each preserve truths the others may miss.

The quarrel is not chiefly over facts.

It is over interpretation.

This is why noble readers must be careful. Facts may be carried into policy quickly. Interpretation should be invited to walk, sit, drink water, and stop waving a sword before it reaches the council table.

## Forest Gnomes: Relation, Not Disorder

Master Stonyeye describes forest gnomes as confirmed gnomish kin, fey-touched, somewhat nomadic, socially nimble, and magically difficult to classify. His practical warnings are valuable. A noble house dealing with forest gnome communities should not expect their magic, settlements, customs, or religious observances to fit neat guild categories.

Lord Moonshade’s corrective is equally necessary.

Forest gnome life should not be mistaken for disorder merely because its order is relational rather than bureaucratic. Their customs are shaped by woodland, path, shrine, story, animal, season, and local compact. They may appear indirect where they are in fact precise. They may appear evasive where they are in fact observing courtesies the noble reader has not yet earned the right to notice.

For governance, this has practical consequences.

A noble officer should not approach forest gnomes as though dealing with a chartered guildhall. Nor should one treat them as rustic children needing firm instruction. Neither error will produce trust, and one suspects both will produce laughter in one’s absence.

When dealing with forest gnomes:

* secure trust before demanding clarity;
* ask who may properly speak for a path, grove, camp, or household;
* do not dismiss charms, bells, seasonal rites, or animal signs as nonsense;
* do not assume elven influence means elven origin;
* do not assume indirect answer means refusal;
* do not mock the rabbit.

The final point appears absurd until it is not.

## Rock Gnomes: Structure, Not Superiority

Master Stonyeye is especially strong on rock gnomes, and especially partial.

His account of rock gnome craft, recordkeeping, clothing, tools, magical engineering, bureaucratic habits, and Teenkwidar’s institutional culture is indispensable. Noble readers dealing with rock gnome communities should pay very close attention to his practical advice. Rock gnomes will notice sloppy terms, unclear obligations, imprecise measures, and contracts that rely upon goodwill where definition was required.

Lord Moonshade’s correction is that visibility is not normality.

Rock gnomes are more legible to noble houses because they produce ledgers, guild structures, devices, licenses, workshops, and formal records. This does not make them the true form of gnomish life. It makes them easier for courts to read.

The distinction matters.

A noble house that assumes rock gnome habits are gnomish habits will misread forest gnomes. A noble house that assumes rock gnome order is mere fussiness will be robbed, out-negotiated, embarrassed, or possibly asked to sign twelve copies of the lesson.

When dealing with rock gnomes:

* bring written proposals;
* define terms precisely;
* do not touch tools without permission;
* respect licenses, guild categories, and craft standing;
* expect long memory in trade;
* assume hidden compartments are present, literal or otherwise;
* never mistake paperwork for weakness.

A rock gnome asking for clarification is not stalling.

He is deciding how expensive your mistake should become.

## Deep Gnomes: A Matter of Limited Governance

The matter of deep gnomes is the least immediately useful to the noble reader.

Master Stonyeye is correct to refuse confirmation. Lord Moonshade is correct that absence from records is not proof of absence. The traditions, rumors, cave accounts, dwarven silences, strange place-names, and elven fragments gathered by both scholars are interesting.

Very interesting.

Possibly important.

Eventually.

For present governance, however, deep gnomes remain speculative.

The question of deep gnomes belongs chiefly to archivists, dwarven antiquarians, mining scholars, deep-road surveyors, and those rare noble heirs who can say “subterranean ethnography” without visibly hoping for rescue.

Until such a people presents itself to treaty, tithe, court, road, border, military obligation, trade negotiation, or marriage alliance, the matter remains closer to disciplined cryptid-hunting than governance.

This is not meant as mockery.

Cryptid-hunting, when performed by competent scholars rather than excitable younger sons with lanterns, may preserve useful fragments of truth. Lord Moonshade’s treatment of deep gnome rumor is valuable as a study in scholarly uncertainty. Master Stonyeye’s caution is valuable as a restraint upon idiocy.

Still, the noble reader should draw no policy from deep gnome speculation beyond the following:

* do not fund armed searches into dwarven depths;
* do not publish rumor as fact;
* do not offend dwarven holds by asking theatrical questions;
* do not assume all small stone-marks are children’s scratches;
* do not build law upon a people no one can prove exists.

In this matter, the correct noble policy is patient non-interference.

A rare and underused art.

## On Gnomish Religion

Though this concordance chiefly concerns the threefold comparison of the gnomish peoples, no noble reader should separate gnomish culture from gnomish worship too sharply.

Master Stonyeye is helpful in describing layered religious practice: the old gnomish gods as household, clan, craft, burrow, road, and settlement powers; the Gorundian Triad as public, civic, military, legal, and charitable structure.

This distinction is politically useful.

A gnome who honors Garl, Segojan, Flandal, Baervan, Baravar, or Rill privately may also honor Pelor, Heironeous, or Pholtus publicly and sincerely. This should not be mistaken for duplicity. It is layered loyalty.

Lord Moonshade’s broader correction is also sound. Layered worship is not confusion. A shrine may answer more than one need because a household has more than one problem.

For noble houses, the recommendation is straightforward:

Do not treat gnomish household devotion as disloyalty to the Triad.

Do not treat forest gnome reverence for Ehlonna as elven mimicry.

Do not treat rock gnome guild prayers as mere trade superstition.

Do not treat hidden Baravar rites as criminal without evidence of criminal use.

Do not treat Urdlenite signs lightly.

That last point admits no clever qualification.

## Errors Most Likely Among Noble Readers

The following errors are common enough to deserve naming.

### Error One: Treating Rock Gnomes as Small Dwarves

Rock gnomes are not dwarves with smaller tools and more opinions.

They share certain interests in craft, stone, engineering, and underground work, but their culture is more iterative, more flexible, more contractually nimble, and often more experimental.

A noble who treats gnomish engineering as lesser dwarven engineering will pay too much for the wrong bridge and deserve the invoice.

### Error Two: Treating Forest Gnomes as Small Elves

Forest gnomes are not elves in miniature.

They may share woodland habits, fey-touched customs, songs, herbal knowledge, and magical aesthetics with elven neighbors, but they remain gnomish in kinship, humor, scale, adaptation, and social practice.

A noble who treats forest gnome elders as charming woodland ornaments may find the path home becoming unusually educational.

### Error Three: Treating Deep Gnomes as Fact

Deep gnomes may exist.

They may not.

Until confirmed, they are not an administrative category.

No wise noble house taxes, treaties, threatens, recruits, accuses, or marries into a rumor.

### Error Four: Treating Norwin’s Tone as Evidence

Master Stonyeye’s irritation is often entertaining.

It is not itself proof.

### Error Five: Treating Faelar’s Elegance as Proof

Lord Moonshade’s prose is often beautiful.

Beauty is not itself evidence.

There.

I have now offended both traditions equally enough to proceed.

## Practical Recommendations for Noble Houses

For noble houses expecting contact with gnomish communities, I recommend the following:

### With Rock Gnomes

* appoint one literate officer responsible for all terms, measurements, and obligations;
* assume negotiations will be remembered longer than you prefer;
* respect tool ownership and workshop boundaries;
* place technical questions before social questions;
* do not condescend to their size;
* do not improvise contract language;
* provide competent clerks, not merely polite ones.

### With Forest Gnomes

* seek introduction through trusted local intermediaries;
* accept that location, authority, and custom may be described relationally;
* ask permission before entering groves, camps, shrines, or hidden paths;
* respect Ehlonna rites and local forest observances;
* allow time for indirect answer;
* never assume hospitality means political submission.

### With Mixed Rock-Forest Families

* do not ask which child “counts as what”;
* do not comment on twins as though they are a breeding curiosity;
* do not assume household practice follows one lineage only;
* ask the family what customs they observe, then believe them.

### With Rumors of Deep Gnomes

* record;
* compare;
* consult dwarves carefully;
* do not send adventurers unless there is already a concrete hazard;
* resist the noble urge to turn a mystery into an expedition with banners.

Mysteries hate banners.

So do many people.

## On Policy and Scholarship

The noble reader must remember that scholarship is not policy.

Scholarship informs policy.

It does not replace judgment.

Master Stonyeye’s report offers practical caution, especially valuable to guilds, officers, mages, and negotiators. Lord Moonshade’s commentaries offer interpretive caution, especially valuable to courts, diplomats, patrons, and those who might otherwise mistake administrative legibility for truth.

Neither should be read alone.

A policy based only on Master Stonyeye risks becoming efficient, insulting, and blind to meaning.

A policy based only on Lord Moonshade risks becoming elegant, slow, and vulnerable to practical surprise.

The noble house requires both.

It must see the mechanism and the custom.

The contract and the courtesy.

The map and the path-song.

The pocket and the charm.

The guild seal and the rabbit.

Again, do not mock the rabbit.

I include the warning twice because noble readers often require repetition when the matter does not involve inheritance.

## Final Counsel

Master Stonyeye is rarely wrong in observation, though often undisciplined in conclusion.

Lord Moonshade is rarely careless in interpretation, though occasionally too pleased with the length of the road by which he arrives.

Between them lies something approaching wisdom.

The noble reader should neither laugh off Master Stonyeye’s practical warnings nor drown in Lord Moonshade’s beautiful cautions. Gnomish peoples are neither a single administrative category nor a charming collection of eccentricities for court amusement. They are kin-groups, households, guilds, wanderers, priests, inventors, traders, charm-workers, elders, children, and possible hidden cousins whom no one has yet confirmed without sounding like they spent too long underground.

In governance, the first duty is not to understand perfectly.

It is to avoid acting stupidly while understanding remains incomplete.

Treat rock gnomes as precise partners.

Treat forest gnomes as relational neighbors.

Treat deep gnomes as unconfirmed theory.

Treat gnomish religion as layered loyalty.

Treat both Master Stonyeye and Lord Moonshade as useful, difficult scholars whose greatest agreement is this:

Gnomes are not to be underestimated.

Nor, if one is wise, understood too quickly.
