Zara & Jinwing — Second Draft, Private Quarters

The door is already open.

Not wide—just enough that Jinwing doesn’t have to knock.

Zara knew she was coming.

Inside, the cabin is dim and warm, lanternlight swaying with the motion of the ship.

Zara stands near the wall.

Barefoot.

Half-dressed.

The spider-silk bodysuit clings to her like it was grown there.

Her hand rests against the wood—fingers spread, feeling the grain, the faint vibration of the hull.

She drags them slowly along it.

“…you can hear it breathe, if you stand still long enough,” she says.

Then, a small glance back—just enough to acknowledge who she’s speaking to.

A flicker of dry amusement.

“You already know that.”

A beat.

“Of course you do.”

Her fingers press once more into the wood.

Deliberate.

Respectful, even.

“Wood remembers,” Zara adds quietly.

“Better than most courts.”

Now she turns fully.

“Good,” she says. “You came.”

She doesn’t move to greet Jinwing.

Doesn’t close distance.

Instead, she returns her attention to the spider-silk, her hand sliding from the wall to her own forearm.

Watching. Feeling.

“This—”

A soft exhale.

“—this is why I stay like this.”

She traces the line of her arm, the silk shifting with the motion.

Measured. Intentional.

“In my true form… this is gone.”

Plain. Clinical.

“No texture. No immediacy.”

A glance toward Jinwing.

She adjusts slightly—just enough to meet her where she lives.

“You feel the difference more than most,” Zara says.

“Between bark and skin. Between root and hand.”

A small tilt of her head.

“I suspect you don’t confuse them.”

She reaches for a bracer, but pauses again.

Her fingers tap lightly against her ribs.

“Rain becomes… information,” she continues. “I know it falls. I do not feel it.”

A faint narrowing of her eyes.

“Here? I know the first drop.”

She fastens the bracer.

The pressure settles over the silk.

Her eyes close—just for a moment.

“…and this,” she murmurs, “I feel.”

She rolls her wrist.

Then, unconsciously, her free hand finds the edge of the table again.

Resting there.

Grounding.

Zara notices Jinwing noticing.

Of course she does.

A faint smile.

“Tell.”

She flexes her fingers lightly against the wood.

“I do that,” she says. “More than I should.”

A small shrug.

“I like knowing where I end.”

Her gaze sharpens slightly.

“You and I—” a small gesture between them “—we both wear shapes that are… compromises.”

She studies Jinwing.

“Yours for control.”

A beat.

“Mine for sensation.”

Another piece of armor—shoulder.

She settles it into place.

The silk compresses beneath it.

Again—that flicker of awareness.

“I suspect,” Zara adds dryly, “if we compared notes, we might arrive at something resembling a whole creature.”

A brief sidelong glance—just enough mischief to register:

“And yes… the more scandalous advantages of being soft-skinned.”

She clocks Jinwing’s reaction.

Moves on immediately.

Her tone shifts.

Quieter now.

“But there is a cost.”

She lifts the cuirass, holding it for a moment.

“Every hour spent like this… it becomes easier to forget which form is mine.”

Her eyes meet Jinwing’s.

“And which one is… safer.”

The cuirass goes on.

Heavier now.

More distant.

Her hand returns—again—to the table’s edge.

Just for a second.

Just to feel it.

Then she pulls away from it.

Deliberately.

“I’m going to see the king.”

Simple. Direct.

“I intend to renew my oaths.”

A beat.

“I may not leave that room.”

No performance.

Just fact.

“You tried to save me.”

A small nod.

“I won’t forget that.”

Gauntlet.

She pulls it on slowly.

Flexes her fingers.

Less sensation now.

She notices.

Moves on.

“I’ve made arrangements.”

Her gaze returns to Jinwing.

Sharper now. Focused.

“There was… an object placed in my care.”

A pause.

“The Greenfather’s Core.”

Even naming it shifts something in the room.

Subtle.

Alive.

“The Raven Queen found it intolerable.”

“Kursk wanted it removed from the forest.”

“And I was the only one available who could both understand it… and survive proximity.”

She steps closer.

This is no longer casual.

“It does not belong to me.”

Flat. Certain.

“It never did.”

A beat.

“It will be delivered to you.”

Not if.

Not maybe.

“A trusted agent. Not one of the obvious ones.”

A flicker of dry humor.

“I’ve learned better than that.”

She studies Jinwing carefully now.

Measuring.

“You won’t need to guess what it is,” Zara says.

“You’ll feel it.”

A pause.

“And more importantly… it will feel you.”

Then—clean, direct:

“You are to return it.”

No flourish.

No apology.

“To where it belongs.”

A slight narrowing of her eyes.

“Not where it is safe. Not where it is useful.”

A beat.

“Where it is right.”

She lets that settle.

“This is not a contingency,” Zara adds.

Quiet. Firm.

“I am asking you to do this whether I walk out of that room… or not.”

A small tilt of her head.

Almost curious.

“You understand the difference.”

She straightens.

Mask settling back into place.

“I considered vaults. Wards. Loyal men.”

Dismissed with a breath.

“They would have tried to keep it.”

Her eyes fix on Jinwing.

“You won’t.”

Final adjustment to her armor.

“If I return,” she says lightly, “we’ll pretend this conversation never happened.”

A faint flicker of amusement.

“If I don’t…”

A small shrug.

“Try not to let anything grow where it shouldn’t.”

And just before she passes—

one last, quieter line.

Almost an afterthought.

“It deserves better than all of us.”

Then she’s gone.

And now it’s not just a burden—

It’s a charge.

### **“Before the Door Closes” — Dawn Ride 

The sky is still deciding what it wants to be.

That thin, gray-blue hour where the world hasn’t quite committed to morning.

The carpet lifts from the *Second Draft* without ceremony—just a quiet rise into cold air and open space.

---

Zara is already armored.

Mostly.

Cuirass, shoulder, one gauntlet secured—

the other left off.

Deliberately.

---

Her cloak is pinned back enough that the wind can reach her.

It catches her hair, lifts it, pulls it loose from its careful order.

She lets it.

---

She stands near the edge, one hand bare, fingers brushing the weave of the carpet.

Testing it.

Feeling it.

---

“…this,” she says quietly, “I would miss.”

A slow inhale.

The air is cold, moving, alive against her skin.

Her face.

Her hand.

---

“As a dragon, this would be… distant.”

A faint exhale.

“Not absent. Just… delayed.”

She turns her hand slightly, letting the wind press against her palm.

---

“Here?”

A small, almost private note of satisfaction.

“I feel it immediately.”

---

The carpet shifts with a current.

Without thinking, her hand reaches out—

finding Thalrik’s shoulder.

---

It stays there.

Not for balance.

Not quite.

---

“You’re steady,” she says.

Simple. Observed.

---

A pause.

Then, quieter:

“I appreciate that.”

---

Her hand lingers a moment longer—

then withdraws, not abruptly, but with awareness.

---

For a time, they ride in silence.

No flirting.

None of the easy, sharp-edged banter.

---

Something else has settled in its place.

---

Zara lifts her chin slightly into the wind, eyes half-lidded as she feels it across her face.

---

“I’m going to walk in,” she says eventually.

“The last stretch.”

A glance toward the distant city.

“The Temple of Heironeous deserves that much theater.”

Dry.

But respectful.

---

“They’ll say I was teleported away when the dragon appeared.”

A faint tilt of her head.

“A contingency. Sensible. Expected.”

A ghost of a smile.

“Let them.”

---

She flexes her bare hand once, feeling the air again before it disappears beneath steel.

---

“The truth is less useful.”

---

A beat.

Then—

---

“The king may kill me.”

No hesitation.

---

“In the temple.”

---

She turns her head slightly toward him—not fully, but enough.

---

“As is his right.”

---

The wind shifts.

Stronger now.

---

“I will accept his judgment.”

Clean. Final.

---

A longer pause.

---

“I have done enough to earn the possibility.”

No defense.

No excuse.

Just… accounting.

---

Her expression softens—barely.

---

“You tried to keep me alive.”

A small nod.

“I noticed.”

---

That’s as close as she gets.

---

She looks forward again.

The city is closer now.

The temple spire catching the first light.

---

“I won’t ask you to interfere.”

A faint breath.

“You won’t.”

Not a question.

---

“And if you did…” a slight tilt of her head “it would be for you, not for me.”

---

Silence again.

---

Her bare hand drifts—fingers brushing the carpet’s edge, grounding herself in the sensation.

---

Then—

once more—

she reaches out.

---

Her hand rests on his shoulder.

Brief.

Deliberate.

---

“…you’ve been good company,” Zara says.

Simple.

---

A faint trace of something warmer:

“Unexpectedly so.”

---

Her hand leaves.

---

She straightens slightly as the city rises to meet them.

The mask is returning.

Piece by piece.

---

But not completely.

Not yet.

---

“One last favor,” she adds.

Almost casual.

---

“Don’t let them make this smaller than it is.”

A glance at him.

Sharp again.

---

“Whatever happens.”

---

The wind catches her hair again.

Her cloak.

Her bare hand.

---

For just a few more seconds—

she lets herself feel it.

---

Then she reaches for the waiting gauntlet.

And prepares to land.
