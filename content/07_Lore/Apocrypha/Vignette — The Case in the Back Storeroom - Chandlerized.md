# Vignette — The Case in the Back Storeroom

The case came in through the rear entrance of Admiral’s Vice.

That was smart.

The front room had tables, cards, glasses, dice, and the kind of staff who could turn a locked delivery into a folk ballad before the first paying fool lost his purse. You didn’t bring mystery through the gaming floor unless you wanted rumor to put on shoes and sprint.

So the case went into the back storeroom.

Thalrik’s back storeroom.

It was the kind of room that knew things and had decided not to testify. Wine casks leaned in the shadows. Spare table legs waited like broken alibis. Locked strongboxes sat where honest men would have kept ledgers, and ledgers sat where dishonest men would have kept knives. There were two rugs nobody trusted and the faint smell of expensive tobacco trying to pretend it had never met cheap sawdust.

Guaco was already there.

Nobody had invited him. Nobody had told him. Nobody had even looked in his direction and thought the word “valuable.”

But Guaco had the gift.

He could smell coin through oak, pastry through stone, and mischief through three locked doors and a priest’s denial. In what order, even he probably didn’t know. Men spend their whole lives chasing lesser callings and call it civilization.

Rykeena was there because she noticed things before they were done happening.

Norwin was there because someone had said “warded case,” and that was apparently enough to summon a gnome wizard without chalk, incense, or basic dignity.

And sitting on top of the black lacquered case, boots swinging like she owned the place and had already stolen the deed, was Pippa “Lucky Spark” Nackle.

Pippa was a forest gnome with red curls, quick hands, and a smile that looked like innocence had taken up burglary as a hobby. She had the bright, sweet face of someone who might bring you tea, and the eyes of someone who had already counted the exits, the silverware, and your weaknesses. Her dimples were weaponized. Her laughter came easy. Her conscience probably required a map.

She had stayed with the case.

Guarding it, technically.

Reading the attached note first, absolutely.

The case was long, broad, black-lacquered, and reinforced in bronze. Green silk straps crossed its body. Warding glyphs slept beneath the finish like faint green veins under rich skin. There was no public crest, no formal writ, no proud family mark declaring ownership to the room.

Just a small wax seal stamped with one letter.

**Z.**

Guaco looked at Pippa.

“Pippa.”

She smiled down at him.

“Master Guaco.”

“No.”

“What?”

“That voice.”

“I was only using your name.”

“You were putting garnish on it.”

Pippa leaned forward, chin in her hands.

“Well, it’s a handsome name when said properly.”

Guaco’s eyes narrowed.

Rykeena’s face didn’t move. That meant she was enjoying herself. Some people laughed. Rykeena filed the laugh internally and used it later as evidence.

Norwin, tragically, looked up from the ward-lines.

Pippa’s attention slid to him with the clean precision of a dealer spotting a nervous rich man.

“And Master Norwin.”

Norwin froze.

“You look like you know what every little glowing line does.”

“I would not claim every line.”

“So modest.”

“It is not modesty. It is methodological restraint.”

Pippa sighed happily.

“Oh, that’s terrible. Say more things like that.”

Rykeena watched Pippa glance from Norwin to Guaco with the smooth, cheerful malice of a professional dealer choosing which mark to ruin first.

“She’s working both of you. Professionally.”

Guaco’s eyes narrowed.

“I noticed.”

Norwin’s chin lifted by half an inch, precise and offended.

“I am not being worked.”

Pippa hopped down from the case and landed beside him light as a bad decision. She looked up through red curls and those dimples that ought to have been registered with the watch.

“That is exactly what men say right before they tell me how a lock works.”

Norwin opened his mouth.

Stopped.

Guaco pointed at him.

“Don’t teach the pretty card shark burglary theory.”

Pippa put a hand over her heart.

“Pretty?”

Guaco gave her a flat look.

“You heard the important part.”

“I did. I’m ignoring it.”

Rykeena stepped closer to the case and plucked the envelope from beneath the upper strap.

Pippa immediately covered her mouth.

Rykeena looked at her.

“You read this.”

Pippa’s shoulders shook.

“I guarded it.”

“You read this.”

“I guarded it with informed enthusiasm.”

Rykeena broke the seal the rest of the way.

Norwin leaned in.

Guaco leaned in.

Pippa was already giggling.

Rykeena unfolded the note and read in a voice so dry it could have kept meat through winter.

> “Open in private if you wish.  
> This is for when the time is right.  
> I will need you to handle the straps.  
> —Z”

Silence.

Not dignified silence.

That other kind.

The kind that falls over a room when everybody has the same thought and none of them want their gods hearing it.

Pippa bent double, giggling into both hands.

Guaco stared at the note.

Norwin stared at the case.

Rykeena folded the paper once.

“Well.”

Guaco lifted one finger.

“No.”

Rykeena looked at him.

“I haven’t said anything.”

“You were about to.”

“I was going to say this is private.”

“That’s worse.”

Norwin cleared his throat.

“There are several innocent explanations.”

Guaco turned slowly.

“Name one.”

Norwin opened his mouth.

Closed it.

Pippa nearly collapsed.

Rykeena held up the note.

“Straps.”

Norwin rallied.

“Many specialized devices require straps.”

Guaco said, “Stop helping.”

Pippa sidled toward Guaco now, because flirting with men four feet and under was apparently not a preference but a calling from whatever small and terrible gods watched over forest gnomes.

“Come now, daddy. Aren’t you curious?”

The room died.

Not went quiet.

Died.

Norwin stopped breathing like someone had unplugged him.

Rykeena turned her head very slowly toward Pippa. Her expression remained unchanged except for her eyes, which had gone bright with horrible professional interest.

Guaco stared at her.

Pippa smiled sweetly.

“What?”

Guaco lifted one finger.

“No.”

“I only meant—”

“No.”

“You are known to be a family man.”

“That does not help you.”

Pippa folded her hands behind her back, all innocence and weapon-grade dimples.

“With many children.”

“Still not helping.”

Rykeena said, deadpan, “She has built a defense. It is structurally poor.”

Norwin, recovering by inches, murmured, “The social implications are... catastrophic.”

Guaco pointed at Pippa.

“I have children with better judgment than you.”

Pippa brightened.

“Are they handsome too?”

Guaco closed his eyes.

“Gods save me from pretty gnomes with no survival instinct.”

Rykeena said, “You walked into that one.”

“I walked into a storeroom,” Guaco said. “Apparently that was enough.”

Pippa leaned toward him again.

“So you are curious.”

“I am always curious.”

“Good.”

“That is not the same as suicidal.”

“Master Norwin looks curious too.”

Norwin stiffened.

“I am academically concerned.”

Rykeena said, “That is gnome for curious.”

Guaco said, “That is gnome for already guilty.”

Norwin leaned closer to the hinge without touching it.

“There is a viewing seam.”

Guaco shut his eyes.

“Gods save us.”

Pippa whispered to Norwin, “Could you open it just a little?”

“No.”

“Could you determine whether opening it just a little would be safe?”

Norwin paused.

“That is a much more reasonable question.”

Guaco pointed at Pippa.

“You are a menace.”

She beamed.

“Thank you.”

Rykeena said, “For clarity, this is not my idea.”

Guaco said, “Nor mine.”

Pippa said, “It is a little mine.”

Norwin produced a narrow silver tool from inside his sleeve.

Guaco stared.

“Why do you have that?”

“For situations requiring a narrow silver tool.”

“That answer makes me tired.”

Norwin inserted the tool near the hinge.

The ward-lines shimmered green.

Everyone leaned back.

Nothing exploded.

Nothing bit him.

No terrifying countess with secrets stepped out of the air to ask why mortals couldn’t behave around luggage.

The lid lifted half an inch.

Just enough.

All four leaned in.

Inside was a dense, confusing arrangement of polished bronze brackets, black leather loops, folded straps, green silk padding, locking rings, curved supports, reinforced buckles, and cushions.

So many cushions.

For five seconds, nobody said a word.

Then Guaco said, very quietly:

“What in the hells is that?”

Norwin squinted.

“It appears to be a modular support frame.”

Rykeena’s eyes narrowed.

“It has thigh braces.”

“Stabilizers,” Norwin said.

Pippa bit her knuckle.

Guaco leaned a fraction closer.

“And cushions.”

“Impact absorption.”

Rykeena looked at Norwin.

“Impact from what?”

Norwin did not answer fast enough.

Guaco pointed at the opening.

“That is a chair.”

“It is not a chair.”

“It has a seat.”

“That does not make it a chair.”

“It has straps.”

“Many respectable devices have straps.”

Rykeena said, “Name three.”

Norwin said nothing.

Pippa whispered, “Some assembly required.”

Guaco shut the lid.

Hard.

The latch clicked.

The wards settled back into place, glowing faintly with the smug discretion of magic that had seen everything and signed nothing.

Rykeena slid the note back beneath the strap exactly where she found it.

“We saw nothing.”

Guaco nodded.

“We saw nothing.”

Norwin said, “We saw insufficient data.”

Guaco glared.

Norwin corrected himself.

“We saw nothing.”

Pippa raised a hand.

“I saw cushions.”

“Pippa.”

“And thigh braces.”

“Pippa.”

“And enough leather to make a barmaid blush and a carpenter ask questions.”

Rykeena’s mouth twitched.

Guaco pointed toward the storeroom door.

“Out. All of us. Before Thalrik arrives and makes this somehow worse.”

---

Elsewhere in Admiral’s Vice, Thalrik stopped mid-sentence.

He had been talking to one of the floor captains about replacing the marked ivory chips before anyone important noticed, which was exactly the sort of sentence that described his life now: respectable crime with inventory controls.

Then his eyes narrowed.

The floor captain paused.

“Captain?”

Thalrik didn’t answer.

He turned his head toward the rear hallway.

Not alarmed.

Not suspicious.

Worse.

Dread.

The old dockside kind.

The kind a man develops after surviving smugglers, gamblers, creditors, women with secrets, gnomes with tools, and Guaco with spare time.

Something was happening.

Something involving him.

Something he had not authorized.

Something, somehow, funny.

His beard twitched.

“No,” he muttered.

The floor captain blinked.

“No what?”

Thalrik held up one hand.

“Quiet.”

He listened.

There was nothing to hear but Admiral’s Vice preparing to become Admiral’s Vice: glasses set in rows, chairs dragged into place, cards riffled and squared, dice cups checked, lamps trimmed, and one floor man quietly insisting that probability had been lying to him for years.

Still.

From the back corridor came the faintest sound.

Pippa giggling.

Thalrik closed his eyes.

“That’s bad.”

Then Guaco’s voice, muffled but distinct:

“No diagrams!”

Thalrik opened his eyes.

“That’s worse.”

The floor captain went very still.

Respectable men have instincts. Criminal men have better ones. The floor captain had just enough of both to step out of the way without being asked.

Thalrik started toward the back.

Then stopped.

Turned.

Reached under the counter.

And came up with a crowbar.

The floor captain looked at it.

“Case jammed, captain?”

“Maybe.”

A beat.

Thalrik hefted the crowbar once.

“People too.”

Then he walked fast.

---

The storeroom door opened exactly as Pippa was trying to tug Norwin away from the case by the sleeve.

Thalrik stood in the doorway.

Crowbar in one hand.

Expression carved from pure consequence.

Everyone froze.

Guaco’s eyes flicked to the crowbar.

Rykeena’s flicked to the case.

Norwin’s flicked to the hinge.

Pippa’s flicked to absolutely everything and then lit up like a festival lantern.

Thalrik looked at the four of them.

Then at the case.

Then at the note tucked beneath the strap.

Then back at them.

“No.”

Guaco lifted both hands.

“We saw nothing.”

Thalrik pointed the crowbar at him.

“That was too fast.”

Rykeena said, “We saw very little.”

Thalrik turned the crowbar toward her.

“That was too precise.”

Norwin cleared his throat.

“The ward integrity remains intact.”

Thalrik aimed the crowbar at him.

“That was too technical.”

Pippa beamed.

“I saw cushions.”

Thalrik stared at her.

She clasped both hands under her chin.

“Lovely cushions.”

The crowbar lowered a fraction.

Thalrik inhaled.

Slowly.

“Out.”

No one moved.

He raised the crowbar again.

“Let me clarify. This is a tool for opening cases. It is also a tool for explaining to lingering guests that lingering was a terrible, terrible idea.”

Guaco nodded once.

“I respect the clarity.”

Rykeena was already moving.

“Come on, Norwin.”

Norwin looked wounded.

“I should at least verify—”

“No,” three people said at once.

Pippa leaned toward Guaco as they passed Thalrik.

“You have to admit, he looks very commanding with the crowbar.”

Guaco said, “I admit nothing.”

Thalrik pointed at Pippa.

“You. Especially out.”

She gave him a bright little curtsey.

“As you command, Captain Trouble.”

“Out.”

She slipped past him, still giggling.

At the door, Rykeena paused.

Not turning fully back.

Just enough.

“For the record, the note was visible.”

Thalrik’s eyes narrowed.

“That is not a defense.”

“No,” she said. “But it is why this is Zara’s fault.”

Then she left.

Guaco followed.

Norwin was last, moving with the tragic gravity of a man being separated from a solvable mystery.

Thalrik watched them all clear the threshold.

Then he stepped into the hall, looked both ways, and said loudly:

“If I see one ear at this door, I’m charging admission and taking fingers as deposit.”

Pippa’s voice floated back from farther down the hall.

“What about very small ears?”

“Especially those.”

Giggling retreated.

Thalrik shut the door.

Bolted it.

Then stood there for a moment, crowbar in hand, staring at the case.

The storeroom was quiet now.

Properly quiet.

Wine casks. Dust. Locked boxes. The smell of sawdust and old cards.

And the case.

Black lacquer.

Bronze bands.

Green silk straps.

A single mark.

**Z.**

Thalrik walked over slowly.

The note was still tucked beneath the strap.

He pulled it free.

Read it.

> “Open in private if you wish.  
> This is for when the time is right.  
> I will need you to handle the straps.  
> —Z”

He read it again.

Then he looked at the crowbar in his hand.

“Gods help me,” he muttered. “They were right to be unbearable.”

He set the crowbar on a crate within easy reach.

Not because he needed it.

Because sometimes a man needed emotional support, and iron was more reliable than people.

He placed one hand on the latch.

The ward-lines brightened.

Not aggressive.

Recognizing.

The first latch clicked.

Then the second.

Then the third.

The seal breathed out a thread of green light, folded inward, and vanished.

Thalrik paused.

“Keyed to me.”

A dry little laugh.

“Of course it is.”

He lifted the lid.

Inside, beneath dark velvet packing and shaped padding, lay the same confusing nightmare of leather, bronze, silk, buckles, braces, loops, cushions, and straps.

Seen through a half-inch crack, it had looked scandalous.

Seen fully open, it looked worse for about three seconds.

Then his mind caught up.

The shapes resolved.

Not a chair.

Not furniture.

Not whatever horror Norwin’s diagramming instincts had probably started building in his head.

A saddle.

A dragonback saddle.

Built for a dwarf.

Low seat. Reinforced back. Locking thigh braces. Windward grips. Bronze anchoring plates. Runes for pressure and shock. Straps meant to pass around scale ridges and lock behind the neck plates of something enormous.

Not a horse.

Not a gryphon.

Not even an airship rig.

A dragon.

His dragon.

No.

Not his.

That thought came too quickly, and he shoved it aside with the practiced speed of a man dodging emotional collapse by reflex.

Zara.

Zara had sent him a saddle.

For when the time was right.

He stared at it.

Then he reached in and touched one of the leather straps.

Perfectly worked. Strong. Supple. Expensive enough to make priests invent new sermons.

A second note was tucked into one of the padded braces.

Smaller.

Folded once.

He opened it.

> I had it made properly.  
> I dislike dropping friends.  
> Also, you are very small.  
> —Z

Thalrik blinked.

Then his mouth twitched.

Then he laughed.

Not loud at first.

Just a huff.

Then another.

Then the laugh came properly, low and helpless, filling the back storeroom until the wine casks had to suffer through joy like everyone else.

He sat down on a crate, still holding the note.

“Very small.”

He laughed again.

Outside the door, very faintly, Pippa giggled.

Thalrik stopped.

Looked at the door.

Picked up the crowbar without standing.

“Pippa.”

Silence.

Then, from beyond the wood:

“…yes?”

“Run.”

Small feet pattered away.

He waited until the hallway was quiet again.

Then he turned back to the saddle.

The ridiculous, beautiful, impossible thing.

Dragonback tack made for a dwarf conman turned proprietor, sent by an ancient bronze dragon hiding behind noble titles and courtly murder-smiles.

He ran his hand over the bronze fitting.

A grin took hold despite his best efforts.

“Oh, lass.”

He shook his head.

“You are completely mad.”

A beat.

Then softer, almost fond:

“And gods damn me…”

He picked up one of the straps and tested the pull.

“…I am absolutely going.”
