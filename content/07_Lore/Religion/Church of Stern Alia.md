# Church of Stern Alia

> [!quote] Common Alian teaching
> **The shield is not honored because it is strong.  
> It is honored because something sleeps behind it.**

The **Church of Stern Alia** is an old, severe, and comparatively small faith devoted to the Oeridian **Mother of War**, also called the **Shield Mother**.

Stern Alia is the divine mother of both [[Heironeous]] and [[Hextor]]. Her church teaches law, discipline, household protection, legitimate succession, and the obligation of the strong to preserve what has been entrusted to them.

Her clergy are not known for warmth.

They are known for remaining at the door when everyone else has retreated through it.

## Place in Gorundia

The Church of Stern Alia is lawful and respected in Gorundia, but it is not a member of the official [[Church of the Triad in Gorundia|Triad of the Light]].

The official Triad consists of:

- [[Heironeous]]
- [[Pelor]]
- [[Pholtus]]

Alian priests may participate in royal ceremonies, military musters, noble judgments, and actions involving other lawful churches. Their paladins have marched beside the militant orders of the Triad.

They remain, however, somewhat apart.

Most Gorundians encounter the church during:

- marriages;
- presentation of noble heirs;
- questions of legitimate birth;
- inheritance disputes;
- household oaths;
- military funerals;
- guardianship of children;
- surrender negotiations;
- and moments when someone must physically stand between a household and disaster.

The church maintains the **Registers of Observance**, which record noble presentations, formally witnessed courtships, marriages, and matters affecting legitimate succession.

The Church of Alia is not interested in whether a marriage is romantic.

It is interested in whether the children inherit without starting a war.

## Doctrine

The church teaches that strength creates obligation.

A parent owes protection to a child.

A lord owes order to a household.

A commander owes discipline to an army.

A judge owes restraint after judgment has been completed.

A shield exists because something behind it must survive.

Alian teaching is stern, but it is not Hextorian. The church recognizes an important distinction between **authority** and **ownership**.

Dependents are not possessions.

Children are not property.

Soldiers are not tools to be discarded for vanity.

Protection that exists only to demand submission is merely domination wearing a respectable face.

This distinction can be difficult to hear in an Alian sermon. The prayers are full of walls, discipline, obedience, correction, duty, and necessary judgment.

The difference becomes obvious when matters turn ugly.

An Alian asks what is being protected.

A Hextorian asks who is obeying.

## The Mother of War

Stern Alia is not a goddess of pacifism.

Her paladins wear heavy armor, carry battered shields, defend fortifications, and hold lines that should have broken. Her clergy may bless armies, approve a lawful campaign, witness a surrender, or condemn a commander who abandons those placed under his protection.

They do not believe that all wars can be prevented.

They believe that war does not release anyone from obligation.

Civilians remain households rather than obstacles.

Prisoners remain people under authority rather than ownerless property.

Terms sworn before the Mother remain binding after the tactical situation changes.

A defeated enemy who has lawfully yielded is judged. Killing them afterward is appetite, not discipline.

## Gorundia and the Dominion

The Church of Stern Alia predates the present division between Gorundia and the Dominion.

Before [[Hextor]] became the supreme state religion of the Dominion, Alia, Heironeous, and Hextor were worshiped together in an older and deeply uncomfortable religious order.

When Hextor’s priesthood imposed its supremacy, worship of Heironeous became inseparable from resistance. The resulting religious struggle helped drive the Orenic breakaway and its alliance with Gorundia.

Stern Alia did not leave with one son or remain exclusively with the other.

Her church survived on both sides of the border.

Gorundian and Dominion Alians recognize many of the same prayers, rites, seals, marriages, and clerical offices. This does not make them politically neutral. A Gorundian Alian generally considers herself Gorundian. A Dominion Alian generally serves the Dominion.

They may—and sometimes do—fight one another.

Their shared faith instead provides a surviving language of procedure. Even during war, Alian clergy may carry:

- formal declarations;
- surrender terms;
- prisoner lists;
- ransom offers;
- death notices;
- requests to recover bodies;
- household evacuations;
- and proposals for temporary battlefield truces.

A military priest is a combatant.

A recognized Shield-Witness traveling beneath proper seal is an envoy.

The distinction matters to the church, even when it matters very little to anyone else.

## The Courtesy of the Shield

Alian warriors fighting on opposite sides observe an old martial custom known as the **Courtesy of the Shield**.

When circumstances permit, each offers the other one opportunity to yield. A lawful surrender is witnessed and honored. The tokens and names of the dead are returned to their households when possible.

If neither yields, they fight.

The church sees no contradiction in this.

The Mother has two sons.

She did not prevent their war.

## Public Reputation

Alian clergy are widely regarded as:

- humorless;
- severe;
- stubborn;
- judgmental;
- reliable;
- difficult to intimidate;
- and almost impossible to hurry.

They rarely offer emotional comfort. They are more likely to arrange a burial, secure an inheritance, repair the gate, inventory the grain, place guards around the children, and then ask why everyone is still standing about crying when there is work to be done.

Their paladins are not glamorous.

They are walls with opinions.

## Theron Sylvanranth

The late [[Theron Sylvanranth]] notably favored Stern Alia.

This was considered unusual. Theron was an ancient elf, a political operator, a military organizer, and a man with little apparent need for the household religion of an Oeridian mother-goddess.

Those familiar with Alian doctrine found the choice less surprising.

Theron believed that power was held temporarily, that survival required preparation, and that duty continued beyond personal comfort or recognition. He spent much of his life building structures intended to outlive him.

He did not explain his faith often.

That was also very Alian.

## The Iron Lady

For many years, the preeminent public representative of Stern Alia in the capital was a formidable woman generally called **the Iron Lady**.

Her encounters with the party were tense.

During a visit to Bard’s Gate, she managed to offend both [[Beringer]] and [[Wonderful]]. The resulting disagreement ended when Wonderful suplexed the Iron Lady.

This did not improve interfaith relations.

Penance was imposed.

Wonderful paid for the construction and continued maintenance of a shrine to Stern Alia directly across from [[The Blood and Sand]].

The shrine is properly built, legally endowed, and cleaned frequently.

It needs to be.

Despite repeated admonitions, its location across from one of Bard’s Gate’s drinking and fighting establishments ensures that the stones often smell of spent beer. Acolytes wash the steps. Arena patrons apologize. The steps smell of beer again by morning.

The church considers this an ongoing test of discipline.

Bard’s Gate considers it urban planning.

Wonderful continues to pay for the cleaning.

## Common Symbols

The most common image of Stern Alia is the austere face of an Oeridian woman.

Other Alian symbols include:

- a woman’s face above a shield;
- a hand resting upon a closed gate;
- a shield before a small flame;
- an iron key carried beside a strip of white cloth;
- and a dark shield marked with the name of the person or household being protected.

Household shrines are often small and plain: a carved face, a shallow lamp, a clean cloth, and enough space for family tokens or written names.

They are rarely decorative.

They are taken very seriously.

## Common Sayings

> **What is held must be handed on.**

> **Name what stands behind your shield.**

> **Judgment ends when judgment is complete.**

> **The wall does not hate the storm. It holds.**

> **Strength without shelter is only appetite.**

> **The Mother does not promise peace. She commands that something survive it.**
