# The Triad Churches - Ranks and Offices

> “Mercy, valor, and truth. Three blades in one scabbard. Try not to be the poor bastard they draw it for.”
> - [[Sorek Redmarch]]

The Triad of Gorundia consists of three allied but independent churches:

- [[Pelor]] - mercy, healing, honest labor, dawn, and the protection of the suffering
- [[Heironeous]] - valor, chivalry, righteous war, oaths, and knightly judgment
- [[Pholtus]] - law, truth, order, courts, and the exposure of falsehood

The churches cooperate closely, especially in royal matters, plague, heresy, infernal corruption, and treason. In moments of grave crisis, they may act together as the [[Inquisition of the Light]].

Their internal structures differ sharply.

- Pelor is primarily clerical, charitable, and healing-focused.
- Heironeous is paladin-led, with clerics secondary but militant.
- Pholtus is clerical, legalistic, investigative, and procedurally terrifying.

# Church of Pelor

## Core Character

The Pelorite church is the church of dawn, mercy, healing, honest labor, and hard compassion. Its priests tend hospitals, bless fields, feed the poor, treat soldiers, and stand between common folk and despair.

Pelorites are warm in manner, but not weak. Mercy is their first instinct, not their only tool.

When mercy has been abused, Pelorites can become frightening.

## Pelorite Rank Structure

| Rank | Function |
|---|---|
| Solar Prelate | National head of Pelor’s church in Gorundia |
| Dawn Bishop / Sun-Bishop | Regional authority over a major temple district |
| Father / Mother Superior of the Dawn | Head of a temple, monastery, hospital-house, or major shrine |
| Sun-Priest / Hearth-Priest / Field-Priest | Ordained clergy serving communities, shrines, armies, and hospitals |
| Acolyte of the Dawn | Junior clergy in training |
| Lay Brother / Lay Sister of the Sun | Sworn lay servant, often attached to hospitals or charity houses |

---

## Pelorite Honorifics and Irregular Offices

### Dawnfather / Dawnmother

A Dawnfather or Dawnmother is not simply a rank. It is an honored title for a priest whose divine favor, field service, and spiritual authority place them outside ordinary temple politics.

A Dawnfather may not command bishops or temple administrators by office, but their words carry weight because Pelor’s blessing is visible upon them.

This title is especially appropriate for roaming priests, miracle-workers, adventuring clergy, and those called directly by divine signs.

### [[Lyosha Ohotnik]] as Dawnfather

[[Lyosha Ohotnik]] is a Dawnfather of Pelor.

He is not part of the normal Pelorite administrative hierarchy. He does not sit as a temple superior, bishop, or prelate. Instead, he is a roaming cleric and adventurer whose authority comes from direct divine favor, demonstrated miracles, and unusually high Power Investiture.

In practical terms, Lyosha is the Pelorite equivalent of a Heironean Venerated Shield.

He is:

- outside the ordinary rank ladder
- recognized by the faithful
- difficult for temple officials to ignore
- not easily commanded by administrators
- expected to act where Pelor’s light calls him
- a witness, healer, and holy agent rather than a desk-bound church officer

This makes him politically awkward in the best possible way. He has no neat box on the chart, which means every bureaucrat in three churches quietly hates the paperwork.

### Dawn Witness

A Dawn Witness is one called to observe and bear testimony to divine action.

This may include:

- a miracle
- a sacred oath
- a divine judgment
- a holy calling
- a condemnation
- an omen given under Pelor’s light

The office is testimonial rather than administrative. A Dawn Witness may not command a temple, but their word can anchor a religious finding.

---

## Pelorite Specialist Offices

### Mercy-Knight / Paladin of the Dawnfather

A Pelorite holy warrior.

Mercy-Knights are not common village healers with swords. They are sent when mercy has been weighed and violence is now the merciful act.

They act against:

- undead
- plague cults
- tyrants
- monsters preying on the helpless
- those who exploit mercy as weakness

### Hospitaler of the Dawn

A healer-priest or sworn lay specialist trained for temple hospitals, battlefield care, plague houses, and disaster relief.

Typical duties:

- surgery
- triage
- battlefield recovery
- hospice care
- plague treatment
- care of the poor
- healing-house administration

### Plague-Sun Examiner

A Pelorite specialist sent into disease outbreaks, corpse sites, poisoned wells, rot-cults, and suspected Incabulos activity.

They determine:

- whether disease is natural
- whether quarantine is required
- whether divine contamination is present
- how the dead must be handled
- whether mercy now requires fire

### Breadwarden

A lay or ordained Pelorite officer responsible for food charity, temple granaries, kitchens, famine relief, and public distribution.

Breadwardens are beloved in good times and politically dangerous in famine.

### Labor-Blesser

A Pelorite attached to farms, mills, guilds, road crews, mines, and workshops.

They bless honest labor, tools, harvests, apprenticeships, and dangerous work.

---

## The Radiant Inquiries

The Radiant Inquiries are Pelorite investigative bodies. They do not govern ordinary temples and do not replace bishops, prelates, or hospital superiors.

They exist to examine spiritual danger within Pelor’s own light.

### First Radiant Inquiry - Inquiry of Mercy

Examines failures of charity, abuse of sanctuary, neglect of the sick, corruption in alms houses, and clergy exploiting the helpless.

Common saying:

> “They count the bread and ask who went hungry.”

### Second Radiant Inquiry - Inquiry of Dawn

Examines miracles, omens, prophetic claims, divine visions, new saints, blessed figures, and unusual manifestations of Pelor’s favor.

Common saying:

> “They watch the sunrise twice.”

### Third Radiant Inquiry - Inquiry of False Flame

Examines false holiness, corrupted light, infernal imitation, undead sanctity, necromantic compromise, and righteous violence crossing into damnation.

Common saying:

> “They arrive when the light is real, but the shadow is wrong.”

### Dawn Marshal of the Third Radiant Inquiry

A Dawn Marshal is a field commander and senior examiner of the Third Radiant Inquiry.

A Dawn Marshal may examine:

- priests
- paladins
- miracle-workers
- blessed layfolk
- relics
- shrines
- omens
- holy military actions
- claims of divine light
- corrupted mercy

[[Mathren Solgard]] holds this office.

### Warden of False Flame

A Pelorite investigator of false, corrupted, exaggerated, infernal, fey-twisted, necromantic, or unstable claims of divine light.

This office covers:

- false miracles
- unauthorized revelations
- fraudulent saints
- corrupted healers
- heretical mercy cults
- holy signs that cast the wrong shadow

[[Sir Mathren Solgard]] examined [[Druuna Sunseeker]] under this authority and judged her ignorant rather than heretical.

### Keeper of the Black Threshold

A grave Pelorite office concerned with the point where holy purpose risks becoming corruption.

This includes:

- forbidden tools used for righteous ends
- soul-taint
- shadow-magic
- necromantic compromise
- infernal contamination
- the lie that evil becomes clean when aimed at the correct target

It is not a rank.

It is a burden.

[[Sir Mathren Solgard]] holds this office, earned after testifying against his own mentor, Dawn Captain Armand Helior, who crossed the Black Threshold.

### Examiner of Unclean Light

A field title for specialists trained to identify monsters, undead, fiends, cult-servants, and corrupted agents that mimic holy signs or exploit mercy.

An Examiner of Unclean Light hunts things that hide behind:

- compassion
- sanctuary
- healing
- false repentance
- counterfeit miracles
- stolen holiness

This is one of [[Sir Mathren Solgard]]’s titles.

## Pelorite Lay Offices

### Sunward

A sworn lay guardian of a Pelorite temple, hospital, shrine, or charity house.

Sunwards are not paladins. They are disciplined guards trained to protect the helpless, control crowds, defend healers, and hold plague cordons.

### Hearthwarden

A lay officer responsible for the physical care of a Pelorite temple or hospital-house.

Hearthwardens oversee kitchens, bedding, fuel, laundry, warmth, water, clean rooms, guest quarters, and charity halls.

A good Hearthwarden can run a hospital under siege with three turnips, two blankets, and pure moral violence.

### Basin Brother / Basin Sister

A lay hospital servant responsible for washing patients, cleaning wounds, preparing bodies, laundering linens, and maintaining cleanliness.

They are usually older, practical, and not impressed by rank.

Common saying:

> “Every man bleeds the same into a basin.”

### Field Almoner

A lay charity officer who distributes coin, bread, blankets, tools, and medicine outside temple walls.

They know poverty street by street.

They are also excellent sources of information, because hungry people talk to the person with bread.

### Grave Tender

A lay servant attached to Pelorite burial rites.

Different from a common gravedigger. Grave Tenders prepare bodies respectfully, comfort families, watch for signs of disease, and ensure the poor are not dumped like refuse.

### Dawn Runner

A young lay messenger attached to a temple.

Dawn Runners carry healer summons, emergency messages, temple writs, charity lists, plague warnings, and requests for sanctuary.

# Church of Heironeous

## Core Character

The Church of Heironeous is the church of valor, righteous war, knightly conduct, and sacred oaths.

Unlike most churches, the Heironean church in Gorundia is led by its paladin orders. Clerics exist and are respected, but they are secondary to the paladin hierarchy. Even Heironean clerics tend to be militant, armored, sword-wielding, hard-bitten priests of war and judgment.

This church does not produce many soft robe-priests. It produces men and women who can bless a banner, quote doctrine, and then split a cultist from collarbone to belt buckle.

---

## Heironean Paladin Track

| Rank | Function |
|---|---|
| High Sword | Absolute head of the Heironean church in Gorundia |
| Patriarch-General | Senior regional or army-level paladin commander |
| Shield-Commander | Leader of knightly hosts, temple companies, or militant orders |
| Ordained Knight | Full paladin of Heironeous |
| Temple Knight / Sword Brother / Sword Sister | Holy warrior below full paladin status |
| Squire of the Invincible | Trainee attached to the militant orders |

## Heironean Clerical Track

| Rank | Function |
|---|---|
| Sword-Bishop / Bishop-Militant | Senior cleric overseeing temples, doctrine, relics, and rites |
| High Chaplain | Senior cleric attached to armies, courts, or great noble houses |
| Sword-Chaplain / Banner Chaplain | Battlefield, Bannerhall, or knightly-order chaplain |
| Sword-Priest / Battle-Priest | Ordained Heironean cleric, usually armed and armored |
| Acolyte of the Invincible | Clerical trainee taught scripture, arms, armor care, and battlefield ritual |

## Authority Principle

In ordinary temple administration, a Sword-Bishop may outrank many individual knights.

In matters of sacred war, knightly judgment, doctrine in action, crusade, or public religious command, the paladin hierarchy takes precedence.

The High Sword stands above both tracks.

---

## Heironean Honorifics and Irregular Offices

### Venerated Shield of Heironeous

The Venerated Shield is an honorific for a paragon outside the ordinary command hierarchy.

A Venerated Shield may not hold formal office, but their reputation, divine favor, and proven service make them impossible to treat as an ordinary knight.

They are living examples of Heironean virtue: courage under divine burden, duty without vanity, and valor that does not need permission to act.

The current living Venerated Shield of Heironeous is [[Dame Cwenheld of Marsten]].

Cwenheld earned the title through a lifetime of battlefield courage, sacred duty, and the infamous rescue of the infant [[Beringer]] from the slaughter at Villa Marsten. Her testimony of divine warning, her ride across two counties, and her stand against the black-armored killers became part of Heironean legend.

The title does not make her the head of the church.

It makes her something more awkward.

When [[Dame Cwenheld of Marsten]] speaks on courage, duty, or divine warning, even bishops and paladins listen carefully. Not because she outranks them on paper, but because Heironeous has already answered that question in steel.

---

## Heironean Specialist Offices

### Bannerhall Chaplain

A Heironean chaplain assigned to the Bannerhall.

Duties include:

- opening solemn prayer
- maintaining sacred order during rites
- blessing the Closing of the Blades
- witnessing knightly declarations
- rebuking disorder when the hall crosses from rowdy into profane

A good Bannerhall Chaplain can silence a room full of drunk armored killers without raising his voice.

That is either sanctity or witchcraft. Best not to ask.

### Sword-Chaplain

A militant cleric attached to an army, noble host, knightly order, or fortress chapel.

Sword-Chaplains:

- bless banners
- hear battlefield confessions
- tend the dying
- record oaths
- judge cowardice and dishonor
- fight when needed

They are usually Clerics mechanically, but with armor, weapons, and enough grit to make robe-priests nervous.

### Oath-Keeper

A Heironean specialist who records, preserves, and interprets martial and knightly oaths.

They handle:

- vows of knighthood
- sworn bonds
- surrender terms
- wartime compacts
- penitent service
- extraordinary sacred promises
- oathbreaking charges

An Oath-Keeper is not just a clerk. They are a witness to the moral architecture of the kingdom.

### Duel Adjudicator

A Heironean expert in lawful combat, trial by combat, and ritual challenge.

They determine:

- proper weapons
- valid champions
- terms of yield
- whether magic is permitted
- whether a duel was clean
- whether victory settles the matter

Their least favorite sentence:

> “Technically, that counts.”

### Relic Warden

Guardian of Heironean relics, saintly arms, sacred banners, holy shields, blessed swords, and battlefield trophies.

Relic Wardens are responsible for custody, authentication, transport, ritual display, and protection from theft or corruption.

They tend to be humorless for excellent reasons.

### War-Cantor

A Heironean specialist trained in martial hymns, cadence, morale, battlefield liturgy, and sacred memory.

War-Cantors keep soldiers moving, steady fear, and preserve the names of the fallen.

### Shield Hospitaller

A Heironean battlefield healer and support priest.

Unlike Pelorite hospitalers, Shield Hospitallers focus on keeping warriors fit for duty.

Their bedside manner is usually:

> “You are not dead. Therefore you are still assigned.”

### Penitent-Knight Warden

A specialist who supervises disgraced knights, oathbreakers, noble offenders, and warriors serving under sacred punishment.

They oversee:

- penitent campaigns
- restricted arms
- religious probation
- atonement quests
- dishonored but salvageable nobles

### Muster Priest

A Heironean priest assigned to army musters, crusade logistics, and holy war preparation.

They certify:

- banners
- troop oaths
- battlefield rites
- chain of command
- sacred eligibility for war

---

## Heironean Lay Offices

### Shieldward

A sworn lay guardian of a Heironean shrine, relic, banner, or armory.

Shieldwards are disciplined armed retainers below temple knights.

### Banner-Keeper

A lay officer responsible for sacred and military banners.

They maintain, repair, store, and carry banners during ceremonies and musters.

A knight may carry the sword, but the Banner-Keeper carries the memory.

### Sword-Servant

A lay attendant assigned to an Ordained Knight, Sword-Chaplain, or temple armory.

They maintain swords, shields, armor, scabbards, belts, surcoats, oil, whetstones, and spare straps.

A veteran Sword-Servant may know more about knightly conduct than half the young idiots wearing spurs.

### Oath-Scribe

A lay scribe trained to record martial, noble, and sacred oaths.

They do not interpret doctrine like an Oath-Keeper, but they preserve the exact words.

Their favorite phrase:

> “Say that again, my lord. Precisely.”

### Spurwarden

A lay officer responsible for squires, pages, training gear, dormitories, stable discipline, and the small daily machinery of knightly formation.

Spurwardens are usually retired soldiers or widowed knightly retainers.

They know which squires are brave, which are bullies, and which are going to get someone killed.

### Armory Provost

A lay master of the temple armory.

Oversees weapon stores, armor issue, repairs, training blades, spare shields, confiscated arms, and battlefield salvage.

This is the person who knows when someone drew a sword they were not supposed to have.

### Roll-Keeper

A lay records officer who tracks temple knights, squires, casualties, vows, service terms, penitent duties, and muster obligations.

This is a great “boring” NPC who can detonate a scandal by saying:

> “He was not assigned there.”

# Church of Pholtus

## Core Character

The Church of Pholtus is the church of law, truth, order, investigation, judgment, and the One True Way.

Its clergy are judges, truth-readers, prosecutors, investigators, canon lawyers, and procedural nightmares wearing white.

Pholtans do not need to be loved.

They need the record to be correct.

Their temples often attach directly to courthouses, archives, and high courts.

---

## Pholtan Rank Structure

| Rank | Function |
|---|---|
| First Magistrix / First Magistrate of the White Court | National head of the Pholtan church in Gorundia |
| High Justiciar of the One Way | Senior regional judicial authority |
| Sister-Magistrix / Brother-Magistrate | Court priest, investigator, and legal-religious authority |
| Truthkeeper | Cleric trained in truth rites, divination, testimony, and trial blessing |
| White Advocate | Religious prosecutor or canon lawyer |
| Lantern-Judge | Judge over lesser religious, legal, or doctrinal matters |
| Scrivener of the One Way | Ordained or semi-ordained legal scribe and record keeper |
| Acolyte of the White Flame | Junior clergy in training |
| Lay Clerk of Pholtus | Non-ordained legal clerk, witness handler, or archivist |

---

## Pholtan Specialist Offices

### Truthkeeper

A Pholtan cleric trained in truth rites, testimony, divination, court blessing, and falsehood detection.

Truthkeepers are called when:

- testimony conflicts
- witnesses contradict themselves
- lies must be stripped from court
- magical deception is suspected
- divine truth is required before judgment

### Sister-Magistrix / Brother-Magistrate

A Pholtan court-priest and legal-religious authority.

They may investigate, observe, certify evidence, issue findings, and represent the White Court in royal or noble proceedings.

[[Arweena]] fits this lane cleanly.

### Lantern Examiner

A specialist trained to use Pholtan white or silver flame rites to reveal hidden corruption.

Lantern Examiners inspect:

- illusions
- glamours
- forged documents
- hidden writing
- infernal marks
- false relics
- concealed disease signs
- magical disguises

### White Advocate

A Pholtan religious prosecutor or canon lawyer.

White Advocates build formal cases involving:

- heresy
- perjury
- magical fraud
- cult activity
- forbidden pacts
- religious treason
- false witness

### Confessor of the Straight Path

A Pholtan examiner trained to obtain precise confession and distinguish guilt, ignorance, coercion, and deliberate falsehood.

They are not warm confessors.

They are scalpels with notebooks.

### Seal-Keeper

A custodian of Pholtan seals, court certifications, religious warrants, and official findings.

Seal-Keepers authenticate:

- court documents
- temple judgments
- divine findings
- excommunications
- execution approvals
- purified copies of corrupted records

### Chain Auditor

A Pholtan specialist who tracks custody and procedural integrity.

They follow:

- evidence
- prisoners
- relics
- corpses
- sealed testimony
- confiscated goods
- trial documents

When a Chain Auditor arrives, someone has already lied about where the body was.

### Calendar Clerk

A Pholtan timing specialist.

They establish:

- when a writ was issued
- when a warning was given
- when an oath became active
- whether a deadline was met
- whether delay invalidates or confirms guilt

Never underestimate someone who can kill a noble defense with “seventeen minutes late.”

### Heresy Surveyor

A field investigator who inspects shrines, chapels, schools, estates, village rites, and unauthorized religious practice.

Heresy Surveyors are especially active where local custom, noble privilege, and bad theology have been left alone too long.

Which is to say: everywhere interesting.

### White Witness

A rare Pholtan title for one who has seen, certified, or survived a major divine truth event.

Unlike a Truthkeeper, a White Witness may not be a regular court specialist. Their authority comes from having stood in the presence of revealed truth and lived to testify.

---

## Common Pholtan Duties

Pholtans are called when:

- testimony conflicts
- a noble lies under oath
- illusion or glamour is suspected
- a contract may be infernal
- plague signs require legal quarantine
- a corpse, relic, or document has been tampered with
- succession depends on records
- treason must be proven cleanly
- everyone else is yelling and someone needs to be insufferably correct

---

## Pholtan Lay Offices

### White Clerk

A trained lay clerk of the Pholtan courts.

White Clerks copy testimony, maintain court records, prepare writs, and verify filings.

They are not ordained, but they are dangerous because they know exactly what was said, when, and by whom.

### Lantern-Keeper

A lay officer responsible for maintaining Pholtan lanterns, silver-flame lamps, courtroom lights, and ritual illumination.

In the White Court, this is a sacred-trust role. If a lantern fails during testimony, people notice.

### Seal-Bearer

A trusted lay officer who physically carries sealed writs, court orders, religious warrants, and certified findings.

Seal-Bearers are often escorted, because the paper they carry can ruin nobles.

### Ledger-Warden

A lay archivist-accountant responsible for financial, legal, and institutional records.

Tracks fines, fees, property forfeitures, court payments, temple expenses, witness stipends, prison costs, and confiscated goods.

A Ledger-Warden is proof that Pholtus weaponized accounting.

### Chain Clerk

A lay clerk who records custody of prisoners, evidence, relics, corpses, and sealed documents.

They maintain the chain of custody.

If a corpse “moves,” a Chain Clerk is the first person who becomes quietly terrifying.

### Door Witness

A lay witness stationed at court doors, confession rooms, sealed chambers, archives, or interrogation spaces.

Their job is simple:

- who entered
- who left
- when
- carrying what
- under whose authority

Door Witnesses are the human equivalent of “don’t make me check the logs.”

### Inkward

A lay officer responsible for official inks, prepared parchment, anti-tamper dusts, waxes, and document preservation.

This sounds minor until forged documents become plot-critical.

Then the Inkward becomes the tiny dragon sitting on the hoard.

### Clock-Brother / Clock-Sister

A lay timekeeper attached to a Pholtan court or archive.

They certify the time of testimony, writ issue, execution, confession, oath, or deadline.

They are hated by everyone who was “basically on time.”

# The Triad in Joint Action

When the three churches act together, their roles are clear:

| Church | Question Asked |
|---|---|
| Pelor | Can mercy still live here? |
| Heironeous | Is this just, brave, and honorable? |
| Pholtus | Is this true and lawful? |

When all three agree, the result is almost impossible for nobles, courts, or commoners to dismiss.

When they disagree, everyone suddenly discovers pressing business somewhere else.

# Triad Crisis Roles

## Inquisition of the Light

A formal joint action of the three churches.

Usually invoked against:

- infernal corruption
- heresy
- Hextorite infiltration
- plague cults
- undead outbreaks
- treason involving magic or false oaths
- noble crimes too dangerous for ordinary courts

## Triad Field Commission

A smaller investigative or emergency body.

Typical members:

- one Pelorite healer or Dawnfather-type witness
- one Heironean paladin or Sword-Chaplain
- one Pholtan Truthkeeper or Sister-Magistrix
- armed temple retainers
- scribes
- witnesses
- corpse-handlers if the gods are in a bad mood

## Standard Litany of Joint Action

Pelor:

> “Mercy shines even in shadow.”

Heironeous:

> “Valor strikes where mercy cannot.”

Pholtus:

> “Truth endures when both have failed.”

Response:

> “By light, by steel, by law - the darkness shall break.”

# Joint Triad Specialist Roles

## Lightbearer

A Triad field investigator carrying authority from all three churches.

A Lightbearer usually bears:

- a sun-token of [[Pelor]]
- a sword or shield token of [[Heironeous]]
- a white lantern or seal of [[Pholtus]]

Used for:

- remote cult sites
- plague villages
- noble estates under suspicion
- corrupted shrines
- infernal contracts
- undead signs
- politically sensitive investigations

## Triad Advocate

A legal-religious representative trained to argue before mixed church and royal courts.

Usually Pholtan-trained, but familiar enough with Pelorite and Heironean doctrine to avoid starting a doctrinal tavern brawl in formal session.

## Mercy Examiner

A joint Pelorite-Pholtan specialist who determines whether a prisoner, cultist, noble, or corrupted priest is eligible for mercy.

They consider:

- repentance
- coercion
- magical influence
- disease
- spiritual corruption
- danger to others

## Confessor-Militant

A joint Heironean-Pholtan specialist used for captured knights, enemy officers, oathbreakers, and treasonous soldiers.

They determine what was done, under whose authority, and whether the accused still has honor enough to answer cleanly.

## Sword of Last Mercy

A joint Pelorite-Heironean role.

A holy executioner, paladin, or war-priest tasked with ending a threat cleanly when mercy no longer means sparing life.

This role is rare, grave, and never ceremonial.

## Witness-Clerk

A joint procedural recorder attached to Triad actions.

They record:

- who was present
- what was said
- what rites were performed
- what magic was used
- who objected
- who failed to object
- what was seen by divine witness

A Witness-Clerk is proof that sometimes the scariest person in the room is the one with ink on their sleeve.

## Mercy Bailiff

A lay officer jointly recognized by Pelor and Pholtus.

Handles prisoners, the sick, protected witnesses, repentant cultists, and those under sanctuary review.

They make sure the accused is neither abused nor conveniently murdered.

## Sword Bailiff

A lay officer jointly recognized by Heironeous and Pholtus.

Handles armed prisoners, disgraced knights, noble detainees, trial-by-combat arrangements, and oathbreaking suspects.

They know how to be polite to dangerous people while counting exits.

## Corpse Witness

A lay specialist attached to plague, murder, battlefield, or heresy investigations.

They record condition of bodies before burial, burning, sanctification, or dissection.

Often trained by Pelorites and Pholtans together.

They know whether the dead were moved, staged, poisoned, diseased, or marked.

## Sanctuary Steward

A lay official responsible for people under church protection.

Tracks:

- who claimed sanctuary
- when
- under what grounds
- who visited
- what food and medicine were provided
- whether the sanctuary claim still holds

This is a great role in political intrigue, because sanctuary is where law, mercy, and power all start side-eyeing each other.

---

# DM Notes

The churches should feel distinct at the table.

Pelorites offer food, water, warmth, and mercy, then become frightening when mercy is abused.

Heironeans stand in armor, speak in oaths, and measure people by courage under duty.

Pholtans ask precise questions, record exact answers, and make liars sweat through their good clothes.

The Triad is strongest when it does not feel like three versions of the same priest. They should overlap in purpose, not personality.

Pelor heals the wounded.

Heironeous holds the line.

Pholtus writes down who broke it.
