# Pelor

> “Everyone loves Pelor until mercy starts costing money.”  
> — Sorek Redmarch

**Pelor** is the god of the sun, healing, mercy, strength in hardship, agriculture, and the protection of ordinary folk. Within [[Church of the Triad in Gorundia]], he stands as one of the three official pillars of the state religion, alongside [[Heironeous]] and [[Pholtus]].

Where [[Heironeous]] is the sword raised in defense, and [[Pholtus]] is the lamp held over judgment, Pelor is the hand laid upon the wounded.

His priests bless fields, tend the sick, shelter refugees, comfort the dying, and remind knights that the people being “defended” are not decorative background furniture in a heroic mural.

## Place in the Triad

Within the Triad, Pelor represents **mercy, preservation, renewal, and compassion**.

His church does not reject law or valor. It insists that both must serve life rather than pride. Pelorites are often the ones standing between righteous fury and a ditch full of widows.

The three common Triad virtues are often rendered as:

- **Courage** through [[Heironeous]]
- **Mercy** through Pelor
- **Truth** through [[Pholtus]]

In formal rites, Pelor’s place is usually the second blessing: after the vow to defend, before the declaration of lawful witness. This reflects the common saying:

**The sword may win the field, but the sun decides what grows there afterward.**

Very poetic. Almost enough to make people forget the battlefield smells terrible.

## Worship in Gorundia

Pelor is deeply loved among the common folk of Gorundia.

His shrines are found in villages, healing houses, roadside chapels, military camps, plague wards, orphanages, and farming communities. Even settlements that cannot support a full priest often maintain a sun-stone, dawn altar, or small golden disk set above the door of the local healing room.

Among peasants, soldiers, refugees, mothers, elderly folk, and the sick, Pelor is often the most personally beloved member of the Triad.

A farmer may honor [[Heironeous]] at muster and swear truth before [[Pholtus]], but when the child burns with fever, it is Pelor’s name whispered in the dark.

## Common Titles

Pelor is known by many titles in Gorundia, including:

- The Shining One
- The Sun Father
- The Dawnlord
- Lord of the Healing Light
- Keeper of the Warm Hand
- The Mercy That Rises
- The Golden Witness
- The Light Over the Furrow

The title **Sun Father** is especially common among rural folk, though some formal theologians prefer **The Shining One** or **Lord of the Healing Light**.

Formal Pelorite clergy tend to use elevated titles.

Commoners tend to use whatever title they learned from their grandmother, because grandmothers outrank theologians in every village that wishes to survive winter.

## Domains and Concerns

Pelor is invoked over:

- Healing
- Mercy
- Sunlight
- Agriculture
- Protection of the weak
- Recovery after war
- Resistance to disease
- Honest labor
- Renewal after corruption
- Comfort for the dying
- Shelter for refugees and the poor

Pelor’s power is strongly associated with warmth, golden light, clear dawn, open fields, clean water, and the driving away of sickness, undeath, despair, and unnatural corruption.

Pelorite theology teaches that light is not merely revelation, but nourishment. Sunlight reveals truth, yes, but it also grows wheat, warms children, dries tears, and makes rot retreat.

Pholtines like the first part.

Pelorites keep pointing at the wheat.

## The Pelorite Temperament

Pelorites are often assumed to be soft.

This is a mistake commonly made by people who have never watched a village priest spend three days in a plague house, then walk out and tell an armed tax collector to wait.

The church teaches mercy, but not weakness. Pelor’s clergy are expected to be patient, brave, practical, and difficult to intimidate. A good Pelorite priest may speak gently, but will not necessarily move.

Their courage is not usually the bright, trumpet-backed courage of knights. It is the stubborn courage of a healer who keeps working while the roof burns.

In Gorundia, this gives Pelorites a quiet but powerful moral authority. Even nobles who dismiss them as sentimental tend to tread carefully around beloved local priests.

Killing a knight may start a feud.

Mistreating a Pelorite who saved half the village from fever may start a riot.

## Priests and Lay Servants

Pelor’s clergy include ordained priests, battle-chaplains, healers, shrine-keepers, monastic caretakers, wandering dawn-brothers and dawn-sisters, and lay servants trained in medicine, herbcraft, midwifery, and burial care.

Not all Pelorite servants are spellcasters. Many are simply competent, exhausted people with clean hands, sharp eyes, and very little patience for noble excuses.

Common roles include:

- Village healer
- Temple physician
- Military chaplain
- Refugee warden
- Dawn preacher
- Plague tender
- Harvest blesser
- Orphan keeper
- Hospitaller
- Sunwarden of a road shrine

Pelorite temples often keep careful lists of the sick, hungry, displaced, orphaned, widowed, and elderly. This makes them beloved by commoners and quietly annoying to officials hoping such people would become someone else’s problem.
## Church Ranks and Holy Warriors

Pelor’s church in Gorundia is primarily clerical, charitable, and healing-focused. Its ordinary authority rests with priests, temple superiors, hospital heads, bishops, and the national Solar Prelate rather than with militant orders.

For the full Triad comparison and formal offices, see [[The Triad Churches - Ranks and Offices]].

The common Pelorite hierarchy runs broadly as follows:

- **Solar Prelate** - national head of Pelor’s church in Gorundia
- **Dawn Bishop / Sun-Bishop** - regional authority over a major temple district
- **Father / Mother Superior of the Dawn** - head of a temple, monastery, hospital-house, or major shrine
- **Sun-Priest / Hearth-Priest / Field-Priest** - ordained clergy serving communities, shrines, armies, and hospitals
- **Acolyte of the Dawn** - junior clergy in training
- **Lay Brother / Lay Sister of the Sun** - sworn lay servant, often attached to hospitals or charity houses

Unlike the church of [[Heironeous]], Pelor’s church is not led by paladins. Pelorite holy warriors exist, but they are exceptional instruments of mercy under arms, not the ruling spine of the institution.

They are most often called **Mercy-Knights**, **Paladins of the Dawnfather**, or simply **Dawn Paladins** in less formal speech.

A Pelorite paladin is sent when mercy has been weighed, the innocent remain threatened, and violence has become the cleanest remaining act of compassion.

They commonly act against:

- undead
- plague cults
- fiends
- tyrants
- monsters preying on the helpless
- corrupted healers or false saints
- those who exploit mercy as weakness

The Pelorite ideal is not wrath first. The old teaching is clear: illumination before judgment, mercy before wrath, and decisive action only when the devouring dark will not relent. See [[Pelorite Litanies and Prayers]].

This makes Pelorite paladins different in temperament from many Heironean holy warriors. A Heironean paladin asks whether the blow is brave, just, and honorable. A Pelorite paladin asks whether any mercy remains possible, and if not, how to end the threat without becoming part of it.

That sounds gentle until one remembers that “ending the threat” may involve a sunlit mace through a vampire’s jaw.

## Temples and Shrines

Pelorite temples are usually built to catch the morning light.

Even humble shrines often include an east-facing window, a polished brass or gold-painted disk, sunburst carvings, warm-colored stone, or painted beams that catch dawn.

Large temples often contain:

- A public healing hall
- A chapel of dawn
- A hospice or infirmary
- Herb gardens
- Kitchens for the poor
- A room for childbirth
- A chamber for the dying
- Clean water access
- Sleeping quarters for travelers, refugees, or the sick

Unlike some faiths, Pelor’s church does not consider poverty spiritually decorative. Feeding the hungry is not a metaphor. It means feeding the hungry.

This has caused budget arguments since the dawn of organized religion, because apparently compassion needs receipts.

## Pelor and Agriculture

Pelor is strongly honored by farmers, though he does not wholly replace the older rural reverence given to the [[Velari]].

In much of Gorundia, Pelorite rites coexist with local agricultural gods, field spirits, seasonal blessings, and village customs. A farmer may ask Pelor for sun, the Velari for the health of the soil, and his dead grandfather to stop letting foxes into the hens.

The Triad does not generally object to these older practices unless they turn corrupt, bloody, or openly hostile to royal law.

Pelor’s agricultural role is especially important during:

- Spring planting
- First dawn blessings
- Summer growth rites
- Drought prayers
- Harvest thanksgiving
- Protection against blight
- Consecration of wells
- Cleansing of tainted fields

His priests are often called when crops fail in a way that seems unnatural.

## Pelor and War

Pelor is not a war god, but no Gorundian army marches far without him.

Pelorite battle-chaplains serve in camps, field hospitals, siege lines, and plague wards. They heal the wounded, bless the dying, comfort frightened levies, and sometimes stand between victorious soldiers and helpless prisoners.

This last duty has made them unpopular with certain commanders, which is usually a sign they are doing it correctly.

In military doctrine, Pelor’s role is not to inspire killing, but to preserve what war would otherwise devour. His priests remind armies that victory without mercy curdles into atrocity.

A Heironean chaplain may bless the charge.

A Pelorite chaplain waits where the charge ends.

## Pelor and Undeath

Pelor’s church is deeply opposed to undeath.

To Pelorites, undeath is a violation of natural rest, mercy, and renewal. The dead should be honored, mourned, and released, not chained to hunger, hatred, or sorcerous vanity.

Pelorite clergy are therefore often involved in:

- Sanctifying graves
- Cleansing haunted sites
- Laying restless dead to rest
- Opposing necromancers
- Treating spiritual corruption
- Healing life-draining wounds
- Protecting burial grounds

Their opposition to undeath sometimes overlaps with the clergy of [[Wee Jas]], though the two faiths approach death very differently.

Pelorites emphasize peace, warmth, and release.

Jasidin clergy emphasize order, propriety, and the correct handling of death’s mysteries.

They can work together.

They do not necessarily enjoy it.

## Pelor and Pholtus

Pelorites and Pholtines cooperate constantly, especially in courts, refugee crises, plague law, and investigations of abuse.

They also argue constantly, because mercy and procedure have never met without immediately requesting separate rooms.

Pelorites often believe [[Pholtus]] is too cold, too rigid, and too willing to mistake documented order for justice.

Pholtines often believe Pelorites are too forgiving, too trusting, and too willing to let compassion muddy lawful clarity.

Both are sometimes correct, which naturally makes the arguments worse.

The strongest Triad councils require both voices. Pelor asks what suffering the law will cause. Pholtus asks what corruption mercy may excuse.

A wise ruler listens to both.

Then probably wishes he had become a beekeeper.

## Pelor and Heironeous

Pelorites and Heironeans are usually close allies.

Both faiths value courage, protection, and sacrifice. Heironean knights often revere Pelorite healers, especially after discovering that bleeding to death is bad for honor.

The tension comes when Heironean glory outruns Pelorite mercy.

A Heironean may see a hard strike as necessary justice. A Pelorite may ask whether the helpless, surrendered, poor, or frightened are being protected from that “justice.”

In noble courts, Pelorites often act as the brake on martial pride. This does not always make them popular.

It does often make them necessary.

## Pelor’s Symbols

The most common symbol of Pelor is a golden sunburst, often with a human face, open rays, or a bright disk.

In Gorundian usage, Pelorite symbols often include:

- Gold or brass sun disks
- Wheat beneath a rising sun
- A hand cupping light
- A dawn ray over a shield
- A sunburst over a field
- A golden circle above a white road
- A lit brazier at sunrise

Pelorite vestments are usually white, gold, yellow, warm cream, or sun-faded brown. Rural priests often wear practical robes, work aprons, or traveling cloaks rather than jeweled finery.

A village Pelorite with mud on his boots is usually more useful than a court priest with a golden collar wide enough to serve soup in.

## Holy Days and Customs

Pelorite observances are tied to dawn, planting, harvest, healing, and renewal.

Common rites include:

- Dawn prayers
- Blessing of fields
- Consecration of wells
- Naming of infants at sunrise
- Healing vigils
- Plague-fast rites
- Harvest thanksgiving
- Funeral dawn-watch
- First light oaths
- Cleansing of sickrooms

Many Pelorite rites begin in darkness and end at sunrise. This reflects the central teaching that despair is real, night is real, suffering is real, but none of them are sovereign forever.

Pelorite funeral customs often include a dawn lamp kept beside the body until burial, symbolizing warmth, memory, and the hope that the soul does not go alone into darkness.

## Saints and Local Heroes

Gorundian Pelorites honor many local saints, healers, martyrs, and protectors.

These may include:

- Priests who died in plague houses
- Knights who defended hospitals or refugees
- Lay healers who served through war
- Monks who preserved seed grain during famine
- Miracle-workers tied to dawn, wells, or cleansing light
- Commoners who gave their lives to protect the helpless

Pelor’s church is more comfortable than many noble institutions with honoring low-born saints. This has occasionally irritated aristocrats, especially when the local shrine to a dead washerwoman draws more pilgrims than the family chapel of Lord Whoever the Unbearable.

## Pelorite Sayings

Common Pelorite sayings include:

- “The dawn does not ask who earned it.”
- “Mercy is not blindness.”
- “Warm the living. Honor the dead. Defy the dark.”
- “A field is not saved by praising the plow.”
- “No wound is holy because it was made by a noble hand.”
- “Stand in the light long enough, and lies begin to sweat.”
- “Feed them first. Preach when they can hear you.”

That last one is very Pelorite, and has ruined countless dramatic sermons.

## Social Standing

Pelor’s church is widely trusted but not always politically dominant.

Among the nobility, [[Heironeous]] usually carries more prestige. Among magistrates, courts, and severe officials, [[Pholtus]] often carries more procedural authority.

But among commoners, soldiers, farmers, refugees, and the sick, Pelor’s moral weight is immense.

A Pelorite priest may not command a baron’s knights, but if he publicly condemns a baron’s cruelty, people listen.

This makes the church both beloved and dangerous.

Not dangerous in the theatrical dagger-in-the-dark way.

Dangerous in the “everyone knows Father Merek is right, and now the baron has to pretend not to be a bastard” way.

## In Gorundian Politics

Pelorite clergy are often drawn into disputes involving:

- Refugees
- War wounded
- Half-blood settlements
- Forced labor
- Prisoner treatment
- Plague quarantine
- Famine relief
- Corrupt local officials
- Abusive nobles
- Desecrated graves
- Unnatural blight
- Undead attacks
- Magical corruption of land or body

They are rarely neutral where suffering is obvious.

This does not mean Pelorites are rebels. Most are loyal to the Crown and the Triad. But they believe authority exists to protect life, not merely to arrange it into taxable rows.

A Crown that listens to Pelor gains legitimacy among the people.

A Crown that ignores Pelor may still have law and swords.

For a while.

## Summary

Pelor is the healing sun of the Triad: merciful, protective, patient, and far harder than he first appears.

His church feeds the hungry, tends the sick, blesses fields, shelters refugees, opposes undeath, and reminds Gorundia that the realm is not made only of nobles, banners, and court decrees.

Within [[Church of the Triad in Gorundia]], Pelor stands equal beside [[Heironeous]] and [[Pholtus]].

In noble halls, he may not always be the favored voice.

In villages, sickrooms, battle camps, and graveyards, he may be the only voice people trust.

That is power of a different kind.
