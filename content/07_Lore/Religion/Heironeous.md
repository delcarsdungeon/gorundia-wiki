# Heironeous

> “Every noble says he honors Heironeous. Then the bill comes due, and suddenly half of them discover a deep personal interest in legal nuance.”  
> — [[Sorek Redmarch]]

**Heironeous** is the god of valor, chivalry, honor, righteous battle, courage under duty, and the lawful defense of civilization. Within the [[Church of the Triad in Gorundia]], he stands as one of the three official pillars of the state religion, alongside [[Pelor]] and [[Pholtus]].

Where [[Pelor]] is mercy and healing, and [[Pholtus]] is law and truth, Heironeous is the sword raised in defense of both.

His church is the most culturally favored of the Triad among the Gorundian nobility. Not officially, naturally. Officially, all three pillars are equal. Unofficially, noble courts tend to find the god of swords, oaths, banners, and glorious battlefield deaths more flattering than the god who audits testimony or the god who asks whether the peasants have eaten.

Gorundia is a knightly kingdom.

Heironeous fits it like a gauntlet.

## Place in the Triad

Within the Triad, Heironeous represents **courage, honor, righteous arms, sacred oaths, and martial duty**.

The three common Triad virtues are often rendered as:

- **Courage** through Heironeous
- **Mercy** through [[Pelor]]
- **Truth** through [[Pholtus]]

In formal rites, Heironeous is usually invoked first when the matter concerns war, knighthood, noble duty, defense of the realm, or trial by combat. His blessing sanctifies the vow to stand, fight, endure, and, if necessary, die without making everyone else die first because one was too proud to bend.

The Heironean position within the Triad is simple:

**Mercy must be protected.  
Truth must be defended.  
Law must have steel behind it.**

This is noble, stirring, and extremely useful to people who already own armor.

## Worship in Gorundia

Heironeous is widely honored across Gorundia, but his worship is strongest among:

- knights
- noble houses
- soldiers
- squires
- military chaplains
- fortress households
- border baronies
- militant orders
- lordly courts
- officers of the Crown

His temples often double as military chapels, oath halls, reliquaries, armories, training yards, and places where young nobles are taught the sacred art of not embarrassing their ancestors in public.

Even common soldiers honor Heironeous, though usually in more practical language than priests prefer. A soldier’s prayer to Heironeous is often less “make me glorious” and more “let my shield hold and let the idiot beside me remember his training.”

Both are valid.

## Common Titles

Heironeous is known by many titles in Gorundia, including:

- The Invincible
- The Archpaladin
- The Valorous Knight
- Lord of Righteous Battle
- The Thunderer of the Just Field
- The Sword Above the Oath
- The Shield of Lawful Courage
- The Blue Blade
- The Unbroken Standard
- Lord of the Line

Among knights, **The Invincible** and **The Archpaladin** are the most common elevated titles.

Among soldiers, **The Blue Blade** and **Lord of the Line** are often heard.

Among nobles trying to sound especially pious at banquets, all titles become longer, worse, and somehow less sincere.

## Domains and Concerns

Heironeous is invoked over:

- valor
- chivalry
- righteous war
- honorable combat
- defense of the innocent
- sacred oaths
- knightly conduct
- lawful trials by combat
- just punishment
- military discipline
- courage under fear
- protection of realm and Crown
- resistance to tyranny
- the defeat of dishonorable evil

Heironean worship is not mere battle-lust. His clergy are very clear on this point, usually because someone in the room needs reminding.

War is not holy because it is war.

A battle becomes righteous only when it is fought under just cause, lawful authority, honorable conduct, and the defense of what must not be abandoned.

Which is a beautiful doctrine, and also why every ambitious lord tries to hire a chaplain with flexible hearing.

## The Heironean Temperament

The Heironean ideal is controlled courage.

Not rage.

Not vanity.

Not romantic nonsense about dying beautifully in front of witnesses.

A true servant of Heironeous stands when duty requires it, fights when justice demands it, and accepts consequence without flinching. Glory may follow, but glory is not the point. Glory is what other people say afterward, usually while not mentioning the mud, blood, screaming, and latrine fever.

Heironeans value:

- courage under command
- restraint in victory
- truth in oath
- defense of the helpless
- loyalty to lawful authority
- contempt for cowardice
- hatred of tyranny
- readiness to be judged by the same standard one imposes on others

The last part is where many nobles begin looking for refreshments.

## The Dominion and the Hextorite Break

Heironeous has always stood in opposition to [[Hextor]], but in Gorundia this rivalry is not abstract theology. It is history burned into the kingdom’s bones.

By the later Dominion wars, the [[Dominion]] had shifted from brutal imperial statecraft into open Hextorite doctrine. Its armies marched beneath the red gauntlet, with Tyrant Knights, priest-officers, commissars, coerced soldiers, and slave levies all bound into a system of obedience through fear.

For Gorundia, this hardened the old political enemy into a sacred enemy.

The Dominion was no longer merely an eastern empire seeking land, tribute, and submission. It had become the living contradiction of Heironean order: strength without honor, discipline without conscience, law without mercy, and victory as domination.

That shift did not create Gorundia’s independence.

Gorundia was already its own realm.

But it made compromise impossible.

The Dominion preached strength through chains.

Gorundia answered with strength through oath.

The Dominion raised the red gauntlet.

Gorundia raised the blue blade.

From that point onward, the struggle was remembered not only as a war of borders, but as a war of gods, ideals, and lawful souls.

## The Battle of Red Gorge

[[The Battle of Red Gorge]] in 1190 OR was not the founding moment of Gorundian independence. It was the last great Dominion war, the battle that finally broke the Dominion’s westward pressure and secured what Gorundia had already claimed.

By Red Gorge, Gorundia already stood as a kingdom. What remained in question was whether it could survive the Dominion’s return in force.

The Dominion host advanced under Hextorite banners, supported by Tyrant Knights, Scourge Commissars, coerced soldiers, slave levies, and priest-officers of the iron hierarchy. Gorundia met them with a coalition of noble houses, knightly orders, elven allies, disciplined men-at-arms, and battlefield priests of Heironeous.

The battle became one of the great sacred memories of the Heironean church in Gorundia.

Count [[Alastair Amadis]] shaped the field itself into a weapon, using terrain, feigned retreat, elven fire, and coordinated counterattack to break the Dominion advance. [[House Moffet]] shattered the Dominion right through its disciplined retreat and ravine trap. At the center, [[House Vogel]] paid in blood, with Maletryx and Maxen Vogel slain before young [[Galmyr Vogel]] rallied the remaining knights beneath the manticore banner.

Witnesses later claimed that lightning split the sky as Galmyr invoked Heironeous and the Dominion center faltered.

Both sides preserved rumors of divine intervention.

Gorundian soldiers swore they saw Heironeous riding with the knights, lightning flashing from his blade.

Dominion survivors told darker stories of a six-armed avatar of Hextor stalking the battlefield and slaughtering men without mercy.

Whether these were true avatars, battlefield visions, priestly propaganda, or the kind of terror memory becomes after enough men die in mud, Red Gorge became a holy battle in the memory of both nations.

For Gorundia, Red Gorge proved the kingdom could stand against Hextor’s empire and survive.

For the Heironean church, it became the great lesson of sacred warfare:

**Valor is not clean.  
Honor is not safe.  
But the line must hold.**

## Paladins Above Priests

The Church of Heironeous in Gorundia is unusual among the Triad because it is **paladin-led**.

Clerics are respected. Sword-priests, battle-chaplains, bishops-militant, and temple clergy all have important roles. They bless banners, maintain doctrine, preserve relics, hear confessions, teach scripture, and tend the ritual life of the church.

But the living authority of Heironeous rests most visibly in the paladin orders.

For the full comparative structure, see [[The Triad Churches - Ranks and Offices]].

The principle is simple:

**The word of Heironeous is proven in action.**

A priest may interpret the rite.

A paladin embodies the oath.

This does not make every paladin wiser than every cleric. That would be convenient, and therefore suspicious. But in matters of sacred war, knightly judgment, crusade, martial doctrine, battlefield command, and public religious authority, the paladin hierarchy takes precedence.

The [[High Sword]] stands above both tracks as the supreme national authority of the Heironean church in Gorundia.

## Heironean Rank Structure

The Heironean church maintains two interlocking tracks: the paladin track and the clerical track.

### Paladin Track

- **[[High Sword]]** - absolute head of the Heironean church in Gorundia
- **Patriarch-General** - senior regional or army-level paladin commander
- **Shield-Commander** - leader of knightly hosts, temple companies, or militant orders
- **Ordained Knight** - full paladin of Heironeous
- **Temple Knight / Sword Brother / Sword Sister** - holy warrior below full paladin status
- **Squire of the Invincible** - trainee attached to the militant orders

### Clerical Track

- **Sword-Bishop / Bishop-Militant** - senior cleric overseeing temples, doctrine, relics, and rites
- **High Chaplain** - senior cleric attached to armies, courts, or great noble houses
- **Sword-Chaplain / Banner Chaplain** - battlefield, Bannerhall, or knightly-order chaplain
- **Sword-Priest / Battle-Priest** - ordained Heironean cleric, usually armed and armored
- **Acolyte of the Invincible** - clerical trainee taught scripture, arms, armor care, and battlefield ritual

In ordinary temple administration, a Sword-Bishop may outrank many individual knights.

In matters of holy war, public judgment, oath in action, and knightly command, the paladin track carries greater authority.

This arrangement is very Heironean: two chains of command, both armed, both convinced they are necessary, and both somehow still preferable to letting nobles improvise theology.

## Clergy and Holy Warriors

Heironean clergy are rarely soft temple scholars in Gorundia.

A Heironean priest is usually trained in arms, armor, battlefield discipline, military liturgy, sacred law, and the conduct of war. Some are excellent fighters. Many are veterans. Even those who are primarily clerical tend to have the posture of someone who has slept in armor and hated it correctly.

Common Heironean servants include:

- paladins
- temple knights
- sword-priests
- battle-chaplains
- banner chaplains
- oath-keepers
- relic wardens
- duel adjudicators
- war-cantors
- shield hospitallers
- muster priests
- penitential wardens
- temple squires
- armory provosts
- oath-scribes
- banner-keepers

The church is not merely a spiritual body. It is a military, legal, noble, and ceremonial institution woven deeply into the Crown’s authority.

Which is exactly as powerful and dangerous as it sounds.

## Heironean Temples and Chapels

Heironean temples in Gorundia are usually built like places that expect trouble.

Even urban temples tend to have thick doors, stone courtyards, armories, training yards, oath halls, and rooms for relics or sacred banners. Border temples may look more like fortified keeps than places of prayer.

Common features include:

- oath stones
- sword altars
- shield walls
- reliquary armories
- training yards
- lists of the honored dead
- halls for squires and temple knights
- chapels for battlefield confession
- banner vaults
- courts for knightly judgment

Heironean decoration favors blue, silver, steel, polished stone, lightning motifs, crossed blades, open gauntlets, and standards hung above altars.

Village shrines are simpler. A sword over a stone. A shield painted blue. A bell rung at muster. A list of local dead read at midsummer.

It does not take marble to remind men to stand.

## Heironeous and the Nobility

Heironeous is the most visibly noble of the Triad gods in Gorundia.

This does not mean he belongs to the nobility. It means the nobility has spent generations acting like he does, because nobles have never met a sacred symbol they did not try to embroider onto their own cloak.

Noble houses invoke Heironeous during:

- knighthoods
- military musters
- declarations of war
- trials by combat
- noble oaths
- investitures
- martial funerals
- banner blessings
- household rites of succession

A house with strong Heironean devotion is expected to produce disciplined knights, keep its oaths, protect its dependents, and answer royal summons without whimpering into the upholstery.

Naturally, not every house succeeds.

That is why Heironean chaplains have sermons.

## House Orenic and the Rough Faith

[[House Orenic]] is one of the great examples of Heironean devotion outside the polished heartland style.

The Orenics are deeply religious, and their devotion is clearly Heironean, but it is not courtly, perfumed, or embroidered into harmless pageantry. Their faith is rougher, louder, harder, and more direct.

Where a heartland Heironean noble may speak of honor beneath stained glass, an Orenic is more likely to speak of standing in the breach while some poor bastard tries to take your head off.

Both may be sincere.

Only one is likely to break the table while explaining it.

Orenic Heironean worship emphasizes:

- endurance
- physical courage
- storm and steel imagery
- blunt oathkeeping
- loyalty tested under pressure
- contempt for weakness disguised as refinement
- defense of kin, land, and lawful order
- heroic violence when duty demands it

Their version of Heironeous is not less orthodox. It is less polished.

In court, this can make the Orenics seem crude to the heartland houses. On campaign, it makes them useful in the way a locked gate is useful when wolves arrive.

## House Ravenster and Formal Devotion

[[House Ravenster]] is also noted for deep religious seriousness, though its expression differs from Orenic devotion.

Ravenster piety tends to be colder, more formal, more dynastic, and more bound to royal legitimacy, lawful order, and the sacred obligations of high station. Where Orenic devotion smells of iron, rain, and sweat, Ravenster devotion smells of polished stone, sealed letters, and old bloodlines behaving as though the gods personally signed their genealogy.

This is not necessarily hypocrisy.

Ravenster takes religion seriously because power must be justified, watched, and restrained by sacred order. Their Heironean leanings are less about battlefield roaring and more about disciplined authority, lawful command, and the burden of noble duty.

A Ravenster lord may not shout about courage.

He may simply expect everyone in the room to understand that cowardice is beneath discussion.

This makes them impressive.

It also makes them exhausting, because solemn people with lawful authority are apparently a renewable resource in Gorundia.

## Heironeous and Pelor

Heironeans and Pelorites are usually close allies.

Both faiths honor courage, protection, sacrifice, and the defense of the innocent. Heironean knights often revere Pelorite healers, especially after discovering that bleeding to death is bad for honor.

The tension comes when Heironean justice outruns Pelorite mercy.

A Heironean may see a hard strike as necessary justice. A Pelorite may ask whether the helpless, surrendered, poor, or frightened are being protected from that justice.

In noble courts, Pelorites often act as the brake on martial pride.

Heironeans do not always enjoy this.

They often need it.

The strongest servants of Heironeous understand that mercy is not weakness. It is one of the things valor exists to defend.

The worst servants of Heironeous treat mercy as decorative softness.

Those people usually require a sermon, a duel, or a shovel.

## Heironeous and Pholtus

Heironeans and Pholtines cooperate through law, testimony, trials, oaths, treason investigations, and religious courts.

They also irritate each other constantly.

Heironeans value honor, courage, and action. Pholtines value truth, procedure, and lawful precision. This means a Heironean may want to act when dishonor is obvious, while a Pholtine asks whether dishonor has been proven in the proper form before the proper witness under the proper seal.

This can make Heironeans impatient.

It can also prevent them from becoming sanctified thugs with good posture.

Pholtus reminds Heironeous that righteous anger is not evidence.

Heironeous reminds Pholtus that lawful clarity without courage can become cowardice with a ledger.

Both churches are necessary.

Both know this.

Neither is always gracious about it, because clergy are still people and therefore a design flaw with opinions.

## Heironeous and Hextor

The hatred between Heironeous and [[Hextor]] is central to Gorundian Heironean identity.

Hextor represents tyranny, conquest, fear, domination, and the use of law as a weapon of subjugation. Heironeous represents righteous authority, courage, honor, and the defense of civilization through disciplined arms.

To Gorundians, the difference is not abstract.

The Dominion showed them what Hextorite order looked like:

- coerced levies
- slave soldiers
- priest-officers
- terror discipline
- commissars
- punishment as doctrine
- obedience without honor
- strength without mercy

This is why Heironean language in Gorundia often frames Hextor not merely as an enemy god, but as the corruption of everything Heironeous protects.

A coward may flee the line.

A Hextorite may hold the line for evil.

The second is worse.

## Heironeous and Wee Jas

Heironeans have a complicated relationship with [[Wee Jas]].

Wee Jas is lawful, dignified, magically serious, and concerned with proper death. These are qualities Heironeans can respect.

She is also strongly associated with Suel tradition, death priestesses, and forms of magical authority that many Gorundians regard with suspicion. The Dominion stink does not help. Neither does the fact that most soldiers prefer their death clergy to arrive after the battle, not calmly discuss metaphysical custody beforehand.

In practice, Heironean orders may cooperate with Jasidin clergy in matters of:

- burial rites
- battlefield dead
- inheritance after noble death
- necromantic abuse
- undead containment
- oath-bound relics
- lawful custody of remains

The relationship is respectful, wary, and not exactly warm.

Which is still better than most noble marriages.

## Symbols and Vestments

The common symbol of Heironeous is a lightning-wreathed sword or a silver-blue blade raised upright.

In Gorundian usage, Heironean symbols often include:

- blue and silver swords
- lightning over a shield
- a mailed fist gripping a blade
- crossed swords beneath a crown of light
- blue banners edged in silver
- an upright sword before an oath-stone
- a shield marked by a lightning stroke

Heironean vestments are usually blue, silver, white, and steel grey. Paladins and militant clergy often wear armor as vestment, with cloaks, tabards, or surcoats bearing the church colors.

Court chaplains may dress more formally, but even then the look tends toward military restraint rather than jeweled softness.

A Heironean priest who jingles too much is probably either very important or very lost.

## Holy Days and Customs

Heironean observances are tied to oaths, battle, knighthood, discipline, and remembrance.

Common rites include:

- blessing of blades
- oath renewals
- knightly vigils
- muster prayers
- battlefield absolutions
- reading of the honored dead
- sanctification of banners
- trial by combat rites
- shield-blessing before campaign
- penitent service vows
- memorials for fallen commanders
- consecration of new fortress chapels

Many Heironean rites take place at dawn or beneath storm skies. Lightning is treated as a sign of divine attention, though serious priests caution against interpreting every thunderstorm as a personalized message from the Archpaladin.

This warning has not stopped young knights from doing exactly that.

## Trials, Duels, and Oaths

Heironeous is closely tied to trial by combat, knightly challenge, sworn testimony, and martial law.

This does not mean the church approves of every duel.

A lawful duel must be properly witnessed, properly declared, and fought under legitimate terms. Heironean duel adjudicators exist because nobles, when left unattended, will turn hurt feelings into theology with weapons.

Heironean authorities may be called to judge:

- whether a challenge is valid
- whether a champion may stand
- whether magic is permitted
- whether surrender was offered
- whether dishonorable tactics were used
- whether the result settles the matter
- whether a duel was merely murder with a prettier hat

Oaths are treated with similar seriousness.

A sworn word before Heironeous is not decorative speech. It is a sacred bond.

Breaking such an oath may not always be criminal.

It is always dangerous.

## Saints, Exemplars, and Living Symbols

The Heironean church honors saints, martyrs, paladins, battle-chaplains, oath-keepers, and knights who embodied courage under divine burden.

Gorundian Heironeans are especially fond of figures who stood between danger and the helpless at terrible cost.

One of the greatest living examples is [[Cwenheld]], the **Venerated Shield of Heironeous**.

The Venerated Shield is not an ordinary rank. It is an honorific for a living exemplar whose courage, sacrifice, and divine burden place them outside normal command politics.

Such a person may not command the church on paper.

On the battlefield, in court, or before a frightened young knight, paper becomes less interesting.

Cwenheld’s faith is often summarized as:

**Stand between the innocent and the blade.**

That is Heironean theology stripped of polish and left with the edge exposed.

## Heironean Sayings

Common Heironean sayings include:

- “Stand where fear tells you to kneel.”
- “The oath weighs more than the sword.”
- “Valor without honor is only appetite.”
- “The line holds because someone chooses pain.”
- “A coward may live. He does not stand.”
- “Steel remembers the hand that lied.”
- “Mercy is guarded by the brave.”
- “Do not pray for glory. Pray not to fail.”
- “The shield is sacred because someone stands behind it.”
- “If the cause is just, face judgment with your helm off.”

That last one has ruined several noble excuses.

## Social Standing

Heironeous has immense prestige among the Gorundian nobility.

A house that is visibly Heironean gains an aura of martial legitimacy. Its knights are expected to be disciplined. Its oaths are expected to matter. Its lord is expected to answer the Crown in times of war.

This prestige can be sincere.

It can also be exploited.

The church knows this. The best Heironean clergy are therefore deeply suspicious of noble pageantry that praises honor while dodging duty. They have seen too many banners over too many cowards to be impressed by embroidery alone.

Among commoners, Heironeous is respected rather than loved in the Pelorite sense. He is the god prayed to when raiders come, when levies muster, when a son takes up spear, when the gate must hold, or when someone powerful needs to be told no by a person with armor and divine backing.

That is not soft affection.

It is trust of a harder kind.

## The Crown and the Paladin-King

King [[Rimmand II]] is not merely a patron of Heironeous.

He is a paladin of Heironeous.

This fact gives the Heironean church enormous prestige at court, even within the official equality of the [[Church of the Triad in Gorundia]]. Gorundia’s state religion remains the Triad, not a Heironean theocracy, but the king’s personal calling is impossible to ignore.

A decree from Rimmand may be royal law.

A vow from Rimmand carries the weight of a paladin-king.

That distinction matters.

It also explains why Heironean ceremony sits so naturally at the center of noble life. The Crown’s public image is already knightly, martial, oath-bound, and sacredly invested in righteous defense. The king does not need to favor Heironeous by statute. His life does it for him.

This creates quiet pressure inside the Triad.

[[Pelor]] and [[Pholtus]] remain equal pillars in doctrine and law, but every priest in Gorundia knows the king’s own soul was claimed by the Archpaladin. Heironean authorities benefit from that prestige, and the best of them are careful not to abuse it.

The worst of them call that restraint “prudence” while quietly enjoying the view from the largest chair.

## Prince Porthos and the Sunward Balance

The emergence of Prince [[Porthos Ryomar]] as a paladin of [[Pelor]] has changed the religious reading of the royal house.

Where King [[Rimmand II]] embodies Heironean kingship through valor, oath, and righteous arms, Porthos carries Pelor’s light: mercy, healing, protection, and compassion for the suffering.

This does not weaken the Crown’s Heironean identity.

It broadens it.

To many clergy, especially those who still believe theology should occasionally survive contact with politics, the father and son together form a powerful Triad image:

**The king as the sword.  
The prince as the healing light.  
The law still watched by Pholtus.**

Pholtines, naturally, have noticed that last part and are prepared to mention it at length.

Porthos’ calling also reassures Pelorites who worry that Gorundian noble culture leans too heavily toward steel, honor, and battlefield glory. A Pelorite prince-paladin is a public reminder that mercy is not a lesser virtue, and that the Crown’s sacred duty does not end when the enemy falls.

For commoners, this may matter even more. A king who stands for courage is respected. A prince who stands for mercy may be loved.

That can be politically inconvenient.

So, naturally, it is important.
## In Gorundian Politics

Heironean clergy and paladins are often drawn into disputes involving:

- noble misconduct
- knightly oaths
- trials by combat
- military discipline
- battlefield atrocities
- treason
- crusades
- succession rites
- royal musters
- unlawful duels
- dishonored knights
- infernal or Hextorite influence
- sacred judgments involving war or noble duty

Because the nobility favors Heironeous, the church has great influence at court.

That influence is a blessing when paladins restrain noble corruption.

It is a problem when nobles try to drape self-interest in blue and silver and call it valor.

The Crown relies on the Heironean church for military legitimacy, oath structure, holy witnesses, and public symbols of lawful force. A royal command backed by Heironean paladins carries a different weight from one backed by scribes alone.

Scribes may be right.

Paladins make people stop walking toward the door.

## Summary

Heironeous is the sword of the Triad: valorous, disciplined, oath-bound, and charged with defending mercy and truth through righteous strength.

His church is the most noble-facing of the three Triad faiths and the most closely tied to knighthood, war, banners, trials by combat, and sacred duty.

In Gorundia, Heironeous is not merely a god of battle.

He is the answer to Hextor.

He is the memory of Red Gorge.

He is the god invoked when the line must hold, when the oath must be kept, and when fear has excellent reasons but duty does not care.

Within [[Church of the Triad in Gorundia]], Heironeous stands equal beside [[Pelor]] and [[Pholtus]].

In noble halls, he is often favored.

In war, he is indispensable.

And in the better stories, he is the reason someone stands between the innocent and the blade.

The faith’s courtly importance is heightened by King [[Rimmand II]] himself, who is a paladin of Heironeous. Yet the royal house now also bears a Pelorite sign through Prince [[Porthos Ryomar]], whose calling as a paladin of [[Pelor]] gives the Crown a rare living image of Triad balance: sword, mercy, and law under one royal roof.
