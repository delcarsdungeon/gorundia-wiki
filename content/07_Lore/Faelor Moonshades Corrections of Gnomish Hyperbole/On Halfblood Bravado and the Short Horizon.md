# On Halfblood Bravado and the Short Horizon

> [!info] In-World Context
> A scholarly analysis attributed to [[Faelar Moonshade]]. Its observations concerning slavery, generational trauma, and survival culture are not entirely without merit.
>
> Its conclusion that the halfbloods are only partially civilized is pure Faelar: prejudice dressed in polished language so the reader must walk several paragraphs before discovering the knife.

It is sometimes said that the halfblood possesses unusual courage.

This is flattering.

It is also incomplete.

Courage requires an understanding of danger, followed by a decision to accept it. What is commonly praised among the halfblood districts is often something less disciplined: the refusal to permit danger, humiliation, tomorrow, or basic arithmetic to interfere with whatever must be proven in the present moment.

A halfblood laborer will cross an unsafe scaffold rather than allow another man to reach the crane first.

A halfblood youth will answer an insult from three armed men because silence would be witnessed.

A halfblood fighter will remain in the pit after the contest is plainly lost, not because victory remains possible, but because retreat would survive longer in the memory of the crowd than the injury will survive in his body.

This is not properly courage.

It is reputation defended at ruinous interest.

Among peoples secure in name, household, law, and inheritance, a public slight may be endured because tomorrow remains available for correction.

Among the halfbloods, status is less stable. A man’s place may depend upon whether those around him believe he can be pushed aside.

Consequently, the smallest contest becomes a referendum upon the whole person.

A place in line.

A disputed coin.

A shoulder struck in passing.

A woman’s attention.

The final cup poured from a bottle no one particularly wanted until two men wanted it at once.

Each becomes a challenge because each is witnessed, and what is witnessed must be answered.

Their celebrated _macho culture_, to borrow the inelegant human phrase, is therefore not merely an enthusiasm for violence.

It is a system of public credit.

Every insult creates debt.

Every retreat compounds it.

Every bruise paid willingly announces that the bearer remains solvent.

This explains much.

It excuses less.

## The Shortened Horizon

One must also ask why the halfblood so often chooses immediate standing over future safety.

The answer is not, as certain tavern philosophers have suggested, that they are incapable of understanding consequence.

Many halfbloods are shrewd judges of danger.

Indeed, those raised in [[The Dregs]] often perceive threats that pass unnoticed by citizens accustomed to doors that lock, guards who answer, and laws that recognize their mothers’ names.

Their difficulty lies elsewhere.

They have inherited a shortened horizon.

Slavery does not teach patience in the civilized sense.

It teaches attention to the next order, the next meal, the next beating, and the next opportunity to conceal something from the master.

The slave who plans ten years ahead is a dreamer.

The slave who survives until morning is successful.

These habits do not vanish because a chain is struck off.

A proclamation may free a man in an afternoon.

It cannot teach his household to trust tomorrow.

Nor should it surprise us that many halfblood families remain governed by immediate appetite, immediate anger, immediate loyalty, and immediate danger.

Their grandparents were property.

In some districts, their parents were property.

A considerable number are themselves former slaves, though Gorundian society has developed the charming administrative habit of calling them refugees once the purchase records become diplomatically inconvenient.

We are speaking of a people perhaps one generation removed from the collar, two from the whip, and scarcely one from the habits those instruments produced.

The phrase is unpleasant.

History is under no obligation to become false merely because accuracy has poor manners.

## Institutions and Habits

Gorundia has absorbed these people rapidly.

They have entered guilds, watches, military companies, taverns, workshops, and civic offices.

They possess wages, contracts, weapons, surnames where records permit them, and increasingly the protection of law.

These are substantial advances.

They are not the same as civilization.

Institutions may be erected faster than the habits required to sustain them.

A guild charter can be copied in a day.

Literacy requires years.

Trust in law requires a generation in which the law does not repeatedly betray those asked to trust it.

Thus the halfblood district may acquire lamps, paved streets, licensed businesses, elected advocates, and three competing brands of intolerable distilled liquor while remaining socially governed by customs better suited to a slave barracks or mercenary camp.

Material progress is swift.

Civic temperament is slower.

Humans should recognize this phenomenon.

They experience it whenever presented with a new machine and immediately discover seven ways to injure themselves with it.

## A Transitional People

The halfbloods are not incapable of civilized life.

That conclusion would be both cruel and unsupported.

They are simply very new to it.

Their recklessness, public aggression, obsession with reputation, and preference for present victory over future advantage should therefore be understood as transitional traits: survivals of bondage carried into freedom, where they are mistaken for pride because pride is more pleasant to inherit than fear.

Given time, education, stable households, fair courts, and examples worth imitating, these habits may soften.

But it is sentimental nonsense to pretend they have already done so.

Freedom has reached the halfbloods.

Civilization is still attempting to catch up.
