# Lord Faelar Moonshade's Review of Master Norwin Stonyeye's Formal Account Concerning the Destruction of Harrowmere Enclosure

> [!quote] Lord Faelar Moonshade  
> There are few scholarly experiences more irritating than watching an alarming conclusion rest upon sound mathematics.

## Commission and Scope

At the request of a member of His Majesty’s Council, I have reviewed Master Norwin Stonyeye’s formal account and thaumaturgical justification concerning the destruction of [[Harrowmere Enclosure]], now [[Harrowmere Caldera]].

The report was delivered under royal and archmagical notice, addressed to His Majesty [[King Priam Ryomar]] and Acting Archmage [[Sapphire Flame]]. It concerns an abandoned royal fortress, corrupted by hostile powers, allegedly converted into a sacrificial resonance engine tied to swamp, foundation, water table, ley contact, and a broader lattice of unholy sites.

Master Stonyeye’s conclusion is severe:

That the fortress could not safely be demolished by mundane means.

That its visible architecture was merely the shell of a deeper occult instrument.

That the Volcano working did not merely destroy Harrowmere, but corrected it.

That the new caldera represents not only a strategic denial of enemy function, but potentially a future royal airship harbor.

This is, even by the standards of wartime magical filings, ambitious.

I therefore consulted the relevant sources, reviewed Master Stonyeye’s thaumaturgical structure, requested specialist opinion, corresponded with [[Baroness Thunderhold]], and undertook an initial comparison against known patterns of sacrificial lattice architecture and geomantic injury.

My finding is inconvenient.

Master Stonyeye’s magical analysis is sound.

His ethical analysis is not equally complete.

Naturally, this means we must now do real work.

## Consulting Specialist

Because Master Stonyeye’s report rests heavily upon geomantic disruption, ley-contact pathology, swamp-borne ritual diffusion, and volcanic redirection, I sought opinion from Professor Maerin Dolvath, Chair of Lithic Thaumaturgy and Subterranean Harmonics at the Royal College of Thaumic Studies.

Professor Dolvath is a human earth-mage of excellent reputation, poor patience, and no known affection for either elven literary habits or gnomish theoretical vanity. He has written extensively on fault-memory, foundation resonance, and ritualized stone contamination. His monograph **Pressure Beneath Prayer: Subsurface Harmonics in Sanctified and Desecrated Ground** remains unread by most ministers, which is a shame, as it would cure several of them of speaking.

Professor Dolvath reviewed Master Stonyeye’s core thaumaturgical claims.

His summary was unpleasantly direct:

> “The gnome is right about the pattern. I dislike the size of the hammer. I cannot fault his reading of the nail.”

A useful sentence.

A terrible precedent.

## On Master Stonyeye’s Thaumaturgy

I will begin with what must be granted.

Master Stonyeye’s thaumaturgical assessment appears, in its central structure, correct.

Harrowmere was not merely a corrupted fortification. The report persuasively identifies a layered ritual mechanism involving:

- the central sacrificial chamber;
    
- participatory foundation stones;
    
- swamp-sunk footings;
    
- contaminated water table;
    
- lateral diffusion through peat, roots, mire, and old channels;
    
- underlying ley-contact points;
    
- sympathetic relation to a broader lattice of unholy sites.
    

The distinction between architectural destruction and ritual destruction is not only valid, but essential.

A fortress may be defeated by broken walls.

A ritual engine may survive them.

Master Stonyeye is correct that ordinary razing could have shattered visible structures while leaving buried geometry intact. He is correct that scattered prepared stone might have become lesser anchors. He is correct that damaged nodes can become less predictable rather than harmless. He is correct that an occult pattern requires more than matter, but matter in relation.

That last point is especially important.

It is also annoying, because he states it well.

## Professor Dolvath’s Technical Review

Professor Dolvath agreed with the following findings:

First, that Harrowmere’s swamp environment likely enhanced diffusion rather than merely complicating construction.

Second, that foundation-level corruption cannot be assumed inert after surface demolition.

Third, that ley-contact contamination is best understood as parasitic imposition upon local current rather than corruption of the ley itself.

Fourth, that violent lithic transformation could break, bury, melt, and disorder the functional relationships required by a sacrificial resonance engine.

Fifth, that volcanic transformation may, in rare circumstances, cauterize a geomantic wound by destroying the ritual geometry that keeps it open.

He further agreed that the Volcano working was theoretically suited to disrupting Harrowmere’s profile: stagnant water, buried rot, enclosed sacrifice, lateral seepage, and hidden foundations.

To quote him again, because bluntness is apparently contagious:

> “A siege engine would break the corpse. Volcano burned the disease out of the bones.”

This is strong confirmation.

Regrettably.

## On What Cannot Be Attacked

For intellectual honesty, and also because future readers are tiresome when they think omissions are victories, I will state clearly what cannot fairly be challenged.

Master Stonyeye did not act upon a trivial site.

He did not mistake a mundane ruin for a mystical threat.

He did not select Volcano merely for spectacle.

He did not misunderstand the difference between ley destruction and ley redirection.

He did not propose that volcanic force is inherently profane.

He did not ignore the distinction between destroying matter and destroying pattern.

He did not fail to recommend verification.

He did not conceal the extremity of the measure.

His thaumaturgical roll, to use the vulgar adventuring phrase, was excellent.

There.

The gods have witnessed me say it.

May they now grant me patience.

## On the First Problem: Authority Is Not Merely Intent

Master Stonyeye’s first line of defense is that [[The Venerated Shield of Heironeous]] and the paladins present had already committed to destroying Harrowmere Enclosure.

This matters.

It does not settle the matter.

There is a difference between authorizing demolition of a profaned fortress and authorizing sudden volcanic creation within royal lands.

The paladins intended to raze Harrowmere. They had siege equipment. They possessed holy authority over the immediate profanation. Their purpose was righteous and militarily sensible.

Master Stonyeye altered the scale, nature, aftermath, and legal consequence of the operation.

A wall-breaking becomes terrain-making.

A demolition becomes geological event.

A ruined fortress becomes a caldera.

A royal asset, however corrupted, becomes unrecoverable in its prior form.

A military problem becomes a territorial transformation.

This does not mean he was wrong.

It means his report too quickly treats equivalence of objective as equivalence of authority.

The objective was destruction.

The method was volcanic alteration.

Those are not administratively identical, no matter how many clever sentences are placed between them.

## On the Second Problem: Notice

This is where the matter becomes less abstract.

I wrote to [[Baroness Thunderhold]], whose lands and household were near enough to observe the event’s consequences.

Her reply may be summarized as follows:

> “Yes. Quite aware. I saw the smoke plume from my window. That was all the notice I was given.”

I have rendered the tone with restraint.

The original carried the emotional temperature of a kettle just before violence.

This is not a minor concern.

A volcanic working is not a private correction to a ritual diagram. It announces itself to the sky, the soil, neighboring settlements, military scouts, farmers, hunters, refugees, monsters, priests, opportunists, enemy observers, and every nervous noble with a window facing the wrong direction.

Smoke is not notice.

Ash is not consultation.

A plume visible from a baronial window is not a memorandum.

Master Stonyeye’s report acknowledges royal concern after the fact. It does not sufficiently address local noble notice before the act, nor does it explain whether neighboring authorities were warned, evacuated, briefed, or given opportunity to secure roads, livestock, watch posts, civilian populations, water sources, or messenger lines.

One may say there was no time.

Perhaps.

But the report must then say so plainly, and with evidence.

“Necessary speed” is not a universal solvent for obligation.

It is a claim requiring proof.

## On the Third Problem: Environmental Consequence

Master Stonyeye’s report is strong on magical consequence and comparatively thin on ecological consequence.

This is a wizardly habit.

Wizards often remember that land contains magic before remembering that it also contains frogs.

A Volcano working in swamp terrain does not merely reshape ley expression. It may alter:

- drainage patterns;
    
- water temperature;
    
- local acidity;
    
- ash deposition;
    
- peat stability;
    
- marsh gas pockets;
    
- fish and amphibian populations;
    
- root systems;
    
- insect cycles;
    
- migratory bird patterns;
    
- potable water access;
    
- disease patterns;
    
- grazing margins;
    
- road foundations;
    
- and the seasonal behavior of every creature with the poor judgment to live near humans.
    

Some of these changes may be beneficial.

Some may be catastrophic.

Most will be complicated.

Professor Dolvath noted that volcanic uplift within saturated ground may temporarily seal contaminated channels while creating new pressure gradients elsewhere. Heated subsurface water may migrate unpredictably. Ash can enrich soil in the long term while poisoning water in the short term. Cooled flows may create stable foundations, but also dam old drainage and flood settlements that thought themselves uninvolved in royal thaumaturgy.

Master Stonyeye recommends water sampling over one lunar cycle.

Good.

That should have been the beginning of an environmental plan, not a reassuring appendix.

## On the Fourth Problem: The Swamp Was Not Empty

The report treats Harrowmere as an abandoned fortress and unholy engine. It does not give sufficient attention to the surrounding living territory.

Swamps are not blank spaces where inconvenient castles can be volcanically corrected without consequence. They are inhabited environments, even when sparsely settled. They are hunting grounds, hiding places, herb sources, fishing grounds, birding waters, old paths, illegal paths, refugee paths, smugglers’ paths, and the homes of species that do not attend royal hearings because no one invited them and they lack pockets.

If the enemy used the swamp as a ritual medium, then cleansing the medium matters.

But cleansing is not the same as obliteration.

Master Stonyeye may answer that the Volcano working did not obliterate the swamp, only the site and its local formation.

Very well.

How far did the thermal shock reach?

How far did ash fall?

Which channels were sealed?

Which were diverted?

What creatures fled?

What creatures died?

Which villages downstream now drink from altered water?

Were local marsh-folk, hunters, woodsmen, charcoal burners, fishermen, or shrine-keepers consulted after the fact?

The report does not say.

That silence is not fatal to the magical justification.

It is fatal to the claim of complete responsibility.

## On the Fifth Problem: Sacred Violence

I accept that Harrowmere had become a profane instrument.

I accept that breaking the instrument was necessary.

I accept that Volcano may have been the only available working capable of achieving simultaneous surface destruction, substructure sterilization, channel cauterization, and geomantic redirection.

But the realm should be careful with the phrase “corrective destruction.”

It is a useful phrase.

It is also one tyrants will adore.

To destroy severely and then call the destruction correction is sometimes accurate. A surgeon amputates. A priest burns a plague blanket. A paladin smites a possessing fiend. A firebreak saves a forest by wounding it.

But “correction” must never become a perfume for force.

The test is not whether the destruction can be poetically justified after success.

The test is whether it was bounded, necessary, proportionate, witnessed, reviewed, and followed by repair.

Master Stonyeye addresses necessity.

He partly addresses proportionality.

He recommends review.

He does not sufficiently address boundedness, witness, notice, or repair.

Those omissions matter because Volcano is not a narrow knife.

It is a divine-sized hammer borrowed by an arcane hand.

## On The Venerated Shield of Heironeous

Master Stonyeye is careful to note that [[The Venerated Shield of Heironeous]] and the paladins present had already judged Harrowmere must be destroyed.

This is fair.

It is also a shield placed somewhat too prominently.

The Venerated Shield of Heironeous is not merely a knight of good reputation, nor a convenient witness in armor, nor a local holy officer whose presence may be cited as one cites an approving quartermaster. She is, by all credible accounts, a living saint of the Invincible’s service. I do not use that phrase decoratively. There are lives in which divine favor is inferred. In hers, it appears to have grown weary of subtlety.

I concede freely that the hand of Heironeous rests upon her.

That does not make her a command structure.

So far as the records available to me show, The Venerated Shield holds no ordinary leadership office within the formal hierarchy of the Heironean Church. She is not a bishop in armor. She is not a temple marshal issuing ordinary standing orders. She goes where she may, stands where she must, and speaks with the terrible informal authority granted to those whom the faithful believe the god has already answered.

This distinction matters.

The Venerated Shield does not need to give orders in order to move men.

The least of her suggestions may cause devout Heironeans to spring into motion with the speed of soldiers who have mistaken obedience for breathing. A raised brow from such a figure can carry more weight than a signed directive from a lesser prelate, because saints are inconvenient that way. They distort procedure by existing too persuasively.

Thus her presence supports the righteousness of destroying Harrowmere. It strongly supports the seriousness of the profanation. It lends grave moral force to the conclusion that the site could not stand.

It does not automatically sanctify every possible method.

The Venerated Shield’s holy authority over profanation, cleansing, and righteous destruction is immense in practical effect, even where it is not bureaucratic in form. Yet she is not, so far as the filing shows, a geomantic officer of the Crown empowered to approve the creation of a caldera within royal lands.

This reduces the concern of rogue action.

It does not remove the concern of scale.

## On Sapphire Flame’s Review

Because the report was addressed also to Acting Archmage [[Sapphire Flame]], I assume the Crown has or will seek formal arcane review.

It should.

Not because Master Stonyeye’s equations are likely wrong. Irritatingly, they are not.

But because a royal record must distinguish between:

- whether the spell worked;
    
- whether the spell was necessary;
    
- whether the spell was authorized;
    
- whether the spell was proportionate;
    
- whether the spell caused remediable harm;
    
- whether future procedures must change;
    
- and whether the caster should be praised, censured, or both.
    

The last category exists for precisely this kind of person.

## On Verification

Master Stonyeye recommends several sensible examinations:

- three-circle thaumic survey;
    
- aerial mage-sight or sanctified observation;
    
- divine consultation by Heironean clergy;
    
- geomantic sounding by earth-wise casters;
    
- water sampling over one lunar cycle;
    
- ward-stake testing for sympathetic pull.
    

These are sound.

I would add:

- seasonal water monitoring for one full year;
    
- ash and soil testing at increasing distances from the caldera;
    
- survey of downstream settlements and marsh usage;
    
- animal die-off and migration report;
    
- local disease tracking;
    
- structural stability review of cooled stone before military construction;
    
- consultation with druids, marsh-priests, or local nature clergy where available;
    
- testimony from Baroness Thunderhold and any affected vassals;
    
- review of whether enemy observers responded to the plume;
    
- legal finding on destruction of former royal fortification by extraordinary means;
    
- prohibition on development planning until cleansing and stability are independently confirmed.
    

The last point is important.

Do not build an airship yard on a magical scar because the scar happens to be flat in places.

That is how future historians get to write sentences beginning, “They were warned.”

## On Vice Admiral Thalrik and the Airship Yard Question

The postscript is astonishing.

Not because the proposal is foolish.

It is not foolish.

That is what makes it astonishing.

Master Stonyeye argues that the new caldera may be developed as an airship docking, repair, refit, and resupply station. He identifies elevation, visibility, firm volcanic foundations, swamp-defense, separation of functions, isolation, and symbolic transformation of wound into royal instrument.

These are real advantages.

Professor Dolvath reluctantly agreed that cooled volcanic stone may provide excellent foundation opportunities once stability is confirmed. [[Vice Admiral Thalrik]]’s interest is unsurprising. Airships prefer elevation, clear approaches, secure anchors, and defensible ground not easily reached by carts full of angry men.

However.

Vice Admiral Thalrik is not merely an airman with practical opinions about docking pylons.

He is a figure of rapidly increasing consequence, a man whose reputation includes an energetic gathering of titles, coin, offices, favors, vessels, licenses, influence, and strategically useful friendships with both hands and, if rumor may be indulged, possibly his teeth. He is scandalous in the way ascending men often are: too useful to dismiss, too successful to ignore, and too surrounded by opportunity for comfortable observers to believe every opportunity arrived by accident.

Let me be precise.

I do not accuse Vice Admiral Thalrik of manipulating Master Stonyeye into casting Volcano.

The thaumaturgical case does not require such a theory.

Nor do I claim that Master Stonyeye’s magical judgment was shaped by future industrial convenience. His diagnosis of Harrowmere as an occult wound appears sound. His remedy, however severe, appears magically defensible under the stated conditions.

But it would be negligent not to observe that the postscript converts an emergency geomantic catastrophe into a potentially lucrative and strategically significant airship development site with remarkable speed.

One may therefore ask, safely and properly:

Who first saw the caldera as an airship yard?

When was Vice Admiral Thalrik consulted?

Did his interest precede the postscript, follow it, or shape it?

Was Master Stonyeye’s strategic imagination encouraged by an airman already skilled at recognizing how ruin may be turned into infrastructure, infrastructure into authority, and authority into yet another rung on an increasingly crowded ladder?

Again, these are questions.

Not accusations.

Questions are what scholars use when libel would be emotionally satisfying but legally unwise.

The Crown should separate three issues that Master Stonyeye’s postscript places too close together:

First, whether Harrowmere required extraordinary magical destruction.

Second, whether the resulting caldera is stable, cleansed, and safe.

Third, whether Vice Admiral Thalrik, or any office connected to him, should be involved in planning, funding, controlling, profiting from, or administratively inheriting the proposed airship yard.

The destruction of Harrowmere must be judged first as an emergency corrective act.

Only after independent verification should the Crown consider whether to transform the scar into an airship station.

Otherwise, the realm risks establishing a grotesque precedent:

Destroy first.

Discover utility second.

Invite an ambitious admiral to explain why the smoking crater is actually a procurement miracle.

This is how kingdoms teach clever servants to make disasters useful before asking whether they were lawful.

## On Baroness Thunderhold’s Position

Baroness Thunderhold’s irritation deserves more than courtly sympathy.

From her perspective, a major magical geological event occurred within sight of her household without warning sufficient to secure people, livestock, roads, messengers, or political composure.

A baroness who sees a smoke plume from her window is not merely startled.

She is being informed, by the sky, that someone else has made a decision with consequences for her region.

This is poor governance.

Even if the decision was magically correct.

Especially if the decision was magically correct.

The more justified the act, the more important the notice. If royal servants expect local nobles to trust extraordinary measures, then local nobles must not learn of them through smoke.

A realm cannot function if every wizard with accurate diagnostics may alter geography and then send explanation afterward.

Nor can a realm survive if every emergency action must wait upon a committee, seal, witness, and tea service.

There lies the tension.

Master Stonyeye’s report addresses one side of it.

The Council must address the other.

## Ethical Findings

I offer the following provisional ethical findings.

First, if Harrowmere was as described, destruction was justified.

Second, if the buried resonance engine was as described, ordinary demolition would have been dangerously insufficient.

Third, if the Volcano working behaved as Master Stonyeye states, the spell was thaumaturgically proportionate to the occult problem.

Fourth, the report does not sufficiently demonstrate that the social, environmental, political, and jurisdictional consequences were considered before action.

Fifth, the lack of notice to Baroness Thunderhold is a serious failure unless delay would have materially endangered the operation.

Sixth, post-facto strategic utility must not be allowed to launder procedural deficiency.

Seventh, Master Stonyeye’s willingness to submit to examination is proper and should be taken literally.

Eighth, no punishment should be considered until verification is complete.

Ninth, no praise should be made public until the same is true.

Tenth, the Council should prepare to discover that Master Stonyeye was both right and impossible.

These categories are not mutually exclusive.

Wizards have made careers of that inconvenience.

## Recommended Council Response

I recommend the Council adopt the following response:

1. Accept Master Stonyeye’s filing as provisionally credible on thaumaturgical grounds.
    
2. Commission independent verification under Sapphire Flame, with Professor Dolvath or equivalent earth-mage consultation.
    
3. Request formal testimony from The Venerated Shield of Heironeous concerning urgency, timing, authority, and observed site corruption.
    
4. Request written statement from Baroness Thunderhold concerning notice, local effects, and political impact.
    
5. Order immediate environmental and water monitoring.
    
6. Delay all airship yard planning until after cleansing confirmation and structural review.
    
7. Establish emergency procedure for corrupted royal holdings where extraordinary magical destruction may be necessary.
    
8. Clarify that holy or military authority to destroy a profaned site does not automatically authorize major geomantic alteration unless delay poses unacceptable danger.
    
9. Clarify that in cases of imminent occult danger, after-action review may suffice, but only if urgency is demonstrated.
    
10. Require Master Stonyeye to assist the investigation.
    

This last recommendation has the added benefit of making him explain himself to committees, which may serve justice even if no formal censure is required.

## On Master Stonyeye’s Character as Reflected in the Filing

It would be easy, and emotionally satisfying, to paint Master Stonyeye as reckless.

The filing does not support so simple a portrait.

He is not careless.

He is not frivolous.

He is not ignorant of consequences.

He is, however, dangerously confident when his diagnostics are correct.

This is an old hazard among powerful casters. Accurate understanding of one layer of reality can produce impatience with layers governed by title, grief, local authority, muddy roads, frightened peasants, birds, fish, baronesses, and the political meaning of smoke.

Master Stonyeye saw the occult wound.

He identified the correct magical instrument.

He applied it.

The question is whether he sufficiently saw everything else that would be standing nearby when the mountain arrived.

I suspect he saw more than his critics will claim.

I also suspect he saw less than the land deserved.

## Final Assessment

Master Norwin Stonyeye’s **Formal Account and Thaumaturgical Justification Concerning the Destruction of Harrowmere Enclosure** is an excellent magical defense and an incomplete civic one.

The core thaumaturgy appears sound.

The identification of Harrowmere as a sacrificial resonance engine is persuasive.

The claim that conventional demolition would have been insufficient is credible.

The use of Volcano as geomantic severance, cauterization, and pattern destruction is, under the stated conditions, defensible.

This must be said plainly.

It is also plainly insufficient.

A realm is not made only of ley lines.

It is made of landholders, neighbors, marsh creatures, downstream water, frightened households, military signals, divine authority, legal boundaries, royal memory, and the expectation that one does not learn of volcanic correction by looking out a window.

Master Stonyeye saved the land from a hidden wound.

He may also have given the land a visible scar without asking everyone who had to live beside it.

Perhaps the scar was necessary.

Perhaps it will heal cleanly.

Perhaps it will even become, after proper review, a royal airship station of immense value.

But necessity must not be allowed to become a habit.

Correction must not become vanity.

And no servant of the Crown, however brilliant, should be permitted to discover that making a mountain is easier than notifying a baroness.

Master Stonyeye’s science is, to my considerable irritation, above reproach.

His procedure is not.

Let the Crown verify the first, repair the second, and resist the temptation to confuse an accurate wizard with a complete one.
