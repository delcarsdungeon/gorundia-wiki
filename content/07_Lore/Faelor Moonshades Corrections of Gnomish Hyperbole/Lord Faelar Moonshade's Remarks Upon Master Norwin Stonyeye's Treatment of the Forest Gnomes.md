# Lord Faelar Moonshade's Remarks Upon Master Norwin Stonyeye's Treatment of the Forest Gnomes, Being the First of Several Necessary Clarifications Concerning Gnomish Peoples, Elven Witness, Woodland Kinship, and the Difference Between Disorder and Older Forms of Harmony

> [!quote] Lord Faelar Moonshade  
> A scholar may complain of moss upon the stones, yet the moss records the rain more faithfully than the stone records the sky.

## Preliminary Courtesy

I have read Master Norwin Stonyeye’s report on the gnomish peoples with genuine interest, professional appreciation, and only intermittent sorrow.

That last point should not be mistaken for insult. Sorrow is among the nobler reactions available to the historian when encountering a lively mind pressed too closely against the narrow window of its own training.

Master Stonyeye is plainly learned. He is observant, technically precise, and commendably unwilling to repeat vulgar errors merely because they have acquired the dignity of old ink. His insistence that rock and forest gnomes are kin, rather than separate species, is both accurate and necessary. His refusal to confirm the existence of deep gnomes in the absence of reliable testimony does him credit. His warnings to the Gorundian Mages’ Guild concerning forest gnome charm-work are, if inelegantly phrased, not without practical value.

There.

Let it not be said that I began without generosity.

Now we may proceed to the damage.

## On Master Stonyeye’s Tone

Master Stonyeye describes forest gnomes as “a footnote written by a butterfly.”

This is charming.

It is also wrong in precisely the way clever remarks often are: not false enough to dismiss, but shaped to flatter the speaker’s impatience rather than the subject’s nature.

Forest gnomes are not a footnote to gnomish history. They are one of its oldest surviving marginalia, which is a very different matter. A footnote is appended after the argument has already found its shape. Marginalia lives beside the text, corrects it, jokes with it, stains it, contradicts it, and occasionally preserves the only true reading after the formal page has been copied too many times by hands that understood grammar better than meaning.

I do not expect Master Stonyeye to enjoy that distinction.

I do expect him to record it.

## On the Importance of Forest Gnomes

Among the gnomish peoples presently known to Gorundian scholarship, forest gnomes are, in my judgment, the most important for the study of ancient kinship between the small folk, the woodland courts, the surviving elven polities, and the old fey roads that once threaded the western forests before human roads taught the land to prefer straight lines.

This is not because forest gnomes are “more authentic” than rock gnomes. Such thinking is foolish. A jewel is not less true than an acorn because it lies beneath stone rather than leaf.

Rather, forest gnomes matter because they preserve modes of life that other civilized peoples have too often translated into metaphor.

They remember that a boundary may be sung.

They remember that a path may change its mind.

They remember that a fox at a naming ceremony is not automatically a symbol, a superstition, or a pest. Sometimes it is merely a fox. Sometimes it is not. Wisdom begins in knowing that one has not yet earned the right to decide.

Master Stonyeye finds this irritating.

Naturally.

He is a wizard.

## Concerning Harmony

The forest gnome’s relation to the natural world is not primitive religion wearing leaves for dignity. Nor is it, as Master Stonyeye implies, a confused mixture of “arcane theory, hedge-wizardry, druidic ritual, fey compact, folk charm, illusion craft, herbal practice, and superstition.”

It is true that forest gnome practice contains all these things.

It is not true that their coexistence implies confusion.

The error lies in assuming that a distinction drawn by academies is therefore a distinction honored by the world.

A brook does not divide itself into hydraulic principle, song, boundary marker, fish-road, childhood memory, and minor shrine. It is all of these while remaining water.

Forest gnomes understand this in ways that rock gnomes often analyze, high elves too often ceremonialize, and humans generally pave over before asking what was lost.

The forest gnome does not ask whether a spell is arcane, divine, druidic, fey, or ancestral before deciding whether it will keep wolves from the lambing hollow.

He asks whether it keeps the lambs alive.

This is not disorder.

It is prioritization.

## Citation from the Aetheryn Mossleaf Fragments

The oldest fragment in the Allaric Archives that clearly refers to forest gnome rites is commonly catalogued as the _Aetheryn Mossleaf Fragment_, though the title is modern and somewhat clumsy. The original leaf-strip identifies its subjects as the “small keepers beneath the hazel bells,” which later copyists, with predictable cowardice, softened into “woodland auxiliaries.”

The relevant passage reads:

> “They do not command the green. They request, recall, trade, flatter, warn, and remember beside it. Their charms are not laws cast upon branch and beast, but courtesies made durable.”

Master Stonyeye would likely object to the lack of procedural classification.

That is precisely why the passage remains useful.

## The Matter of Elven Affection

Master Stonyeye remarks that elves are genuinely fond of forest gnomes, and then, unable to leave a clean observation unmarred by soot, compares this affection to the treatment of tallfellow halflings: “affectionate, protective, lightly insulting, and draped in enough graceful superiority to make any sensible person consider arson.”

This is a vivid sentence.

I appreciate vividness.

I take polite offense anyway.

Elven affection for forest gnomes is not merely the indulgence of the tall toward the short, the ancient toward the brief-lived, or the courtly toward the rustic. Such failures have occurred, yes. I will not defend every soft-handed lordling who ever addressed a forest gnome elder as though speaking to a clever child with charming shoes.

But the deeper tradition is not condescension.

It is recognition.

The high courts of old Nilanthalas and Elenlindë did not regard forest gnomes as ornaments. They regarded them as necessary witnesses of the living boundary between settlement and wildwood. Forest gnomes crossed places where elven heralds could not go without declaring a presence. They carried songs between villages, warnings between groves, and small truths between larger powers too proud to hear them from one another.

If elves smiled upon them, it was not always downward.

Sometimes it was across.

Master Stonyeye, being trained by stone, may have mistaken height for direction.

## Citation from the Greenward Concordance

The _Greenward Concordance of Nilanthalas_, preserved in partial copy in the Allaric Archives, records forest gnome intermediaries during the later woodland disputes of the western marches. The Concordance names them not as subjects, nor as clients, but as “listeners of the root-edge,” a phrase difficult to render in Common without losing both intimacy and office.

One passage describes a forest gnome speaker standing between an elven ranger-captain and a village of charcoal-burners:

> “She spoke no decree. She placed three seeds upon the stone, and both parties knew the grove had already judged them.”

This is not decorative folklore.

This is jurisprudence by shared symbol.

One may dislike such methods. One may prefer stamped writs, sworn testimony, and measured boundary stones.

Yet the grove survived.

So, for that matter, did the charcoal-burners.

## On Forest Gnome Magic

Here Master Stonyeye’s report becomes most valuable and most wounded by its own irritation.

He correctly notes that forest gnome magic does not maintain clean divisions between formal arcana, folk custom, druidic rite, fey compact, and illusion. He correctly notes that it produces results. He correctly warns that these results cannot safely be dismissed by formal guild examiners.

Then he commits the standard institutional error: he calls the practice undisciplined.

I suggest a gentler and more accurate term.

Relational.

Rock gnome magic, as Master Stonyeye describes it, excels in repeatability, calibration, mechanism, documentable function, and reliable transfer between trained practitioners. This is a magnificent achievement. No archive, city, bridge, warded vault, or civilized kettle should undervalue it.

Forest gnome magic is not built for the same purpose.

It is local, responsive, situational, and context-bound. It does not seek first to dominate a principle, but to enter relation with a place, creature, mood, season, fear, scent, echo, or remembered path. It works poorly when severed from these things because these things are not decorative. They are components.

To a guild examiner, “the mood of the creek” is nonsense.

To a forest gnome charm-worker, it is an observed condition of flow, sound, temperature, animal behavior, recent rain, and whatever lesser presence has been sulking under the alder since midsummer.

The phrase is poetic.

It is not empty.

A lesser scholar confuses the two.

## On the Fey-Touched Quality

Master Stonyeye is correct that forest gnomes display fey-touched qualities. Their larger eyes, lighter builds, subtle movements, and instinct for concealment all align with accounts from Araisal, Nilanthalas remnants, and older elven road-songs.

But one must take care.

To call a people “fey-touched” can mean many things. It may refer to ancestry, long proximity, spiritual affinity, cultural exchange, adaptive magic, or simply the effect of living for generations near thresholds where the world wears thin.

Forest gnomes are not little fey.

Nor are they failed elves.

Nor, despite certain delightful village tales, are they “laughing mushrooms taught to walk by moonlight.”

They are gnomes whose history runs beside old green powers.

That is already interesting enough without embroidery.

## Concerning Ehlonna

Master Stonyeye admits, with visible personal discomfort, that many forest gnomes honor Ehlonna.

He also admits that he does not fully understand this.

This may be the wisest sentence in the report.

Forest gnome devotion to Ehlonna is not reducible to elven influence, though elven influence is often present. Nor is it merely an adoption of woodland fashion. Ehlonna offers forest gnome communities a divine grammar for sanctuary: the protection of gentle beasts, young growth, hidden clearings, safe passage, and beauty that survives because someone keeps watch over it.

To a people small in body, subtle in movement, and long accustomed to survival through concealment, Ehlonna is not only a goddess of flowers and deer.

She is the holy defense of the unarmored.

Master Stonyeye sees wreaths, antlers, whispered prayers, and rabbits at naming ceremonies.

The forest gnome sees a world in which the vulnerable must be noticed before they are devoured.

This is theology.

With rabbits, yes.

One does not invalidate the other.

## Citation from The Moon-Bell Homilies

A woodland devotional preserved in late Nilanthalasi script, _The Moon-Bell Homilies_, includes a prayer believed to have passed through forest gnome communities before entering elven liturgical notation:

> “Lady of the leaf-shadow, let the small foot pass where the iron boot would stumble. Let the antler turn aside the spear. Let the burrow-child sleep while the owl keeps count.”

This is not high elven composition. The cadence is wrong, the imagery too close to ground-level, the prayer too practical. It is almost certainly forest gnome or forest gnome-adjacent.

The phrase “while the owl keeps count” appears in three later charm-bundles from Araisal. In one, it is tied to child protection. In another, to concealed travel. In a third, amusingly, to the prevention of tax inspection.

Religious continuity is rarely tidy.

That does not make it imaginary.

## On the “Color-Drained” Myth

Master Stonyeye objects strongly to the forest gnome myth that rock gnomes were once brighter, freer, or more fey-touched, and that stone, ink, tools, and regulation diminished them.

His objection is fair.

The myth is not ethnographic fact. It is not biological history. It should not be treated as an account of gnomish divergence.

However, myths are rarely useless merely because they are not literal.

The tale tells us how some forest gnomes understand the difference between themselves and their rock kin. They do not necessarily mean that rock gnomes lost pigment or joy in any measurable sense. They mean that rock gnomes chose durable enclosure over living entanglement. They mean that tools remember differently than trees. They mean that a people can become safer and yet less permeable to wonder.

Is this flattering to rock gnomes?

No.

Is it fair?

Not entirely.

Is it meaningless?

Certainly not.

Master Stonyeye’s counterclaim that rock gnomish restraint is “called taste” is, in turn, less an argument than a defensive waistcoat buttoned very tightly.

Still, I admire the tailoring.

## On Norwin's Repeated Elven Remarks

I have counted, in Master Stonyeye’s forest gnome section and its neighboring remarks, several direct or indirect criticisms of elves. We are said to patronize, categorize, smile too knowingly, delay too long, over-ceremonialize, and take centuries to decide upon hinge-metal.

Some of these accusations are unjust.

Some are merely exaggerated.

A few are regrettably well observed.

Let the record show that I object to the tone more than the entire substance. A scholar may strike the bell if the bell deserves it, but he should not then complain that the sound continues.

The long memory of elves is not always condescension. Sometimes it is grief mistaken for distance. Sometimes it is caution bought at terrible cost. Sometimes, yes, it is vanity wearing a patient face.

But Master Stonyeye should remember this: when forest gnomes learned from elves, they did not only inherit solemnity. They inherited songs of boundary, medicinal lore, star-paths, warning signs, rites of guesthood, and the craft of listening to wood before cutting it.

When elves learned from forest gnomes, we inherited laughter under leaves, better ways to speak to small doors, and the useful knowledge that a solemn procession can be improved by hiding bells in the hems of the third-ranking herald.

History is not a ladder.

It is a canopy.

Some branches are simply louder about the sun.

## Proper Scholarly Handling of Forest Gnome Sources

Any future examination of forest gnomes should proceed according to the following principles:

- Do not treat indirect answers as evasions until you understand the local metaphor.
    
- Do not treat charms as superstition until you have tested whether they function.
    
- Do not assume elven influence means elven origin.
    
- Do not assume fey influence means unreliability.
    
- Do not assume informality means lack of tradition.
    
- Do not ask for a map if what you require is permission.
    
- Do not step inside a mushroom ring while holding iron, silver, or an attitude.
    
- Do not describe a shrine as “mixed use” in the hearing of its keeper.
    
- Do not laugh at the rabbit.
    

The rabbit may be irrelevant.

It may also have seniority.

## Final Assessment

Master Norwin Stonyeye’s report on forest gnomes is valuable, lively, frequently correct, and written with the composure of a man forced to admit that a spell can work without first submitting itself to committee review.

His central facts are sound. Forest gnomes are gnomish kin. They are fey-touched. They are associated with Araisal, old Nilanthalasi regions, and wandering woodland enclaves. Their magic is hybrid, practical, and difficult to classify. They often honor the gnomish gods, Ehlonna, and certain local powers in overlapping ways. Elves are genuinely fond of them.

His central failure is interpretive.

He mistakes harmony for muddle.

He mistakes relational magic for disorder.

He mistakes elven affection for simple condescension.

And, most predictably, he mistakes his own irritation for evidence.

This is not a grave sin. Many useful scholars have begun there.

The better ones continue.

Forest gnomes are not a problem to be solved by taxonomy. They are a people who preserve, in small bodies and laughing customs, an old lesson most kingdoms forget as soon as they build walls:

The world is not made of separate things.

It is made of relations.

Stone and root.

Burrow and branch.

Charm and theorem.

Fox and footpath.

Gnome and elf.

Memory and moss.

Master Stonyeye has given us the stone.

For that, he deserves thanks.

Now let us turn it over and see what grows beneath.
