# Lord Faelar Moonshade's Third Set of Remarks Upon Master Norwin Stonyeye's Treatment of the Deep Gnomes, Being a Necessary Meditation on Unconfirmed Peoples, Subterranean Myth, Dwarven Silence, Gnomish Impatience, and the Scholarly Usefulness of Not Knowing

> [!quote] Lord Faelar Moonshade  
> Some doors are hidden because no one has found them. Others are hidden because they have judged the knocking and found it wanting.

## Preliminary Admission

Let us begin with the rarest and most beautiful flower in scholarship:

An admission of ignorance.

I do not know whether deep gnomes exist.

Neither does Master Norwin Stonyeye.

Nor, despite their expressions of granite certainty, do most dwarven informants.

Nor do miners who have spent six days underground with insufficient light, insufficient water, and a theological commitment to exaggerating whatever tapped back from the dark.

No confirmed Gorundian diplomatic record names a deep gnome people. No accepted treaty identifies their holdings. No census, royal writ, temple register, guildroll, inheritance claim, sworn witness table, or accepted map places them securely within the world as Gorundian scholars currently understand it.

This is important.

It is not conclusive.

The absence of a people from our records may mean they are not there.

It may also mean they have had the exquisite wisdom to avoid our records.

## On Master Stonyeye’s Position

Master Stonyeye handles the question of deep gnomes with greater restraint than one might expect from a man otherwise inclined to treat ambiguity as a personal insult committed by fog.

He refuses confirmation. Good.

He gathers recurring rumors. Good.

He warns against armed expeditions. Very good.

He advises caution, comparison, and respectful inquiry. Excellent.

Then, having reached the edge of genuine methodological humility, he recoils slightly and calls most of the material nonsense.

This is where we must pause.

Not because he is wrong.

Much of it may be nonsense.

But nonsense is not a category of evidence. It is a mood.

A myth may be false in event and true in structure.

A rumor may be wrong in fact and precise in fear.

A miner’s tale may invent a face and preserve a sound.

An old elven song may turn a trade encounter into moonlit allegory and still remember that someone small once stood beneath the mountain before the dwarves sealed the eastern doors.

Master Stonyeye knows machinery.

He is less comfortable with echoes.

This is unfortunate.

The deep, if it teaches anything, teaches through echoes.

## On the Phrase “Unconfirmed”

“Unconfirmed” is one of the most abused words in responsible scholarship.

Used well, it means: the evidence is insufficient for assertion.

Used poorly, it means: I do not wish to think about this until someone brings me a labeled corpse, preferably notarized.

Master Stonyeye uses the term mostly well.

Mostly.

His caution is admirable, but his impatience stains the page. He would like the deep gnomes either to exist in a way that can be measured, interviewed, classified, and compared, or to stop troubling the footnotes.

Unfortunately for him, hidden peoples rarely organize themselves for the convenience of external review.

If deep gnomes exist, they may owe nothing to Gorundian categories.

They may not call themselves gnomes.

They may not call themselves anything outsiders can hear.

They may regard surface kinship as a quaint overreach, dwarven cartography as vandalism with rulers, and elven speculation as moonlight spilled where no one asked for light.

On that last point, they may have a case.

## The First Myth: The Small Hands Beneath Dern Kalduhr

The oldest possibly relevant source in the Allaric Archives is a damaged passage from the _Lament of the Sealed East-Gate_, an elven copy of what appears to be a dwarven mourning chant from the years surrounding the withdrawal and sealing of Dern Kalduhr.

The language is difficult. The copy is late. The scribe was probably working from oral recitation, and the margin contains three separate complaints about dwarven vowel-weight.

Still, the passage is worth preserving.

> “Before the hammer slept and the gate learned silence,  
> the little hands beneath the lower stair  
> struck no anvil and swore no oath,  
> yet left the stone remembering passage.”

The traditional interpretation is that “little hands” refers to dwarf children.

This is possible.

It is also lazy.

The phrase “beneath the lower stair” does not indicate a nursery, clan hall, or refugee chamber. In dwarven architectural poetry, lower-stair imagery usually refers to thresholds below the formal settlement layers: drainage halls, abandoned cuttings, survey tunnels, root-caves, and the old passages that respectable dwarves insist were never used for smuggling.

If the line does not refer to children, it may refer to an older or parallel subterranean people known to the dwarves but not counted among them.

Do I claim these were deep gnomes?

No.

Do I claim the possibility should make Master Stonyeye stop calling every inconvenient subterranean tradition “interesting nonsense” with the weary indulgence of a man correcting a kettle?

Also no.

I do not expect miracles.

## The Second Myth: The Pale Listeners

A much stranger reference occurs in the _Starless Itinerary of Vael Tatharion_, a travel manuscript attributed to an elven scout-scholar of the late western dispersal period. The original is lost. The Allaric Archives preserve a partial blue-ink copy, itself copied from a water-damaged vellum said to have come through Araisal by way of a half-elven monastery.

I include this chain of custody because Master Stonyeye enjoys certainty, and I wish to be kind enough to deny him any.

The relevant passage reads:

> “Below the third root of the mountain, where no fern remembers light, we heard tapping. Not the dwarf-code, not the worm-click, not the fall of water. Three measures, pause, two measures, pause, seven measures. The guide placed his hand upon the wall and would not speak until the sound ceased. When asked what passed, he answered: ‘Those who listen without lanterns.’”

Later in the same text:

> “We left copper wire, salt, and a mirror no larger than a child’s palm. In the morning the salt remained, the wire was braided, and the mirror showed only stone.”

Again, no confirmation.

No visible people.

No names.

No treaty.

Only rhythm, refusal, exchange, and an altered object.

Master Stonyeye might call this insufficient.

He would be correct.

He might call it useless.

He would not.

Because he is not a fool.

Annoying, certainly.

But not a fool.

## The Third Myth: The Mountain Names Before Dwarves

Master Stonyeye shows reluctant interest in the rumor that deep gnomes know mountain names older than dwarven settlement. He is right to do so, though his annoyance at plausibility is almost audible.

The Allaric Archives contain several elven place-name lists from the earliest surviving western surveys. These lists include names marked as “heard below” or “not ours, not dwarven.” The notation appears rarely and inconsistently, but it appears.

Examples include:

- **Tharn-Ul Keth**, glossed as “stone sleeping under its own name.”
    
- **Mibrel Nokh**, glossed as “the cold bell no hand rings.”
    
- **Sivren-Dael**, glossed as “where the dark drinks sound.”
    
- **Kund-Velith**, glossed as “the narrow mercy beneath weight.”
    

The forms do not resemble high elven, wood elven, classical dwarven, or any known old human tongue.

Could they be corrupted dwarven mining terms?

Yes.

Could they be elven misunderstandings of cave features?

Certainly.

Could they represent older subterranean nomenclature preserved through contact with an unknown small folk?

Also yes.

The honest scholar must sit with these possibilities without immediately choosing the most comfortable chair.

Master Stonyeye, I suspect, has already measured the chair.

## Dwarven Silence

No study of deep gnome rumor can proceed without addressing dwarven silence.

Dwarves know more than they say.

This is not accusation. It is weather.

Dwarves know more than they say about old shafts, sealed failures, clan debts, lost passages, bad bargains, beneath-roads, things heard in mines, and why certain doors are bricked over with no inscription except a mason’s mark and a prayer to whatever god was closest.

When asked directly about deep gnomes, dwarven answers tend to fall into several categories:

- “No.”
    
- “Not ours.”
    
- “Old nonsense.”
    
- “Who told you that?”
    
- “Do not go there.”
    
- “Ask the stones, if you like being dead.”
    
- silence accompanied by drinking.
    

These are not equal answers.

The first may be denial.

The second may be jurisdiction.

The third may be embarrassment.

The fourth is intelligence gathering.

The fifth is warning.

The sixth is religion wearing a helmet.

The seventh is dwarven.

A careful scholar does not collapse all of these into absence.

## The Myth of the Stone That Heard Lies

Master Stonyeye finds the claim that deep gnomes can hear lies spoken through stone “probably metaphor,” though he cannot dismiss the possibility of tremor-reading combined with magic.

Here he comes very near wisdom, then looks irritated to find himself there.

There are three traditions worth comparing.

First, certain dwarven judges are said to hold hearings in chambers where the accused must stand barefoot upon a single black slab. The slab is not magical in any confirmed court record, yet testimony taken there is reputed to be unusually brief.

Second, old elven hunter-songs speak of “ground-listeners” who could tell whether a stag, boar, elf, or armored man had crossed a ridge by placing their cheek to exposed rock.

Third, rock gnome engineering texts, as Master Stonyeye would surely know better than I, include devices capable of detecting vibration through walls, gears, and pressure plates.

Put these beside the deep gnome rumor.

What emerges is not proof.

It is a pattern.

A subterranean people, if adapted to darkness and stone, might plausibly develop nonvisual truth customs. Not lie detection in the priestly sense, perhaps, but embodied reading: breath, stance, pressure, tremor, hesitation, shifting weight, pulse through sole and floor.

To such a people, a liar might sound different.

Not morally.

Physically.

A myth then says: they hear lies through stone.

This is exactly how knowledge survives when no one writes the manual.

## The White Silence Below

One tale, preserved only in a late Araisal fireside collection called _Stories Told While the Snow Refused to Stop_, describes “white gnomes” living below a mountain lake. The tale is plainly embroidered. It involves a talking fish, a silver comb, two impossible doors, and a shepherd whose wife is probably the moon in disguise because rural storytellers cannot leave well enough alone.

Still, its description of the hidden folk is curious:

> “They had eyes like milk in winter and hands that moved as though touching harp-strings in the air. They did not speak while the boy watched, yet every one of them turned when the old one struck the wall with a bone needle.”

This resembles other rumors: pale coloring, silence, nonverbal communication, stone-sound.

The story says they returned the lost shepherd-child unharmed, but marked his left palm with three gray dots. Thereafter, he could always find his way home in fog.

This is probably myth.

Probably.

But myths choose their gifts carefully. The power to return through obscurity is exactly the sort of blessing a hidden subterranean guide-folk might acquire in surface imagination.

If deep gnomes exist, I doubt they mark children with dots.

But I would examine the palm anyway.

## On Master Stonyeye’s Nose Outrage

Master Stonyeye nearly refuses to record an Orenic tunnel crew’s claim that deep gnomes have no noses, declaring it false, insulting, and physiologically offensive.

Here I must reluctantly support him.

No people should be defined by the least observant survivor of an underground work crew.

The “no noses” claim may arise from poor light, cloth masks, mineral dust, facial coverings, naturally flatter features, or the simple fact that terrified humans are bad at faces when the faces are smaller than expected and leaving.

It may also reflect symbolic misunderstanding.

In many folk traditions, the nose signifies curiosity, appetite, pride, and kinship. To say a hidden people has “no nose” may mean they do not pry, do not eat with outsiders, do not breathe surface air, or do not share ordinary gnomish social signs.

Or it may mean someone saw a shadow and became stupid.

Both remain possible.

## The Deep Gnome as Mirror

The greatest scholarly danger is not that we will falsely confirm deep gnomes.

It is that each people will invent the deep gnome it needs.

Dwarves may imagine them as failed miners, trespassers, or old neighbors best left unnamed.

Rock gnomes may imagine them as lost cousins, distorted by darkness into silence and excessive seriousness.

Forest gnomes may imagine them as root-kin, keepers of the underwood where trees dream downward.

Elves may imagine them as witnesses beneath history, preserving names we forgot to ask for.

Humans will, inevitably, ask whether they trade.

All of these images are useful.

All of them are suspect.

The unknown people becomes a bowl. Everyone pours themselves into it.

A responsible scholar must notice the bowl.

## Flexing With Myth, Since Master Stonyeye Has Forced My Hand

Permit me now to be indulgent.

The _Deeping Song of Aerlith Voss_, a nearly impossible text preserved in three inconsistent versions, describes a descent beneath “the mountain that had not yet agreed to be a mountain.” The poem is useless as geography. It is valuable as mythic structure.

The most relevant stanza reads:

> “There were no stars beneath the stone,  
> yet small lamps moved where no hand showed.  
> There were no mouths beneath the stone,  
> yet counsel passed from load to load.  
> There were no names beneath the stone,  
> yet every turning knew its own.”

A literal reader will find this maddening.

Good.

Literal readers deserve exercise.

The stanza suggests light without visible bearers, communication without speech, and place-knowledge without inscription. These align, broadly, with deep gnome rumor clusters.

Does this prove deep gnomes?

No.

It proves that elven myth preserved the idea of an intelligent, silent, subterranean presence associated with guidance, movement, and nameless mapped space.

That is not nothing.

The difference between “nothing” and “not enough” is the difference between ignorance and discipline.

Master Stonyeye should appreciate discipline.

He keeps threatening people with it.

## A Proposed Typology of Deep Gnome Rumor

For future scholars, I recommend separating deep gnome accounts into four categories.

### Category One: Contact Rumors

These involve direct sighting or exchange.

Examples: small pale figures, stolen tools returned altered, food left untouched but metal braided, tapping replies, children guided out of caves.

These are the most dramatic and least reliable.

### Category Two: Stone-Lore Rumors

These involve knowledge supposedly held by deep gnomes.

Examples: old mountain names, hidden water routes, fault lines, safe passages, sealed doors, places dwarves avoid.

These may preserve real subterranean knowledge even if the people attached to it are misidentified.

### Category Three: Sensory Rumors

These involve unusual perception.

Examples: seeing in darkness, hearing through stone, sensing lies, navigating without light, speaking by tap or vibration.

These are biologically and magically plausible enough to be dangerous to dismiss.

### Category Four: Moral Rumors

These describe what deep gnomes “are like.”

Examples: silent, joyless, suspicious, fair, cruel, ancient, humorless, patient, greedy, saintly, pale, soulless, wise.

These should be treated as mirrors of the teller until proven otherwise.

Especially “humorless.”

Nearly every people accused of humorlessness is laughing somewhere private.

Usually at the accuser.

## Correcting Master Stonyeye’s Guild Recommendations

Master Stonyeye advises the Guild not to send armed expeditions into dwarven depths looking for deep gnomes.

Correct.

He advises collecting mining accounts.

Correct.

He advises comparing stone-marks.

Correct.

He advises investigating recurring legends.

Correct.

He advises not publishing speculation as fact.

Correct, though one senses this line pained him less than it pains most scholars.

I would add the following:

- Interview dwarven elders only after demonstrating knowledge of what not to ask.
    
- Treat refusal as data, not obstacle.
    
- Record sound-patterns exactly; do not “regularize” tapping into convenient meter.
    
- Preserve children’s cave-rhymes. They often outlive official shame.
    
- Compare names of forbidden tunnels across languages.
    
- Track offerings left at sealed stone, especially where no temple claims the custom.
    
- Consult forest gnome root-lore; they may preserve downward myths dismissed by rock gnomes as rustic nonsense.
    
- Consult rock gnome engineering records; they may contain practical traces misfiled as abandoned survey data.
    
- Do not bring lanterns into spaces where local guides tell you not to bring lanterns.
    
- Do not assume silence means welcome.
    
- Do not assume silence means absence.
    
- Do not assume silence means ignorance.
    
- Above all, do not knock twice where the old warning says knock once.
    

That last instruction may be superstition.

It may also be courtesy.

The wise survive by respecting both until distinction becomes necessary.

## Final Assessment

Master Norwin Stonyeye’s treatment of deep gnomes is cautious, useful, and insufficiently enchanted by uncertainty.

This is forgivable. He is a rock gnome, a wizard, and a man whose soul appears to have been assembled from calibration tools and professional grievance. His instincts demand evidence. Good. Without such people, archives become gardens of rumor, and everything grows vines until no one can find the door.

But evidence has stages.

First comes pattern.

Then recurrence.

Then comparison.

Then contradiction.

Then the slow separation of what was seen, what was feared, what was wished, what was hidden, and what remains after everyone has stopped performing certainty.

Deep gnomes remain unconfirmed.

Let that stand.

But around the absence gather old sounds: tapping below sealed stairs, mountain names without known parentage, pale listeners in starless passages, gifts altered overnight, dwarven refusals shaped like warnings, and myths in which the stone remembers small hands.

This is not proof.

It is invitation.

Master Stonyeye would prefer an interview, a map, and a properly witnessed declaration of kinship.

So would I.

Until then, we have fragments.

And fragments, if handled with patience, are how lost peoples first return to history.

Or how wise peoples remain hidden from fools.

The distinction is not yet ours to make.
