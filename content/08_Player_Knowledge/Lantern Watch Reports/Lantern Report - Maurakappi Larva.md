**Report: On the Maurakappi Larva**  
_Prepared by Silk, Lantern Watch Bard’s Gate Cell_

---

**1. Description**

- Specimen resembles a pale, segmented grub the length of a man’s finger.
- When placed against warm flesh, it attempts to burrow—slowly, but persistently.
- Emits a faint resinous odor; pheromone traces detectable even outside a host.

_Comment: I’ve had rats less eager to move in. Don’t touch it bare-handed unless you want a new tenant._

---

**2. Function (from elven records)**

- Implantation fogs the host’s mind—dulling initiative, sharpening obedience.
- Host exudes a calming pheromone field: tempers cool, arguments fizzle, blades lower.
- Primary use: acts as a **translator** for the ant-folk. Pheromone “speech” becomes words, words become pheromones.
- Originally coercive: slaves and abductees were forced into the role.
- Later legitimized: the Emerald Throne declared Maurakappi lands sacrosanct in exchange for silk, armor, and tools. Larval hosts became diplomats of a sort.

_Comment: Imagine letting bugs crawl your brain just to make peace. Elves called it wisdom. I call it desperate._

---

**3. Political Sensitivity**

- The “Pact of the Queens” forbids trespass into their colonies. Breaking it insults the queen herself.
- Hosts were recognized at court as political intermediaries.
- Old suspicion lingers: even volunteer hosts were _too_ calm, too pliant. Records note they seldom disobeyed orders, even when foolish.

_Comment: The elves wrapped it in silk and ceremony, but everyone could smell the leash._

---

**4. Modern Context (our findings)**

- The Ashroot Nest has been subverted. Kursk has revived **forced implantation**.
- Reports suggest larvae can now act as **surveillance nodes**—relaying impressions back to the nest.
- This turns any host into a walking ear for Kursk.
- Child implantations (ex: Blue Sylvanranth) leave lifelong temperament shifts.

_Comment: That’s not diplomacy anymore. That’s blackmail in your bloodstream._

---

**5. Risks & Recommendations**

- Do not allow implantation. The fog is subtle but pervasive; you’ll think it’s still you. It won’t be.
- Destroying a larva may draw attention—pheromone trails might be “missed” by the queen’s court.
- If one is implanted, removal is risky. Elves whisper of surgical extractions, but survival was… not guaranteed.
- Keep this specimen alive until we decide whether it’s leverage or evidence.

_Comment: Useful tool, dangerous toy. Handle like you would a vial of plague—and expect someone already knows we have it._

---

**6. Speculative Note**

- If Kursk runs a network of hosts, he effectively has a shadow telegraph—faster than riders, harder to intercept.
- Question: are these larvae one-to-one with a queen, or can Kursk “spoof” a queen’s signal? If the latter, he may be impersonating the will of the colony itself.

_Comment: If he’s forged their queens’ voices, then the ants themselves might not know they’ve been hijacked. Which means this isn’t just our problem—it’s theirs too._

---

**End Report**  
_Signed, Silk_
