# Mage-Delivered Field Report

## To the Lord Marshal of Gorundia

### Concerning the Night Incident at North Granary House, Blackvale

**Delivered by arcane courier under seal from Blackvale County Seat**  
**Filed by:** Maester Gethin Marr, witness-mage attached to the Countess’ provisional household  
**Date:** Third night following the seating of Dame Dana Sylvanranth as Countess of Blackvale  
**Location:** North Granary House, village of Eddenford, Blackvale

My Lord Marshal,

I write under the Countess’ authority and by my own witness, as the matter has already begun to outrun the facts.

At approximately the second bell after midnight, a detachment of the Dragonguard under the adopted command-name **Rook** entered the village of Eddenford to execute a sealed county warrant. The warrant concerned the seizure of duplicate grain ledgers believed to have been retained unlawfully by **Marden Pell**, formerly a Bordella granary factor and tithe clerk.

The ledgers had been requested twice in daylight by the Countess’ officers. Pell refused both requests, claiming the books to be “Blackvale property, not Crown property.” The Countess then ordered their seizure before the records could be destroyed or removed. The decision to proceed at night was made on tactical grounds: fewer bodies in the street, less opportunity for organized resistance, and reduced chance of the ledgers being moved.

This reasoning was sound in a narrow military sense.

It was ruinous in every other sense.

The Dragonguard approached masked and helmed, as is their standing custom. They carried no raised banners beyond the Countess’ seal and the writ itself. No local reeve accompanied them. No priest witnessed the entry. No village elder had been informed.

The door was opened by force after Pell refused entry.

At this point, the chapel bell was rung. By whom, I cannot yet confirm. Within minutes, between thirty and fifty villagers had gathered in the lane outside North Granary House. The crowd shouted demands that the soldiers “raise the helms” and “show Blackvale a face.” Several present repeated language almost certainly drawn from Father Oswin Marth’s recent sermon at Saint Aldren’s Field.

The Dragonguard did not answer the crowd except to order them back.

The crowd did not comply.

A clay lamp was thrown. It struck the shoulder plate of a Dragonguard called **Tallow**, broke, and spilled oil. No fire took. Stones followed. One struck **Brindle** at the helm. A second struck Pell’s daughter, who had come into the doorway screaming that her father was being murdered.

The detachment formed a shield line.

During this movement, **Nial Harrow**, son of Caddoc Harrow the miller, age approximately fifteen, pushed or fell into the shield line. Witnesses disagree. Dragonguard **Morrow** struck him with the rim of a shield. The blow drove the boy backward against the mounting stone beside Pell’s door. He suffered a fractured skull and died shortly before dawn.

No blade was drawn against the crowd.

No spell was cast.

No villager was deliberately targeted after Harrow fell.

The ledgers were recovered. Their contents appear significant. Initial review shows irregular grain diversions, false storage tallies, and names written in cipher. I have identified at least three markings consistent with Dominion ledger-cant previously associated with false creditor insertions. I am not yet prepared to state the ledgers prove Dominion funding, but they strongly suggest hostile manipulation of Blackvale supply accounts.

This will not matter to the street.

By sunrise, the village had already named the event **the Mask-Raid**.

By the third bell, the phrase **“No Masks in Blackvale”** had been painted on the east wall of the mill.

By noon, the story circulating in nearby villages was that the Countess’ faceless soldiers murdered a miller’s son to steal village bread.

The following points require your attention:

1. The Dragonguard acted under lawful warrant.
    
2. Their tactical discipline likely prevented a larger slaughter.
    
3. Their lack of local witness, visible accountability, and public name-giving has transformed a lawful seizure into the appearance of occupation.
    
4. The Countess understands the security problem clearly. She does not yet appear to accept the full political danger of the symbol her guard now represents.
    
5. Father Oswin did not preach rebellion. His sermon has nevertheless furnished rebels, malcontents, and frightened villagers with a holy phrase they can carry in their mouths without shame.
    
6. Dominion agents, if present, need only repeat what already happened.
    

My assessment is that Blackvale is not yet in rebellion.

It is, however, beginning to decide what rebellion would call itself.

The dead boy will be the first banner if this is not answered quickly and publicly.

I recommend the following:

- A lawful inquest under Crown, county, and Triad witness.
    
- Public reading of the recovered warrant.
    
- Public accounting of the seized ledgers, with necessary cipher portions withheld only under seal.
    
- Temporary requirement that Dragonguard performing civil duties within market, chapel, or granary bounds raise their visors unless in immediate danger.
    
- Assignment of a local Blackvale officer to accompany any future Dragonguard civil enforcement.
    
- Immediate compensation and burial honor for the Harrow family, without admission of unlawful killing until the inquest rules.
    

The Countess can still hold Blackvale.

But not by being merely right.

Respectfully submitted under seal,  
**Maester Gethin Marr**  
Witness-Mage of the Provisional Blackvale Household
