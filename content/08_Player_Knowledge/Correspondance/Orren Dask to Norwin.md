## **Letter from Orren Dask to Norwin Stoneyeye**

_Carefully penned, margins clean, a few corrections scraped and rewritten rather than blotted._

---

**Master Stoneyeye,**

First, my thanks—both for your advice in taking this position, and for the letter you were kind enough to provide. I suspect the latter carried more weight than I was told, though no one here has been so impolite as to confirm it directly.

Whatever doors it opened, I have done my best not to walk through them looking like an idiot.

So far, I believe I have succeeded… marginally.

---

Blackvale is… ordered.

That is the word I keep returning to. Not prosperous, not calm—_ordered_. The granaries, the gates, even the flow of people through the streets all seem to fall into patterns that are not entirely organic. There is an underlying structure to it, and I have been permitted to observe a portion of that structure in operation.

My role, such as it is, is not especially glamorous.

I sit, for the better part of the day, and **watch**.

Not directly, of course—through relayed impressions, reports, and the occasional anchored sensor. It is, in essence, the wizard’s equivalent of minding a bank of lenses and mirrors. When something deviates, I note it. When patterns emerge, I compare them. When something does not fit, I pass it upward.

You will be pleased (or perhaps unsurprised) to hear that I have found a certain… niche in this. The work favors patience and pattern recognition over raw power, and for once I am not the least impressive wizard in the room simply because my reserves are not prodigious.

That said, I am also keenly aware of where I am _not_ being invited.

The summoning and binding work is handled by more senior practitioners. I have seen enough to recognize the quality, but not enough to speak to its full scope, and I will not pretend otherwise.

---

I did, however, witness one of the system’s more… active components.

An **invisible stalker** was tasked to follow up on an anomaly flagged near the east silos. I did not see it in the conventional sense—naturally—but I observed the _effects_ of its passage and the responses it generated within the network.

It is… remarkably efficient.

No hesitation. No interpretation. It moves, it observes, it reports. There is a purity to the function that I find almost admirable. If the rest of the system is the web, the stalker is the hand that reaches down when something touches the thread.

I confess, somewhat sheepishly, that I envy the clarity of that role.

My own duties amount to deciding _which threads matter_.

---

This is where I begin to feel less comfortable.

The system does not merely record events. It correlates them. That is expected. What is less expected is the **speed** at which it arrives at conclusions, and the **confidence** with which those conclusions are treated.

There are moments—small, but recurring—where I am presented with a sequence of observations and the accompanying assessment, and I find myself thinking:

> “Yes… but how did we get there?”

The answer is always: “The system indicates.”

Which is not, strictly speaking, an answer.

---

I hesitate to commit this next point to writing, but you asked for candor.

At certain anchor points—particularly those with elevated vantage or long-term placement—I have had the distinct impression that the observation is… _shared_.

Not interfered with. Not altered. Simply… accompanied.

It is as though the system is being **looked through**, rather than merely operated.

I lack the summoning discipline to test that hypothesis in any meaningful way. Had I pursued that branch more rigorously, I might be able to confirm whether what I am perceiving is residual, parasitic, or entirely imagined.

As it stands, I am left with an unevidenced discomfort, which is the least satisfying category of conclusion.

---

Of the personnel:

**Arch-Provost Skell** is exactly what one would design if one wished to embody the concept of administrative authority. He is efficient, direct, and possessed of a physical presence that discourages frivolity. He has been fair in all of my interactions with him.

More importantly, he trusts the system.

Completely.

I am not yet certain whether that is a strength or a vulnerability.

---

**Ser Bronnel** is… direct.

He has been polite to me. I have no complaints.

I would prefer to continue having no complaints.

---

If you intend to send anyone here—or if your earlier instincts prove correct and I am simply ahead of the lesson—you may wish to keep the following in mind:

- The system is not passive. It is **evaluative**.    
- Patterns matter more than individual acts.    
- Repetition is noticed more quickly than magnitude.    

And perhaps most importantly:

> If you do something unusual once, it is an event.  
> If you do it twice, it is a pattern.  
> If you do it three times, it is _attention_.

---

I will continue my work here and report anything further of note, though I suspect the most important elements are those I am not yet equipped to fully understand.

As ever, I remain grateful for your guidance—particularly when it proves, in retrospect, that you were correct.

**Orren Dask**

---

_Postscript:_  
If you have any refinements on low-energy summoning theory—particularly regarding stability and observation overlap—I would be eager to review them. Purely academic interest, I assure you.
