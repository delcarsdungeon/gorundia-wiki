Lady Jinwing,

I received your note.

I read it three times.

Then I put it down, because I thought perhaps the first three readings had made me worse at understanding it, which is a thing that can happen when one is trying very hard to understand something properly and therefore becomes a danger to furniture, ink, nearby birds, and one’s own dignity.

Then I read it again.

I think I understand.

No.

That is too bold.

I think I am beginning to understand.

You are right.

I should not say that you are what a druid is supposed to be.

I did not mean it as a burden. I meant it as praise, but praise can still be a burden if it is shaped wrongly. I see that now. Or I see it more than I did yesterday, which is not the same thing, but is at least facing the correct direction.

You are not “more druid” because you are old in the way old forests are old, or because you carry lost things, or because you speak with patience when I am making a complete compost heap of myself.

You are Jinwing.

That is already enough.

More than enough.

Not more than enough in a hierarchy way. I am trying not to do the hierarchy thing. I know druids are not supposed to be ladders. Trees are not ladders unless one is a squirrel or a child being irresponsible. They are themselves. Roots and branches and shade and fruit and rot and seed. No part is “more tree” than another, even if some parts are higher and more likely to be struck by lightning.

That may not be the best metaphor.

But I think you know what I mean.

A druid is one who does their best to foster balance, and who cares for nature in such a way that balance is preserved while providing for the needs of all.

I wrote that down.

Not because I doubt it.

Because I do not wish to lose it.

It is a cleaner definition than any of the ones I was given as a boy, and kinder than most. Most definitions I heard were about rites, lineage, fluency in old songs, moon-counts, proper staff-wood, seasonal taboos, and whether one pronounces the third line of the spring blessing with the old vowel or the newer city vowel, which the elves do notice, by the way. They are polite, but they notice. Thalanir hums when I get it wrong. It is a very elegant hum. Devastating.

But your definition has room in it.

That frightened me a little.

Not because it is wrong.

Because if it is right, then I cannot hide behind being only a poor copy of something older.

I have been thinking of myself as a man keeping scraps. Human scraps of elven rites. Local scraps of forest customs. Little garden prayers, city water, halfling planting songs, Pelorite dawn blessings, old Verdant words I am not sure I deserve to say aloud.

I thought that made me less.

You are saying it does not.

You are saying I am judged by the care, not the costume.

That is a dangerous mercy.

I mean that in a good way.

Mostly.

I am not sure I know what to do with it.

The elves here attend the blessings. They sing the lines we cannot sing properly. They stand with us, even when we are clearly doing the human version of something older and finer. I used to think they were being kind because we were harmless.

Perhaps they are being kind because the Tree is alive.

Perhaps they are being kind because Auraleth is happy.

Perhaps that is enough.

That sentence feels too simple. I keep wanting to add clauses to it, like moss over a stone, but I will resist.

Mostly.

Also, I should say this plainly, because if I do not say it plainly, I will say it badly later in person, probably while holding something breakable:

You are very beautiful.

I know.

I know that is not the point.

I also know that after the Countess Zara incident, which I am trying not to call an incident even though it was one, I should be more careful about saying such things.

But with her, the word escaped because I was startled.

With you, I am less startled and more… steadied.

That may be worse.

No, not worse.

Different.

Countess Zara is beautiful like a blade laid on velvet. One understands immediately that it is fine work, and also that touching it carelessly would be a mistake one might only make once.

You are beautiful like a clear morning after a storm has spent itself. Not soft. Not harmless. But whole. Graceful in a way that makes the rest of us remember we have elbows, shoes, and unresolved spiritual problems.

That was too much.

I apologize.

I am leaving it in because scratching it out would make the ink look guilty.

What I mean is this: you have grace.

Not the court kind. Not the kind that knows where to place a hand or when to smile so everyone else feels properly arranged.

The other kind.

The kind that makes space for a frightened man to become less frightened without making him feel small for having been afraid.

You have been patient with me.

I do not know what to do with that either, except try to become worth the patience.

Not worthy. That sounds like a shrine plaque. I am currently opposed to plaques.

Worth the patience in the practical sense.

Less foolish.

More careful.

Still honest, but perhaps not honest at the volume of a dropped bucket.

I will try to stop saying that you are what a druid is supposed to be.

I will try to say instead that you are a druid who is wholly herself.

And that I am a druid who is trying.

That feels smaller.

But also truer.

The Great Tree does not seem troubled by my being human.

Auraleth does not seem troubled by our imperfect songs.

The elves keep singing with us.

The roots keep drinking.

The leaves keep returning.

Maybe I have been waiting for someone ancient to declare that I am permitted to be what I have already been doing.

That is embarrassing.

But many true things are embarrassing when they first come into the light.

Please understand, I will still be nervous when I see you.

This is not because you are cruel.

You are not.

It is because you understand too much, and you understand it gently, which is somehow more difficult to stand before than anger.

Anger lets a man brace.

Grace makes him notice what he is carrying.

I will try not to ramble when next we speak.

That is probably false.

I will try to ramble less harmfully.

That may be possible.

Thank you for your correction.

Thank you for your patience.

Thank you for reminding me that a druid is not proven by being ancient, powerful, impressive, or properly shaped according to someone else’s story.

A druid cares for the balance.

A druid serves the living world.

A druid provides where need and balance can meet.

A druid tries.

I can do that.

I think.

No.

I can do that.

Respectfully, nervously, and with the western channel now flowing somewhat better than it did yesterday,

Tovan Reedmere  
Junior Grovewarden of the Great Tree
