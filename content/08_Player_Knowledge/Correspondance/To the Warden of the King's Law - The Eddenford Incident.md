# Legal Incident Report

## To Thalrik Vaultforge, Warden of the King’s Law

### Re: Death of Nial Harrow During Execution of County Warrant at North Granary House

**Filed under provisional Blackvale authority**  
**Delivered by arcane courier and sealed witness-copy**  
**Reporting officer:** Maester Gethin Marr, witness-mage  
**Civil authority concerned:** Dame Dana Sylvanranth, Countess of Blackvale  
**Military personnel concerned:** Dragonguard detachment under adopted command-name Rook

## I. Matter Reported

During the lawful execution of a county warrant at North Granary House in Eddenford, Blackvale, a civilian youth named **Nial Harrow** suffered a fatal head injury after contact with a Dragonguard shield line.

The deceased was approximately fifteen years of age, son of **Caddoc Harrow**, miller of Eddenford.

The incident occurred during a night seizure of suspected withheld grain ledgers from **Marden Pell**, formerly a granary factor under House Bordella.

## II. Authority for Action

The warrant was issued under the seal of **Dame Dana Sylvanranth, Countess of Blackvale**, seated by royal writ following the execution of Ponzio Bordella and the removal of surviving Bordella claimants from immediate county authority.

The warrant authorized:

- entry into North Granary House;    
- seizure of grain ledgers, duplicate ledgers, cipher ledgers, tithe rolls, and associated correspondence;    
- detention of Marden Pell if he obstructed the warrant;    
- use of necessary force to prevent destruction or removal of records.    

The warrant did not authorize punitive violence, seizure of private coin, general search beyond account-storage rooms, or arrest of Pell’s household absent active obstruction.

## III. Personnel Present

**Dragonguard Detachment**

- **Rook**, adopted command-name, officer in field command;    
- **Tallow**, Dragonguard;    
- **Morrow**, Dragonguard, shield contact with deceased;    
- **Brindle**, Dragonguard, struck by thrown stone;    
- **Ash**, Dragonguard;    
- two further Dragonguard names recorded under sealed annex.    

**County Witness**

- Maester Gethin Marr, witness-mage and reporting officer.    

**Civilian Persons of Interest**

- Marden Pell, former Bordella granary factor;    
- Ellyn Pell, daughter of Marden Pell;    
- Caddoc Harrow, miller;    
- Nial Harrow, deceased;    
- Reeve Harl Wendt, absent at time of action, arrived after injury;    
- Father Oswin Marth, not present at incident, but his recent sermon appears relevant to crowd language.    

## IV. Sequence of Events

1. Dragonguard detachment arrived at North Granary House after midnight.   
2. Rook announced the warrant at the door.    
3. Marden Pell refused entry and denied the Countess’ authority to seize “Blackvale books.”    
4. Rook ordered forced entry.    
5. Door was breached. Pell was restrained inside the front hall.    
6. Ellyn Pell shouted into the street that masked soldiers had entered the house.    
7. Chapel bell rang. Source unknown.    
8. Crowd gathered in lane outside North Granary House.    
9. Crowd demanded that the Dragonguard “raise the helms” and “show faces.”    
10. Rook ordered the crowd to stand back.    
11. Clay lamp thrown, striking Tallow.    
12. Stones thrown. Brindle struck on helm. Ellyn Pell struck in shoulder or cheek by thrown stone; injury minor.    
13. Rook ordered shield formation at the doorway and lane approach.    
14. Nial Harrow entered or was forced into close contact with the shield line.  
15. Morrow struck outward with shield rim.    
16. Harrow fell backward, striking head on stone.    
17. I ordered immediate cessation of forward movement and attempted stabilization.    
18. Harrow remained alive but insensible.    
19. County healer arrived too late to reverse injury.    
20. Harrow died before dawn.    

## V. Magical Findings

No combat spell was cast by the Dragonguard.

No charm, fear, illusion, or compulsion magic was detected on the crowd at the time of my examination.

Residual traces on the chapel bell rope are inconclusive. There may have been ordinary handling by multiple persons. I found no reliable arcane residue.

The seized ledgers do contain cipher markings. At least three notations are consistent with Dominion-style creditor insertion marks. This finding is preliminary and should be reviewed by a Crown cipher clerk or trusted Pholtan auditor.

## VI. Evidence Secured

The following items are secured under county seal:

- two primary grain ledgers;    
- one duplicate tithe roll;    
- one private cipher book hidden behind false backing in Pell’s account chest;    
- three loose correspondence slips with initials only;    
- wax scrap bearing partial seal, likely not Bordella;    
- broken clay lamp from street;    
- blood-marked stone from site of Harrow’s injury;    
- Morrow’s shield, dented and blood-marked at lower rim;    
- Brindle’s helm, stone-scored.    

## VII. Legal Issues

The warrant appears valid.

The seizure appears lawful.

The timing was not unlawful.

The absence of a local reeve or priestly witness was not unlawful, but it was reckless under present county conditions.

The killing of Nial Harrow may fall under one of three readings:

1. **Lawful force during riotous obstruction**, if Harrow is judged part of the attacking crowd and the shield strike proportionate.    
2. **Manslaughter by excessive force**, if Morrow struck without immediate necessity.    
3. **Accidental death during lawful crowd control**, if Harrow was pushed or stumbled into the shield line and Morrow’s action was reflexive.    

Current witness statements conflict.

Morrow has not fled, denied action, or refused inquiry. He states: “I struck to make space. I did not see his age.”

This is plausible.

It is also insufficient.

## VIII. Political Risk

The street has already decided this was murder.

The law has not.

The space between those two facts is where Blackvale may burn.

The phrase **“No Masks in Blackvale”** is spreading. It will become a shield for lawful grievance, unlawful resistance, and Dominion exploitation alike unless addressed.

The Dragonguard’s anonymity is now a legal liability. A man cannot easily bring complaint against “Morrow” if no civil registry can identify which Morrow struck the blow, where he is quartered, who commands him, and under what articles he may be disciplined.

If the Dragonguard are to enforce civil warrants, their adopted names must be made sufficient for legal process.

A hidden birth name is not required.

A reachable legal identity is.

## IX. Recommendations for the Warden of the King’s Law

I recommend:

1. Immediate appointment of a King’s Law examiner to review the death.    
2. Temporary suspension of Morrow from civil warrant duty pending ruling.    
3. Public confirmation that Morrow remains under lawful inquiry, not hidden by the Countess.    
4. Creation of a Dragonguard civil registry for Blackvale operations, recording adopted name, command-name, billet, and accountable officer.    
5. Requirement that county warrants executed after sunset include either:    
    - local reeve witness,        
    - priestly witness,        
    - Crown legal witness,        
    - or written explanation for urgent exception.        
6. Formal review of the seized ledgers by trusted Crown auditors.    
7. Protection for Marden Pell until he can be questioned. If the ledgers prove Dominion contact, he will be valuable. If they do not, his mistreatment will become another grievance.    
8. Compensation to the Harrow household, framed as royal duty toward the innocent dead, not purchase of silence.    

## X. Closing Assessment

The Countess’ authority remains lawful.

The Dragonguard’s action remains defensible.

The death remains unresolved.

Blackvale will not distinguish those three facts unless the King’s Law does so first.
