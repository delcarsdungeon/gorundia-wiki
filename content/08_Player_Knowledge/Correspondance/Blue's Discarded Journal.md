## Blue’s Journal

> _Recovered writings attributed to Blue Sylvanranth. Entries appear fragmented, out of order, and written over time._

>[!quote] “I called her daughter. Maybe that was my sin. She laughed like my own, fought like my own—and in the end, it may as well have been her sword that took my blood. How do I mourn one child and forgive the other when they share the same grave in my heart?”
> — Sorek Redmarch

# Entry

Strength is law. Weakness is decay.  The strong act.  The weak ask why.  Order must be earned in blood.

They say obedience is freedom.  Jeddal Crawn said I had no value until someone paid for me. So _he_ did.  Then they came for me. Said I was worth saving. Said I wasn’t what he bought.  I still smell his wine sometimes. I think the larva liked him.

They pulled it out of me.  I thought I’d die. I didn’t.  I just started hearing myself again—or maybe it’s them, without the whispering.  Kursk still visits in the dreams.  He asks questions I already answered.  Sometimes I wake up and can’t remember which answers were mine.

The strong do what must be done.  The weak hide behind virtue.

# Entry

Bertram came to the tavern. I think I made him laugh.

Sir Beringer was there that night too. He always looks like someone took the world away from him and he’s still trying to give it back. Wonderful teases him, but she loves him. I can see it in the way she stands near him when he’s brooding, pretending not to watch his face. He doesn’t believe he deserves her. Maybe he doesn’t, but she does.

Orin comes by sometimes, the big beast himself. Everyone else goes quiet when he enters a room, like the air forgets how to move. But he’s kind. He talks to everybody like they matter. He scares me and makes me feel safe at the same time.

Guaco’s the halfling who runs half the tavern without owning it. He’s got that warm-house smell about him—bread and beer and laughter. Always asking if you’ve eaten. He talks about his wife and children the way priests talk about miracles. I like him. It’s easy to believe the world’s still good when he’s in it.

Jinwing confuses me most of all. She’s the one who keeps everyone together, like a mother who knows too much. She scares me because she sees through me. Every time I think I’ve hidden something, she looks right at it and says nothing. That’s worse than being caught. She’s kind, but I don’t understand her kindness. It feels like sunlight on a wound.

# Entry

Bertram stopped by again today. I don’t think he even noticed I’d brushed the ash out of my hair, but he smiled when I poured his drink. He has the sort of smile that makes you forget what you were angry about a moment before. I used to think I couldn’t feel stillness; he makes it easy.

I caught myself watching the light on his armor. It isn’t polished well, not like a noble’s, but it looks real. Like him. He talks to me as if I matter. Not “girl,” not “barmaid.” Blue.

He came back tonight. Said something stupid and funny and I laughed too hard. My ribs hurt afterward but it felt good. The noise in my head goes quiet when he’s around. Kursk’s voice, the fire, all of it—gone for a while. I’d forgotten what quiet could be.

Sometimes he reaches out to steady me when the room’s crowded. Just his hand on my arm. Nothing improper. It’s enough to burn through every thought I have. I tell myself it’s fine, that I’m not one of those girls who swoons. But when he leaves I can still feel where his fingers were.

I dreamed about him. Not the usual dreams with the commands and the smoke—just him, smiling. It wasn’t even a real memory, but I woke up calm. That’s never happened before.

He makes everything else seem far away. The anger. The lessons. The drills. I can’t tell if that’s love or some kind of sickness, but I want it anyway. I want him to touch my hand again. I want to hear him laugh. I want—

(The ink trails off and blots.)

It happened. It really happened. It was... what it what I think it's supposed to be. It was just a night. One night. But it’s the only one that feels real when I try to remember who I am.

She cries alot now. I dont understand.  
He knows what it means to hurt, but he did that?

He looked so sad the night she told me. She said he wouldnt even look at her. Just stared at the floor like she was already gone.

He said its always been that way, that we’re the practice rounds before the real thing.

He says Beringer will never marry her, that the world wont let him.  
That shes lucky if he even remembers her name when hes old.

I asked Wonderful if she thinks it’s true.  
She said no, but she looked away when she said it.  
I think she knows.

I dont like this world very much. It keeps breaking the people who try to love in it.

He said it was the pheremones.  
That it wasnt me, it was just what I am.  
Like I cast some kind of spell without meaning to.  
He couldnt even look at me when he said it, just kept his hands folded like he was praying.

I said I knew what I was doing.  
He said no, I didnt.  
Then he said he was sorry, that he loved Cornelia, that he had to be good.  
Good for who?  
He left before I could ask.

I sat there after, trying to feel ashamed, but mostly I just felt hollow.  
Like somebody blew out a candle inside me and forgot to open a window.

Wonderful still cries about Beringer. I used to think she was too proud to hurt like that, but now I get it.  
She keeps saying, “He meant well.”  
So did Bertram. That doesnt make the ache stop.

Kursk says this is what happens when flat teeth touch our kind, that they always find a reason to call it a mistake.  
He says they tell themselves it was a potion or a curse or a fever, anything but want.  
He says it’s not hate, it’s just nature.  
I dont want to believe him.  
But I can’t find any proof he’s wrong.

Cornelia’s name tastes like iron now. She was kind to me, and I want to hate her, but I can’t.  
She’s everything I’ll never be — quiet and proper and forgiven.  
He chose her because she’s safe.  
I’ll never be safe.

I keep telling myself it was just the pheremones, just heat and chance and accident.  
But then I remember his hand on my back, how careful he was not to hurt me, how he said my name like it meant something.  
And I know he’s lying.  
Maybe we both are.

# Entry

They called me daughter. Sylvanranth. Said I had noble blood. Said I was supposed to be someone. Sorek taught me how to walk like a lady, to ride and fight like a knight, and how to laugh through tough times - we half-bloods are good at that. He’s patient. He looks at me like I matter. Like a Father. I want to make him proud. I think he knows how scared I am. I love and hate it in the capital - parties and dances, but I'm a monster, and the daughter of a monster. The men want me, but not to love. 

Orin took me in. made me his squire. Said if I’m going to serve, I’ll serve standing up. Kendal’s my shadow now. She’s smarter than me. Braver too. She believes in things. 

Real things. I wear the ring and the dreams stop. It’s quiet for the first time in years. I forget what quiet feels like. Sometimes I worry if I take it off, I’ll hear them again. Sometimes I almost want to. Dana looks at me like she wants to throw me off a balcony, but she still checks my sword knots. Maybe that’s her way of caring.

# Entry

I think about when I met Cornelia at the Mono staree a lot.  

She was at the nunnery as a 'guest', not a saint. They sent her there because of Bertram. They called it “atonement.” I call it exile. She was supposed to marry Vogel. Then one night with 'the wrong man', and suddenly she’s locked behind walls, praying for forgiveness she doesn’t believe in. The sisters whispered about her like she was contagious. She smiled anyway. 

I didn’t realyze who she was at first. Just another sad woman with kind eyes. She treated me like an equal, even though I was a half-blood that talked like a dockhand. When I called her _Lady,_ she winced. Said, “Not anymore. Just Cornelia.” 

We talked at night when the others slept. She asked if I’d ever been in love. I said once. She asked his name. I said Bertram. And then it was quiet for a long time. We didn’t need to say the rest — it hung there between us, heavy as the bells. 

She didn’t hate me. She should have, but she didn’t. She just said, “He never meant to hurt anyone. Least of all you.” I believed her. I still do. 

She told me she hated it there — the prayers, the silence, the pretending. She said they’d locked her up to make her pure again, but all she felt was smaller. I told her purity’s just another word for cage. She laughed. Real laughter, not the kind people use to keep from crying. 

When I left, she hugged me. She said, “Take care of him if you ever see him again.” I said, “You too.” And we both pretended that was a joke. 

Later, when she was back, she and Bertram found their way to each other again. He looked happier than I’d ever seen him. And she—she glowed, like someone who finally remembered who she was. 

I’m happy for them. I am. Truly. 

But sometimes, when the world goes quiet, I can still hear her voice saying, “Take care of him.” And I wish she hadn’t trusted me to.

# Entry

Theron’s kind. Too kind. The voice says he’s lying, but I don’t think he is. When he talks, I feel like I have a choice again. The others trust him. I want to. But the dreams come when I look at him too long. Kursk’s voice mixed with my own: “He burned Elderbrook. He burned your mother.” I can’t tell which part is true. Dana said not to listen to ghosts. She’s one to talk. Theron moved into the castle. Everyone acts like it’s normal, but it isn’t. The air feels heavier with him here. He walks like the walls belong to him, and everyone pretends they don’t notice. He’s polite to me. Too polite. The kind of polite you use with strangers or children. I think he’s trying. I think he’s failing.

# Entry

Braga is mean and nice all at once. She yells like thunder and then feeds you soup like your ma. She calls me “kid” and “idiot” in the same breath. She scares most of the men, which I think she likes. Sometimes I catch her staring at Orin and she gets this look, soft but worried, like she’s afraid the world will take him away. Then she spots me watching and tells me to go scrub something. 

Orin’s wife, Baroness, whatever—she’s something else. I like her even when she’s shouting. She said once, “You’re not here to look pretty. You’re here to live through the day.” I like that rule. 

Kendal is GREAT. All capitals, great. She always smells like soap and ink. She talks a mile a minute but every word matters. She helps me with letters and I help her clean armor, which seems fair. She says if we weren’t squires we’d probably be thieves or poets. I told her I’d be both. She laughed until she cried.  Sometimes I think she’s the only reason I haven’t lost my mind in this castle. 

Sir Beringer and Dame Venom are in love now. Everyone can see it. Even the cooks gossip about it. They move around each other like they’ve done this before in another life. It’s strange and beautiful and a little sad because Wonderful still looks for him in crowds. I wish someone could love her that way. I wish someone could love me that way. Venom scares me a little, but she’s kind underneath all that steel. She looks at Beringer like she’s daring the gods to take him. 

We went to the Lothlindor fields. They said it would be easy—just goblins. It wasn’t. The forest there smells wrong, like blood before it spills. I killed some goblins. It felt awful. I thought it would feel righteous or brave, but it just felt like being cold on the inside. They scream like people. One of them couldn’t have been older than Kendal. He had a knife made out of bone. I keep seeing his eyes. I tried to clean the blood off and it just smeared darker. 

Beringer found me behind the wagon after. I couldn’t talk. He sat there anyway. Didn’t say much, just stayed. Venom came too. She said, “It’s supposed to hurt, child. That’s the part that keeps you from turning into them.” She took me away, then she cut my hair - back to my old self. She sang beautiful sad elf songs, and used scented oils. I felt better. 

Said it was “too heavy for marching.” I think she meant something else. The air felt lighter after. The mirror in my mess tin looked more like me again. 

Kendal said I look like a wolf now. I said good. Wolves don’t cry after fights. She said that’s not true, but she hugged me anyway. 

There was a party tonight—Baron Orin’s big night. The banners were everywhere, black and orange, like fire caught in silk. Braga looked fierce and proud, and when Orin kissed her hand everyone cheered like they were in a play. Kendal made me promise not to knock anything over, but I wanted to dance. 

The music started and my feet just moved. I can’t help it. I love dancing. Always have. Mama used to say I could dance before I could walk. I spun till I forgot where I was, till the air felt light again. 

Then I saw them. The flat-teeth. They always stare when I dance. Like they’re waiting for me to fall, or snarl, or do something wild to prove them right. Some smiled that tight little smile that means how exotic, and some just shook their heads. I tried not to care. I kept moving. But every turn felt heavier after that.

# Entry
Darius was there—Sir Darius, half-elf, half-something else. He moves funny, like he’s always ready to duck. Outsiders know each other. You can smell it. He said I danced like the music owed me money. I told him that’s because it does. 

He laughed, and I liked the sound of it. He’s a mess—hair never stays down, shirt half untucked—but he’s kind. He doesn’t flinch when I grin. Doesn’t stare at my tusks like they’re weapons. He asked if I wanted to step outside where it was cooler. We ended up near the stables, still laughing. He said he couldn’t dance worth a damn, so I showed him. He’s not wrong. But he tried. That counts. 

He kissed me. I didn’t stop him. I liked it. It felt like summer, like something easy for once. It wasn’t Bertram. It didn’t quiet the noise like that, but it didn’t hurt either. It was just a moment where nobody wanted to change me. 

Kendal caught us coming back in. She wiggled her eyebrows and said, “About time someone kissed you for real.” I threw a roll at her. 

Darius is like me—half of one thing, half of another, never enough of either. Maybe that’s why it felt safe. For one night, we didn’t have to be proof of anything. Just two mongrels dancing like nobody was watching. 

Even if everyone was.

# Entry

Theron found out. I don’t know how. Maybe one of the guards saw us, or maybe he always knows everything that makes me happy. He didn’t yell. He just told me to come see him. His voice was calm, the way it gets before storms. 

He asked if I’d “been improper.” I told him it was just dancing. He said dancing doesn’t leave straw in your hair. 

Then he started talking about duty and expectations and how people like us can’t afford to give anyone more reasons to whisper. I told him it was just a kiss. He said that’s how mistakes start. 

He said Darius would only ever want what he couldn’t keep. He said he was protecting me. He sent Darius away. Theron watched my reaction, arms folded like stone. He didn’t look angry. He looked tired. I hate that look more than yelling. 

He told me I should focus on my training, that I have a future now, that I’m not a tavern girl anymore. I wanted to scream that the tavern girl was the only part of me that still knew how to smile. 

Kendal tried to cheer me up. She said Darius will come back when things quiet down. I told her not to say things she doesn’t believe. Venom patted my shoulder and said, “At least he left you the dog.” It was supposed to be funny. I almost laughed. 

Lucky’s still here. He doesn’t care about noble blood or propriety or who I kiss. He just wants food and scratches and a warm place to sleep. Sometimes I look at him and think, you’re lucky, little one. You don’t know how bad people can be. 

And how bad I am.

# Entry

They let me practice again today. Magic, they call it. I mean it is, but mostly it’s buckets. Buckets of water, buckets of patience. I wanted to try fire once, but Braga said, “No more burning things, girl.” So water it is. The safe kind. They say I have talent, but not the right kind. Or maybe I’m just the wrong kind of person. 

When I get it right, the water glows a little. Kendal claps. It’s the only applause I ever get. When I get it wrong, Venom laughs and calls me “pup.” I think she means it kindly, but it still feels like a leash. 

Braga treats me like I’m one of her hunting dogs. “Sit,” “stay,” “fetch.” Sometimes I think she forgets I understand words. She shouts, then smiles, then throws me a bone. That’s not a metaphor; she actually threw me a lamb bone once. Said I’d earned it. Everyone laughed, so I laughed too. 

She isn’t cruel. She just doesn’t see it. None of them do. To her, I’m a good soldier. A useful trick. I heal, I fight, I create water. I don’t belong. 

Venom’s better. She calls me “child” or “pup.” She means it like a compliment, I think. She says it’s better than being called “thing.” Sometimes she ruffles my hair. Sometimes she gives orders like she’s afraid I’ll break. I’d rather she just yell. It’s easier that way. 

Dana confuses me. She's mean to everyone, but watches me like she cares - as much as Orin but totally opposite face. 

Theron’s worse. He’s not mean—he’s measured. Every time I see him, I feel like I’m being weighed. He calls me Blue, but it sounds like a question. Sometimes he looks at me like he’s waiting for me to explode. Sometimes like he’s wondering why I haven’t yet. 

He renamed the dog. Said “Lucky” was childish. Said he needed something proper. Now he calls him Verdict. The dog comes when he says it, wagging like he doesn’t know it means mine. Theron said, “A name shapes what a creature becomes.” I guess that’s why he never says mine with real warmth - he prefers to use the elf word. 

Sorek’s still kind. He brings me sweets from town and tells stories until I laugh. Then Theron walks by and Sorek stops mid-sentence. Just stops. Like a switch flipped. I don’t blame him. Everyone does what Theron wants. Even me. 

Sometimes when I’m practicing I make the water spin in the air until it looks like a mirror. I try to see the person they think I am. All I ever see is a pet doing tricks for her keepers. 

Verdict wags his tail when I finish. He doesn’t know he’s not mine anymore. Neither do I.

# Entry

He said he wanted to “talk about presentation.” I thought he meant armor polish or posture or how to stop tripping over my own boots. He handed me a little card instead. The Dentist. Fancy script, gold leaf. I thought it was a joke. It wasn’t. 

He said it would help. Help me fit in. Help the nobles take me seriously. “A disguise,” he called it. He said it like it was strategy. Like my face was armor that needed reshaping. 

I laughed, because what else do you do when your 'father' tells you to file down your own teeth? Told him I’d seen a play once where the princess wore false teeth to kiss better. He didn’t laugh. He just looked tired. Like I’d missed something important. 

I kept the card all day. Put it in my pouch. Took it out again. Thought maybe he was right. Bertram wouldn’t have flinched if I didn’t have tusks. The courtiers wouldn’t stare when I smile. Maybe Wonderful wouldn’t have cried so much if she looked more like the flat teeth. Maybe it’s easier to be loved when you look like them. 

Kendal told me not to think like that. Said I was fine as I am. I said sure, easy for her to say—she doesn’t make children cry just by grinning. 

That night I dreamed about my mother. She laughed at me, the way she always used to—soft, wicked, proud. She said, “Don’t you dare hide that smile, Bluebird. The world’s ugly enough.” I woke up and threw the card into the hearth. Watched it curl and burn. Theron came in just as it turned to ash. He didn’t stop me. Just watched. Didn’t say a word. Didn’t have to. 

I keep telling myself he meant well. That he just wants to protect me. That’s what they all say when they want to change you: it’s for your own good. 

Maybe he really thinks I need fixing. Maybe he’s right. But I can’t do it. If I file them down, what’s left that’s mine? 

Sometimes I think he sees someone else when he looks at me. Someone wild and beautiful and long gone. I don’t know her name, but I can feel her ghost between us when he talks. 

It hurts worse than Bertram ever did. At least Bertram told me what I was losing. Theron just wants me to smile smaller. 

Sorek says not to let them reshape me. Says if they file down Blue Sylvanranth, all they’ll have left is blood and regret. I didn’t understand what he meant at first. I think I do now.

Got yelled at today. Again. 

Jinwing gave one of her talks. “Discipline, awareness, humility.” That tone that makes you feel like you’re six. Kendal and I got scolded for “talking during watch.” I was trying to keep her awake, that’s all. But no—apparently that’s a crime now. 

Then she said we shouldn’t be wearing armor unless ordered. Well duh, we’re squires. What are we supposed to wear, choir robes? Kendal tried to explain but Jinwing just kept going. She’s not mean, she just forgets we’re people sometimes. She talks like the world is her classroom. 

Thranduil was there too. He never says anything TO ME, just watches. I feel his eyes like a knife at my neck. He looks at me the way people look at snakes before they strike. He wants to kill me. I can see it in him. Maybe he’s waiting for permission. 

Kendal says I’m imagining it. I’m not. You can tell when someone’s measuring where they’ll cut first. 

Maybe this is where they're going to bury me. 

The air in the forest smells like old smoke. Maybe that’s why it started again. Kursk’s scent hit me like lightning. All the lessons, all the training—gone. Just orders and memory and heat. He said I’d forgotten who I was. Said Vogel remembers strength. Said the King is weak. I don’t know if he was lying or if I just needed him to make sense. It felt good to stop fighting the noise.

They never told me the truth. 

They said she left. That she ran back to the forest. That she went to Kursk. That he took her from us. That’s what Father said. That’s what he said—Theron Sylvanranth, the great lord, the man who saves everyone else. 

He looked me in the eye when he said it. I remember how calm he was. “She’s gone back to the wilds,” he told me. “To him. She made her choice.” 

He lied. He burned her. He killed her. He burned my mother and threw her ashes over the wall and told everyone she’d run away. 

And they all nodded. They all smiled. They all pretended not to smell the smoke. Sorek said it was for the best. Dana said not to dig up the past. But they knew. They all knew. 

Kursk’s voice says I saw it happen. He says I remember the flames if I just look long enough. Sometimes I think I can see her hand in the firelight, reaching for me. Sometimes I think it’s mine. 

He said Father did it to protect me. To hide what I was. To keep the bloodline clean. 

If that’s protection, then I’d rather burn too. 

They call him “kind.” He was kind enough to lie to me every day. 

He killed her. And they let me thank him for it, for everything.

Bertram’s face in the firelight again. I can’t tell if it’s guilt or worship. Maybe both. If I ever see him again, I’ll tell him the truth: it meant something. It’s the only thing that ever did. Kendal deserves better friends than me. Better everything. If this is what strength looks like, maybe I was never meant to be strong. 

Vogel says I’ll be made whole. The Raven’s hands will smooth the cracks. Hextor’s prayer: “From conflict, purity.” They say a devil’s voice burns away doubt. Maybe that’s the only way to be clean. 

When you find this, and end me, I hope you don't make Kendal watch. Tell Bertram it was real. Tell Orin I’m sorry I wasn’t a better squire. Strength through obedience. Obedience through silence. I hope the gods aren’t listening. 

I know what I have to do. I'm the only one that can get close. The raven knights are coming. There's a new voice, she promises quiet. I've hidden the ring under my matress - give it to Kendal, and please take care of Lucky - he's innocent.

## After the Fire

The handwriting is uneven — angry scratches softened by smudged ink and half-burned candle wax. Several pages are torn out, others blackened where she’s pressed the quill too hard.

They say I’m free.  
Free to walk the halls, free to think, free to breathe.  
The locks on the doors make that word sound funny.

The captain comes to talk at night — not to me, at me. Tells me I’ve done something great, something righteous. That I’ve burned the rot from the world.

If that’s true, why does it still smell like ash?

They gave me a mirror. I threw it across the room. The reflection didn’t look like me anyway.

I asked him what comes next. He smiled like it was a secret.  
He smiles like everything’s a secret.

Sometimes I wonder if I’m still the experiment.

The Raven Lady came today. Not the real one — her shadow.  
She touched my cheek and said, “You did well, child.”

My mother used to say that too.

I didn’t say anything back. I don’t think she expected me to.  
I think that’s the trick with them — they like obedience to sound like reverence.

I asked to see Sophia or Braga - to try and explain. I'm told she’s safe “for now.”  
He says it like a gift. I wish he’d stop pretending kindness is mercy.

I can’t sleep. Every time I close my eyes I see Father burning again.  
Sometimes he’s screaming. Sometimes he’s quiet. Sometimes he’s smiling.  
I don’t know which is worse.

I thought killing him would make the noise stop. It didn’t. It just made space for new noise — his voice, Vogel’s voice, the Lady’s voice — all talking over each other.

I asked for paper because I thought writing might help.  
Now I wish I hadn’t. Words don’t make sense when you don’t believe them anymore.

The captain showed me the courtyard. There’s blood on the stones — old, brown, hard as varnish. I asked whose it was.  
He said, “Yours, if you keep asking.”

He said it kindly.

I laughed. I don’t think I was supposed to.

I tried to pray.  
I don’t even know which god to start with.  
Pelor? Heironeous? Daern? They wouldn’t listen. Not to me.

Maybe I should try Hextor again. At least he answers.

No. I won’t. Not yet.

I think they’re planning something.  
Servants won’t meet my eyes. There’s incense burning in every corridor, sweet and heavy, the kind that hides rot.

I here that he keeps calling me “necessary.” I hate that word.  
It sounds like a death sentence dressed up in ceremony.

If I’m to die here, I want to see the sky first.  
I want to dance one more time, even if it’s just in this cell.

I dreamed about Bertram again.  
He was smiling. Or maybe it was the devil using his face — I can’t tell anymore.  
I told him I was sorry. He said it didn’t matter.  
Then he turned into fire.

I woke up shaking and laughed until I cried. Maybe that’s how madness starts.

There’s music tonight. Soft, distant — the kind they play for noble dinners.  
I can smell wine.

I wonder if they are drinking to me. To the good little weapon who burned her own blood.

If they come for me tomorrow, I hope they look me in the eye.  
I hope they see there’s still someone here, even if I don’t know her name anymore.

Maybe Blue died with him. Maybe that’s mercy.  
Maybe that’s the only mercy left.

## Closing Statement


“She was my best friend, and I didn’t see it. I thought she was just tired, hiding the same way I do—behind jokes, chores, noise. I saw the cracks and I told myself it was nothing, that she’d talk when she was ready. She didn’t. She kept her secrets from me too.

Everyone wants answers and I don’t have any. We were squires together, sisters in all but name, and I didn’t do enough.

I keep praying to Daern for strength and to Heironeous for courage, but it feels like the prayers fall back down before they reach anywhere. Maybe that’s what happens when you fail someone who trusted you.

They’ll talk about justice soon. I don’t even know what that word means anymore.”  
— Katherine Kendal
