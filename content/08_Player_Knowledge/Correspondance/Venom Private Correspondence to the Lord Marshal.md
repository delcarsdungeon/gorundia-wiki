_Transmission through the gnomish relay to Corven, for the Lord Marshal._

> **_Melda indonyo._**
> 
> Beloved of my heart.
> 
> Before Corven decides whether he ought to translate that, tell him I know perfectly well he can.
> 
> Sunhollow is ours.
> 
> More importantly, **Baron Valdrik Terevon deserves the credit for having it left to take.**
> 
> He maintained the investment, preserved his men, resisted every temptation to purchase speed with bodies, and knew his own ground better than any engineer we brought with us. When we gave him a tool he had never seen employed in war, he did not attempt to command its use merely because the siege was his.
> 
> He listened.
> 
> Then he told us where to put it.
> 
> He was right.
> 
> If you have honors to distribute when this is finished, remember that. If you have lands and titles to settle, remember it twice.
> 
> Sunhollow has been ill-used by its masters for too long. Terevon behaved throughout like a man who intends to remain responsible for the people living upon it after the banners come down.
> 
> We could use more of those.
> 
> The Badger worked.
> 
> I dislike having to put that sentence into the world.
> 
> Brindlewick is consequently unbearable.
> 
> We entered beneath the inner works, broke the defense from behind, and gave the Baron the opening he needed. The gnomes performed extremely well. Their little Crabs are ugly, awkward, surprisingly difficult to kill, and considerably more useful than I expected.
> 
> I suspect this describes gnomes generally.
> 
> Do not relay that portion back to them.
> 
> The final resistance was Ashen Wing and Iron Covenant. They had managed to summon a greater devil into the keep. It was large enough to be inconvenient and small enough to follow us through the corridors, which I consider poor planning on the part of Hell.
> 
> It is no longer our problem.
> 
> I have also acquired an executive officer.
> 
> **Aelric Vanner.**
> 
> You may know the name. If not, I apparently have spent enough of my life crossing his path for both of us.
> 
> I first met him when he was a lieutenant and I was young enough to believe officers became wiser when promoted.
> 
> I met him again perhaps twenty years later. He had acquired rank, several scars, and no detectable improvement in manners.
> 
> Then again, years after that, during a campaign neither of us remembers fondly.
> 
> I did not recognize him immediately this time.
> 
> Eventually he mentioned an action I remembered, and I realized who was standing in front of me.
> 
> Then I made the mistake of doing the arithmetic.
> 
> Beringer, **the old bastard is still soldiering.**
> 
> He was not young the first time.
> 
> He looks as though Pelor should be standing beside his bed with the last rites prepared, walks bent enough that a strong wind ought to finish him, and has now survived long enough to regard nearly every tactical error as something he has personally watched kill men before.
> 
> He is excellent.
> 
> I intend to keep him until one of us dies, which is apparently no longer a safe wager.
> 
> We have also picked up a dwarf named **Dorrik Slatemantle** to command the sappers.
> 
> Dorrik and I have reached an understanding concerning dwarven engineering.
> 
> He maintains that elves put too much empty space inside buildings.
> 
> I have explained that this is because we occasionally stand upright.
> 
> Relations remain cordial.
> 
> The enemy losses were severe. Ours were not light, but they were acceptable for what we took. The formal return will give you the numbers without forcing Corven to stand here listening to me enumerate dead men through another man's mouth.
> 
> One thing from that return bears repeating here.
> 
> The Badger is slow.
> 
> It is temperamental.
> 
> It requires enough maintenance personnel to constitute a minor religious order.
> 
> At one point I seriously considered setting it on fire.
> 
> **We need more of them.**
> 
> Not many.
> 
> Enough.
> 
> Give Terevon whatever consideration you can spare. He earned more here than I did.
> 
> I remain well.
> 
> I hope you are the same.
> 
> **Venom**
> 
> And Corven:
> 
> You may stop pretending you did not understand the first line.
