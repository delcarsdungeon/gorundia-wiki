## Paid Sending to Thalrik — Gravel via Brindlewick & Sons Arcane Dispatch

The message reaches Thalrik in the clipped, bloodless cadence of a hired sending.

No warmth.

No familiar voice.

No street noise behind it.

Just a professional man reading exactly what he was paid to read, and resenting none of it because resentment is probably an added service.

“Arcane dispatch delivered by **Master Pellovar Brindlewick**, licensed correspondent of **Brindlewick & Sons Arcane Dispatch, Writs, Warnings, and Discreet Unpleasantness**.”

A pause.

“Sender: Gravel.”

Another pause.

“Recipient: Thalrik Vaultforge.”

The voice flattens further.

“Message follows.”

A tiny clearing of the throat.

“Thalrik.”

“Dregs getting ugly.”

“Refugees coming in from Vogel lands.”

“Flat-teeth scared, broke, angry.”

“Vogel coin worthless.”

“Markets laughing.”

“Fights started.”

“Fires too.”

“Druuna holding our streets best she can.”

“Flat-teeth do not trust halfbloods.”

“Correction: flat-teeth do not trust halfbloods more than usual.”

“Halfbloods do not appreciate being blamed for bread, coin, war, weather, and whatever else frightened humans need a face for.”

“Several beatings.”

“One knife fight.”

“Two burned rooms.”

“Correction: two burned rooms I know about.”

“Rumors worse.”

“Children involved.”

“Correction: I do not know if sold, abandoned, hidden, or stolen.”

“Correction to correction: does not matter enough. It is bad.”

A pause.

The wizard’s professional tone hesitates for the smallest possible moment.

Then continues.

“I do not know the whole city.”

“I know the Dregs.”

“And down here it is going sideways.”

“People with Vogel coins got laughed at.”

“Then robbed.”

“Then some fools decided every halfblood in reach was part of it.”

“Correction: not some fools.”

“Enough fools.”

“Druuna keeping peace near us.”

“For now.”

“Barely.”

“Guaco has been mostly in the wind, if you see him first, tell him halflings feeding folk are helping.”

“If you got anyone with pull, tell them Dregs needs food, guards, and someone shutting moneychangers up before more fires start.”

“Correction: before more people set fires because moneychangers have faces.”

Another pause.

The wizard continues, still perfectly formal.

“Tell Rykeena her brother is probably being fancy somewhere while we are catching bricks.”

“Correction: do not tell Rykeena that unless funny.”

A longer pause.

“Correction: tell her.”

The wizard inhales carefully.

“Sender note: message length exceeds base dispatch.”

Another pause.

Then he reads on, because the words are apparently part of the paid text.

“What do you mean I pay by the word?”

A pause.

“This is ridiculous.”

“Do not write that.”

A smaller pause.

“Correction: apparently you wrote that.”

Another pause.

“You charge for corrections?”

A very controlled silence.

Then:

“You little ink-fingered bastard.”

Another pause.

“Correction: leave that in.”

A final pause.

“Well STOP.”

The wizard’s voice returns to its original commercial neutrality.

“Message ends: STOP.”

The spell cuts.
