To: Norwin Stoneyeye  
Bard’s Gate  

Norwin, I am pleased to see that time, distance, and apparent access to funding have not improved your bookkeeping. It would have worried me if they had. 

I remember the griffin. You insisted it was positioned incorrectly relative to the courtyard’s ley flow. I recall pointing out that it was, in fact, a statue. You were not persuaded. 

I have received word of your letters. Joric, Tarn, and Rudvik have each mentioned them—individually, and with varying degrees of restraint. You have, as expected, chosen well. Each possesses the particular flaw that makes your offer irresistible: **they are capable of becoming more than they are currently permitted to be**. This is, I assume, intentional. 

You ask for encouragement. You will have it—within limits. I will not _push_ them toward you. I will, however, ensure they understand exactly what you are offering:

- freedom of inquiry
- accelerated practical experience
- and the distinct possibility of learning something the University would prefer remained theoretical

If they choose you with full awareness of those terms, I will not interfere. If they do not understand them, I will. 

You describe your workshop as “enchantment-focused.” That gives me pause. You are brilliant, Norwin. That has never been in question. Your restraint, however, has always been… externally reinforced. I trust you. I do not trust the absence of friction. See that you remember the difference.

As for your invitation— Bard’s Gate has always been an interesting place to observe what happens when structure loosens and ambition fills the gaps. Your description suggests the process is accelerating. That alone makes it worth consideration. Do not mistake that for acceptance. 

I will not promise a visit. But neither will I dismiss the possibility. If nothing else, I am curious to see what you have built— and whether it remains yours. 

Take care of your students, Norwin. Not just their work. 

--- V. Erinleaf
