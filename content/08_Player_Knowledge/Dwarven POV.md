## Dwarves Looking at the Other Peoples

## Dwarf Baseline

Dwarves do not think of the world as flat.

This is the first thing other peoples get wrong.

Humans draw borders across fields.  
Elves remember forests.  
Halflings know roads and kitchens.  
Gnomes map mechanisms.  
Dwarves think in depth, weight, seams, foundations, water tables, tunnels, old claims, and who held which pass when the snow came down.

A dwarf’s private opinion of most surface folk is:

> “You live on the roof and somehow think you own the house.”

Dwarves are not one clean block, either.

- **Mountain dwarves** care little for surface borders drawn by humans, elves, or kings. A valley mouth, sheep slope, mine road, glacier pass, spring basin, or old pasture tied to their halls is theirs. Full stop.
- **Hill dwarves** deal more often with humans, charters, tolls, courts, mixed settlements, and contracts. They know how to manage surface politics. They would usually prefer not to.

Mountain dwarves think hill dwarves are over-socialized, compromised, and far too patient with nonsense.

Hill dwarves think mountain dwarves are proud, impractical, and one bad harvest away from needing the very trade deals they sneer at.

Both agree humans are exhausting.

---

### Mountain Dwarves and Hill Dwarves

**Mountain dwarf private take:**  
Hill dwarves are cousins who learned to smile at humans and now call it diplomacy.

Mountain dwarves respect hill dwarven endurance, tradecraft, and adaptability. They also think hill dwarves tolerate too much surface foolishness: noble titles, tariffs, court etiquette, seasonal fashions, and human contract law written by people who think sixty years is a long time.

**Mountain dwarf polite version:**  
“They do necessary work.”

**What they mean:**  
“Someone has to talk to the roof-dwellers.”

**Hill dwarf private take:**  
Mountain dwarves are kin, stone-true and iron-hard, but gods below, they make every conversation with humans ten times harder.

Hill dwarves respect mountain dwarven purity of custom and depth of memory. They also know that “we do not recognize your border” sounds wonderful in a drinking hall and less wonderful when a duke sends tax assessors with cavalry.

**Hill dwarf polite version:**  
“They keep the old ways.”

**What they mean:**  
“And leave us to clean up the paperwork.”

**Common dwarf saying:**  
“Mountain remembers. Hill negotiates. Both send invoices.”

---

### Humans

**Dwarf private take:**  
Humans are useful, brave, short-sighted, loud, and dangerously fond of declaring things settled.

Dwarves respect human effort. A human who works hard, pays fair, keeps oath, and maintains tools can earn honest regard. But human kingdoms rise and fall so quickly that dwarves have trouble taking their borders seriously.

Mountain dwarves especially do not care what duke, count, prince, or mayor claims the land above their halls this century. If the valley feeds their sheep, guards their road, vents their mines, or holds their dead, it is dwarven.

Hill dwarves understand human law better. They know how to write contracts humans can pretend were their idea. They know which nobles pay, which priests witness fairly, which reeves skim, and which families confuse “ancient right” with “grandfather stole it.”

They would rather be forging.

**Dwarf polite version:**  
“Humans are energetic.”

**What they mean:**  
“They make decisions before the stone has cooled.”

**Mountain dwarf saying:**  
“A human line on a map is ink. A dwarven claim is stone.”

**Hill dwarf saying:**  
“Get it witnessed, sealed, copied, and stored somewhere humans cannot burn.”

---

### Human Nobles

**Dwarf private take:**  
Human nobles are customers with swords and hereditary impatience.

Dwarves understand hierarchy. They have kings, thanes, guildmasters, elders, masters, apprentices, oath-lines, and clan-rights. They are not offended by rank.

They are offended by shallow rank.

A human noble whose grandfather took a hill and now calls it ancient patrimony is funny until he taxes a dwarven road or meddles with a mine charter.

Hill dwarves know how to manage nobles: flatter the title, bind the terms, over-document everything, and make sure the noble’s steward understands the penalties.

Mountain dwarves prefer a simpler method: close the gate.

**Dwarf polite version:**  
“His lordship has a strong sense of his rights.”

**What they mean:**  
“He has not yet found the older contract.”

**Common dwarf saying:**  
“A noble’s memory begins with inheritance. A dwarf’s begins with the first cut in the stone.”

---

### High Elves

**Dwarf private take:**  
High elves are beautiful, ancient, skilled, and slippery as water over polished slate.

Dwarves respect high elven craft, magic, memory, and patience. They also distrust their evasive speech, lighting games, and habit of turning direct questions into tragic mist.

Mountain dwarves find high elves insufferable because high elves act ancient while living above ground in decorative towers.

Hill dwarves find them useful but difficult. At least elves understand long time. Unfortunately, they also think long time excuses not answering a simple question.

**Dwarf polite version:**  
“The elder folk have deep memories.”

**What they mean:**  
“And shallow invoices.”

**Common dwarf saying:**  
“Ask an elf for a yes or no and he’ll hand you moonlight in a bowl.”

---

### Wood Elves

**Dwarf private take:**  
Wood elves are the sensible elves, until they start arguing with roads.

Dwarves respect wood elves more than high elves in practical matters. Wood elves understand territory, water, old claims, seasonal use, and the difference between ownership and stewardship. That makes them easier to deal with.

Until a quarry, road, bridge, or mine touches something the wood elves consider alive, sacred, or “not yours in the way you think.”

Mountain dwarves understand sacred land. They simply think forests are too emotionally fragile about it.

Hill dwarves can work with wood elves, but the contract must include every spring, grove, trail, animal path, and tree that has apparently developed legal standing.

**Dwarf polite version:**  
“The forest folk respect old places.”

**What they mean:**  
“They make trees into litigants.”

**Common dwarf saying:**  
“A wood elf will bless your well, then sue your culvert.”

---

### Grugach / Wild Elves

**Dwarf private take:**  
Grugach are what happens when elves stop pretending and start shooting.

Dwarves do not dismiss them. Anyone who can hold forest territory for generations against monsters, kings, goblins, and stupidity deserves respect.

But dwarves do not like negotiating with people who define “border” as “where the arrows start.”

Mountain dwarves understand this better than most. They also hold hard boundaries. The difference is that dwarves mark theirs with stone, gates, cairns, and warning runes. Grugach apparently expect everyone to notice the mood of the moss.

**Dwarf polite version:**  
“They are fierce guardians.”

**What they mean:**  
“They need signs.”

**Common dwarf saying:**  
“A grugach border is a feeling followed by a shaft.”

---

### Halflings

**Dwarf private take:**  
Halflings are good folk, sharp folk, and much less harmless than they look.

Dwarves generally like halflings. Halflings respect food, family, hearth, craft, trade, and quiet competence. A halfling innkeeper who keeps good beer and fair measures is a blessing on the road.

Dwarves also know halflings can run smuggling rings, kitchen intelligence networks, debt webs, and entire neighborhoods without raising their voices.

Mountain dwarves tend to underestimate halflings until the supplies arrive exactly where needed, three days early, through a road no one officially used.

Hill dwarves know better.

**Dwarf polite version:**  
“The hin are capable householders.”

**What they mean:**  
“They know every door, every debt, and every appetite.”

**Common dwarf saying:**  
“A halfling with a ledger is taller than he looks.”

---

### Gnomes

**Dwarf private take:**  
Gnomes are clever cousins of the small world, but they do not know when to stop improving things.

Dwarves respect gnomish precision, mechanisms, mathematics, lenses, gears, and clever work. They distrust gnomish enthusiasm.

A dwarf improves a hinge until it works for two hundred years.  
A gnome improves a hinge until it whistles, folds sideways, counts visitors, and explodes during fog.

Hill dwarves employ gnomes carefully.

Mountain dwarves put them in supervised workshops with stone walls.

**Dwarf polite version:**  
“Gnomes have lively minds.”

**What they mean:**  
“Bolt down the tools.”

**Common dwarf saying:**  
“A gnome asks ‘why not?’ because he has not yet paid for repairs.”

---

### Halfbloods

**Dwarf private take:**  
Halfbloods are judged by work, oath, and trouble brought.

Dwarves are often less obsessed with blood purity than humans or elves, but that does not make them cuddly. A half-orc, half-elf, or other mixed-blood person who works hard, pays fair, keeps oath, and does not bring chaos may be accepted in practical ways.

That acceptance may still be cold.

Dwarves respect proof more than plea. They may not care what your father was if your masonry holds. But if your masonry fails, they will remember your father, mother, cousins, boots, and breakfast habits.

Hill dwarves are more used to halfbloods in towns, trade, mercenary companies, and mixed households.

Mountain dwarves are slower, colder, and less flexible. They do not hate quickly. They also do not accept quickly.

**Dwarf polite version:**  
“Let the work speak.”

**What they mean:**  
“We will judge after the third winter.”

**Common dwarf saying:**  
“Blood is loud. Stone waits.”

---

### Orcs

**Dwarf private take:**  
Orcs are ruin with teeth.

Full-blooded orcs are not seen as another people to bargain with. Not by sane dwarves. Orcs fight, burn, spoil, flee, and destroy what they cannot hold. They foul wells, break tools, slaughter flocks, collapse tunnels, burn grain, and leave dead things in springs.

For dwarves, this is not abstract. Orcs are mine-breakers, gate-rushers, tunnel-defilers, corpse-pilers, and old enemies in the dark.

Mountain dwarves hate them with ancestral clarity.

Hill dwarves may speak more softly in mixed company, but the feeling is the same.

**Dwarf polite version:**  
“Orcs are not treated with.”

**What they mean:**  
“Seal the gate. Count the children. Bring axes.”

**Mountain dwarf saying:**  
“An orc in retreat is just a fire looking for kindling.”

**Hill dwarf saying:**  
“If the orcs are running, check the wells before you cheer.”

---

### Clergy / Holy Orders

**Dwarf private take:**  
Human priests are useful, sincere, and much too confident for people who subcontract wisdom to the sky.

Dwarves respect working faith: rites over the dead, blessings over tools, oaths before witnesses, healing after battle, and priests who know how to keep panic out of a hall.

They dislike religious authorities who interfere with clan law, mining rights, ancestral tombs, or dwarven death customs.

Hill dwarves can work with the Triad. They understand temple courts and oath-witnessing.

Mountain dwarves keep their own rites first and tolerate outsiders second.

**Dwarf polite version:**  
“The holy folk serve their people.”

**What they mean:**  
“Good. Let them not presume to serve ours without asking.”

**Common dwarf saying:**  
“A priest may bless the hammer. He does not swing it for you.”

---

### Wizards and Scholars

**Dwarf private take:**  
Wizards are dangerous craftsmen with poor shop discipline.

Dwarves respect mastery. A proper wizard who studies, records, tests, and accepts limits can earn respect. A wizard who improvises near load-bearing stone is a public menace.

Scholars are useful when they preserve records, translate old marks, read treaties, or remember where the bodies are buried. They are less useful when they mistake knowing a thing for owning it.

Hill dwarves often employ scholars and mages through contracts.

Mountain dwarves allow them only where permitted and usually under watch.

**Dwarf polite version:**  
“Learned folk have their uses.”

**What they mean:**  
“Keep them away from the supports.”

**Common dwarf saying:**  
“Magic is like blasting powder. Useful, loud, and best handled by someone who still has eyebrows.”

## Dwarves About Their Own Rotten Elements

Dwarves know their own sins.

They know about clan hoarders, oath-lawyers, mine tyrants, guild cheats, tunnel smugglers, drunkards, debt-breakers, cruel masters, false assayers, corpse-robbers, and elders who call cowardice “tradition.”

They do not like outsiders pointing it out.

A corrupt dwarf is not merely a criminal. He is a crack in the hall. And cracks are either repaired, sealed, or cut out before the mountain finds them.

Hill dwarves are especially ashamed of contract-corruption: predatory charters, debt traps, monopoly games, and Galibano-style arrangements where law becomes a pickaxe in someone else’s ribs.

Mountain dwarves see this and say, “This is what comes of too much surface dealing.”

Hill dwarves say, “This is what comes of mountain fools refusing to handle the surface work themselves.”

Both may be right. Annoying, but there it is.

**Dwarf public version:**  
“The matter is before the clan.”

**Dwarf private version:**  
“He shaved weight, lied under seal, and brought shame into the ledger.”

**Hill dwarf response:**  
Audit, restitution, contract seizure, public censure, exile if needed.

**Mountain dwarf response:**  
Strip the mark, break the tools, close the gate behind him.

## The Dwarf Politeness Weapon

Dwarves do not do airy politeness. They do dry politeness, contract politeness, and “I am not insulting you because the roof is low and there are witnesses” politeness.

### Translation Table

**“That will need work.”**  
It is bad.

**“That will need considerable work.”**  
It is terrible.

**“Who built this?”**  
Someone must be blamed.

**“Interesting joint.”**  
Wrong joint.

**“Brave choice.”**  
Unsafe choice.

**“It may hold.”**  
Do not stand under it.

**“Surface work.”**  
Flashy, temporary, or both.

**“Human measure.”**  
Too short, too rushed, too optimistic.

**“Elven answer.”**  
Pretty words. No weight.

**“Gnome solution.”**  
Clever, overbuilt, and likely to move when it should not.

**“Halfling arrangement.”**  
Informal, effective, and suspiciously comfortable.

**“We have records.”**  
You are already losing.

**“The stone remembers.”**  
We have records older than your kingdom and worse manners than your lawyer.

**“Aye, if the contract says so.”**  
No, unless forced.

**“No.”**  
No.

**“No, and now I’m annoyed.”**  
Still no, and the price just went up.

> “Humans build fast because they know they won’t be here to fix it.”

> “A human border is where the ink dried. A dwarven border is where our dead are buried.”

> “High elves speak like light through glass. Pretty, bent, and never where you thought it was.”

> “Wood elves understand old places. Shame they think every shrub has standing.”

> “Grugach need cairns, signs, and possibly trousers.”

> “Halflings are good folk. Never trust a people less because they can reach your purse from below eye level.”

> “Gnomes make fine tools. Then they keep going.”

> “A halfblood’s work either holds or it doesn’t. Same as yours.”

> “Orcs do not retreat. They spread the fire behind them.”

> “A priest may speak for the gods. The beam still needs bracing.”

> “A wizard with patience is a craft-brother. A wizard in a hurry is weather indoors.”

> “Hill dwarves know humans. Mountain dwarves know better.”

> “Mountain dwarves know stone. Hill dwarves know who is trying to steal it.”

## The Core Dwarf Horror

Dwarves are quietly horrified because most other peoples treat permanence like a mood.

Humans redraw borders.  
Elves hide decisions in poetry.  
Wood elves give trees legal standing.  
Grugach refuse signs.  
Halflings know too much.  
Gnomes improve past safety.  
Halfbloods bring social noise before the work is even tested.  
Clergy bless what they did not build.  
Wizards stress the foundations.  
Orcs burn, break, spoil, and flee through the smoke.

Mountain dwarves believe safety comes from stone, depth, gate, clan, and old claim.

Hill dwarves believe safety comes from contract, trade, leverage, witness, and knowing exactly how humans lie.

The dwarf worldview is basically:

> “What holds, matters. What endures, matters more. Everything else is surface weather.”
