# Halfling Tree Farm Economics

## Backlinks
- [[Jinwing]]
- [[Gorundia Religion]]
- [[Hearthguard Halflings]]

## Overview

This article assumes a **300-acre managed tree farm** operated under **halfling TL4 agricultural practice**, using mostly **human, elf, or half-elf labor**, with **tier-one druidic support** from Jinwing and associated wood elves in the short term. The long-term goal is to attract formal support from druids of **Sheela Peryroyl**, who strongly prefer managed timber cultivation over the destruction of old-growth forest.

This is not a magical money fountain. It is a disciplined, low-waste, medium-term woodland enterprise producing:

- timber
- firewood and byproducts
- charcoal
- long-term strategic fuel and construction stock

The key strength of the operation is not raw speed, but **efficiency, sustainability, and expandability**.

## Assumptions

### Management
- Halflings run planning, spacing, coppicing schedules, storage, waste control, and charcoal quality.
- Their TL4 agricultural knowledge improves woodland yield without requiring the whole society to be TL4.
- They reduce waste, improve rotation discipline, and get more usable product from the same acreage.

### Labor
- Most laborers are human, elf, or half-elf.
- Foresters are ideally skilled elf or half-elf professionals, as they help reassure druids and improve long-term timber selection.
- Woodcutters and charcoal burners can be ordinary skilled rural labor.

### Druid Support: Tier One
Tier-one druid support represents:

- modest growth encouragement
- soil and root health
- blight prevention
- guidance away from destructive cutting practices

This is not full accelerated magical forestry. It is best understood as nature being helped along rather than forced.

## Annual Revenue: 300 Acres

The following numbers assume a mature, functioning operation rather than a first-year startup.

### Timber
With TL4 halfling management and tier-one druidic support, timber output rises substantially over a TL3 human baseline.

**Estimated annual timber revenue:**  
**$21,000–$42,000**  
**52.5–105 gold**

### Firewood and Byproducts
Waste wood, branchwood, scrap, and lower-grade cuts remain valuable.

**Estimated annual firewood and byproduct revenue:**  
**$4,500–$9,000**  
**11.25–22.5 gold**

### Charcoal
Charcoal is the real profit engine. Halfling precision matters most here, and even light druidic support helps keep harvest cycles reliable and healthy.

**Estimated annual charcoal revenue:**  
**$12,000–$27,000**  
**30–67.5 gold**

## Total Revenue

**Estimated total annual revenue:**  
**$37,500–$78,000**  
**93.75–195 gold**

## Annual Expenses: 300 Acres

### Labor
A plausible labor structure for 300 acres:

- 1 halfling overseer
- 2 skilled foresters
- 12–15 woodcutters and teamsters
- 4–6 charcoal burners
- seasonal extra hands as needed

**Estimated annual labor cost:**  
**$21,000–$24,000**  
**52.5–60 gold**

### Maintenance and Upkeep
Includes:

- carts
- axes, saws, wedges, and pit tools
- sheds and storage yards
- road and track upkeep
- animal feed and tack
- kiln or pit maintenance

**Estimated annual maintenance cost:**  
**$4,500–$6,000**  
**11.25–15 gold**

### Druidic Relationship Costs
Tier-one druids are not simply salaried staff. Their costs are partly political and partly material:

- gifts
- sacred set-asides
- noninterference agreements
- ritual hospitality
- selective land protection

**Estimated effective annual cost:**  
**$3,000–$6,000**  
**7.5–15 gold**

## Total Expenses

**Estimated annual total expenses:**  
**$28,500–$36,000**  
**71.25–90 gold**

## Net Profit

**Estimated annual net profit:**  
**$9,000–$42,000**  
**22.5–105 gold**

### Realistic Working Profit
For a stable, competently run operation that is doing well but not operating at fantasy-perfect maximum:

**Likely working annual profit:**  
**$18,000–$30,000**  
**45–75 gold**

That is the number I would actually use at the table unless something exceptional happens.

## Startup and Ramp-Up Notes

A new tree farm does not hit full performance immediately.

### Early Phase: Year 1
- roads cleared
- sheds built
- coppice areas marked
- foresters survey the land
- druids judge whether the owners are full of crap

Revenue is reduced in this phase unless the land already has useful managed timber on it.

### Development Phase: Years 2–4
- charcoal production becomes more regular
- waste drops sharply
- tree rotation starts making sense
- foresters begin selecting for future quality timber

### Established Phase: Year 5+
The farm begins to behave like a true renewable woodland enterprise rather than a glorified logging camp.

## Independent Income Calculation

Under **GURPS 4e**, **Independent Income** gives **1% of starting wealth per level per month**.

At **TL3**, standard starting wealth is **$1,000**.  
That means:

- **1 level of Independent Income = $10/month**
- **= $120/year**
- In your economy, that equals **120 copper/year**
- Or **0.3 gold/year**

### If the 300-acre tree farm is personally owned
Using the realistic annual profit figure:

- **Low working profit:** $18,000/year
- **High working profit:** $30,000/year

Divide by $120/year per level:

- **$18,000/year = 150 levels**
- **$30,000/year = 250 levels**

So by strict raw math, this is far beyond what Independent Income is meant to model on a normal character sheet.

## Practical GURPS Interpretation

This means the farm should **not** be treated as ordinary Independent Income bought level by level unless the campaign is using very large social-scale advantages.

Instead, treat it as one of the following:

### Option A: Patrimony / Estate Income
Best choice for a noble or landholder.

The tree farm is part of an estate and supports:

- household expenses
- retainers
- horses
- political obligations
- military overhead

In this model, the income is mostly setting-facing, not pocket-money-facing.

### Option B: Signature Holding
Treat the farm as a major asset whose returns matter in domain play, not as literal monthly spending cash for adventuring gear.

### Option C: Capped Character Benefit
If you want to convert some of it into personal GURPS advantage value without going insane, a reasonable cap would be:

- **Independent Income 5–10** for the owner’s personal draw
- the rest remains tied up in estate operations, wages, reinvestment, obligations, and local politics

That gives:

- **II 5 = $50/month = $600/year**
- **II 10 = $100/month = $1,200/year**

That is still substantial for a character, but doesn’t turn the sheet into comedy.

## Best Table Ruling

For campaign sanity:

- Use the **45–75 gold/year** figure for domain-scale profit.
- Let the owner personally realize only a fraction of that as ready personal money.
- Treat the rest as:
  - reinvestment
  - wages
  - favors
  - gifts
  - militia support
  - prestige
  - leverage with druids, smiths, and local powers

## Strategic Value

The true value of the operation is larger than its cash return.

### It provides
- renewable timber
- reliable charcoal
- a non-destructive alternative to cutting old-growth
- political goodwill with woodland factions
- a future bridge to druids of [[Gorundia Religion|Sheela Peryroyl's faith]]

### It also creates pressure
- nearby lords may resent its efficiency
- charcoal buyers become dependent
- rivals may attempt sabotage, overtaxation, or legal interference
- druids may withdraw support if the operation slides toward greed

## Final Take

A **300-acre halfling-run, tier-one druid-supported tree farm** is a very solid holding.

It is not “one weird trick to become king.”  
It is, however, exactly the kind of boring-looking asset that ends up quietly funding soldiers, smiths, and influence while louder idiots are still arguing about honor.
