## Nobles of Gorundia Looking at the Other Peoples

## Gorundian Noble Baseline

Gorundian nobles are not one race. Most are human, but noble courts include half-elves, old elven-blooded houses, dwarven charter families, halfling reeves and merchant-clans, gnome scholars, and the occasional halfblood elevated by war, service, marriage, or scandal.

The elvish diaspora especially complicates things. Many noble houses have elven clients, tutors, spouses, heralds, artists, wardens, or distant bloodlines. This lets nobles say very enlightened things in public while still arranging seating like a caste diagram.

To Gorundian nobles, race matters.

But rank matters more.

A common elf is still common.  
A titled half-elf is still titled.  
A dwarf master with a royal charter must be respected.  
A halfling mayor with money and men is not “cute.”  
A half-orc knight with land is a problem the heralds must learn to pronounce.

Their private opinion of most peoples is:

> “Everyone has a place. Trouble starts when they forget it — or when we are forced to admit we placed them badly.”

Gorundian nobles can be gracious, brave, cultured, and genuinely protective. They can also be class-soaked peacocks with swords, old debts, and inherited opinions they mistake for law.

---

### Commoners

**Noble private take:**  
Commoners are the body of the realm: necessary, numerous, emotional, and alarming when gathered in groups.

Good nobles see commoners as their duty. Bad nobles see them as scenery that pays taxes. Most fall somewhere in between and call that balance.

They respect loyal tenants, veteran soldiers, skilled guildsmen, good reeves, and old village families. They fear mobs, preachers, grain shortages, and anyone who starts using words like “rights” near a market square.

**Noble polite version:**  
“The people are the strength of the realm.”

**What they mean:**  
“The people are the strength of the realm when properly supervised.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“A village is loyal until hunger teaches it politics.”

---

### Peasants and Serfs

**Noble private take:**  
Peasants are honest, stubborn, superstitious, and dangerously aware of who actually grows the food.

Nobles romanticize peasants when they are singing harvest songs and paying dues. They become much less sentimental when the same peasants hide grain, shelter deserters, or ask why the lord’s fifth hunting preserve needs another fence.

**Noble polite version:**  
“Our tenants are simple, faithful folk.”

**What they mean:**  
“They are watching us more closely than we prefer.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“The plowman bows low, but he knows where the stones are buried.”

---

### Knights and Soldiers

**Noble private take:**  
Soldiers are the realm’s bones. Knights are what soldiers become when someone adds money, horses, and poetry.

Nobles respect martial service deeply. Even soft-handed nobles understand that Gorundia was built and preserved by armed men and women doing bloody work. A brave common soldier can earn more real respect than a useless lordling.

That said, nobles prefer soldiers who remember who gives orders.

**Noble polite version:**  
“The sword-arm of the realm must be honored.”

**What they mean:**  
“And kept pointed away from succession disputes unless invited.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“A loyal captain is worth three cousins.”

---

### Humans

**Noble private take:**  
Humans are the proper center of the realm, mostly because humans built the current paperwork.

Most Gorundian nobles assume human custom is the default. Even houses with elven or mixed blood often frame themselves through human law, feudal inheritance, oaths, land, marriage, and war-service.

They admire human adaptability and ambition, while quietly pretending those same traits are “tradition” once a noble house has held land for three generations.

**Noble polite version:**  
“Human law binds the realm together.”

**What they mean:**  
“We wrote the rules while everyone else was grieving, hiding, migrating, or arguing with trees.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“Custom is memory wearing a crown.”

---

### High Elves

**Noble private take:**  
High elves are magnificent, irritating, prestigious, and never as neutral as they claim.

Gorundian nobles covet high elven approval. An elven tutor, singer, herald, swordmaster, architect, or bride adds instant refinement to a court. Old elven blood can make a house seem ancient, graceful, and just mysterious enough to charge more for horses.

But nobles also distrust high elves. They are too evasive, too patient, too aware of ancient claims, and too good at making a room feel like the human baron is a guest in his own castle.

**Noble polite version:**  
“The elder folk honor us with their counsel.”

**What they mean:**  
“They are useful, beautiful, and absolutely judging the tapestries.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“An elf at court is either an ornament, an ally, or a witness. Pray he is only the first.”

---

### Wood Elves

**Noble private take:**  
Wood elves are respectable when they are wardens, scouts, archers, druids, or picturesque allies — difficult when they start having opinions about roads.

Nobles like wood elves better than grugach because wood elves can attend court, make treaties, dress properly, and speak without threatening to turn someone into compost.

But wood elves still make nobles nervous. They insist the land has rights, that old groves matter, that rivers remember, and that hunting preserves are not automatically sacred just because a count put a fence around them.

**Noble polite version:**  
“The forest folk keep old wisdom.”

**What they mean:**  
“They are excellent scouts until they begin auditing our drainage.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“A wood elf can bless your orchard or condemn your bridge with the same soft voice.”

---

### Grugach / Wild Elves

**Noble private take:**  
Grugach are what happens when elves stop attending meetings.

Nobles fear and romanticize them. A grugach warrior is terrifying, exotic, useful in war, and impossible at dinner. They are seen as brave, proud, violent, and tragically uncivilized — which is noble-speak for “will not recognize our jurisdiction.”

Some noble houses respect them as ancient forest defenders. Others see them as bandits with better cheekbones.

**Noble polite version:**  
“The wild elves are fierce guardians of old places.”

**What they mean:**  
“They ignore titles, borders, tax law, and dinner seating.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“A grugach does not trespass. He simply disagrees that you own the forest.”

---

### Dwarves

**Noble private take:**  
Dwarves are invaluable, stubborn, expensive, and far too hard to intimidate.

Nobles respect dwarven craft, banking, stonework, mining, siegecraft, and oath-keeping. A chartered dwarf clan can be one of the strongest assets a noble house possesses.

They are also infuriating. Dwarves negotiate like mountains with accountants. They remember debts. They demand written terms. They do not flutter when threatened by titles.

**Noble polite version:**  
“The dwarven clans are pillars of the realm’s prosperity.”

**What they mean:**  
“They are difficult to cheat and therefore morally exhausting.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“Never threaten a dwarf with old law. He brought a copy.”

---

### Halflings

**Noble private take:**  
Halflings are charming, useful, well-connected, and much more politically dangerous than they look.

Nobles love halfling cooks, stewards, household managers, merchants, reeves, innkeepers, and discreet messengers. Halflings make courts run. They feed everyone, hear everything, and somehow know who is sleeping badly before the chamberlain does.

Many nobles underestimate them anyway because halflings are small, polite, and usually holding bread.

That is how the halflings get you.

**Noble polite version:**  
“The hin bring warmth and steadiness to any household.”

**What they mean:**  
“They know every secret before the herald does.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“A halfling servant is invisible until you need him. A halfling enemy is invisible until too late.”

---

### Gnomes

**Noble private take:**  
Gnomes are brilliant, necessary, and impossible to keep on topic.

Nobles value gnome tutors, scribes, wizards, engineers, artificers, archivists, and accountants. A good gnome can make a court look learned and efficient. A bad gnome can make a court look investigated.

Nobles dislike gnomes asking direct questions about inheritance, military spending, old maps, missing clauses, or why the duke’s seal appears on two contradictory documents.

**Noble polite version:**  
“The gnomish mind is a treasure of precision.”

**What they mean:**  
“Do not leave him alone with the archives.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“A gnome with a question is a candle. A gnome with records is a fire.”

---

### Halfbloods

**Noble private take:**  
Halfbloods are useful, awkward, brave, dangerous, and socially expensive.

Gorundian nobles vary wildly here. Some houses, especially those touched by elven diaspora or frontier necessity, are more accepting. Others treat halfbloods as living irregularities: fine as soldiers, scouts, bastard retainers, bodyguards, or distant cousins — less fine as heirs, spouses, priests, or public symbols.

A halfblood who is loyal and competent may be praised privately. A halfblood who demands recognition becomes a political problem.

**Noble polite version:**  
“Such people often possess remarkable strength of character.”

**What they mean:**  
“We value them most when their status remains conveniently undefined.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“A halfblood at the gate is a guard. A halfblood at the table is a question.”

---

### Orcs

**Noble private take:**  
Orcs are enemies, raiders, shock troops, border memories, and the reason half-orc politics are never simple.

Most Gorundian nobles see orcs through war stories, Dominion associations, old raids, and frontier fear. Even nobles willing to employ or tolerate half-orcs usually draw a hard line at full orcish bands.

An orc chief with discipline may be treated as a military reality. An orc ambassador would be treated as a siege engine that learned etiquette.

**Noble polite version:**  
“Orcish peoples have long stood outside the realm’s peace.”

**What they mean:**  
“We expect violence and will congratulate ourselves if we are wrong.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“Never ignore an orc. Never trust the first silence.”

---

### Drow

**Noble private take:**  
Drow are a diplomatic nightmare wearing a religious panic.

Most nobles do not know enough about drow to have clean opinions. They know elves fear them, priests distrust them, old stories blacken them, and any public association with them could wreck a court faster than a bastard at a wedding.

A drow guest would be treated with terrifying politeness: watched, fed, flattered, isolated, and discussed in twelve private rooms before dessert.

**Noble polite version:**  
“The matter requires discretion.”

**What they mean:**  
“No one say ‘drow’ loudly until we know which priest, elf, and spymaster are already listening.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“Some guests are seated by rank. Some are seated by blast radius.”

---

### Foreigners

**Noble private take:**  
Foreigners are useful until they start explaining how things are done elsewhere.

Gorundian nobles can be hospitable, especially to envoys, merchants, mercenaries, scholars, and exiles with money or leverage. But they also tend to assume foreign customs are charming at best and suspicious at worst.

Foreigners become much more acceptable once they accept Gorundian titles, contracts, marriages, gods, laws, or military command.

**Noble polite version:**  
“Our realm has long welcomed honorable guests.”

**What they mean:**  
“Behave, pay, swear, marry, or leave.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“A foreign custom is charming once. Repeated, it becomes a policy concern.”

---

### Clergy / Holy Orders

**Noble private take:**  
The clergy are necessary, powerful, inconvenient, and worst of all, sometimes right.

Nobles respect the Triad deeply. They need priests for legitimacy, healing, trials, oaths, marriages, burials, and public morality. A noble house without clerical support looks naked.

But nobles also dislike clerics who remember that divine law outranks noble comfort. Heironeans can shame a coward. Pelorites can expose cruelty. Pholtans can turn a rumor into sworn testimony with paperwork and a glowing lantern.

**Noble polite version:**  
“The holy orders are the conscience of the realm.”

**What they mean:**  
“And consciences should remember who funds the roof.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“A priest blesses your house. A good priest also knows where it leaks.”

---

### Wizards and Scholars

**Noble private take:**  
Wizards are investments with opinions.

Every noble wants magic, but no noble wants a wizard who believes access to power makes him a political philosopher. Court mages, tutors, seers, and archivists are essential — and dangerous if neglected.

Scholars are safer, unless they are historians. Historians are just assassins who use dates.

**Noble polite version:**  
“Learning ennobles the realm.”

**What they mean:**  
“Learning is excellent when it supports our claim.”

**Common noble saying:**  
“A wizard sees the unseen. A historian sees the inconvenient.”

## Nobles About Their Own Rotten Elements

This is where Gorundian nobility gets twitchy.

Nobles know some of their own are monsters.

They know about cruel lords, bought judges, forced marriages, inheritance games, hunting accidents, forged charters, quiet poisonings, bastard coverups, abused servants, and “family matters” that somehow require six armed men and a priest.

They do not like outsiders saying it.

A noble criminal is not merely a criminal. He is a stain on the system that justifies every noble’s power. This is why noble justice can be both slow and savage. A disgraced noble must be handled carefully — or spectacularly — depending on whether the goal is concealment or example.

**Noble public version:**  
“Lord Edric’s conduct has brought sorrow upon his house.”

**Noble private version:**  
“Edric is a pig, his steward is a coward, his wife knows, and his brother has been waiting six years.”

**Noble polite version for scandal:**  
“The matter is being contained.”

**What they mean:**  
“Someone is about to lose title, land, liberty, blood, or all four.”

## The Noble Politeness Weapon

Nobles do not usually insult directly. They classify, rank, and imply.

A noble insult should sound like a compliment until the victim realizes three servants heard it.

### Translation Table

**“An old family.”**  
Important, possibly inbred, definitely sensitive.

**“A rising house.”**  
New money with sharp elbows.

**“A practical man.”**  
Low manners, useful skills.

**“A man of strong convictions.”**  
Difficult, possibly dangerous.

**“A woman of unusual independence.”**  
She does not obey the expected men.

**“Their household is lively.”**  
Scandal lives there and has its own room.

**“He has battlefield manners.”**  
He is rude, but currently useful.

**“She has courtly instincts.”**  
She is lying beautifully.

**“A complicated inheritance.”**  
Someone is probably illegitimate, murdered, or both.

**“A matter for the family.”**  
Do not ask unless you want knives.

**“A matter for the Church.”**  
We have lost control of the scandal.

**“A matter for the Crown.”**  
Someone important is in very deep trouble.

**“He is loyal in his way.”**  
He is not loyal enough.

**“She understands discretion.”**  
She knows where bodies, letters, or lovers are buried.

**“We shall remember this.”**  
This is now a feud.

**“You honor us with your candor.”**  
You have been rude in public.

## Sample Noble Lines About Everyone

> “Commoners are the realm’s heart. This is why one must not let panic reach it.”

> “Humans built Gorundia because someone had to stop arguing and raise the walls.”

> “High elves lend grace to a court, provided one remembers they hear insult in furniture.”

> “Wood elves are excellent allies until your road crosses a tree they have emotionally adopted.”

> “The grugach are kin to civilization in the way thunder is kin to music.”

> “Dwarves are expensive because quality is expensive. They are unbearable for the same reason.”

> “Halflings are harmless in the same way keys are harmless.”

> “Gnomes should be treasured, encouraged, funded, and watched.”

> “A halfblood knight is still a knight. But gods help the herald who must explain that to three grandmothers.”

> “Orcs do not trouble me when they shout. I worry when they learn patience.”

> “The clergy keep us honest. This is often inconvenient and occasionally fatal.”

> “A wizard in service is an asset. A wizard with a theory is weather.”

## The Core Noble Horror

Gorundian nobles are quietly horrified because everyone else seems to misunderstand the central truth of the realm:

Order is fragile.

Commoners think order is oppression.  
Clergy think order should be holy.  
Wizards think order should be rational.  
Halflings think order should be kind.  
Gnomes think order should be documented.  
Dwarves think order should be contractual.  
Elves think order should be ancient.  
Halfbloods think order should make room.  
Orcs think order is what survives the axe.

Nobles believe order must be inherited, enforced, performed, defended, and occasionally lied about until the danger passes.

The noble worldview is basically:

> “The realm is a table laid for dinner during a storm. Someone must hold the roof down, someone must decide who sits where, and someone must pretend the leaks are under control.”
