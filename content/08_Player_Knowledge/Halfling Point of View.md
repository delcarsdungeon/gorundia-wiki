## Halflings Looking at the Other Races

### Humans

**Halfling private take:**  
Humans are sweet, brave, exhausting creatures who build kingdoms the way toddlers build towers: too high, too fast, and then everyone acts surprised when the blocks hit someone.

They admire human energy. They also find it horrifying. Humans wake up one morning and decide they need a crusade, a new tax office, a gallows, three marriages, and a cavalry reform before lunch.

**Bless-your-heart version:**  
“Humans do have such big feelings, don’t they? Best make sure they’ve eaten before they start legislating.”

**What they mean:**  
“Hide the knives, the banners, and the hard liquor. The tallfolk are about to make history again, and history never pays for damages.”

---

### Nobles

Halflings do not see “noble” as a race, but they treat it like one because, come on, look at them.

**Private take:**  
Nobles are humans who were told as children that their tantrums had heraldry.

Halflings are quietly baffled by how nobles can make eating, sitting, walking, marrying, dying, and apologizing into separate political emergencies. Neltara expecting her companions to sit with her is a perfect cultural clash: halflings treat companions more as equals than servants, while Gorundian court etiquette starts hyperventilating into its sleeve.

**Bless-your-heart version:**  
“Oh, he has a very old family. That must be such a comfort to him.”

**What they mean:**  
“He has mistaken genealogy for a personality.”

---

### Elves

**Private take:**  
Elves are lovely, elegant, and deeply committed to making every problem older than breakfast.

Halflings admire elven beauty, music, craft, and memory. They are also quietly horrified that elves can carry a grudge so long it needs its own chair at dinner.

High elves especially trigger the halfling “oh dear” reflex. Too much etiquette. Too much tragedy. Too much “we must consider the sorrow of the Third Branch beneath the Moon of Regret” when the actual question was whether anyone wants more potatoes.

**Bless-your-heart version:**  
“Elves do feel things very deeply. Eventually.”

**What they mean:**  
“They are one harp song away from explaining why your soup has political implications.”

---

### Grugach / Wood Elves

**Private take:**  
The forest elves are terrifying, but refreshingly direct.

Halflings are often less horrified by grugach violence than by high elven manners. A grugach might kill you for crossing a boundary. A high elf might invite you to tea, imply you crossed a boundary, and make you realize three days later that you’ve been cursed by seating order.

**Bless-your-heart version:**  
“Well, at least the wild ones tell you when you’re unwelcome.”

**What they mean:**  
“Put the pie down slowly and back away from the tree line.”

---

### Dwarves

**Private take:**  
Dwarves are sturdy, honorable, useful, and emotionally constipated enough to qualify as architecture.

Halflings respect dwarven craft and reliability. They like that dwarves usually mean what they say. They do not like that dwarves can turn “I dislike your cousin’s bookkeeping” into a feud lasting eight generations and three tunnels.

**Bless-your-heart version:**  
“Dwarves are very dependable. Once they’ve finished being right.”

**What they mean:**  
“They would rather die than admit the stew needs salt.”

---

### Gnomes

**Private take:**  
Gnomes are delightful until they become educational.

Halflings like gnomes. They are smallfolk cousins in the great tallfolk disaster zone. But gnomes ask questions. So many questions. They will dismantle your lock, your recipe, your family scandal, and possibly your oven to see how it works.

**Bless-your-heart version:**  
“Such curious little minds.”

**What they mean:**  
“Do not let him near the pantry inventory unless you want a thesis.”

---

### Half-Orcs / Halfbloods

**Private take:**  
Poor dears. Dangerous poor dears. Hungry poor dears. Sit down, love.

Halflings are probably some of the least immediately hostile people toward halfbloods, because halfling culture has a strong “feed first, judge later” reflex. But they are not stupid. They know half-orcs carry social danger with them. Not always because of what the half-orc will do — often because of how everyone else reacts.

Neltara arriving with a half-orc knight, half-orc wizard, and half-orc smuggler-father is exactly the kind of thing that makes Gorundian court culture briefly blue-screen. Halflings would understand the social bomb and still serve tea.

**Bless-your-heart version:**  
“He looks like he’s had a hard road. Give him two servings.”

**What they mean:**  
“If he explodes, I’m blaming whoever made him feel unwelcome before dessert.”

---

### Orcs

**Private take:**  
Orcs are what happen when nobody teaches boys table manners and then gives them axes.

This is unfair, obviously. Also common. Halflings fear full-orcs more than half-orcs, partly because of border stories, partly because halflings are bite-sized and know it.

But they also pity them in a very halfling way: “No hearth, no proper songs, no grandmother telling you not to be an ass — well, of course they turned out that way.”

**Bless-your-heart version:**  
“They’ve had so little home-training.”

**What they mean:**  
“Do not open the round door. Get Arvoreen’s spear.”

---

### Drow

**Private take:**  
Absolutely not. Unless they are guests. In which case: absolutely not, but with tea.

Halflings may be less mythologically panicked than elves, but they are not idiots. If elves suddenly go pale and stop composing poetry, halflings notice. The drowic-language issue in court is a political bomb precisely because elves hear it as direct contact with a nightmare.

**Bless-your-heart version:**  
“Well, he is very striking.”

**What they mean:**  
“I have counted the exits, the knives, the children, and the silverware.”

---

### The Church / Paladins

**Private take:**  
Good people. Very good. Terrible dinner guests when they’ve found purpose.

Halflings respect the Triad. They especially like Pelor and can work with Heironeous. Pholtus makes them nervous because halflings prefer truth served with context and gravy.

When paladins start marching, halflings know the world has become dangerous in a very specific way. Not evil-dangerous. **Righteous-dangerous.** That is worse in some ways, because righteous people skip breakfast and call it discipline.

**Bless-your-heart version:**  
“Such conviction. I do hope someone kind is advising them.”

**What they mean:**  
“They are about to do something irreversible while sober.”

## Halflings About Their Own Rogue Elements

This is where it gets good.

Halflings are **quietly horrified** by their criminals because halfling criminals weaponize the very things halflings consider sacred: hospitality, kinship, trust, guest-right, shared meals, hearth access.

A human thug kicks in a door.  
A halfling rogue knows which auntie keeps the spare key, who owes whom for flour, and which child saw the reeve’s clerk sneaking out after midnight.

That is terrifying.

Guaco and Neltara clocking each other instantly as professionals is perfect: halfling criminality is subtle posture, exits, hands, placement, charm, and timing. “Game recognizes game” is already in the notes.

**Halfling public version:**  
“Oh, young Merric always was lively.”

**Halfling private version:**  
“Merric would steal the buttons off a corpse and sell the widow thread.”

**Bless-your-heart version for a rogue:**  
“He’s clever. We’re all praying he learns wisdom before someone teaches it to him with a brick.”

**What they mean:**  
“He is a menace, but he is our menace, so outsiders may form a line.”

## The Halfling Politeness Weapon

Halflings do not usually insult directly. They classify disaster through hospitality.

### Translation Table

**“Have you eaten?”**  
You are upset, drunk, dangerous, or all three.

**“Sit down a moment.”**  
You are making everyone nervous.

**“That’s a very bold choice.”**  
That is a terrible idea and I want witnesses.

**“Well, you know your own business best.”**  
You plainly do not.

**“She’s got a strong spirit.”**  
She is a walking court case.

**“He means well.”**  
He causes damage in a familiar pattern.

**“They’re from an old family.”**  
They are professionally unbearable.

**“Bless his heart.”**  
That man is too stupid to curse properly.

**“Bless her twice.”**  
She is dangerous and may be right.

**“We’ll send something over.”**  
You are in trouble, and the neighborhood has noticed.

**“No, no, stay for supper.”**  
You are not leaving until we know whether you are a threat.

## Sample Halfling Lines About Everyone

> “Humans are lovely, but you mustn’t let them gather in groups larger than six without snacks or they form governments.”

> “Elves remember everything except how to say what they mean.”

> “Dwarves are good folk. Hard as stone, twice as stubborn, and only slightly easier to season.”

> “Gnomes are proof that curiosity is a virtue right up until it finds your spice cabinet.”

> “Half-orcs? Feed them first. Most people improve after pie, and the ones who don’t are easier to spot.”

> “Paladins are dear souls. But when they stop smiling, hide the children and label the wine medicinal.”

> “A noble at table is like a goose in a pantry: loud, entitled, and somehow always standing in the way.”

> “Our own thieves? Oh, sweetheart, every family has mice. Some just learn to wear waistcoats.”

That last one is the whole vibe. Halflings are not naïve. They are **civilized enough to be horrified quietly** and **practical enough to keep the kettle on while judging everyone in the room.**
