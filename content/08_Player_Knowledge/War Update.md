# Where the War Stands

It can be difficult to see victories while you are still standing in the middle of the war.

Every road secured reveals another road beyond it. Every enemy broken leaves survivors. Every conspiracy uncovered exposes the next layer beneath it.

But make no mistake:

**The realm is in a dramatically stronger position because of what you have done.**

## The Great Forest

A few months ago, Kursk was expanding.

His forces controlled much of the Great Forest, the Grugach were scattered and poorly supplied, and his mines gave him the resources to keep building his strength.

You took those mines from him.

That victory is now paying dividends every single day.

The Grugach are being equipped with new weapons and fresh arrows. Armor is being produced in quantity. There is enough material flowing out of the captured mines that aid can now be sent onward to the High Elves as well.

The balance of power in the forest has reversed.

Kursk still holds his strongest enclaves, but he no longer rules the forest around them.

**The Grugach do.**

Woodland patrols now range through nearly the entire forest, avoiding only Kursk's hardened core holdings. The Qazaq, after Orin's efforts with them, have abandoned Kursk entirely.

Kursk is isolated, weakened, and increasingly cornered.

Much of the horror still roaming the forest is no longer even under his control. The Graylife he unleashed has begun spreading wild, attacking friend and foe alike.

He created a nightmare.

You have reduced him to hiding inside it.

## The Green Awakens

And something else has happened in the Great Forest.

Something that, not long ago, would have sounded impossible.

**The Grand Druid is awake.**

The Great Druids have assembled.

Those who were weakened, scattered, sleeping, wounded, or diminished have been restoring their strength. Ancient circles that had not gathered in living memory are gathering again.

And they have begun a Great Ritual.

The Graylife corruption spread through the forest on a scale that no ordinary spell could hope to cleanse. Grove by grove, stream by stream, grave by grave, it could take generations to undo what Kursk unleashed.

The druids intend something considerably more ambitious.

If the ritual succeeds, they believe they can invoke **Purification across an enormous portion of the Great Forest at once.**

Not merely destroy monsters.

Not merely burn corrupted groves.

**Heal the forest.**

Clean poisoned soil.

Break Graylife infestations.

Release places that have been spiritually strangled for years.

Give living things the chance to reclaim ground that has belonged to death.

There are no guarantees. The ritual is immense, the corruption is deep, and Graylife has had years to work itself into roots, corpses, streams, and forgotten places.

But the important thing is this:

For years, everyone asked how much more of the forest Kursk would corrupt.

Now the question is how much of it the druids can take back.

That change did not happen because Kursk grew careless.

It happened because his mines were taken.

Because the Grugach were armed.

Because his allies were peeled away.

Because his freedom of movement was broken.

Because enough pressure was taken off the surviving powers of the forest that they finally had the time, strength, and security to gather.

**The Grand Druid is awake because there is finally a forest left worth saving.**

And the Great Druids believe they may actually be able to save it.

# The Larger Picture

Consider where the realm stood before all of this began.

Kursk was expanding through the forest.

The Grugach were scattered and undersupplied.

The ancient powers of the forest were dormant, diminished, or divided.

Graylife spread with almost nothing capable of stopping it.

The Dominion could treat Gorundian air power as a curiosity.

Vogel's political machine still stood.

Blackvale belonged to the old order.

House Moffet could be isolated and destroyed.

Royonad faced a Dominion that still believed reconquest was simply a matter of sufficient pressure.

Bard's Gate was vulnerable to corruption, factionalism, and manipulation.

Enemies chose the pace.

Enemies chose the battlefield.

Enemies made plans.

Everyone else reacted.

That is no longer true.

Now Kursk fortifies the enclaves he has left because he cannot safely control the forest beyond them.

**The Grugach hunt through territory where they once hid.**

The Qazaq have abandoned him.

The mines that once supported his power now arm his enemies.

The Graylife he unleashed is increasingly beyond his control.

**The Grand Druid has awakened.**

**The Great Druids have assembled.**

And somewhere beneath the canopy, one of the greatest works of druidic magic in generations is already beginning, not to contain Kursk's corruption, but to **erase it from the land.**

The Dominion rushes weapons, research, and aerial projects forward because Gorundia now possesses capabilities it did not have before.

The _Second Draft_ flies, and an empire is changing doctrine because of it.

The Dominion struck the Lord Marshal's army from the sky with weapons Gorundia had never faced before.

The army survived.

House Moffet was hit hard enough that the enemy expected collapse.

The Moffets held.

Now Ravenster marches south.

Dame Venom took one of the slowest, most cumbersome, least practical pieces of military engineering anyone had the poor judgment to approve, dragged the **Badger** all the way to where it was needed...

...and proved that the damned thing works.

Blackvale's old government is gone. Dana rules. The King is no longer fighting for possession of the county; he is dismantling the last resistance carefully enough that there will still be a county when he finishes.

The Orenics have given ground, shed blood, and watched the Dominion throw men against their frontier.

Their main defenses still stand.

The reconquest has not happened.

And Bard's Gate?

Bard's Gate grows.

It trades.

It builds.

It takes in people fleeing the war.

It supplies armies.

Its government works increasingly well.

And its Lord Mayor is popular because, in an age of conspiracies, invasions, infernal bargains, corrupted forests, and flying fortresses, she has mastered the apparently legendary art of **giving a damn whether the city functions.**

You have not won the war.

But stop for a moment and look at what the war has become.

Your enemies are no longer executing their original plans.

**They are reacting to yours.**

Kursk is defending what remains.

The Dominion is building counters to weapons you restored.

Vogel's remnants are fighting to preserve scraps of a system that no longer exists.

The old corruption of Blackvale is being dismantled.

The Moffets survived long enough for help to arrive.

The Orenics have turned reconquest into attrition.

The druids are no longer hiding from the corruption of the Great Forest.

They are preparing to cleanse it.

The battlefield you stand on now is not the battlefield you inherited.

The balance of power has changed.

Armies have moved because of you.

Governments have fallen because of you.

Ancient alliances have shifted because of you.

An empire has changed its military priorities because of you.

And powers that had slept while the world grew darker are waking up because, for the first time in a very long while, **victory looks possible.**

You changed the world enough that other people have started believing they can change it too.

That may be the greatest victory you've won yet.

## Blackvale

The King is still in Blackvale, but he is not fighting to determine who controls the county.

**That question has already been answered.**

The Bordella regime is gone.

Dana Sylvanranth is Countess.

The principal towns, roads, markets, granaries, and population centers recognize the new government. The remaining resistance consists primarily of isolated estates, stubborn minor lords, old retainers, and remnants of the previous political machine.

The King's forces are not conquering Blackvale.

They are finishing the job.

And they are doing it carefully because Blackvale feeds the kingdom. Every farm burned and every village ruined would turn a military victory into a national problem.

The slow pace is not weakness.

It is what victory looks like when you intend to govern the people afterward.

## Ironmere and the Moffets

House Moffet has taken a beating.

Greyspur was ugly. Men died. Positions were lost.

But Greyspur did exactly what it needed to do.

It delayed the enemy.

It forced the surviving Iron Covenant into the open.

It bought House Moffet time to mobilize.

And most importantly, **the Moffets are still standing.**

The Iron Covenant remains one of the most dangerous military formations left from the old Vogel order, but it is not an endless army. Its warriors are expensive, difficult to replace, and every loss matters.

Now Duke Glandyr Ravenster is moving south.

The Iron Covenant began this offensive hoping to crush an isolated barony before anyone could intervene.

Instead, they now face Moffet defenders in front of them and the growing weight of Ravenster approaching the battlefield.

They have not destroyed House Moffet.

They may shortly discover they stayed too long trying.

## The Southern War

The Dominion intended its offensive against the Orenics to be a reconquest.

Break the passes.

Cut the roads.

Separate the Orenic territories.

Force Royonad back into Dominion control.

That has not happened.

The Orenics have lost ground around the edges, abandoned exposed positions, and paid heavily for every mile.

But their main defensive network remains intact.

Karsk Pass holds.

The Veyr defenses hold.

The road system connecting the heart of Royonad remains open.

The Dominion has been forced into exactly the kind of grinding border war it hoped to avoid.

The Orenics are not collapsing.

They are making the Dominion fight for every hill, bridge, road, and bloody stone.

The invasion continues.

The reconquest has failed.

## The War in the Sky

The Dominion's aerial attack was horrifying.

It was also revealing.

They brought cloud fortresses, magical interdiction weapons, aerial assault troops, bombardment weapons, and creatures from Hell itself.

They struck the Lord Marshal's army hard.

And they failed to destroy it.

Beringer survived.

The command structure survived.

The army remained intact.

The wounded were saved.

Their infernal attackers were defeated.

And allied reinforcements arrived.

The Dominion proved that it can strike from the sky.

It did **not** prove that it controls the sky.

And there is another detail worth remembering.

Before the restoration of the _Second Draft_, many Dominion aerial programs were slow-moving experiments.

Now they are receiving priority.

New anti-flight weapons.

New aerial vessels.

New magical research.

New biological and arcane projects.

Why?

Because Gorundia once had almost nothing capable of challenging them above the battlefield.

Then you put a warship back into the sky.

**The Dominion is redesigning part of its military because of something you accomplished.**

That is what strategic impact looks like.

## The Badger

And then there is the **Badger**.

Dame Venom got it where it was supposed to go.

Eventually.

Very, very eventually.

The entire project was exactly what everyone feared it would be:

Slow.

Awkward.

Impractical.

A logistical nuisance.

Probably offensive to several established schools of military engineering.

And once it finally arrived?

**Stupidly effective.**

The Badger did not need elegance.

It did not need speed.

It needed to get into position and do the thing it had been built to do.

It did.

There is something deeply satisfying about watching an enemy spend days observing an enormous crawling engineering mistake and then discovering that the mistake was designed specifically to ruin their fortifications.

Dame Venom's operation proved that Gorundia can now move extremely heavy specialized equipment into places that previously would have required either a siege train, a heroic assault, or accepting that the position simply could not be cracked.

Nobody is going to call the Badger graceful.

They are going to ask where the next one is.

## Bard's Gate

And behind all these fronts stands Bard's Gate.

Not burning.

Not collapsing.

Not tearing itself apart.

Booming.

Trade is growing.

The wards are functioning.

The city government is increasingly trusted.

Communities once treated as outsiders are being brought into civic life.

Merchants move goods through the city because the rules increasingly mean something.

Refugees can arrive somewhere capable of absorbing them.

Armies can draw supplies from a functioning commercial center.

And the Lord Mayor is both popular and genuinely interested in making the city work.

In a kingdom fighting wars on several fronts, Bard's Gate has become something extraordinarily valuable:

**a stable place.**

That did not happen by accident.

## The Larger Picture

Consider where the realm stood before all of this began.

Kursk was expanding through the forest.

The Grugach were scattered.

The Dominion could treat Gorundian air power as a curiosity.

Vogel's political machine still stood.

Blackvale was controlled by the old order.

Bard's Gate was vulnerable to corruption, factionalism, and manipulation.

Enemies chose the pace.

Enemies chose the battlefield.

Enemies made plans.

Everyone else reacted.

That is no longer true.

Now Kursk fortifies what remains because he cannot safely leave it.

The Dominion rushes weapons into production because Gorundia has capabilities it did not possess before.

The remnants of Vogel's forces fight to preserve fragments of what once belonged to them.

Blackvale is being rebuilt under new leadership.

The Grugach hunt through forests where they once hid.

Ravenster marches south because the Moffets held long enough for help to matter.

Dame Venom has demonstrated that even Gorundia's most ridiculous engineering projects can become battlefield facts if somebody competent gets them where they need to go.

Bard's Gate grows richer while armies march.

And Gorundia's enemies are increasingly making plans based around one unpleasant question:

**What are they going to do next?**

You have not won the war.

There are still armies in the field, monsters in the forest, traitors in hiding, and very dangerous people working very hard to undo everything you have accomplished.

But the battlefield you stand on now is not the battlefield you inherited.

**You changed it.**

And everyone else is having to live with the consequences.
