# The Black Journals

> “Theron never needed to know a thing was true before preparing for what happened if it was.”
>
> — [[Sorek Redmarch]]

The **Black Journals** were the private working books of [[Theron Sylvanranth]], compiled across decades of royal service, clandestine warfare, political investigation, and carefully delayed vengeance.

They were not diaries.

Theron recorded little about his feelings and almost nothing resembling confession. The Journals instead contain names, debts, relationships, suspicions, routes, architectural weaknesses, coded payments, compromised officers, likely betrayals, and contingencies for events that had not yet occurred.

Some entries describe crimes already committed.

Others describe crimes Theron believed someone might eventually attempt.

A few appear to have been written solely because Theron disliked leaving a possibility unprepared for.

## Appearance

Each Journal is bound in plain black leather.

They bear:

- no title
- no numbering
- no Sylvanranth crest
- no royal seal
- no visible indication of subject or chronology

Theron considered labels an unnecessary kindness to thieves.

The contents are written in his own compressed hand, using overlapping ciphers, personal abbreviations, elven shorthand, false names, and references to documents stored elsewhere.

Most entries are classified under one of three headings:

> **KNOWN**

> **PROBABLE**

> **WORTH PREPARING FOR**

The third category is the largest.

## The Sylvanranth Files

The Black Journals are only the heart of a much larger archive commonly called the **Sylvanranth Files**.

The Files include:

- copied financial ledgers
- intercepted correspondence
- interrogation records
- architectural surveys
- maps of tunnels and forgotten roads
- genealogies and disputed bloodlines
- lists of aliases
- border-crossing records
- informant reports
- temple testimony
- sealed warrants
- samples of forged hands and seals
- records of disappearances considered too convenient to be accidental

The Files are the evidence.

The Black Journals are Theron’s interpretation of that evidence.

This distinction matters. Theron could be mistaken. His conclusions were not treated as proof merely because he wrote them.

They were, however, considered excellent reasons to begin watching someone.

## The Caervane Doctrine

The system grew from [[The Caervane Reckoning]], Theron’s response to the assassination of [[Rowena Caervane]].

Theron did not immediately slaughter the hired blades responsible.

He allowed several of them to believe they had escaped.

He followed them.

The killers led him to smugglers. The smugglers led him to couriers. The couriers exposed political facilitators. The facilitators revealed noble sponsors and foreign handlers operating on both sides of the border.

Theron spent years identifying and destroying every reachable part of the conspiracy.

Its central lesson shaped every investigation he conducted afterward:

> Do not kill the assassin until you know who paid him, who carried the message, who opened the gate, who falsified the watch roll, and where each of them intends to run.

Sorek later reduced the principle to something soldiers could remember:

> “A frightened rat flees. A comfortable rat leads you to the nest.”

## The Long Net

The investigative method developed from the Caervane Reckoning became known within Theron’s circle as **the Long Net**.

Under the Long Net, the discovery of a conspirator does not necessarily result in an arrest.

Instead, investigators quietly determine:

- who the suspect contacts
- where their money originates
- which doors open for them
- which guards look away
- who benefits from their success
- who shelters them after failure
- what route they believe remains secret
- what innocent people must be removed before the strike

Immediate justice is deliberately postponed in favor of complete dismantlement.

This often made the Crown appear inactive.

That appearance was useful.

While conspirators congratulated themselves on royal weakness, the Crown duplicated their records, mapped their refuges, compromised their couriers, sealed alternate exits, replaced escape crews, and positioned trusted agents around everyone likely to run.

The purpose was not merely to catch the obvious traitor.

The purpose was to ensure that when the trap closed, **as few rats as possible escaped**.

## Why the Crown Appeared Passive

In later accounts, the king and his senior officers are sometimes criticized for failing to act while conspiracies gathered strength.

The Black Journals suggest the opposite.

The Crown was waiting for:

- hidden sponsors to reveal themselves
- frightened agents to seek protection
- stolen money to reach its destination
- couriers to lead investigators across borders
- compromised officials to expose their replacements
- escape routes to fill with fugitives
- innocents to be moved clear of the coming violence

The king’s restraint was not indecision.

It was part of the trap.

By the time open action began, many of the strikes had already been planned. Warrants were sealed. Roads were watched. Boats had new crews. Safe houses were compromised. Tunnel exits had been mapped and quietly blocked.

The public saw the reckoning begin in a single night.

Theron had been building it for years.

## Caervane Work

Among Sorek and the older intelligence hands, an unresolved Long Net investigation is called **Caervane work**.

> “No arrests yet. It’s Caervane work.”

The phrase means that the suspect is being watched, their network is being mapped, and silence should not be mistaken for mercy.

## The Black Seal Detail

The [[Black Seal Detail]] became one of the principal organizations responsible for preparing Long Net operations.

Under [[Corven Rill]], called **the Ash Clerk**, Black Seal teams:

- secure compromised locations
- map tunnels and concealed passages
- preserve evidence
- identify unknown exits
- locate hidden records
- separate witnesses from conspirators
- determine which infrastructure can be safely reused
- prepare sites for later royal occupation or investigation

The explorers sent into forgotten tunnels were not simply searching for monsters or smugglers.

They recorded where every passage began, where it ended, who had altered it, and which powerful people had reason to know it existed.

When the reckoning came, fugitives repeatedly discovered that their secret escape routes had been part of the Crown’s plan for months.

# The Closing of the Long Net

A Long Net operation does not usually resemble a battle.

It resembles a series of doors closing.

Targets are isolated and removed in rapid succession before the surviving members of the network understand that the operation has begun.

Some are arrested.

Some are questioned.

Some are offered a final opportunity to cooperate.

Some are killed before they can reach a weapon.

Some are deliberately allowed to run, because even during the closing of the net, one frightened rat may still lead investigators to another nest.

The strikes are often carried out by small teams operating simultaneously across several jurisdictions.

By sunrise, a conspiracy that had spent years growing may simply cease to exist.

# Sample Strikes

## The Toll House

The customs master hears horses outside before dawn.

He does not look through the shutters.

He goes directly to the stove, opens a concealed compartment beneath the stones, and throws his private ledger into the fire.

By the time the royal officer enters, half the pages have curled into ash.

The officer watches them burn.

Then he places an identical ledger on the desk.

Same binding.

Same coded figures.

Same stain from spilled wine on the lower corner.

> “That was the decoy.”

Behind him, two soldiers begin removing the floorboards.

The customs master looks toward the rear door.

A Black Seal agent is already standing there.

## Beneath the Old City

Four conspirators flee through an abandoned drainage passage beneath the city.

They know the route well.

It was built for smugglers two generations ago and does not appear on any current civic map.

At the first junction they find the western passage freshly bricked.

At the second, the iron grate has been chained from the far side.

They take the final route, splashing through black water toward the old tannery outlet.

Sorek is waiting at the far end.

He is sitting on a barrel with his sword laid across his knees.

A lantern burns beside him.

> “Always wondered where this came out.”

Behind the fugitives, boots enter the tunnel.

Sorek rises slowly.

> “You can surrender to me, or explain yourselves to the lads behind you.”

He glances past them.

> “They’ve been standing in sewage for an hour. I wouldn’t count on patience.”

## The River Escape

A financier reaches the quay before sunrise.

His boat is prepared exactly as promised.

The sail is furled. Supplies are aboard. Four hired river-men are waiting at the oars.

They cast off before the city bells begin.

Only when the current carries them beyond the nearest watchtower does the financier notice that none of the men are wearing the blue cords used by his regular crew.

The helmsman removes a folded document from inside his coat.

The royal seal is already broken.

> “You are named in a warrant for conspiracy, murder, bribery, and unlawful dealings with a foreign power.”

The financier looks toward the riverbank.

Too far.

He looks at the dark water.

The helmsman shrugs.

> “You can swim, if the paperwork offends you.”

## The Country Manor

Lord Edraven is eating breakfast when an arrow enters through the open window.

It strikes the table between his hand and his wine cup.

Pinned beneath the arrow is a copied page from one of his coded letters.

One sentence has been circled in red ink:

> *The northern road remains unwatched.*

Outside, mounted soldiers are already sealing the northern road.

Edraven stands quickly and turns toward the servant passage.

His maid closes the hidden panel from the other side.

The lock clicks.

She has served in his household for eleven years.

Her first report to Theron was written ten years and nine months ago.

A voice speaks from the doorway.

> “Sit down, my lord.”

Edraven looks at the letter.

Then at the maid.

Then at the soldiers entering his courtyard.

He sits.

## The Chapel

The priest finishes the morning prayer.

The congregation rises and begins filing toward the doors.

Three senior members of the Triad remain seated in the rear pew.

The priest recognizes each of them.

A Pelorite healer.

A knight of Heironeous.

A Pholtan Truthkeeper.

No accusation is spoken before the congregation.

No chains are displayed.

The senior cleric approaches the altar and inclines his head.

> “Your testimony has been requested.”

The priest glances toward the vestry door.

The Pholtan is already standing beside it.

The knight has moved between the priest and the public entrance.

The Pelorite watches him with something dangerously close to pity.

That is when the priest understands.

This is not a request.

## The Last Safe House

The courier reaches the Eastgate safe house after midnight.

His horse is nearly dead beneath him.

The city behind him is full of bells, soldiers, and rumors of arrests.

He gives the coded knock.

No one answers.

He enters through the concealed cellar door and climbs into the darkened room above.

The safe house is empty.

No food.

No weapons.

No waiting guides.

Only a black-bound book lying open on the table.

A single candle burns beside it.

The courier approaches.

His name is written at the top of the page.

Beneath it are three entries:

> **KNOWN:** Courier operating between the western conspirators and their city agents.

> **PROBABLE:** Will seek shelter at the Eastgate house if the primary network is compromised.

> **WORTH PREPARING FOR:** Allow him to reach the house. He may carry the final list.

The courier hears the cellar door close behind him.

A key turns in the lock.

## The Wrong Target

A strike team enters a merchant’s home expecting to find a conspirator.

Instead, they find the merchant’s wife, two children, and an old servant.

The merchant fled hours earlier.

One of the younger agents curses and turns toward the family.

The team leader stops him.

The orders are explicit.

> **The family is not involved. Remove them before searching the house. No threats. No coercion. No convenient assumptions.**

The family is escorted to a guarded inn under royal protection.

The house is searched.

A hidden room is found behind the counting office.

Inside are foreign coin, copied military schedules, and a list of names.

At the bottom of the list is the name of the agent who wanted to threaten the family.

He is arrested before the search is finished.

Theron prepared for treachery inside his own teams as carefully as he prepared for treachery outside them.

## The Man Who Opens the Door

The soldiers arrive at the home of a minor royal clerk.

The clerk is already awake.

He is sitting at his kitchen table with several bundles of documents tied and arranged before him.

His wife and children are gone.

He sent them to her sister’s farm three days ago.

When the officer presents the warrant, the clerk nods.

> “I wondered how long it would take.”

He pushes the first bundle forward.

> “Those are the payments.”

The second.

> “Those are the names I knew.”

The third.

> “Those are the names I only suspected.”

The officer does not touch them.

> “Why keep them?”

The clerk looks toward the shuttered window.

> “Because I knew what I was doing.”

A long silence follows.

Then:

> “And because I hoped someone better than me was keeping a book.”

He is taken alive.

His testimony breaks three further cells.

# Use in Play

The closing of the Long Net can be run as a series of short linked scenarios rather than one large battle.

Each strike should have a different purpose:

- capture a financier before he destroys evidence
- intercept a courier and follow him to another cell
- extract an innocent witness
- seize a hidden archive
- prevent a conspirator from fleeing across the border
- expose a compromised royal officer
- assassinate a target too dangerous to arrest
- offer a frightened participant one final chance to cooperate

Different player characters or allied teams may handle separate strikes occurring during the same night.

Intercutting between them emphasizes the scale of the preparation.

The apparent royal inaction of the previous years is revealed as deliberate positioning. Every hidden door, bribed guard, false identity, and emergency refuge has already been considered.

The conspirators believed they were escaping through weaknesses in the kingdom.

They were moving along routes Theron had chosen for them.

# Sorek’s Account

> “People said the King waited too long.
>
> They were wrong.
>
> He waited exactly long enough.
>
> By the time the first door came down, the roads were watched, the boats were crewed, and every hole the bastards thought they could crawl into had someone waiting at the other end.
>
> Theron called it preparation.
>
> The King called it restraint.
>
> The men caught inside it called it something else.
>
> By sunrise, the rats finally understood the walls had been built around them.”

## To Be Opened Upon Confirmation of My Death

Do not begin with vengeance.

My death will create movement. Men who feared discovery will contact one another. Those who served me falsely will grow careless. Those who served me faithfully will be watched.

Let them move.

The black volumes contain suspicion, not judgment. Confirm what can be confirmed. Discard what proves false. No person named within is to be condemned solely because I distrusted them. Distrust was my profession, not a verdict.

Secure the archives before securing my remains.

Inform the King plainly. Do not soften the manner of my death for his comfort, and do not permit him to blame himself merely because grief offers fewer complications than strategy.

Sorek will know which doors to close.

Give him the third key.

Dana is not to inherit my enemies.

She has carried enough of my decisions without being asked. Tell her that whatever else may be said of me, I never mistook her for a weapon.

There will be those who believe my death has freed them.

For a little while, allow them that comfort.

Then close the net.

— Theron Sylvanranth
