# The Ones Beneath the Boughs
![[A quiet one.png]]

Among the grugach, they are not spoken of by a single name.

That would be too much like claiming to understand them.

Some call them **the Moss-Born**.

Some call them **the Deep Quiet**.

Some call them **Those-Who-Were-Here-Before**.

Children hear rougher names: **baby-eaters**, **bark-ogres**, **the long-armed ones in the fog**. These names are used by mothers who want children home before dusk and by young hunters pretending they are not afraid.

The elders use none of these names lightly.

They say the deep forest has people of its own.

Not elves.

Not beasts.

Not spirits.

Something older than the clans, older than the names carved after the fall, older perhaps than Nilanthalas itself.

## What Jinwing Was Taught

Jinwing was not taught that they are enemies.

She was not taught that they are allies.

She was taught to leave three things alone:

A deer skull packed with fresh moss.

A circle of stones wrapped in fern.

A place where birds stop singing all at once.

The druids spoke of them carefully, as if the words themselves might carry scent.

They said:

> “We learn the ways of the forest.  
> They are born inside them.”

That was the difference.

A druid studies root, moon, blood, rain, rot, hunger, and renewal. A druid listens until the forest answers.

The Deep Quiet do not ask.

The forest has already answered them.

## Outside the Order

They do not belong to the druid order.

They keep no grove.

They swear no oath to archdruid, clan, crown, or dead king.

They do not attend moots. They do not mark seasons with songs. They do not debate the right shape of sacrifice or whether a burned grove should be mourned for seven years or seventy.

This unsettles the grugach more than they admit.

Because every grugach, however wild, still belongs to something: clan, bloodline, rite, wound, vengeance, memory.

The Deep Quiet seem to belong only to the forest.

Or worse:

The forest belongs to them in ways no elf would dare say aloud.

## The Purity of Their Magic

Grugach magic has forms.

Songs.

Offerings.

Painted skin.

Blood on bark.

Words to Ehlonna, Corellon, Sehanine, or older names whispered sideways.

The Deep Quiet do not cast as elves cast.

No gestures are seen.

No prayers are heard.

No circle is drawn.

A wounded animal calms beneath their hand.

Fog gathers where they stand.

Roots seem to know their weight.

Birds warn them without panic.

A rotten log gives way under an enemy’s foot at exactly the wrong moment.

To the young, this seems like superstition.

To druids, it is worse.

It looks like the forest cooperating without being asked.

That is why some grugach druids speak of them with reverence.

And some with resentment.

## What They Look Like

Descriptions disagree.

Too tall for elves.

Too quiet for ogres.

Too shaped for beasts.

Huge shoulders. Long arms. Heavy hair or moss or both. Faces glimpsed through fog and leaves. Eyes that catch light like animals, but hold it too long.

More than one grugach hunter has mistaken one for a bugbear at a distance.

More than one bugbear has made the same mistake and lived just long enough to regret it.

They wear little that was not grown, found, killed, shed, or shaped by hand. Bark. Hide. Mud. Bone. Root-fiber. Stone. Horn.

No iron.

No bronze.

No silver.

No need.

## Grugach Rules Concerning Them

These are not laws.

Laws are for people who intend to argue.

These are things one simply does.

- Do not follow one.    
- Do not mock one.    
- Do not shoot unless you are already dead anyway.    
- Do not take food left on a mossy stone.    
- Do not step through fern-bound stones.    
- Do not bring fire into a silent hollow.    
- If one watches you from the trees, speak truth or say nothing.    
- If one returns a lost child, thank the forest, not the watcher.    
- If one takes a body, do not demand it back.    

The last rule is the one outsiders dislike most.

The grugach do not explain it well.

## The Baby-Eater Tale

Grugach children are told that the Deep Quiet eat babies.

This is probably not true.

Probably.

What is true is that lost children sometimes return changed.

Quiet.

Unfrightened.

With mud on their feet and leaves in their hair.

Sometimes they speak of a great hairy shadow that carried them through the dark and fed them berries from its palm.

Sometimes they remember nothing.

Sometimes they remember songs no grugach taught them.

And sometimes they do not return at all.

The elders say those children died.

The old women say nothing.

The druids look toward the deep trees and change the subject.

## How the Grugach Feel About Them

The grugach are not easily humbled.

They survived the fall of Nilanthalas.

They fought goblins, dragons, curses, exile, hunger, and centuries of being told they were relics of a broken forest.

They use steel when they must.

They remember kings.

They still bleed for lost banners.

And then there are the Deep Quiet, who appear to have skipped the entire miserable business.

No crowns.

No ruins.

No lost throne.

No old shame.

No debate over what the forest was.

Only what it is.

That makes them hard to hate.

And very easy to fear.

## A Grugach Elder’s Warning

> “You think because we live wild, we are close to the first green.  
> We are not.  
> We are elves. We remember too much. We carry knives, names, songs, dead kings, old insults.
> 
> The Moss-Born carry none of that.
> 
> Do not mistake that for innocence.  
> A wolf is innocent. A flood is innocent. Rot is innocent.  
> Innocent things still kill.”

## What Jinwing Might Believe

Jinwing may have been taught that they are a kind of lesson the forest keeps hidden.

Not better than druids.

Not wiser than elves.

Just less separated.

Druids cross the distance between thought and leaf.

The Deep Quiet never had that distance.

That is the part that should sit under her skin.

Because if their magic is pure, then it is also dangerous. Pure things do not compromise. Pure fire burns. Pure water drowns. Pure forest does not care who had the better claim, the older grief, or the prettier song.

It grows over everyone eventually.
