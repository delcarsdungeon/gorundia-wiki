# Inventory of Seized Effects

## Wilhelm Vogel, Former Duke of Gravendar

### Crown-Herald Record, Marshal’s Custody Copy

**Recorded at Castle Blackvale, 1231 OR, under Crown warrant and the authority of the Marshal’s Office, following the lawful death of Wilhelm Vogel in trial by combat.**

Present and witnessing:

- **Dame Kathreine Kendal**, acting under authority of the Marshal’s Office    
- **Sir Edmar Rusk**, High Provost of the Marshal’s Court    
- **Master Herald Othran Vel**, Crown Herald attached to the royal household    
- Two sworn clerks of the Seals    
- Four men-at-arms of the Crown, named in the custody ledger    

Let it be entered plainly: the arms and armor used by Wilhelm Vogel in the trial were **issued by the Crown**, inspected before the duel, and found to be non-magical. The enchanted arms listed herein were recovered afterward from Wilhelm Vogel’s private armory, chambers, reliquary cabinet, and sealed stores.

Dame Kendal asked that this be stated clearly, since men have a talent for turning facts into rumors and rumors into politics. History, being lazy, usually lets them.

## I. Gear Used in the Trial

### Crown-Issued Trial Harness

The harness worn by Wilhelm Vogel during the trial by combat was plain Crown issue: well-made, clean, and free of magical enhancement. It bore no Vogel arms, no private sigils, and no concealed enchantments.

The harness was inspected before and after the trial. Damage to the armor is consistent with the combat witnessed by the court.

**Disposition:** Returned to Crown evidence custody. Not entered as spoil.

### Crown-Issued Trial Sword

The sword issued to Wilhelm Vogel for the trial was likewise plain, non-magical, and inspected by Heralds and officers of the Marshal’s Court. It was not drawn from the Vogel armory and bears no inheritance claim.

**Disposition:** Returned to Crown evidence custody. Not entered as spoil.

## II. Enchanted Arms and Armor Seized from Wilhelm Vogel’s Private Armory

### The Black-and-Silver Field Harness of House Vogel

Recovered from the private armory adjoining Wilhelm Vogel’s solar.

A complete field harness of blackened steel, chased in silver and edged in restrained gold. The armor bears the old Vogel device across the breast, worked into the steel rather than painted. The workmanship is severe and martial, not courtly. It is handsome in the way a guillotine is handsome: expensive, clean, and built because someone had decided other people were the problem.

The harness includes breastplate, backplate, pauldrons, vambraces, gauntlets, cuisses, greaves, sabatons, gorget, and close helm. The interior is lined in dark leather, carefully maintained. The enchantment marks are laid into the inner plates and reinforced at the joints, showing long-term battlefield use rather than decorative wizardry.

Master Herald Vel identified this as Wilhelm’s principal war harness. Dame Kendal agreed and noted that it had not been present at the duel site.

**Disposition:** Seized under Crown warrant. Held pending ruling on ducal property, treasonous goods, and claims of inheritance.

> Dame Kendal’s note:  
> “It is not unlawful armor. It merely served an unlawful man.”

#### Game Callout: Black-and-Silver Field Harness

**GURPS 4e legal item**

- **Fine full plate harness**    
- **Ornate +2**    
- **Fortify +3**    
- **Lighten 50%**    
- **Deflect +2**

- Weight is reduced by **50%** after armor weight is totaled.    
- Deflect +2 gives a strong magical defensive bonus if using the standard enchantment as written in your campaign.    

Suggested value:

- Mundane fine ornate full harness: already noble-tier expensive    
- Enchantments make it major aristocratic war gear    

**Campaign value:** **500 gold commons**  
That is high, but appropriate for a duke who was rich, paranoid, magically backed, and deeply committed to remaining alive. Unfortunately for him, rules lawyers invented trial gear.

### Wilhelm Vogel’s Ducal War Sword

Recovered from the same private armory, displayed horizontally above the armor rack.

A long knightly sword with a black leather grip, silvered guard, and garnet-set pommel. The blade is bright, well-kept, and etched along the fuller with the words:

> **By Blood, By Oath, By Right**

The sword is not Galmyr Vogel’s ancestral blade. This is Wilhelm’s own ducal weapon: newer, prouder, colder. The balance is excellent, the edge unnaturally keen, and the enchantments are laid with professional restraint. No infernal marks were found by ordinary inspection, though Dame Kendal recommended a clerical review before reassignment.

Master Herald Vel described it as “a proper lord’s war blade.” Sir Edmar Rusk described it as “evidence with a grip.”

Both statements are true, which is how the law becomes annoying.

**Disposition:** Seized under Crown warrant. Held in sealed armory custody.

## III. Items Removed from Wilhelm Vogel’s Body or Surrendered Before Trial

### Ducal Signet Ring of Gravendar

The ring responded to Wilhelm Vogel in the expected fashion. When willed, it displayed the Vogel charge: a heraldic **manticore**, black-bodied and severe, with batlike wings half-spread and a scorpion tail arched above its back.

Master Herald Vel confirmed the display as authentic to the ducal line of Gravendar.

Dame Kathreine Kendal requested that the ring be sealed and not presented to Sir Beringer Amadis except under formal Crown and Heraldic witness.

Not because his blood is in doubt.

It is not.

Because if the ring answers him, the room changes.

He ceases to be merely the man who killed Wilhelm Vogel. He becomes the man the Vogel signet recognizes.

That distinction is the sort of thing that makes lawyers fertile and soldiers tired.

> Dame Kendal’s note:  
> “Sir Beringer’s blood is known. The question is not whether the ring may answer him. The question is what every lord in the room will claim that answer means.”

Sir Edmar Rusk added, dryly:

> “Then let us make certain they all hear the same answer.”

## VII. The Broken Sword of Galmyr Vogel

Recovered from the western reliquary cabinet in Wilhelm Vogel’s private solar.

This item was not displayed. It was not stored with the armory. It was not honored as an ancestral weapon.

It was wrapped in black cloth, bound with silver wire, and placed beneath a cracked wooden icon bearing old Vogel arms. The cabinet was locked. Dust on the cloth suggests it had not been touched recently.

When the sword was uncovered, Dame Kathreine Kendal requested that the junior clerks leave the room.

Master Herald Vel objected, citing completeness of record.

Sir Edmar Rusk looked at him.

The clerks left.

### Description

A knightly sword of older make, simpler than Wilhelm’s ducal blade but finer in the hand. The guard is plain darkened steel. The grip has been rewrapped more than once. The pommel bears an old Vogel mark, worn nearly smooth by long use.

The blade is broken across the lower third.

The break is ugly.

It does not look cut. It looks forced. Snapped, twisted, or broken under pressure by strength or magic. The two pieces were laid together but not joined.

Along the fuller, partly interrupted by the break, are the words:

> **Stand Until Witnessed**

Master Herald Vel identified the sword as almost certainly the blade of **Galmyr Vogel**, father of Wilhelm Vogel and blood-father of Sir Beringer Amadis.

At this identification, Dame Kendal became visibly unsettled.

Her words are entered with her permission:

> “He did not carry it. He did not honor it. He did not destroy it. He hid it where only he could keep hating it.”

No further comment was made for some time.

There are moments when silence is the only civilized thing left in a room.

### Condition and Custody

The sword is broken in two pieces. The enchantments are present but dormant, disrupted, or wounded. It should not be wielded, sold, melted, reforged casually, or placed among common seized arms.

Dame Kendal formally recommended it be marked:

> **Ancestral. Blood-Significant. Contested. Not Spoil.**

Master Herald Vel concurred.

Sir Edmar Rusk added, informally:

> “If anyone steals it, hang them before they explain.”

Not entered as formal code. Spirit accepted.

**Disposition:** Sealed separately under Crown-Herald custody. To be released only by royal order, Heraldic ruling, or bloodline adjudication.

# X. Final Custody Recommendation

Master Herald Vel recommends the following:

1. The **ducal signet**, **chain of office**, and **Galmyr Vogel’s broken sword** remain under Heraldic seal.    
2. Wilhelm’s **enchanted armor and ducal war sword** remain in Crown custody until the legal status of Gravendar succession is settled.    
3. The **coin, jewels, alchemical stores, and reserve weapons** may be held against debts, fines, reparations, or Crown forfeiture.    
4. The **broken sword of Galmyr Vogel** must not be classified as loot.    

Dame Kendal added a final note in her own hand:

> “Some things taken from Wilhelm Vogel are property. Some are evidence. One is neither. The broken sword should be treated as a witness.”

Master Herald Vel allowed the note to stand.

Sir Edmar Rusk added beneath it:

> “Agreed.”

A rare day: everyone in the room understood the assignment, and no gnome had to explain it with a ruler.
