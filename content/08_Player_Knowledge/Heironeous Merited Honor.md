“Pride is a sin. Honor is not. Do not confuse them simply because both may cause a man to stand straight.”

“A knight who has held his ground, kept his oath, defended the helpless, and served faithfully has earned something by those deeds. We call it **Merited Honor**. He may know that he possesses it. He may defend it against slander. He may expect others to acknowledge what he has rightly earned. There is no holiness in pretending a brave man is a coward, or an accomplished captain a fool.”

“Indeed, to deny your own worthy deeds merely so that others may praise your humility is vanity wearing a monk’s robe.”

“But understand the boundary.”

“Merited Honor remembers: _I have done worthy things._”

“Pride declares: _Therefore I am a worthy man whatever I do._”

“Merited Honor says: _Men trust my judgment because I have served them well. I must therefore judge carefully._”

“Pride says: _Men trust my judgment, therefore those who question me are insolent._”

“Merited Honor makes reputation into obligation. Pride makes reputation into privilege.”

“The greater your victories, the heavier this burden becomes. A young knight disgraces himself. A celebrated knight may disgrace his order, his lord, and every man who followed his banner.”

“So take satisfaction in victories honestly won. Wear honors honestly bestowed. Speak plainly of deeds actually done. Heironeous has no need of warriors who lie about their own strength.”

“But remember this: **yesterday’s courage purchases no indulgence for tomorrow’s cowardice.**”

“Your honor is merited because you earned it.”

“And each morning, you begin earning it again.”
