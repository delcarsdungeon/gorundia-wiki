# Blackberry

> *“Bard’s Gate sells you something you didn’t know you wanted. Blackberry sells you what you were going to need anyway.”*
>
> — old road saying

## Overview

**Blackberry** is a prosperous walled market town east of [[Bard's Gate]], standing on the principal road between the Free City and the interior of Gorundia.

It is neither large enough nor rich enough to compete with Bard’s Gate, and its people generally consider that a virtue.

Blackberry serves the farms, orchards, mills, villages, and smallholdings of the surrounding countryside. Grain, fruit, cheese, livestock, beer, cider, preserves, timber, and ordinary manufactured goods pass through its gates in considerable quantities.

The town proper is predominantly human.

The countryside around it is another matter.

Halfling families have held farms and smallholdings around Blackberry for generations, and their orchards, gardens, dairies, berry fields, and mixed farms form a substantial part of the local agricultural economy.

On market days, the distinction between "town" and "country" becomes considerably less obvious.

---

## The Town

Blackberry is enclosed by a substantial stone wall built over the lines of older fortifications.

The town grew before anyone made a serious attempt to plan it.

This remains evident.

The main road passes through Blackberry from west to east, but the streets around it wander according to considerably older arguments involving property lines, wells, workshops, vanished walls, and people whose descendants still insist their grandfather was right.

The center of town is the **Market Square**, where trade, proclamations, public meetings, and most civic business take place.

Nearby stand the Town Hall, several of Blackberry’s better inns, the weighing house, temples and shrines, and the offices used by the town government.

Closer to the gates are warehouses, cart yards, stables, smithies, and businesses serving road traffic.

Farther from the main road, Blackberry quickly becomes quieter and more domestic.

---

## Beyond the Walls

Blackberry is surrounded by intensely cultivated countryside.

There is no sharp line where the town ends and the farming country begins.

Outside the walls lie:

- orchards and berry fields
- grain fields and pasture
- dairies and poultry farms
- vegetable gardens and smallholdings
- windmills and watermills
- roadside inns
- scattered farmsteads
- small halfling communities
- old hedgerows marking properties older than many noble houses

The farms nearest Blackberry are generally prosperous.

Many are relatively small by noble-estate standards but are worked heavily and efficiently, particularly those belonging to halfling families.

A Blackberry farmer may own less land than a tenant on a great estate and somehow coax twice as much food out of it.

Nobody has yet determined whether this is agriculture or spite.

---

# Government

## Mayor Cedric Bale

Until the present uncertainty, Blackberry was governed by **Mayor Cedric Bale**.

Bale had held considerable influence in the town for years and was deeply associated with its established political order.

His office dealt with the Crown, neighboring authorities, merchants, landholders, and Bard’s Gate on behalf of Blackberry.

Officially, he was mayor.

Depending upon which resident one asks, he was also the man who knew every permit, contract, Watch appointment, debt, favor, and useful cousin in town.

Bale had defenders.

He also had an impressive number of people who lowered their voices before discussing him.

### Current Status

**Gone.**

Bale departed Blackberry during the recent upheaval surrounding the arrival of a large group from Grevandar.

No orderly resignation has reached Bard’s Gate.

No clear transfer of authority is known.

Several members of his immediate circle apparently left with him.

---

## Halden Bale

**Halden Bale**, Cedric’s younger brother, served for years as Blackberry’s **Master of Works**.

His office oversaw repairs to:

- walls
- gates
- roads
- wells
- drains
- civic buildings

Critics of the Bale administration frequently observed that Blackberry possessed a remarkable number of civic projects requiring repeated repair.

Halden was seen leaving town with his brother.

---

## Mara Venn

**Mara Venn, née Bale**, Cedric’s widowed sister, held a senior position among the clerks responsible for **licenses, market permissions, and commercial registrations**.

She was never formally one of Blackberry’s great officers.

This did not prevent half the merchants in town from knowing her name.

Mara and members of her household reportedly departed at roughly the same time as Cedric Bale.

---

## The Town Council

Blackberry traditionally maintained a civic council drawn from its established local interests.

Its members represented some combination of:

- merchants and factors
- established landholders
- craft interests
- farming communities
- temple representatives
- senior civic officers

The council advised the mayor on taxation, markets, town expenses, roads, walls, Watch funding, and local disputes.

Supporters described it as cautious and experienced.

Critics described it as a room full of men waiting to discover which way Cedric Bale had already decided to vote.

The truth was probably less tidy.

### Present Position

**Unknown.**

Reports reaching Bard’s Gate do not agree on whether the council remains active, has lost control, has accepted some new arrangement, or is simply attempting to keep Blackberry functioning.

---

# The Watch

Blackberry maintains its own town Watch.

In ordinary times its duties are thoroughly unromantic:

- guarding the gates
- patrolling the market
- dealing with theft and disorder
- inspecting carts and cargo
- keeping the peace at inns
- assisting during fires
- enforcing town ordinances

Blackberry's walls are real defenses, however, and the Watch can be reinforced by local citizens should the town face serious danger.

The present condition of the Watch is uncertain.

---

# Faith

The town supports several temples and shrines appropriate to a prosperous Gorundian market community.

**Pelor** is particularly prominent among the human population, especially among farmers, laborers, healers, and travelers.

The surrounding halfling communities maintain their own hearth-shrines and chapels, particularly honoring **Yondalla**, **Arvoreen**, **Cyrrollalee**, and **Sheela Peryroyl**.

Religious life in Blackberry tends toward the practical.

People pray for rain, healthy children, safe roads, strong fences, honest weights, and livestock possessing enough divine grace not to die immediately after someone pays too much for them.

# Cedric Bale's Reputation

Long before his departure, complaints about Cedric Bale were common.

Few involved dramatic crimes.

Most involved something considerably more irritating.

Permits.

Taxes.

Contracts.

Watchmen.

Property lines.

Fees.

Cousins.

The sort of things capable of making a man hate his government for thirty years without ever becoming interesting enough for a bard.

How much of the following is true remains uncertain.

# Recent Trouble

The collapse of order in parts of [[Grevandar]] has increasingly been felt along the road.

For some time, **refugees have been arriving in Blackberry in small numbers**.

Families.

Displaced laborers.

Servants.

Soldiers without commands.

People carrying what they could fit into a wagon, handcart, or sack.

Blackberry absorbed them as best it could.

At first, there was little reason to believe this was anything more than another consequence of the fighting farther east.

Then the numbers changed.

---

## The Large Arrival

Recently, a **much larger body of people arrived from the direction of Grevandar**.

Reports reaching Bard’s Gate are inconsistent.

Some describe mostly refugees.

Others mention a substantial number of armed men among them.

Still others insist the newcomers were organized enough that calling them refugees is misleading.

What happened immediately afterward is even less clear.

**Mayor Cedric Bale left Blackberry.**

His brother Halden and sister Mara are both believed to have gone with him, along with members of their households.

Whether Bale fled before the newcomers entered, during their arrival, or shortly afterward depends upon which traveler is telling the story.

No reliable explanation has yet reached Bard’s Gate.

# Reputation

Before the present trouble, Blackberry was generally regarded as:

- safe
- prosperous
- provincial
- well supplied
- serious about trade
- suspicious of unnecessary excitement
- more concerned with harvests than politics

People complained about Cedric Bale for years.

People also expected Cedric Bale to still be mayor next year.

That expectation has abruptly failed.

Blackberry is accustomed to wagons arriving.

It is accustomed to refugees passing through.

It is accustomed to arguments over tolls, taxes, grain prices, wandering livestock, and whose hedge has mysteriously moved three feet since last spring.

It is **not** accustomed to the mayor and half his useful relatives disappearing while a large body of strangers enters through the gates.

For the moment, that is about all anyone outside Blackberry can say with confidence.
