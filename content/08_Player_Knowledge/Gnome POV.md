## Gnomes on Humans

**Private take:**  
Humans are brilliant short-term improvisers with no archival discipline and a terrifying willingness to make permanent decisions while emotional.

Gnomes admire humans because humans get things done. They also distrust them because humans get things done **before determining whether the thing should exist.**

**Gnome polite version:**  
“Humans possess admirable decisiveness.”

**What they mean:**  
“They will burn down a barn, call it reform, then ask where the cows went.”

**Common gnome saying:**  
“Never give a human a blank map, a sharp sword, and a grievance on the same morning.”

## Gnomes on Nobles

**Private take:**  
Nobles are humans with hereditary footnotes.

Gnomes find noble etiquette fascinating as a ritualized dominance system, but maddening because nobles keep pretending it is about honor instead of resource allocation, mating access, and who gets to sit closer to the expensive chair.

**Gnome polite version:**  
“His lineage is very thoroughly attested.”

**What they mean:**  
“He has mistaken documentation for merit.”

**Common gnome saying:**  
“Nobles are like rare beetles. Brightly marked, territorial, and difficult to remove from velvet.”

---

## Gnomes on Elves

**Private take:**  
Elves are walking primary sources who refuse to answer direct questions.

This drives gnomes insane.

Elves remember things from 700 years ago, but instead of saying, “Yes, the treaty was signed in spring,” they say, “It was during the third blooming of the white sorrow-vine, when the moon remembered silver.”

Gnomes want dates, names, copies, maps, and access to the restricted library. Elves provide mood.

**Gnome polite version:**  
“Elven oral tradition preserves great depth of meaning.”

**What they mean:**  
“They have weaponized vagueness.”

**Common gnome saying:**  
“Ask an elf what time it is and he’ll tell you why clocks are grief.”

---

## Gnomes on High Elves

**Private take:**  
High elves are beautiful, ancient, precise, and fundamentally opposed to peer review.

Gnomes both idolize and resent high elven scholarship. The work is magnificent. The access controls are absurd. Every book is “not for young minds,” by which elves mean anyone under 600 or not approved by a cousin of someone who once saw the Queen sneeze.

**Gnome polite version:**  
“Their preservation standards are unmatched.”

**What they mean:**  
“They have hidden half the useful world behind curtains and poetry.”

**Common gnome saying:**  
“High elves believe knowledge should be shared freely with those already worthy of knowing it.”

---

## Gnomes on Wood Elves / Grugach

**Private take:**  
Wood elves are what happens when an entire people becomes a warning label and refuses to laminate it.

Gnomes respect grugach fieldcraft enormously. They also find them almost impossible to study because grugach object to being “studied,” often with arrows.

**Gnome polite version:**  
“They maintain an admirably direct relationship with territory.”

**What they mean:**  
“They will kill you for touching the wrong moss, and they may be correct.”

**Common gnome saying:**  
“Never assume a forest is empty just because it has stopped insulting you.”

---

## Gnomes on Dwarves

**Private take:**  
Dwarves are excellent engineers who think innovation ended when their grandfather approved the hinge.

Gnomes admire dwarven craftsmanship deeply. They trust dwarven measurements, stonework, metallurgy, and structural judgment. But dwarves are painfully slow to accept new methods unless the new method is old, expensive, and engraved.

**Gnome polite version:**  
“Dwarves have a healthy respect for precedent.”

**What they mean:**  
“They would rather be buried by a traditional collapse than saved by an experimental brace.”

**Common gnome saying:**  
“A dwarf will improve a design for 300 years and still call it the old way.”

---

## Gnomes on Halflings

**Private take:**  
Halflings are socially brilliant, logistically dangerous, and hiding entire governments inside kitchens.

Gnomes like halflings. A lot. But they are suspicious of any people who can solve disputes with seating arrangements, pastry, and aunties. Halflings look harmless until you realize every grandmother knows who owes money, who lied, who drank too much, and where the good knives are.

**Gnome polite version:**  
“Halfling communities possess remarkable informal cohesion.”

**What they mean:**  
“The pie network knows everything.”

**Common gnome saying:**  
“Never underestimate a people whose intelligence service is disguised as lunch.”

---

## Gnomes on Half-Orcs / Halfbloods

**Private take:**  
Halfbloods are socially mistreated, biologically fascinating, and politically useful in ways everyone pretends not to notice.

This is where gnomes can get gross if unchecked. The better sort of gnome is genuinely sympathetic and curious in a respectful way. The worse sort starts treating halfbloods like a walking dissertation.

Gnomes are often less afraid of half-orcs than humans are, because gnomes are more likely to ask, “What is actually happening here?” rather than “What did my uncle say about tusks?”

But that does not make them saints. It makes them nosy.

**Gnome polite version:**  
“Mixed-heritage populations reveal much about social classification systems.”

**What they mean:**  
“Everyone is being stupid, and I want blood samples.”

**Common gnome saying:**  
“If your theory cannot survive one polite half-orc wizard, it was not a theory. It was a superstition in a hat.”

---

## Gnomes on Orcs

**Private take:**  
Orcs are badly understood, poorly recorded, and best observed from behind cover.

Gnomes do not usually romanticize orcs. They are pragmatic little bastards. Orcs are dangerous. But gnomes also suspect most “orc facts” in human records were written by frightened soldiers, drunk priests, or nobles trying to justify a land grab.

**Gnome polite version:**  
“Orcish social structures require more direct observation.”

**What they mean:**  
“I am not directly observing them. You directly observe them.”

**Common gnome saying:**  
“The problem with studying orcs is that they often have strong opinions about methodology.”

---

## Gnomes on Drow

**Private take:**  
Drow are an entire civilization of redacted footnotes, theological panic, and probable murder.

Gnomes are terrified of drow, but also deeply curious, which is a dangerous combination. A halfling sees a drow and counts the exits. A gnome sees a drow and counts the exits, then wonders what dialect he speaks.

**Gnome polite version:**  
“Drow studies remain underdeveloped due to survivorship bias.”

**What they mean:**  
“Most people who learn anything useful do not come back, which is academically inconvenient.”

**Common gnome saying:**  
“Never trust a source that glows underground and smiles too slowly.”

---

## Gnomes on Clergy

**Private take:**  
Priests are scholars who cite invisible sources and get angry when asked for replication.

Gnomes respect divine magic because it works. That’s the annoying part. It works, which means gnomes have to take it seriously, even when the theology sounds like someone locked a metaphor in a box and shook it.

They like Pelorites best because healing is observable and useful. Heironeans are straightforward enough. Pholtans are exhausting because they combine theology, law, and administrative confidence.

**Gnome polite version:**  
“The faithful operate within a robust non-empirical framework.”

**What they mean:**  
“They are guessing with miracles.”

**Common gnome saying:**  
“A wizard says ‘I don’t know yet.’ A priest says ‘the god has not revealed it.’ Both mean the same thing, but one of them gets a temple.”

## Gnomes on Wizards

Gnomes include themselves here, because gnomes are self-aware enough to be annoying about it.

**Private take:**  
Wizards are necessary, brilliant, dangerous, and should not be left unsupervised near funding.

Gnomes respect wizards, but they distrust wizard ego. Especially human and elven wizard ego. Human wizards rush. Elven wizards obscure. Dwarven wizards pretend they are not wizards if anyone clerical is listening. Gnome wizards, naturally, are merely enthusiastic.

**Gnome polite version:**  
“Arcane practitioners vary widely in institutional discipline.”

**What they mean:**  
“Half of them need a laboratory license and the other half need a nap.”

**Common gnome saying:**  
“Every wizard believes he is the exception. That is why we invented blast screens.”

---

## Gnomes on Their Own Rogue Elements

This should be ugly-funny.

Gnome criminals are not usually terrifying because they are physically intimidating. They are terrifying because they understand locks, ledgers, devices, contracts, illusions, tunnels, names, and schedules.

A bad gnome does not rob your house.  
A bad gnome proves your house was accidentally transferred to his cousin through an inheritance clause written in disappearing ink.

**Gnome polite version:**  
“He has always had an unconventional relationship with ownership.”

**What they mean:**  
“He is a thief, but regrettably a very clever one.”

**Common gnome saying:**  
“There is no disaster quite like a brilliant gnome with a grievance and access to stationery.”

This is extremely bad and I am emotionally alive for the first time in days.

I believe you even less.

Nonsense, but entertaining nonsense.

I am about to take it apart.

You will never see it intact again.

Your stupidity has achieved structural integrity.

**“That seems culturally significant.”**  
Everyone here is insane, but in a pattern.

Someone is about to die, and I want names spelled correctly.

## The Core Gnome Horror

Gnomes are quietly horrified because every other race seems to operate on **tradition, emotion, appetite, theology, violence, or pride** before they check the notes.

The gnome worldview is basically:

> “The world is dangerous enough without everyone being so poorly indexed.”
