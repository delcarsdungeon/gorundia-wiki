# Alden Durathen

> [!quote] Sorek Redmarch
> Alden Durathen will shake your hand, honor the price, and send the goods on time. Cheat him once, and you’ll spend the next year learning how many doors can close without anyone drawing steel.

## Overview

**Full Name:** Alden Durathen  
**House:** [[House Durathen]]  
**Status:** Cousin of the Greyford line  
**Role:** Merchant, trade factor, information-gatherer, quiet family financier  
**Residence:** Usually on the road between Greyford, DeSalle markets, and Araisal trade towns  
**Known For:** Trade connections, discreet information work, loyal support of House Durathen  

Alden Durathen is a cousin of the main Greyford line and one of the quiet reasons [[House Durathen]] still functions better than its poverty should allow.

He is not a knight of songs, banners, and dramatic charges. He is a merchant-noble: a man of contracts, caravans, grain tallies, warehouse keys, bridge tolls, favors owed, and names remembered. He carries Durathen pride without pretending that pride repairs roofs or feeds horses.

Alden has made himself useful in the places where poorer noble houses often die: credit, supply, rumor, delivery, and quiet influence. He has modest wealth, careful trade connections, and the ability to hear three versions of a story before the first official report has dried.

He was a major part of the information net that exposed and constrained the [[House DeSalle]] matter. He did not swing the blade in public, but he helped make sure the blade knew where to fall.

## Appearance

![[Alden Durathen.png|340]]

Alden is in his early 40s, lean rather than broad, with dark hair beginning to silver at the temples and the weathered skin of a man who spends more time on roads than in halls.

He dresses respectably but practically: good boots, durable cloak, a merchant’s satchel, and a signet ring worn without flourish. His clothes are well-made but repaired, in the Durathen habit. He looks like a minor noble who has learned the value of not looking rich in the wrong tavern.

His hands are ink-stained more often than callused, though he is no stranger to a knife or riding crop. He has a steady gaze, a polite voice, and the patience of a man who has watched liars keep talking until they save him the trouble of proving anything.

## Personality

Alden is courteous, measured, and fair by preference.

He likes honest dealing. He honors agreements. He pays on time when paid on time. He does not haggle for sport once a fair price has been reached, and he dislikes merchants who treat cheating as cleverness.

This has led some fools to mistake him for soft.

They usually get one correction.

Alden carries the Durathen ruthlessness, but not in the battlefield form. He does not prefer killing. Killing is noisy, final, and often wasteful. Ruin is cleaner. Ruin teaches more people at once.

If someone wrongs him, cheats his family, or uses trust as a knife, Alden’s response is rarely immediate violence. He prefers to make their credit curdle, their partners drift away, their contracts grow expensive, their witnesses remember inconvenient details, and their private arrogance become public fact.

He will play fair until wronged.

He may even offer a second chance.

But the second chance is not mercy without memory. It is a door. If the offender walks through it honestly, Alden lets the matter rest. If they abuse it, he stops treating the matter as business and starts treating it as a debt.

Durathens answer debts.

## Family Loyalty

Alden is deeply loyal to [[House Durathen]], though his loyalty is practical rather than theatrical.

He helps keep the family solvent. He carries news between Greyford and the wider world. He arranges credit when the ledgers tighten. He knows which merchants can be trusted, which small houses are desperate, which reeves are greedy, and which taverns hear too much.

He does not posture as the savior of the house. That would be vulgar, and worse, inaccurate. [[Roderic Durathen]] governs. [[Elinora Durathen]] keeps the household from fraying. [[Edwyn Durathen]] carries the family’s young ambition. [[Myra Durathen]] turns competence into leverage. [[Myreth Durathen]] remembers the wound.

Alden connects the pieces.

He is especially protective of the younger Durathens because he knows poverty makes noble children vulnerable to bad bargains dressed as opportunity.

## Marriage

Alden is married to [[Celia Durathen]], born Celia Harrowmere, the daughter of a respectable but land-poor household tied to market administration and toll records.

Celia is calm, dry-witted, and more observant than she likes strangers to notice. She manages correspondence, household accounts, small investments, and the quiet social work that lets Alden’s merchant contacts feel less like transactions and more like relationships.

Their marriage began as a practical arrangement and became a genuine partnership. Alden trusts Celia’s judgment completely, especially when it comes to people. He reads ledgers. She reads rooms.

They have the sort of marriage where affection is expressed through small corrections, packed food for journeys, and arguments about whether a risk is “bold” or “idiotic.”

> [!quote] Celia Durathen
> Alden believes every man deserves a fair bargain. I believe some men deserve to be charged in advance.

> [!quote] Alden Durathen
> My wife has saved me from three bad contracts, two ambushes, and one dinner with a DeSalle cousin. The ambushes were less unpleasant.

## Children

Alden and Celia have one son, [[Tomas Durathen]], age 13.

Tomas is bright, serious, and at the dangerous age where boys begin confusing stories of knightly glory with practical career advice.

Alden is deliberately keeping him away from romantic ideas of battle. He does not want Tomas raised on tournament songs, polished armor, and all the nonsense that convinces young men that dying in mud is more noble if someone wrote a banner over it.

That does not mean Alden is raising a coward.

Tomas is expected to ride, shoot, keep accounts, judge horses, read contracts, identify bad roads, respect guards, speak to commoners without contempt, and defend himself if pressed. Alden wants him brave enough to stand firm, but wise enough to know that courage is not the same thing as volunteering to be stupid in public.

Alden’s lesson to his son is simple:

> “A sword is useful. So is a locked gate, a paid debt, a loyal teamster, and knowing which bridge washes out in spring. Learn all of them.”

Tomas sometimes resents being steered away from knightly dreams, especially when stories of [[Edwyn Durathen]] and [[Braga Kharne]] reach the household. Alden does not mock the dream. He simply teaches the cost.

He has taken Tomas to veterans, widows, horse surgeons, and supply wagons after bad weather. Not to frighten him, but to give him the whole picture.

Alden will not forbid his son from taking up arms if duty demands it.

He will forbid him from thinking glory is free.

## Relationship with House Durathen

Alden’s place within House Durathen is unusual. He is not the heir, not the champion, not the court beauty, and not the grieving architect of revenge. He is the cousin with routes, favors, coin, and ears.

This makes him easy to overlook.

It also makes him dangerous.

He is loyal to [[Roderic Durathen]], though he sometimes finds Roderic too cautious. He respects [[Elinora Durathen]] and has often worked with her to stretch limited resources without letting outsiders smell desperation.

He is fond of [[Edwyn Durathen]], but wary of Edwyn’s hunger for recognition. Alden sees brilliance there, but also risk.

He respects [[Myra Durathen]] enormously. Her ambition does not bother him. Careless ambition would. Myra’s is rarely careless.

He fears [[Myreth Durathen]] in the way sensible men fear a candle too near old parchment. He understands her grief. He helped where he could. He also knows exactly how close the family came to becoming criminals in the name of justice.

With [[Braga Kharne]], Alden is careful. He sees her as both kin and consequence: the living proof that DeSalle cowardice and noble carelessness can forge something harder than intended.

## The DeSalle Plot

Alden was a major part of the information net surrounding the [[House DeSalle]] crisis.

His contribution was not dramatic in the bardic sense. No balcony leap. No duel at moonrise. No heroic speech while bleeding decoratively.

He did the useful work.

He tracked movement.

He listened to drivers, toll-keepers, ostlers, carters, low-ranking clerks, and merchants who had learned not to speak too loudly near DeSalle men.

He compared grain orders against troop movement.

He noticed which roads were avoided.

He watched who suddenly had coin, who suddenly feared letters, and who stopped drinking in public.

He identified habits around [[Remy DeSalle|Baron Remy DeSalle]]: where he felt safe, whose company he preferred, what times his guard thinned, and which errands created predictable gaps.

This information fed the Durathen-Thralen revenge network. At first, that network was moving toward murder. Alden knew it. He did not like it. He also did not pretend the grievance was imaginary.

That is the uncomfortable truth of him.

Alden was willing to help bring DeSalle down. He preferred ruin, exposure, and lawful consequence, but he understood why Myreth wanted blood. He did not dismiss her grief as hysteria or impatience. He treated it as a debt seeking form.

Beringer’s intervention came too quickly for Alden to fully change course in the moment.

The family had already been moving. Grief had already become machinery. Information had been gathered, habits marked, routes watched, and the old Durathen-Thralen wound had sharpened itself into something very close to murder.

Alden did not suddenly become calm, wise, and spotless because [[Beringer|Beringer Amadis]] found a lawful road. He was still angry. He still wanted [[Remy DeSalle|Baron Remy DeSalle]] broken. Some part of him still believed blood would have been understandable.

What changed came after.

When the dust settled, Alden understood what Beringer had truly done. He had not denied the family its answer. He had kept the answer from becoming a stain that would follow every Durathen child after them.

That is the gratitude Alden carries.

Not gratitude that DeSalle was spared.

Not gratitude that vengeance was softened.

Gratitude that [[House Durathen]] was kept clean.

Beringer gave the family a road where their dead could be answered in public, under law, without turning Myreth into an assassin, Edwyn into a hired blade, or the Durathen name into a whispered cautionary tale.

Alden did not forget who wronged them.

He also did not forget who kept their vengeance from poisoning the house that survived it.

In the end, the DeSalle matter became a trap instead of a stabbing. Alden helped build parts of the trap without knowing, at first, that anyone would give it lawful shape.
## Moral Code

Alden’s morality is practical, but real.

He believes in fair dealing because commerce collapses without it.

He believes in family loyalty because a small house without loyalty is just a name waiting to be erased.

He believes in second chances because people make mistakes, and because mercy offered once can reveal whether a person is salvageable.

He also believes that deliberate betrayal deserves consequence.

His preferred order of response is:

1. Clarify the wrong.
2. Offer correction if possible.
3. Warn once.
4. Close the account.
5. Ruin the offender cleanly.

He does not enjoy cruelty for its own sake. He does, however, enjoy precision.

## Reputation

Among merchants, Alden is considered honest but not harmless.

Among minor nobles, he is respected as one of the Durathens who understands how the world actually moves: not just by titles and swords, but by credit, food, transport, and rumor.

Among DeSalle loyalists, he is disliked for being “insolent,” which usually means he has records.

Among common traders and teamsters, he is considered fair, memorable, and worth warning if trouble is moving on the road.

Alden’s reputation can be summarized simply:

He will give a man a fair bargain.

He will give a man one chance to repair a broken one.

After that, he starts counting.

## Table Use

Use Alden when the campaign needs:

- A Durathen ally with merchant contacts and practical intelligence.
- A non-knightly noble who still carries steel under the manners.
- A source for routes, supply chains, rumors, credit pressure, and quiet leverage.
- A family man trying to raise a brave son without feeding him heroic nonsense.
- A grounded counterpoint to Edwyn’s charm, Braga’s fury, and Myra’s ambition.
- Someone who can make consequences happen without sending bodies into ditches.

## Roleplaying Alden

Alden speaks calmly, rarely raising his voice. He asks more questions than he answers. He often sounds like he is discussing business even when the subject is revenge.

He prefers exact language.

He dislikes exaggeration.

He does not threaten directly unless the person has already ignored subtler warnings.

Common phrases:

> “Let us keep the account honest.”

> “I will not punish a mistake as betrayal. Do not make me revise the category.”

> “A second chance is not a blank page. It is a marked one.”

> “You may keep the coin. I am more interested in who told you to offer it.”

> “No, my son, courage is not wanting the fight. Courage is knowing what the fight costs and standing anyway.”

> “A dead enemy is a closed book. A ruined one is a lesson others may read.”

## Adventure Hooks

### The Spoiled Contract

Alden discovers that a DeSalle-aligned factor has been tampering with supply contracts meant for minor houses now supporting the Lord Marshal. The numbers are small enough to look accidental, but the pattern is not.

### Tomas Wants a Sword

Tomas Durathen begins seeking knightly training behind Alden’s back, possibly encouraged by a young retainer, Edwyn admirer, or outside manipulator. Alden must decide whether to clamp down or let the boy learn under controlled supervision.

### The Second Chance

A merchant who once betrayed Alden asks for protection, claiming DeSalle loyalists are cleaning up loose ends. Alden did give the man a second chance once. This would be the third.

### Celia’s Letter

Celia intercepts correspondence that suggests someone is mapping Durathen trade routes. She sends the matter to Alden, but also quietly warns Myra, creating a parallel response before Alden knows the full shape of the threat.

### The Quiet Ledger

Alden produces old records that complicate DeSalle claims over Thralen-linked woodland and charcoal rights. The document is legitimate, but proving it means dragging several dead men’s reputations into public view.

### Ruin Instead of Blood

A man involved in the DeSalle affair is found alive, prosperous, and quietly protected. Myreth wants him dead. Alden wants him exposed and ruined. Braga may be asked to decide which answer better honors the dead.

## GM Notes

Alden should feel like a Durathen who chose commerce instead of knighthood, not because he lacked courage, but because he understood leverage.

He is not a pacifist.

He is not a coward.

He simply knows killing is only one form of consequence, and often not the most useful one.

His family loyalty is absolute, but he is one of the people most aware that loyalty can become self-destruction if no one keeps count of the cost.

He should be useful to Beringer, Myra, Edwyn, and Braga, but never servile. Alden helps because the family debt was redeemed rather than wasted.

He remembers who helped.

He also remembers who made help necessary.
