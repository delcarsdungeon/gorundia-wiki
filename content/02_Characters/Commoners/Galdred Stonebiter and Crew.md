# Galdren Stonebiter

**Born:** 1105 OR  
**Race:** Dwarf  
**Origin:** Wyrmlea dwarven enclave  
**Affiliation:** Contracted through Galibano construction interests  
**Occupation:** Architect-engineer, structural inspector, civic maintenance master  
**Current Assignment:** New Serpentis  
**Known For:** Keeping Remy DeSalle’s vanity project from collapsing, flooding, burning, or publicly confessing its sins through masonry.

![[New Serpentis Maintenance.png]]
## Overview

Master **Galdren Stonebiter** is the dwarf architect-engineer responsible for the long-term maintenance of **New Serpentis**, though “responsible” is a word he uses with visible pain.

He does not work for House DeSalle in spirit, and he is not Galibano by blood or name. The **Galibanos hold the contracts, control the suppliers, grease the guilds, and run the paper**. Galdren and his small dwarven team are the expensive technical conscience bolted onto the arrangement because someone eventually realized that plaster, wood, vanity, and municipal optimism are not structural principles.

New Serpentis offends him because it is not entirely bad. If it were simply garbage, he could condemn it and sleep better. Instead, beneath the theatrical white façades, serpent trim, false-marble civic fronts, and envoy-road nonsense, there are some decent old foundations, salvageable drainage lines, and a street plan that could become something respectable with enough time, money, and the public execution of several decorators.

Galdren’s professional tragedy is that he is paid well to patch what should have been built properly the first time.

His private summary of the city:

> “Pretty from the road. Rotten where it has to answer questions.”

## Remy DeSalle’s Title for Him

Remy DeSalle, being Remy DeSalle, insisted on giving Galdren a ceremonial civic title:

**Grand Civic Superintendent of Serpentine Beautification, Structural Continuity, Monumental Waters, Roadway Grandeur, and Associated Architectural Harmonies**

Galdren calls it:

> “That damned sentence.”

On paperwork, he signs:

**Galdren Stonebiter, Superintendent of Structural Continuity**

When drunk, angry, or asked by someone he trusts:

> “Stonebiter. Repairs.”

## Appearance

Galdren is compact even for a dwarf, broad as a foundation block, with iron-grey hair braided tight and a square beard threaded with small bronze measuring rings. He wears good work wool under a battered leather apron and carries chalk, wax tablets, plumb line, measuring cord, and a small hammer polished smooth from decades of tapping stone.

His left eye twitches when someone says “good enough.”

He has the air of a man who has personally inspected every lie in town and assigned each one a projected failure date.

## Personality

Galdren is not warm, but he is fair. He respects competence, direct speech, proper measurements, and anyone who will admit when something is broken.

He despises noble vanity, rushed work, decorative substitutions, hidden timber pretending to be stone, watered lime, and any phrase beginning with “Lord DeSalle wanted…”

He does not rant unless seriously provoked. His usual anger is colder and worse. He points, waits, and asks the guilty party to explain the physics of their optimism.

Common sayings:

> “A wall does not care who paid for it.”

> “Bad lime is delayed murder.”

> “If you wanted it to last, you should have built it as though winter exists.”

> “Do not ask me to bless a lie because someone painted it white.”

## Role in an Investigation

If the PCs investigate New Serpentis, Galdren and his crew are likely the first locals who show honest dealing without needing a bribe, threat, or noble introduction.

They are not rebels. They are not saints. They are working people who know the difference between a repair and a cover-up.

They can confirm:

- New Serpentis is not wholly fraudulent, but large parts are dangerously theatrical.
- Older foundations and some service works are solid enough to salvage.
- Many newer decorative districts were built for appearance, not durability.
- Repairs were often ordered to be invisible rather than permanent.
- Some inspection reports were softened before reaching noble desks.
- Galibano suppliers delivered mixed-quality materials: some excellent, some acceptable, some invoice-grade bullshit.
- DeSalle’s people cared more about envoy impressions than worker safety.
- A serious fire could “solve” parts of New Serpentis in the ugliest possible way.

Galdren will not volunteer everything immediately, but he will answer plain questions plainly. If the PCs act like grandstanding nobles, he gives them exactly what the law requires and no more. If they speak like people trying to prevent deaths, he opens the real books.

## First Meeting Scene

The PCs find the crew behind a half-opened civic arcade near the Serpent Road approach.

The public-facing side is gorgeous: white plaster, painted veining, gold serpent trim, decorative columns, and bright paving meant to impress travelers from the capital and Vogel lands.

Behind the peeled-away façade is warped timber, rushed brick infill, cracked mortar, and a support column shimmed three separate times.

Galdren sees the PCs looking.

He does not hide anything.

He taps the exposed column with his hammer.

Once.

Twice.

Then says:

> “There. That’s New Serpentis.”

A beat.

> “A sermon in plaster. A confession in timber.”

If asked whether it is dangerous:

> “Everything is dangerous. This is merely arrogant about it.”

## Stonebiter’s Bastards

Officially, the crew is listed as:

**The Serpentis Civic Works Continuity Detail**

The laborers call them:

**Stonebiter’s Bastards**

Galdren pretends to hate the name.

He does not.

The crew is mostly human and half-orc, with a small dwarven professional core. They are paid through Galibano contracts, but day-to-day they are loyal to Galdren because he keeps them alive, keeps the work honest where possible, and yells at nobles on their behalf.

# Crew Roster

## Borra Deepdrain

**Race:** Dwarf  
**Role:** Drainage architect and cistern engineer  
**Temperament:** Cheerfully grim

Borra handles drains, culverts, cisterns, runoff, street grades, and the long war between New Serpentis and rain. She has waxed maps full of little black marks showing where water has already won.

She is the only person Galdren trusts without checking twice.

> “Water is honest. It goes where fools give it room.”

Useful information:
- Which streets flood first.
- Which ornamental pools are secretly overflow hazards.
- Which cellars connect to older service tunnels.
- Whether recent flooding was natural or caused by blocked works.

## Naddik Trueplumb

**Race:** Dwarf  
**Role:** Stone auditor and structural inspector  
**Temperament:** Sour, precise, professionally offended

Naddik can identify bad mortar by smell and bad stone by tapping it once with his hammer. He hates façade-work, hidden timber, and the phrase “decorative load-bearing.”

> “That wall is not cracked. It is confessing.”

Useful information:
- Which buildings are merely ugly versus structurally compromised.
- Which stone shipments were substandard.
- Which contractors signed off on impossible work.
- Where repairs were intentionally disguised.

## Vekka Ironrule

**Race:** Dwarf  
**Role:** Surveyor and measuring-chain master  
**Temperament:** Quiet, exacting, mercilessly patient

Vekka keeps the real measurements. Not the civic presentation drawings. Not the DeSalle “vision plans.” The real ones.

She is missing two fingers from an old scaffold failure and remembers the event with the calm bitterness of a woman who has already blamed everyone accurately.

> “I measured it twice. The second time was out of spite.”

Useful information:
- Settlement patterns.
- Street and arcade misalignments.
- Whether new construction matches approved plans.
- Which official maps have been prettied up.

## Brond Halftunnel

**Race:** Dwarf  
**Role:** Foundations, excavation, buried works  
**Temperament:** Old mine-dwarf, dry as quarry dust

Brond handles anything below grade: foundation trenches, voids, old cellars, collapsed drains, sinkage, and the sort of buried surprises New Serpentis pretends not to have.

He has a miner’s sense for hollow ground.

> “There’s hollow under there. Don’t ask me how I know unless you brought rope.”

Useful information:
- Hidden voids beneath civic districts.
- Unmapped older construction.
- Areas likely to sink.
- Whether someone has been using old service spaces.

## Mara Kettlejack

**Race:** Human  
**Role:** Street-works forewoman  
**Temperament:** Practical, loud, hard to impress

Mara runs day crews, street repairs, paving, scaffolds, and emergency work. She has no patience for ceremony and less for nobles who want things “ready for viewing” before they are safe.

She is the crew’s operational backbone.

> “You want it pretty by sundown, or standing by winter? Pick one.”

Useful information:
- Who ordered rushed repairs.
- Which workers were injured and why.
- What work was done at night or under pressure.
- Which officials interfered with safety.

## Ellis Reed

**Race:** Human  
**Role:** Plaster and façade specialist  
**Temperament:** Charming, artistic, morally tired

Ellis makes cheap things look expensive. He can fake marble veining, restore serpent trim, patch plaster, repaint civic glory, and make a structurally suspect arcade look ready for a royal procession.

He hates that he is good at it.

> “I don’t build lies. I repaint them before envoys arrive.”

Useful information:
- Which buildings are mostly theater.
- Where defects were concealed cosmetically.
- Who demanded visual fixes instead of real repairs.
- Which civic monuments are hollow, patched, or flammable.

## Hessa Crowl

**Race:** Human  
**Role:** Teamster and supply wrangler  
**Temperament:** Sharp-eyed, blunt, hard to fool

Hessa manages carts, stone deliveries, timber lots, lime shipments, missing bronze fittings, gate delays, and the quiet little fees that make New Serpentis continue functioning.

She knows the difference between what the invoice says and what arrived.

> “Paper says twelve. Road says nine. Road don’t lie.”

Useful information:
- Supply discrepancies.
- Galibano delivery chains.
- Missing materials.
- Which suppliers are reliable and which are creatively criminal.

## Brace

**Race:** Half-orc / half-blood  
**Role:** Rigging master and emergency shoring specialist  
**Temperament:** Massive, calm, thoughtful

Brace is the man called when something overhead begins making theological noises. He is huge, slow to speak, and trusted absolutely in collapse work.

He understands beams, ropes, counterweights, and panic.

> “Beam’s talking. Best listen before it shouts.”

Useful information:
- Recent collapses or near-collapses.
- Dangerous upper galleries.
- Who was present at emergency repairs.
- Whether damage looks accidental or forced.

## Patchwork

**Race:** Half-orc / half-blood  
**Role:** Mason-apprentice  
**Temperament:** Young, proud, sharp-eyed

Patchwork got her name because she was always sent to patch cracks. She kept the name because her patches hold.

She is young, but she has real talent. Galdren is quietly grooming her for serious engineering work.

> “Patchwork holds. Fancy falls.”

Useful information:
- Fresh cracks.
- Repeated failures.
- Patterns older workers overlooked.
- Which sections are getting worse fastest.

## Kindling

**Race:** Half-orc / half-blood  
**Role:** Demolition hand and fire-watch  
**Temperament:** Cheerfully grim, too aware of flammability

Kindling earned the name after preventing one fire and accidentally starting another during cleanup. He knows exactly how much of New Serpentis is plaster over timber, and it bothers him more than he admits.

He is the crew’s best source on fire risk.

> “This whole district wants to burn. I just hear it whispering.”

Useful information:
- Fire hazards.
- Hidden wooden frames.
- Stored pitch, oil, old scaffolding, and dry lath.
- Where a fire would spread fastest.

## Buttons

**Race:** Half-orc / half-blood  
**Role:** Tool-keeper and supply tally clerk  
**Temperament:** Smallish, precise, dangerous about inventory

Buttons tracks tools, nails, rope, wedges, lanterns, braces, and every chisel issued from the yard. She has a memory like a locked strongbox.

Do not steal from Buttons.

> “I issued twelve chisels. I see eleven. Begin your confession.”

Useful information:
- Missing tools or materials.
- Who borrowed equipment.
- Whether someone accessed the yard after hours.
- Which items were used in suspicious repairs or sabotage.

## Hush

**Race:** Half-orc / half-blood  
**Role:** Dangerous-job labor boss  
**Temperament:** Quiet authority

Hush does not shout. When Hush raises one hand, everyone stops. That habit has saved lives.

He leads the work that requires calm: wall-jacking, scaffold shift, shoring under load, clearing broken beams, and moving men through unstable spaces.

> “Quiet. The wall’s deciding.”

Useful information:
- Who panics under danger.
- Where workers were told to keep silent.
- Which repairs were too risky for normal crews.
- Whether a site has been tampered with.

## Pell “Chalkrat” Harrow

**Race:** Human  
**Role:** Runner, marker-boy, apprentice nuisance  
**Temperament:** Fast, filthy, observant, impossible to remove

Pell marks fresh cracks with chalk, carries messages, fetches food, finds missing tools, hears gossip, and knows where Galdren hides the good biscuits.

Adults forget he is there. This has made him dangerous in the way only clever children can be.

> “I know where the fresh cracks are. Also where Master Stonebiter hides the good biscuits.”

Useful information:
- Who came and went.
- Where new chalk marks appeared.
- Which officials visited after dark.
- What workers say when they think no one important is listening.

# Use at the Table

Galdren and the crew are probably one-and-done NPCs, but they should leave the impression that New Serpentis has honest people trapped under dishonest priorities.

They are not there to become major faction players. They are there to give the PCs their first clean look behind the façade.

Core reveal:

> New Serpentis is not a fake city.  
> It is a salvageable city wearing a fake city like a bad coat.

Galdren’s final useful line:

> “If you want names, I have names. If you want blame, I have ledgers. If you want it fixed, bring money, authority, and a willingness to disappoint every idiot who thinks paint is cheaper than stone.”
