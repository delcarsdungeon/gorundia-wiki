# Volika Coinmane and the Iron Rose

> “I do not like Coinmane. I do not like what she sells, who she watches, or the sums she does in her head when a hungry girl walks through her door.
>
> “But I served in Bard’s Gate. These places exist whether decent men approve of them or not. At least hers has locks on the inside, a healer on call, and a madam who pays what she promised.
>
> “That is not goodness.
>
> “It is better than some.
>
> “In the Dregs, that damned near counts.”
>
> — [[Sorek Redmarch]]

## Overview

**Volika Coinmane** is the dwarven proprietor of the **Iron Rose**, one of the oldest and most successful pleasure houses in [[The Dregs]].

The Iron Rose sells drink, privacy, companionship, sex, bathing, gambling, discreet lodging, and the temporary illusion that the person across the table is listening because they care.

It is cleaner than most establishments of its kind.

It is safer than nearly all of them.

It is not kind.

Volika runs the house according to a simple principle:

> Desperate people will make bargains. A competent businesswoman makes certain they bargain with her.

She recruits heavily among young half-orc women, former slaves, battered wives, abandoned servants, penniless migrants, and anyone else whose available choices are already bad.

Volika does not pretend those choices are good.

She merely insists that hers is usually the one that hurts least.

She is often correct.

That is what makes her difficult to dismiss.

# Volika Coinmane

## Basic Information

**Race:** Dwarf  
**Sex:** Female  
**Height:** Approximately 4 feet 6 inches  
**Build:** Compact, broad, and extremely powerful  
**Role:** Proprietor, madam, accountant, negotiator, and part of her own security  
**Residence:** Private rooms above the Iron Rose  
**Principal Enforcer:** [[Lily]]  
**Public Reputation:** Reliable, predatory, fair within the exact boundaries of her agreements  
**Private Reputation:** Worse than her defenders claim and better than her enemies admit  

Volika is not available for hire.

This is not modesty.

She owns the house, keeps its books, negotiates its arrangements, and occasionally helps remove customers who have misunderstood the nature of consent, payment, or dwarven leverage.

Several patrons have learned that being four and a half feet tall does not prevent someone from throwing a grown man down a staircase.

---

## Appearance

![[Volika Coinmane with Lilly.png|340]]

Volika is compact even by dwarven standards, with broad shoulders, a thick neck, powerful forearms, and the dense physical presence of someone built closer to a fortress foundation than a courtly ideal.

Her hair is golden-blonde, heavy, and usually arranged in an elaborate braided crown. Coins, iron beads, and narrow red ribbons are worked into the braids.

Whether **Coinmane** is an old family name or one acquired from this habit depends upon which story she is telling and whether the listener owes her money.

Her eyes are green, sharp, and generally fixed upon whichever person in the room has most recently mistaken her silence for agreement.

She favors dresses in:

- earth tones
- black
- iron-grey
- muted gold

Her clothing is well made and deliberately practical. Volika’s skirts allow free movement, her shoes provide firm footing, and most of her jewelry has some secondary function as a key, seal, blade, weight, or means of identifying a body.

She does not dress like one of the Iron Rose’s workers.

She dresses like the woman who owns the building and intends to continue owning it after everyone else in the room has died.

---

## Personality

Volika is calm, patient, stubborn, and almost entirely free of shame.

She does not raise her voice unless she has concluded that volume is now useful.

She does not threaten casually.

She does not apologize for the nature of her business.

Volika views most moral criticism as a luxury purchased by people who have never had to decide between hunger, a beating, or a stranger’s bed.

That does not make her compassionate.

It makes her difficult to impress with outrage.

### Mannerisms

- Rolls a coin across her knuckles while listening.
- Looks at a visitor’s hands, boots, and belt before meeting their eyes.
- Remembers exact sums years after everyone else has forgotten the debt.
- Straightens objects while deciding whether someone is worth further effort.
- Refers to serious violence as “correcting the account.”
- Uses **my girls**, **my people**, and **my house** with equal possessiveness.
- Becomes more polite immediately before refusing someone.
- Never repeats an offer in precisely the same form.

---

# Her Argument

Volika’s defense of the Iron Rose is blunt, ugly, and difficult to answer without offering something better.

> “Halfblood girls rut in the dark for free, pregnant at fifteen and dead at twenty, while decent folk avert their eyes and call it unfortunate.
>
> “I offer a locked room, a full belly, coin in her own purse, and Lily downstairs.
>
> “Do not preach to me about purity unless you brought her a better bargain.”

Volika did not create the Dregs.

She did not create half-orc poverty, human prejudice, abusive husbands, hereditary servitude, or the market for frightened young women.

She profits from all of them.

Her favorite moral distinction is that she exploits conditions she did not cause.

This distinction is technically accurate.

It is also the sort of distinction a person develops after many years of sleeping comfortably above other people’s compromises.

---

# Business Philosophy

Volika protects the people who work for her.

She protects them fiercely.

She also regards that protection as sound investment.

A healthy worker:

- earns longer
- attracts regular customers
- learns useful skills
- warns the house about dangerous patrons
- does not steal from desperation
- is less likely to disappear
- is more likely to defend the establishment

Volika provides:

- reliable food
- secure sleeping quarters
- medical treatment
- clean clothing and bedding
- protection from violent customers
- guards for those walking home
- wages paid as agreed
- opportunities to learn reading, accounts, music, or household management

She does not offer these things from charity.

She offers them because badly managed workers become injured, frightened, unreliable, sick, or dead.

Dead workers generate very little repeat business.

> “I keep my people healthy, paid, and difficult to frighten. You may call it mercy if that helps you sleep. I call it competent management.”

Volika can become genuinely fond of her workers.

This does not stop her from calculating their earnings.

Affection and arithmetic live comfortably together in her head.

---

# The Bargain

The Iron Rose does not use chains, locked contracts, or invented debts to prevent workers from leaving.

Volika considers such methods crude.

A worker may leave after settling any legitimate account for advances, clothing, training, or lodging. Volika records these expenses clearly and does not quietly multiply them overnight.

She keeps her bargains.

The problem is that the bargain is often being offered to someone whose alternatives are:

- starvation
- homelessness
- an abusive household
- criminal work
- street prostitution without protection
- a return to an owner or master
- another employer who will take the same liberties and provide less coin

For a former slave escaping an abusive relationship, the Iron Rose can look like freedom.

It provides a private room, regular meals, coin, security, and the right to refuse a customer.

Compared with what came before, it may genuinely be freedom.

Compared with what a decent society might have offered, it is another person purchasing labor from someone with nowhere else to go.

Volika does not concern herself with the difference.

She made the better offer.

She expects credit for that.

---

# Recruitment

## Preferred Prospects

Volika recruits heavily among half-orc women.

This is not based on racial affection.

It is market judgment.

Half-orc women in the Dregs are often:

- physically capable
- accustomed to rough environments
- treated as undesirable by respectable employers
- exoticized by the same people who publicly insult them
- paid poorly
- easily isolated from family or community support
- assumed to be older than they are
- expected to tolerate behavior that would provoke outrage if directed at someone wealthier

Volika sees undervalued labor.

She also sees demand.

She recruits for many positions within the Iron Rose:

- servers
- hostesses
- musicians
- bath attendants
- cleaners
- floor guards
- door staff
- companions
- sex workers

Not every girl Volika approaches is intended for an upstairs room.

That does not make her early interest harmless.

---

## Young Prospects

Volika does not put children to work upstairs.

She is exact about this point.

She has, however:

- watched promising girls grow
- asked about their family circumstances
- learned who controls their wages
- offered small jobs
- arranged introductions
- remembered birthdays
- made certain they knew where the Iron Rose stood
- returned when their circumstances worsened

Volika calls this **cultivating a lead**.

Most other people call it **sniffing around**.

She does not force the issue.

She does not need to.

Dwarves are patient, and poverty has a tiresome habit of returning.

A refusal means that the offer is closed for the moment.

Volika rarely mistakes it for a permanent condition.

> “I heard her say no.”

A pause.

> “I also possess a memory.”

---

## Persistence

Volika is not aggressive once directly refused.

She backs away.

She remains cordial.

She waits.

Then she may approach later with:

- a different position
- better pay
- temporary work
- an offer of lodging
- help during a crisis
- a reminder that the Iron Rose remains available

She does not believe persistence is coercion so long as the final decision remains with the prospect.

This is another technically defensible distinction that becomes less pleasant the longer one watches her practice it.

---

# The Iron Rose

## Exterior

The Iron Rose occupies an old, broad stone building on one of the Dregs’ busier streets.

A wrought-iron sign hangs above the entrance:

> A black iron rose with petals edged in dark red enamel.

A lantern burns beneath it throughout the night.

The building has:

- narrow reinforced windows
- iron-banded doors
- a guarded main entrance
- a discreet side entrance
- upper shutters capable of being barred from within
- several exits not shown to customers

The house does not need loud advertising.

Everyone in the Dregs knows where it is.

---

## Interior

The Iron Rose is warm, dim, and carefully maintained.

The common room contains:

- a polished bar
- small tables
- curtained booths
- a modest musicians’ alcove
- dark wooden screens
- stairs leading to private rooms
- enough open floor for Lily to reach trouble quickly

The décor favors:

- red cloth
- dark wood
- brass fixtures
- ironwork shaped like thorned vines
- painted flowers beginning to fade around the edges

Nothing is truly luxurious.

Everything visible is meant to suggest that it might be.

The private rooms are clean, secure, and unremarkable. Volika understands that comfort is useful, but privacy is what customers actually pay for.

---

## Services

The Iron Rose provides:

- drink
- meals
- gambling
- bathing
- massage
- companionship
- sex
- private meetings
- overnight lodging
- discreet entrances and exits
- temporary storage
- message delivery
- escorted travel home

A patron may pay simply to talk.

Volika charges well for this.

People reveal more when they believe someone is being paid not to judge them.

---

# House Rules

The rules are displayed behind the front desk in Common, Dwarven, Halfling, and a series of blunt pictograms.

1. **Payment is agreed before service.**
2. **A worker may refuse a customer.**
3. **No weapons are drawn inside the house.**
4. **No worker is followed home.**
5. **No violence against staff or guests.**
6. **Private rooms are entered by invitation only.**
7. **What is overheard inside the Rose remains inside the Rose.**
8. **Volika’s ruling is final.**
9. **Lily is not required to warn anyone twice.**

Volika enforces these rules because customers pay for predictability.

She also knows that allowing one man to brutalize a worker teaches every other worker that the rules are decorative.

Decorative rules are bad for morale.

---

# Lily

[[Lily]] is the Iron Rose’s principal doorwoman, bouncer, and most visible argument against improvisation.

She is a female half-ogre standing between eight and nine feet tall. She must duck beneath the main doorway.

Lily is enormously strong, broad, and built with the heavy, rolling muscle of ogre blood rather than the carved physique of a tournament fighter.

She is not graceful.

She does not need to be.

Lily’s duties include:

- checking weapons
- blocking unwanted entry
- removing violent patrons
- escorting workers
- guarding Volika during negotiations
- carrying injured people to the healer
- lifting damaged furniture while Volika calculates its value

Lily is patient with:

- children
- frightened women
- confused drunks
- people who apologize promptly

She is less patient with men who believe size is the only reason she is dangerous.

Volika is part of the house’s security.

Lily is the part customers notice first.

### Marda “Keys” Venn

[[Marda Keys Venn|Marda “Keys” Venn]] serves as house steward and acting manager of the Iron Rose.

When Volika is away, Marda runs the rooms, staff, payroll, security, and daily business of the house. She holds full authority to refuse customers, close the doors, summon the healer, spend emergency funds, and direct [[Lily]].

Volika trusts Marda because she protects the business.

Marda protects the workers because she remembers what it feels like to be counted as part of one.
# Information and Influence

The Iron Rose hears a great deal.

Its customers include:

- laborers
- caravan guards
- minor merchants
- watchmen
- criminals
- soldiers
- sailors
- servants
- officials
- noble retainers who assume changing cloaks changes faces

Volika hears about:

- incoming watch raids
- missing women
- gang disputes
- stolen cargo
- violent customers
- new drugs
- false recruiters
- visiting mercenaries
- noble indiscretions
- people preparing to flee Bard’s Gate

She does not sell every secret.

A house that sells everything soon has nothing worth hearing.

Volika trades information selectively for:

- coin
- protection
- favors
- medical help
- advance warning
- access to officials
- the removal of a dangerous person

She rarely tells anyone the entire story.

She provides the part that creates the outcome she wants.

# Volika and Wonderful

Volika and [[Wonderful]] have a settled but carefully limited relationship.

They can conduct business.

They can share information concerning danger in the Dregs.

They do not discuss [[Blue Sylvanranth]] unless absolutely necessary.

## Blue, the One Who Got Away

In Volika’s private reckoning, Blue was **the one who got away**.

Volika saw a striking half-orc girl growing up in the Dregs with unusual presence, grace, and the ability to draw every eye in a room.

She marked Blue as a future prospect.

At first, Volika’s interest was mild:

- a compliment
- a small job
- an offer of training
- questions about her future
- observations about what she might someday earn

Wonderful noticed.

Wonderful objected.

Volika backed away briefly, then returned by another route.

She became too persistent.

That was the mistake.

---

## The Iron Rose Incident

The exact details of Wonderful’s confrontation with Volika have grown considerably in the telling.

The facts everyone agrees upon are:

- Wonderful entered the Iron Rose furious.
- She ordered Volika to stop approaching Blue.
- Volika attempted to explain that no improper offer had been made.
- Wonderful was not interested in technical distinctions.
- Volika refused to be ordered about in her own house.
- Lily intervened.
- There was screaming.
- There was blood.
- Furniture broke.
- Wonderful proved considerably tougher than Lily expected.
- Volika finally accepted that Blue was beyond her reach.

No one agrees upon who threw the first blow.

In the Dregs, this usually means everyone did.

### Common Versions

According to various retellings:

- Wonderful threw Lily through the front door.
- Lily threw Wonderful through the bar.
- Wonderful broke a chair over Lily and kept the leg.
- Volika bit Wonderful’s hand badly enough to reach bone.
- Wonderful headbutted Volika into her own accounts.
- Blue watched from the stairs.
- Blue was nowhere near the building.
- Sorek arrived and separated them.
- Sorek arrived, assessed the situation, and left again.
- The iron rose sign was bent into its present shape during the fight.
- The sign was never damaged at all.

The story becomes less reliable with every drink.

Its outcome does not change.

---

## The Settlement

When the fight ended, it ended.

This is the Dregs.

Grudges are expensive, bodies are difficult to hide, and no one benefits from maintaining a feud that has already established its boundaries.

Volika agreed to leave Blue alone.

Wonderful agreed that the matter was closed.

Neither apologized.

Neither asked for payment for the furniture.

They have since cooperated when mutual interests required it.

Volika respects Wonderful.

She also considers Blue a lost opportunity.

She is wise enough not to say this where Wonderful can hear.

---

# The Blood and Sand as Sanctuary

The confrontation over Blue created an unwritten rule:

> **The Blood and Sand is not recruiting ground for the Iron Rose.**

Volika does not approach its girls.

She does not send intermediaries.

She does not make offers through customers.

She does not discuss their wages unless they come to her first, away from the establishment, as adults acting on their own behalf.

This is not a city law.

It is a boundary established through blood, property damage, and the mutual understanding that testing it again would be tiresome.

Volika honors it.

Once she gives her word, she keeps it.

That is one of the better things that can be said about her.

---

# Longjaw and Tinnet

When [[Longjaw]] brought [[Tinnet Tiny|Tinnet]] to the [[Blood and Sand]], he was not merely looking for work or a warm room.

He was choosing sanctuary.

Tinnet was young, half-orc, visibly developing early, and already drawing the wrong sort of attention.

In the Dregs, that combination attracts:

- recruiters
- pimps
- gangs
- possessive employers
- men who decide a frightened girl looks older than she is
- women who assure themselves they are offering something better

Volika was not the worst person who might notice Tinnet.

That was not comforting.

Longjaw knew the story of Blue.

He knew that the Blood and Sand was the one establishment in the district where Volika would not recruit, cultivate, or quietly wait upon a promising young girl.

So he brought Tinnet there.

Not because the Blood and Sand was gentle.

Because its people protected what was theirs, and because Volika Coinmane had already learned exactly how seriously they took that claim.

---

## Volika’s Position on Tinnet

Volika has noticed Tinnet.

She is not blind.

She also does nothing about it.

No questions.

No compliments loaded with future meaning.

No harmless errands.

No quiet offer of training.

The Blood and Sand is sanctuary, and Volika does not recruit there.

If asked directly, she will say:

> “The girl is under their roof.”

That ends the matter.

Privately, Volika may still assess Tinnet’s future in the same automatic way she assesses everyone who crosses her sight.

Business habits do not vanish merely because they are not acted upon.

But she will not pursue the lead.

Wonderful paid for that boundary in blood.

Volika considers the account settled.

---

# Relationship with the Blood and Sand

Volika is civil toward the Blood and Sand and generally respects its staff.

The two establishments are not direct competitors.

The Blood and Sand sells:

- drink
- food
- fighting
- noise
- spectacle
- rough fellowship

The Iron Rose sells:

- privacy
- attention
- discretion
- controlled intimacy

They exchange warnings concerning:

- violent customers
- gangs
- raids
- missing workers
- dangerous drugs
- predatory recruiters
- trouble moving through the ward

Volika considers the Blood and Sand badly organized, structurally abused, and sentimental about its people.

She also knows its sentiment has teeth.

That earns respect in the Dregs.

---

# Relationship with the Authorities

Volika cooperates with the city when cooperation serves the Iron Rose.

She provides information about:

- murder
- trafficking
- forced prostitution
- attacks against her staff
- threats to the district
- people attempting to sell children

She does not provide:

- client lists
- private confessions
- lovers’ names
- casual blackmail material
- information merely because an officer asks loudly

Corrupt watchmen dislike her.

Competent watchmen dislike her more carefully.

Volika pays enough tax to claim legitimacy and keeps enough money hidden to remain a citizen of the Dregs.

---

# Moral Character

Volika is not a savior.

She is not a trafficker in chains.

She occupies the large, ugly territory between those things.

She:

- keeps her bargains
- pays as agreed
- permits workers to refuse customers
- protects her employees
- does not sell people
- does not tolerate violence against her staff
- profits from desperation
- recruits the vulnerable
- mistakes improved exploitation for justice
- believes a better offer is the same thing as a good one

Her strongest defense is that many people are safer with her than without her.

Her greatest condemnation is that she thinks this settles the matter.

> [!quote] Volika’s Blind Spot
> “I did not make her hungry.”
>
> Volika considers this a complete moral defense.
>
> It is not.

# Using Volika in Play

Volika works best when the party needs help that is useful, morally compromised, and difficult to obtain elsewhere.

She may:

- hide an abused woman for a night
- provide a clean room to a former slave
- identify a trafficker
- recognize a false recruiter
- arrange discreet medical care
- warn the party about a dangerous patron
- offer employment to someone with no better choices
- expect payment or obligation afterward
- defend her conduct with arguments that are difficult to defeat at the table

The party should not uncover a hidden dungeon full of chained workers.

That would make the problem easy.

The Iron Rose should instead contain people who:

- chose to work there
- dislike the work
- appreciate the protection
- resent Volika
- trust Volika
- owe Volika
- earn more than they could elsewhere
- cannot afford to leave yet
- might defend the house from outsiders who claim to be rescuing them

Volika’s house functions because the choices surrounding it are worse.

Destroying it without replacing what it provides will not save its workers.

It will merely return them to the market without Lily standing downstairs.
