# The Insolence

_“Fastest damn ship I ever set foot on.” — [[Reigar]]_

**The Insolence** is the personal ship of [[Reigar]], a sleek smuggling vessel of the xebec type known for extraordinary speed, maneuverability, and clever modifications designed for evading customs ships and naval patrols.

Though technically a smuggler and runner, the vessel is treated by Reigar with the same pride and affection he shows toward his family.

> “Best woman in my life after my wife and my daughter.”

---


> “The Insolence is her proper name.”

> “But the crew just calls her _the Insolent Girl_.”
# Ship Type

**Xebec** (pronounced _ZEE-beck_)

A long, narrow lateen-rigged vessel combining sails and oars. Xebecs are famous for their speed, shallow draft, and ability to maneuver in confined coastal waters, making them ideal for corsairs, smugglers, and fast traders.

Typical characteristics include:

- three **lateen sails**    
- **long narrow hull** built for speed    
- **shallow draft** allowing river travel    
- **oars** for maneuvering when wind fails    
- excellent **tacking performance**    

Reigar’s ship is an especially well-handled example of the type.

---

# Physical Description

The Insolence is a **long, lean vessel** roughly 80–90 feet in length with a narrow beam designed for speed.

Notable features include:

• black pitch-sealed hull  
• brass and copper trim worn smooth by sea air  
• three tall lateen-rigged masts  
• long forward bowsprit  
• two banks of concealed oar ports  
• a small **fox-head figurehead** carved at the prow

The ship sits low in the water, giving it a predatory profile when under sail.

---

# Command Style

Unlike most captains who command from the stern, Reigar famously **runs the ship from the bow**.

A custom **secondary tiller linkage** runs along the deck rail, allowing him to feel the rudder through a rope steering line connected to the main steering oar.

This lets him:

- see reefs and currents first
- judge wind shifts directly    
- call sail changes instantly    

According to Reigar:

> “Hard to run a ship from the bow unless you can steer from there.”

---

# Maneuverability

The Insolence is legendary among sailors for her handling.

Reigar once described her ability to **tack inside her own wake**, an extremely tight maneuver possible only with a well-balanced hull and experienced sail handling.

> “Quarter rudder. Lateen hauled tight. Oars biting water.  
> She’d pivot like a knife.”

This maneuver allowed the ship to escape larger vessels that required much wider turning radii.

---

# Hidden Compartments

The ship contains a carefully constructed **false cargo deck** within the hold.

Below the visible cargo floor lies a hidden compartment used for smuggling high-value or illegal goods.

The design:

- matches the curvature of the hull    
- passes inspection taps    
- is concealed beneath removable cargo racks    

Only crew familiar with the ship know how to access it.

Reigar’s philosophy:

> “Most captains hide cargo in crates.  
> Better trick is hiding the space.”

---

# Armament

The Insolence carries **no permanent deck artillery**.

Reigar prefers to keep the ship light, fast, and unobtrusive rather than visibly armed like a corsair or warship.

According to him:

> “Weapons make a ship slower and customs officers nervous.”

Instead, the ship relies on:

• speed and maneuverability  
• shallow-water escape routes  
• skilled archers  
• dangerous passengers

When necessary, the ship has carried **temporary ballistae or deck weapons during wartime**, but these are mounted only when absolutely required and removed afterward to keep the vessel light.

Reigar’s preferred defense remains simple:

> “Best weapon on a ship like this is the wind.”

---

# The Tally Marks

Along the stern rail are carved dozens of small marks.

Each one represents a **customs ship successfully outrun**.

Reigar refers to them simply as:

> “Old arguments I won.”

---

# Famous Escape

One of the most famous stories involving The Insolence concerns a customs galley that attempted to intercept the ship at the mouth of a narrow river.

Reigar deliberately sailed upriver toward a sharp bend.

When the galley followed, its wide turning radius trapped it against a mud bank.

The Insolence pivoted around the bend and escaped.

> “Galley needed half the river to turn.  
> The Insolence just danced around the corner.”

---

# Reputation

Among sailors and smugglers alike, The Insolence is considered:

- exceptionally fast    
- difficult to pursue    
- commanded by a dangerously capable captain

To naval authorities, the ship is something else entirely.

A persistent problem.

---
## Crew

The crew of _The Insolence_ are not pirates, though many naval officers have used the word when the ship slipped past their patrols.

They are **professional sailors**, smugglers, and coastal traders who specialize in speed, stealth, and discretion. Most have sailed with [[Reigar]] for years and understand both the vessel’s unusual handling and the quiet rules that govern her trade.

The ship normally sails with a **lean crew**, relying on experience rather than numbers. The core officers have remained with Reigar long enough that each knows the ship almost as well as the captain himself.

### First Mate — Marek Voss

A broad-shouldered human sailor with weathered features and a voice that carries across a gale.

Voss handles discipline, cargo organization, and day-to-day running of the ship when Reigar is busy navigating or dealing with clients. He is steady, practical, and extremely difficult to rattle. Crewmen say that if Reigar is the ship’s instincts, Voss is its backbone.

### Helmsman — Selene Arkwright

A quiet, sharp-eyed navigator who has steered _The Insolence_ through reefs, sandbars, and river mouths that most captains would never attempt.

Selene handles the stern tiller and sail coordination, translating Reigar’s shouted commands from the bow into precise movements of rudder and rigging. Her ability to read currents and shifting winds is one of the reasons the ship can perform the famously tight maneuvers that have embarrassed more than one pursuing warship.

### Wind Reader — Corvin Hale

A minor hedge wizard who serves as the ship’s **weather hand**.

Hale’s magical talents are modest, but his knowledge of wind magic and atmospheric signs has saved the ship countless times. He knows a handful of spells related to wind, weather, and air currents, and uses them sparingly to steady sails, encourage favorable gusts, or help the ship escape a dangerous calm.

Among the crew he is often called **“the Whisperer,”** because when the sails begin to fill unexpectedly, someone inevitably mutters that Corvin has been talking to the wind again.

---

During the **Greyhawk Wars**, _The Insolence_ and her crew briefly operated under letters of marque as **privateers**, targeting enemy shipping in support of allied fleets. Reigar never pretended to enjoy the work.

> “Chasing merchants for a crown’s blessing isn’t much different from chasing them without one.”

When the war ended, the ship returned to her usual trade: fast cargo runs, discreet deliveries, and outrunning anyone who asked too many questions.

Among sailors along the coast, the reputation of _The Insolence_ is simple:

Fast ship.  
Clever captain.  
Crew that knows when to fight — and when to disappear.
