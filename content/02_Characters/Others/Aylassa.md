# Aylassa

# Aylassa

> “Aylassa? She smiles too much for a killer, which is how you know she’s dangerous. Grim assassins want you afraid. Cheerful ones have already decided how the story ends.”
> — Sir Sorek Redmarch

> “Caledrin is adorable. He thinks murder should have minutes, witnesses, and proper punctuation.”
> — Aylassa, on [[Caledrin Vale]]

## Overview

**Aylassa** is the chosen identity of **Vaelrissa of the Glimmering Court**, a former high elven **Silver Veil** guardian-attendant from the Twin Kingdoms.

She is a selective assassin, former court shadow, minor noble by some deeply suspicious paperwork, and romantic partner of [[Thranduil Stormarrow]]. Together, she and Thranduil form a cheerful murder team with some ethics, which is honestly the most terrifying version. Monsters at least have the decency to be predictable.

Aylassa is playful, bright, dangerous, emotionally particular, and deeply lethal. She does not treat killing as grim necessity. She treats it as craft, judgment, theater, flirtation, and occasionally housekeeping.

Her morality is real.

It is not clean.

## Appearance

Aylassa is tall for an elf, lean and graceful, with the compact strength of a trained court killer rather than a battlefield brute. She moves like someone who spent centuries pretending not to occupy space, then decided she was done with that nonsense.

Her eyes are mismatched: one green, one blue. She often works veiled, hooded, or theatrically overdressed, depending on whether she wants to be ignored, remembered, or blamed by name.

## The Silver Veil

Vaelrissa was born into the hidden servant-guardian structure of the high elven courts.

The **Silver Veil** is drawn largely from second and third daughters of minor noble houses: noble enough to stand near power, disposable enough to vanish into service. They serve as handmaidens, attendants, watchers, and quiet blades within the private spaces of the Glimmering Court.

Among humans, this might be called noble service.

Among honest people, it starts looking suspiciously like a slave caste with better embroidery.

Silver Veil agents are trained in:

- Rapier, dagger, and buckler
- Grappling and restraint
- Etiquette and courtly invisibility
- Counter-poison and alchemical basics
- Silent observation
- Protective violence in confined spaces

They are not meant to be seen.

They are meant to be useful.

Aylassa eventually became very tired of both.

## The Fusion

During the **Great Goblin Uprising**, Princess [[Princess Sylvéndil]] was mortally threatened or wounded while [[Teryn]] of the [[Bronze Wind]] fought to protect her. Vaelrissa, as Silver Veil, was close enough to the princess to be caught in the disaster.

An old court mage attempted a forbidden, corrupted version of the ancestor-golem preservation rites. The spell was meant for urns, stone, and ancestral vessels — not living flesh, not dying guardians, and definitely not three souls clinging to one collapsing moment.

The rite caught:

- **Princess Sylvéndil** — the heir, sheltered, romantic, and politically vital
- **Teryn** — Bronze Wind shield-sister, bodyguard, and living weapon
- **Vaelrissa** — Silver Veil shadow-attendant, later known as Aylassa

The result was the **Chorus**: three distinct souls entangled within one body, forming the gestalt identity later known as **Aylassa**.

## The Name Aylassa

The name **Aylassa** was not the name she was born to.

**Vaelrissa** belonged to the Glimmering Court: to silk corridors, silent service, blood-oaths, and the polite fiction that some lives are more ornamental than free.

**Aylassa** came later.

At first, it was the name of the gestalt — the thing made from Vaelrissa, Teryn, and Sylvéndil when the old spell caught all three and refused to let any of them die properly.

But after the separation, Vaelrissa did not return to her old name.

She kept Aylassa.

Not because it was older.

Because it was hers.

## Current Soul Status

The old Chorus has been partially resolved.

- [[Teryn]] is now her own woman.
- [[Princess Sylvéndil]] remains in a phylactery.
- Vaelrissa has chosen to continue living as **Aylassa**.

Aylassa is therefore not merely a mask, illness, or residue of the fusion. She is the chosen continuation of Vaelrissa’s life after the metaphysical wreckage.

She is what survived.

## Assassin’s Code

Aylassa has spent years as a hired assassin, though “hired” is not always the cleanest description. She often accepts low-paying contracts when the victim deserves it, the story pleases her, or the injustice is too ugly to leave alone.

She prefers targets such as:

- Abusers
- Oath-breakers
- Tyrants
- Blackmailers
- Predators
- False heirs
- Men who hide behind title, coin, or locked doors

This should not be mistaken for mercy.

Her code applies most strongly to the named target. Guards, hired blades, retainers, and loyal fools who stand between her and the work are treated as part of the architecture: unfortunate, movable, and sometimes messy.

She is not a hero.

She is an assassin with preferences.

## The Bertram Contract

The party first encountered Aylassa when she had been hired to force a duel and kill [[Bertram Moffet]].

The job failed not because she was defeated, but because the emotional truth of Bertram and [[Cornelia Bordella]]’s love story got under her skin. Once Aylassa realized the contract had been morally misrepresented, she refused it.

This ties directly into [[Caledrin Vale]] and the Gray Circle’s code.

Caledrin’s reaction:

> “Then it was not a contract. It was bait.”

That line defines Vesper perfectly. To Caledrin, assassination requires proof, cause, and clean framing. A contract built on a lie is not professional work. It is bait, dirty business, or someone else’s trap.

Aylassa, naturally, respects this while also making fun of him forever.

## Caledrin Vale and the Gray Circle

Aylassa has ties to [[Caledrin Vale]], the assassin-fixer known by the title **Vesper**. Caledrin was once [[Thranduil Stormarrow]]’s handler and elimination coordinator during the Forest War, and now brokers freelance assassinations through the Gray Circle.

Their relationship is professional respect with mutual exasperation.

Caledrin sees Aylassa as too capable to ignore and too emotionally particular to handle like a standard contractor. She is brilliant, lethal, unreliable when romance or symbolic injustice enters the room, and very reliable once personally convinced.

A private Vesper-style assessment might read:

> **Aylassa** — Accepts moral motive over fee. Unreliable if romance, children, or symbolic injustice complicate the work. Extremely reliable once personally convinced. Collateral discipline: variable. Do not assign to quiet succession matters unless theatrical fallout is acceptable.

Aylassa’s view of him:

> “Caledrin is adorable. He thinks murder should have minutes, witnesses, and proper punctuation.”

## Work for the Crown

Aylassa has also done work for King Rimmand.

Most notably, she assassinated **Malvo** in the capital after his criminal operation became too open for the king to tolerate. Rimmand is a realist: crime cannot be abolished, but it can be forced back into the shadows.

Aylassa delivered the message directly.

Malvo’s head and hands were left on his desk.

The Crown did not declare war on crime.

It reminded crime to lower its voice.

## Lady Silvershire

Aylassa holds the minor title **Lady Silvershire**, a bit of legal absurdity she appears to find much funnier than anyone else does.

She produced the documentation during the Lord’s Muster when challenged on why she was seated among lesser nobles, leaving [[Dana Sylvanranth]] to read the parchment aloud and discover that yes, apparently, this woman had paperwork.

When asked how she acquired the title, Aylassa explained:

> “I did a favor for a nobleman. Nothing scandalous. Just pruning the family tree.”

The title seems to come with a small holding somewhere in Araisal lands, enough to provide income, plausible lodging, and the sort of obscure legal standing that lets Aylassa appear exactly where she should not be.

As usual, she treats this less like a responsibility and more like a knife she found in a desk drawer.

## Romance with Thranduil Stormarrow

Aylassa and [[Thranduil Stormarrow]] are romantically involved.

Their relationship is not soft in any normal courtly sense. It is bright, violent, feral, and weirdly sincere. They are a cheerful murder team with boundaries, which is more than can be said for half the nobility.

All three voices of the old Chorus loved Thranduil in different ways:

- Sylvéndil loved him as an idealized romance.
- Teryn saw him through duty, purity, and the elven cause.
- Aylassa loves him recklessly, joyfully, and without shame.

Now that Aylassa has chosen herself, that love remains hers.

They are dangerous together because they share a similar instinct: some people need killing, and the world is cleaner when the work is done by someone competent.

The difference is tone.

Thranduil is a blade in the dark.

Aylassa is a blade in the dark that giggles.

## Personality

Aylassa is:

- Cheerful
- Theatrical
- Morally selective
- Flirtatious
- Razor-sharp
- Unsettlingly honest when amused
- Capable of sudden tenderness
- Capable of sudden murder
- Much more thoughtful than she pretends

She does not act haunted in the expected way. She does not brood in corners whispering about blood. She smiles, waves, brings sweets, makes jokes, tosses cookies like throwing knives, and then says something so casually horrific that the room needs a minute.

Her horror comes from the brightness.

## Sorek Redmarch Take

> “There’s assassins who kill because coin changed hands, and there’s assassins who kill because the world offended them. Aylassa is the second kind, which sounds better until you remember she’s the one deciding what offended the world.”

## Table Notes

Aylassa works best when played as charming first and horrifying second.

Do not make her grim. Grim is boring. Grim is for men who wear too much black and write poetry badly.

Aylassa should be warm, funny, and casually lethal. She can be kind. She can be moved. She can refuse a contract for moral reasons. She can also kill three hired guards on the way out and complain that one of them bled on her shoes.

That contradiction is the character.
