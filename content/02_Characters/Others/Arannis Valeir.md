# Arannis Valeir

![[Arannis Valeir.png|640]]
**Race:** High Elf  
**Age:** Likely over three centuries  
**Role:** Senior teaching aide, research assistant, academic gatekeeper  
**Affiliation:** [[University of Magery]], [[Vianola Erinleaf]]

Arannis Valeir is the quiet constant beside [[Vianola Erinleaf]]: senior aide, research memory, lecture witness, and the only living person permitted to interrupt her writing without risking academic execution.

He is soft-spoken, exacting, unfailingly polite, and possessed of the kind of memory that makes other scholars nervous. He remembers lectures, citations, corrections, arguments, student errors, and which cup Vianola left her cherry brandy beside three nights ago.

He is not waiting for Vianola to notice him.

That would imply he has been standing still.

He has not.

## Demeanor

Arannis has the serene patience of a man who has watched three generations of students discover the same wrong answer and gently escorted each of them away from it.

He does not raise his voice.

He rarely needs to.

He speaks with precise courtesy, but his politeness is not softness. It is structure. A student who lies to him will not be humiliated, but will discover, five minutes later, that they have admitted everything necessary while being offered tea.

He is not dramatic.

This makes him extremely useful.

## Magical Aptitude

Arannis is a good wizard.

Not a great one.

Vianola says this without insult.

Almost with affection.

He is competent, disciplined, and careful. His strengths are support magic, ward-maintenance, spell analysis, detection work, note-preservation, anti-tamper protections, and classroom safety protocols.

He is not a battlefield mage.  
He is not a grand theorist.  
He is not a rival to Norwin Stoneeye or Vianola Erinleaf.

He is, however, an extraordinarily good scholar.

That distinction matters.

Where Vianola sees the dangerous structure of a spell at once, Arannis remembers every prior version of the argument that produced it. Where Norwin leaps three bridges ahead and hopes the river respects him, Arannis checks whether the first bridge was built according to code.

His magic is careful, conservative, and almost annoyingly reliable.

Vianola values him because he is not trying to be impressive.

This makes him rare.

## Academic Function

Arannis keeps Vianola’s world from collapsing under the weight of its own precision.

He manages:

- lecture notes
- research correspondence
- student petitions
- ward schedules
- restricted text access
- practical demonstrations
- visitor triage
- appointments Vianola claims not to need
- apologies Vianola refuses to write until he drafts the first three versions

He is not merely a secretary.

He is the buffer between Vianola’s mind and the thousand stupidities that would otherwise consume it.

## Marriage

Arannis is married to **Lady [[Maelith Vaelorn]]**, a high elf botanist and long-cycle field researcher attached to Araisal’s old arboretum circles.

Their marriage is considered unusually healthy by elven standards, which means they are often apart for years at a time, correspond in letters too dense for sane people, and reunite every decade or so with the quiet intensity of people who remember that time is a thing to spend, not hoard.

Their separations are not estrangement.

They are rhythm.

Maelith studies living systems across long spans: forest recovery, old-growth root memory, magical bloom cycles, and the way elven-tended gardens become political documents if left alone for three centuries.

Arannis studies Vianola’s lectures, students, research habits, and the thousand small disasters that occur when brilliant people are permitted to schedule their own time.

Both consider the other’s work important.

Both are correct.

## Relationship with Vianola

Arannis’s loyalty to Vianola is not romantic hunger.

It is professional devotion, personal affection, and the particular elven talent for giving thirty years to something without calling it sacrifice.

He has chosen to remain her aide because her work matters, because she matters, and because very few people are willing to stand near the machinery of will and say, calmly:

> “No, Professor, you have already had enough brandy to answer that letter.”

Vianola permits this.

Which is answer enough.

He knew Vianola’s human husband, **Tomas Veyr**, and respected him deeply. After Tomas died, Arannis remained not as a suitor, not as a replacement, and not as a ghost at the window.

He stayed because some friendships become architecture.

And because someone had to remember where the furniture was before grief narrowed the house.

## How Vianola Describes Him

> “Arannis is a good wizard.”

A pause.

> “Not a great one.”

She says this without contempt.

> “Great wizards are often exhausting. They require supervision, moral bracing, and occasionally masonry.”

A faint dry edge.

> “Arannis is better than great in several practical respects.”

Another pause.

> “He is accurate.”

## If Beringer Asks

Beringer’s eyes move briefly toward the door.

> “And Arannis?”

Vianola’s expression sharpens by a degree.

> “Married.”

A pause.

> “Happily, before you ask the next clumsy question.”

Beringer inclines his head, accepting the rebuke.

> “Separated?”

> “Geographically. Not emotionally.”

A faint edge of amusement touches her mouth.

> “His wife studies forests that take eighty years to finish a sentence. Arannis finds this romantic.”

A pause.

> “I suppose it is.”

Then, softer:

> “He is loyal to me because he believes my work matters.”

Another beat.

> “And because someone must remember where I put things when I am pretending not to need help.”

The professor returns fully.

> “Do not reduce loyalty to courtship, Sir Beringer. It is a common error, but an ugly one.”

## Use in Play

Arannis is useful when the party needs:

- a quiet academic contact
- access to Vianola without being eaten alive by scheduling
- clarification of enchantment theory
- practical magical safety advice
- a measured voice in a room full of geniuses behaving badly
- someone who knows Vianola well enough to translate her anger into actionable advice

He is not flashy.

He is not weak.

He is the man who has spent decades standing beside one of Gorundia’s sharpest minds and has never once needed to become louder to be heard.

## Quote

> “The professor is available in theory. In practice, I recommend telling me what you broke first.”
