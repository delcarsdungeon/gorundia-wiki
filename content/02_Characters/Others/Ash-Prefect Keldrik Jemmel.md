Hell yes. This needs to sound like a man who thinks damnation is a promotion package.

Here’s a polished version:

> “At last… I have been recognized.”
> 
> “Not mocked. Not dismissed. Not paraded through court like some waddling relic for braver men to sneer at.”
> 
> “I am stronger now. Faster. Sharper. My name is spoken with fear, as it always should have been.”
> 
> “And what was the price? A wife who scorned me? A son who forgot his place? Small coins for greatness.”
> 
> “You made me ridiculous. You made me small. You laughed while Amadis rose, while Kharne was handed honors, while men beneath me were lifted above me by luck, blood, and spectacle.”
> 
> “But I remember.”
> 
> “I remember every smirk. Every insult. Every little mercy offered like I was some broken dog at your boot.”
> 
> “No more.”
> 
> “The Iron Covenant has given me what Gorundia would not. Power. Respect. Purpose.”
> 
> “And now, by ash and chain and burning blood… you will pay the debt you owe me.”

A more table-punching, start-combat version:

> “Finally, I am seen.”
> 
> “Not pitied. Not mocked. Seen.”
> 
> “I am stronger than I was. Faster. Known. Feared. Respected.”
> 
> “All it cost me was a wife who scorned me and a son stupid enough to disrespect me.”
> 
> “Cheap. So very cheap.”
> 
> “You dishonored me. Again and again. You made me a fool while Beringer climbed and Orin was crowned like some frontier butcher-king.”
> 
> “I have carried every insult in my teeth.”
> 
> “And now I spit them back as fire.”
> 
> “Come then. Laugh again.”
> 
> “I want to hear it when your throats fill with ash.”

And the really smug bastard version, because apparently we’re letting this man become a boss fight instead of a cautionary pamphlet:

> “Do you see me now?”
> 
> “You should. I paid dearly enough.”
> 
> “My wife is gone. My son is gone. My old name is ash. And in return, I have become what I was always meant to be.”
> 
> “Stronger. Faster. Respected.”
> 
> “Not loved. Love is the lie weak men tell themselves when they cannot command obedience.”
> 
> “I wanted honor. You gave me mockery.”
> 
> “I wanted my due. You gave it to Amadis. To Kharne. To every preening favorite with better stories and cleaner sins.”
> 
> “But I remember.”
> 
> “I remember the laughter.”
> 
> “I remember the shame.”
> 
> “And now the shame belongs to you.”

My pick for the encounter: use the second one if combat starts right after. Use the third one if he gets a slow entrance from shadow and flame, because naturally Sir Keldrik Jemmel would rehearse his evil speech in the mirror like a divorced warlock with unresolved LinkedIn energy.
