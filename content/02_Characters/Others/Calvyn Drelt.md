# Calvyn Drelt

**Title:** Magister-Executor of Writs, Guild-Certified Conjurist, Advocate Third Circle of the Capital Lawhall  
**Race:** Human  
**Age:** Late 30s  
**Affiliation:** Capital Merchants’ Guild, Legal Division, Inner Ring  
**Known Alias:** _“The Quill with Teeth”_  
**Status:** Active  
**Function:** Contract enforcement, conjured writ service, guild leverage, legal strangulation of inconvenient people  
**Primary Theater:** Capital, Bard’s Gate corridor, Stormarrow dispute

> “A sword ends an argument. A writ makes the argument survive long enough to ruin your grandchildren.”  
> — Calvyn Drelt

---

## Overview

Calvyn Drelt is what happens when a lawyer learns conjuration and no one has the moral courage to stop him.

He serves the **Capital Merchants’ Guild Legal Division** as a senior enforcement advocate, specializing in binding agreements, trade-route obligations, debt instruments, guild charters, and the cheerful little technicalities by which powerful people steal whole roads without ever drawing a blade.

Calvyn is attached to the disputed **Chartered Agreement of Supply & Transit** involving the **Capital Merchants’ Guild**, the **Free City of Bard’s Gate**, and the **Barony of Stormarrow**. The contract claims Bard’s Gate has ratified the agreement, while Orin Kharne’s assent remains “pending,” a phrase doing enough dirty work to deserve its own tiny gallows. The agreement also attempts to secure Guild passage through Stormarrow without levy or inspection, impose routing through Guild logistics stations, assign a 22% stewardship fee, and extract an 18,000 silver crown “Peace Duty” tied to the Tunneler Incident.

Calvyn’s job is not merely to argue that the agreement is valid.

His job is to make resistance expensive.

---

## Appearance

Calvyn is impeccably groomed in the way of men who believe appearance is a jurisdiction.

He favors severe dark coats, high collars, polished boots, narrow gloves, and a silver chain of office bearing the seal of the Capital Merchants’ Guild. His hair is always neat. His beard, if worn, is trimmed so precisely that it looks notarized.

He does not raise his voice.

He does not gesture wildly.

He does not threaten in the vulgar sense.

He simply explains consequences with the calm patience of a man watching someone drown in paperwork he personally weighted.

His eyes are pale gray-brown, often compared to wet parchment or old coins. Neither comparison improves the room.

---

## Public Role

Calvyn is a licensed advocate of the **Third Circle of the Capital Lawhall**, giving him authority to plead before major mercantile courts, represent chartered guild interests, and certify enforcement writs under guild seal.

His conjuration certification allows him to:

- summon bonded messengers;
    
- produce duplicate writs under seal;
    
- conjure temporary document-chests and evidence boxes;
    
- magically serve notices across controlled distances;
    
- call minor oath-witness spirits in contract disputes;
    
- preserve ink, seals, and signatures against tampering.
    

He is not a battlefield wizard.

This is worse.

Battlefield wizards at least have the decency to explode things. Calvyn makes your own signature explode years later.

---

## Personality

Calvyn is courteous, polished, and absolutely poisonous.

He treats open anger as a confession of weakness. He lets other people shout, threaten, posture, and overcommit. Then he repeats their worst sentence back to them in cleaner grammar and asks whether they wish it entered into record.

He has a gift for finding the single vulnerable clause in a man’s life.

He respects intelligence, discipline, and preparation. He despises improvisation unless he is the one doing it. He has no patience for nobles who assume blood outranks paper, and even less for common criminals who think violence makes them complicated.

His humor is dry, faint, and usually only detectable after the victim has left the room and realized they were insulted.

---

## Methods

Calvyn prefers pressure in layers.

First comes courtesy.

Then comes clarification.

Then comes documentation.

Then comes the sealed notice.

Then comes seizure, injunction, lien, or summons.

Then, if the target remains difficult, comes a carefully selected sheriff, guild bailiff, creditor, magistrate, or priest of Pholtus who has been waiting for this exact moment like a spider with a law degree.

His favorite weapon is not the contract itself, but the secondary obligations attached to it: delivery ledgers, certification marks, witness rolls, toll exemptions, tax declarations, convoy manifests, and route protection claims.

Where another man sees a caravan, Calvyn sees seventeen points of liability wearing boots.

---

## Connection to the Stormarrow Contract

Calvyn did not necessarily draft the entire Chartered Agreement, but he has almost certainly reviewed, strengthened, or weaponized it.

The contract’s most dangerous elements bear his style:

**Pending Assent Language:**  
The agreement treats Orin Kharne’s ratification as expected in good faith, creating a legal presumption that refusal is not neutral but obstructive.

**No Inspection Clause:**  
Stormarrow officials may “observe but not interfere” with Guild shipments unless backed by royal writ or Guild certificate. This places the Guild in the delightful position of certifying itself. Civilization was a mistake.

**Stewardship Fee:**  
A fee of up to 22% of declared value, subject to reappraisal at Guild discretion. In other words, “we decide what you owe after we decide what your goods were worth.”

**Peace Duty:**  
The 18,000 silver crown payment is framed as shared responsibility rather than penalty, avoiding the appearance of direct accusation while still bleeding the target.

**Amnesty Hook:**  
The Guild waives claims over Bard’s Gate losses only if the agreement is fully executed. This turns refusal into retroactive exposure.

**Auto-Renewal and Seizure:**  
Twelve years, automatic renewal, early withdrawal punished by suspension of cargo rights and seizure of waylaid goods. A contract clause with the bedside manner of a bear trap.

## Legal Style

Calvyn’s courtroom style is elegant and predatory.

He rarely accuses directly. Instead, he builds a lattice of facts and lets the implication sit there, ugly and unavoidable.

Favorite phrases:

> “Let us not confuse inconvenience with injustice.”

> “Your objection is noted. It is not, however, persuasive.”

> “The document does not require your enthusiasm.”

> “Intent is admirable. Seal is better.”

> “If my lord wished not to be bound, my lord might have avoided conduct consistent with assent.”

That last one is one of his sharper knives: **conduct consistent with assent**.

If Orin permits even partial Guild passage, accepts even one shipment, fails to formally reject the filing, or lets a local officer cooperate with Guild routing, Calvyn may argue that Stormarrow has functionally accepted the contract.

Because apparently commerce needed sneak attacks.

## Magic

Calvyn is a practical conjurist, not a flashy one.

His spellwork supports his legal profession:

- **Summon Writ-Bearer:** calls a minor bound courier spirit or conjured legal attendant to serve notice.
    
- **Seal Preservation:** prevents alteration or destruction of signed documents.
    
- **Duplicate Instrument:** creates a temporary certified copy of a contract or writ.
    
- **Evidence Chest:** conjures a locked container keyed to his sigil.
    
- **Oath-Echo:** calls forth a brief magical resonance from a witnessed promise or sworn statement.
    
- **Ink of Record:** makes a written statement resistant to water, fire, and mundane erasure.
    
- **Summon Bailiff’s Mark:** places a visible arcane notice on property subject to claim.
    

He can defend himself, but his real threat is procedural. Killing him would be easy compared to undoing what he filed before breakfast.

## Reputation

Among guild lawyers, Calvyn is admired.

Among merchants, he is feared.

Among nobles, he is considered useful until he is pointed at them, at which point everyone suddenly rediscovers their deep ancestral respect for “custom.”

The nickname **“The Quill with Teeth”** began among junior clerks after Calvyn dismantled a smuggling defense by proving the accused had mislabeled the cargo in three incompatible ways across four ledgers. The man was fined, stripped of license, and technically still owns two warehouses he is forbidden to enter.

No one uses the nickname to Calvyn’s face.

Not because he would rage.

Because he would smile.

## Allies and Contacts

**Capital Merchants’ Guild, Legal Division:**  
His home institution and power base. They supply clerks, seals, witnesses, and influence.

**Inner Ring Commercial Families:**  
Calvyn is trusted by old-money merchant houses that prefer clean paperwork over open bribery. Naturally, this means their bribery has better stationery.

**Capital Lawhall:**  
He is not top rank, but Third Circle gives him reach. He has mentors above him and clients beneath him.

**Guild Conjurers’ Registry:**  
Maintains his certification and occasionally uses him to investigate improper magical contracting.

**Certain Pholtine Legalists:**  
He is not necessarily devout, but he shares their fondness for order, exact language, and making guilty people sweat through linen.

---

## Enemies

**Independent Teamsters and Local Brokers:**  
Calvyn’s contracts tend to centralize trade under Guild systems. Small operators hate him, usually for excellent reasons.

**Stormarrow Interests:**  
If Orin or Braga pushes back, Calvyn becomes a natural antagonist: not a villain in a cloak, but a man trying to turn their roads into someone else’s revenue stream.

**Bard’s Gate Underworld:**  
His arrival threatens anyone profiting from informal transit, undercounted cargo, or “creative” tariff practices.

**Honest Reeves:**  
A reeve who takes local authority seriously will hate the Guild’s attempt to bypass inspection. Guaco, for instance, may find Calvyn fascinating in the same way one finds a venomous snake fascinating while backing away with a chair.

## Use at the Table

Calvyn is not a sword fight.

He is a pressure scene.

Use him when the party needs to feel that law can be weaponized as effectively as steel. He should be dangerous without being obviously criminal. His strength is that much of what he does may be technically legal, or at least legal-shaped enough to survive first contact.

He is ideal for:

- serving Orin formal notice;
    
- trying to bind Stormarrow through partial compliance;
    
- threatening caravans with seizure;
    
- confronting Guaco over local reeve authority;
    
- dragging Bard’s Gate into Capital Guild oversight;
    
- forcing Beringer to choose between royal order and local autonomy;
    
- making Braga want to hit him, which is understandable but legally unhelpful.

## Scene Hook: The Served Writ

Calvyn arrives with three clerks, two guild bailiffs, and a sealed ebony document case.

He does not request audience.

He presents notice.

If blocked, he politely asks the blocking officer’s name, title, superior, and authority to refuse receipt.

Then he says:

> “Thank you. Refusal has now been witnessed.”

And one of the clerks writes it down.

Not dramatically.

Worse.

Accurately.

## GM Notes

Calvyn should not be played as mustache-twirling evil. He is worse and more useful: professionally correct, personally cold, and genuinely convinced that commerce prevents chaos.

He may even be right in the abstract.

Unfortunately, humans discovered abstraction and immediately used it to justify fees.

His weak points:

- He relies on legitimacy.
    
- He cannot operate well where royal authority clearly overrules Guild charter.
    
- He hates informal power he cannot document.
    
- He underestimates people who are willing to take reputational damage to do the right thing.
    
- He is vulnerable to proof that the Guild acted in bad faith before Stormarrow’s assent.
    

His strongest angle is partial acceptance. If Orin’s people accepted goods, allowed passage, used Guild routes, paid fees, or failed to formally object, Calvyn can build a case.

His nightmare opponent is someone like Guaco: legally empowered enough to interfere, criminally literate enough to see the scam, and shameless enough to enjoy the fight.
