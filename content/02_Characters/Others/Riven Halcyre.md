# Riven Halcyre

![[Riven Halcyre.png|320]]

> “There are wizards who enter a room and make sure everyone knows it. Robes, rings, perfume, opinions.  
> Halcyre enters a room and makes the room doubt itself.”
> — [[Sorek Redmarch]]

## Basic Information

Born: Unknown  
Race: Half-elf  
House: Unconfirmed  
Known Name: Riven Halcyre  
Alias: The Veilhand  
Affiliation: The Crown; allegedly [[The Ninth Seal]]  
Profession: Wizard, royal operative, infiltration specialist  
Specialty: Silence, invisibility, ward-breaking, controlled movement, quiet extraction  
Status: Active  
Current Assignment: Attached to the Blackvale infiltration team  

## Overview

Riven Halcyre is a half-elven wizard in royal service, though “service” is a word that becomes slippery around him.

He is said to belong to [[The Ninth Seal]], a quiet Crown apparatus that officially does not exist, unofficially performs work nobody wants recorded, and practically speaking is the reason some dangerous problems vanish before they become policies.

If the [[Lantern Watch]] is a lantern raised in the dark, Riven is the hand that quietly closes the shutters first.

He is not a battle wizard.  
He is not a court wizard.  
He is not an academic wizard.  

He is the kind of wizard sent into a guarded castle before dawn so that, by sunrise, three locks have forgotten they were locked, two sentries remember being somewhere else, and one priest of Pelor is standing very still because the floorboards have become political enemies.

Riven currently operates alongside [[Thranduil Stormarrow]], [[Aylassa]], and [[Sir Mathren Solgard]] during the infiltration of [[Castle Blackvale]].

No one is entirely sure who requested him.

That is probably deliberate.

## Appearance

Riven is a lean half-elf of indeterminate age, with the composed stillness common to people who have survived by being overlooked.

He has pale skin with a faint olive cast, dark hair usually worn tied back, and sharp, cold green eyes that never seem surprised. His elven heritage shows in the angles of his face and ears, but not enough to make him welcome among elves or comfortable among humans.

He dresses plainly by wizardly standards: dark traveling clothes, close-fitted gloves, a long coat lined with hidden pockets, and no visible jewelry except a narrow ring of dull silver on his left hand.

His spell focus is not obvious.

Some say it is the ring.  
Some say it is the glove.  
Some say he has no focus at all.  
Some say the focus is whichever object you notice least.

He carries no staff unless he wants someone to assume he is a wizard.

That alone should make everyone nervous.

## First Impression

Riven is quiet, precise, and almost offensively calm.

He does not loom.  
He does not posture.  
He does not explain more than necessary.

People who meet him often come away with the uncomfortable impression that he already knew how the conversation would end and was merely waiting for them to catch up.

He is polite in the way a locked door is polite.

## Public Reputation

Publicly, Riven Halcyre barely exists.

There may be a minor registry entry naming a half-elf mage attached to the capital. There may be payment records under a ciphered stipend. There may be a sealed Crown authorization bearing only the mark of the Ninth Seal.

But no noble publicly claims him.  
No academy lists him as a graduate.  
No temple keeps his birth record.  
No herald admits knowing his parentage.

This absence is too clean to be accidental.

Among people who notice such things, three theories circulate:

- Riven is a royal bastard quietly trained for deniable work.
- Riven is an elven agent loaned to the Crown under old treaty obligations.
- Riven Halcyre is not his first name, and perhaps not his first face.

None of these theories are confirmed.

None are comfortably dismissed.

## The Ninth Seal

Riven is associated with [[The Ninth Seal]], a rumored Crown cell used for sealed operations, impossible errands, and matters too sensitive for ordinary command structures.

The organization’s name appears in no public law.

Its agents do not wear badges.

Its orders are rarely written twice.

What the Ninth Seal actually does depends on who is asked:

- To loyalists, it protects the kingdom from threats that courts cannot safely name.
- To nobles, it is the Crown’s hidden knife.
- To priests, it is a necessary but spiritually hazardous instrument.
- To the Lantern Watch, it is either a cousin, rival, or problem child.
- To Riven, it is “a seal.”

He does not elaborate.

## Known Capabilities

Riven specializes in making guarded spaces unreliable.

His known or suspected magic includes:

- Invisibility
- Silence
- Sound manipulation
- Light control
- Blur
- Haste
- Apportation
- Lockmaster
- Seeker
- Shield
- Anti-surveillance techniques
- Ward-sense or ward-reading
- Quiet electrical or cold attack spells when necessary

He is not helpless in direct combat, but direct combat is usually evidence that someone has failed.

Possibly someone else.

Possibly him.

He dislikes both options.

## Magical Style

Riven’s magic is subtle enough that even other mages sometimes miss the moment it begins.

He does not chant loudly.  
He does not gesture broadly.  
He does not fling visible power unless circumstances have already degraded into vulgarity.

His spells tend to alter conditions rather than announce themselves.

A torch dims.  
A hinge stops complaining.  
A footstep lands where no foot touched.  
A guard hears a sound from the wrong direction.  
A ward blinks, reconsiders, and goes back to sleep.

When Riven works, the world does not break.

It politely fails to notice.

## Personality

Riven is restrained, observant, and difficult to read.

He has a dry wit, though he uses it sparingly and usually at the worst possible emotional moment. He dislikes waste: wasted motion, wasted force, wasted speech, wasted blood.

Especially wasted blood.

Not from squeamishness.

From logistics.

He is not cruel, but he is not gentle. He does not confuse kindness with honesty and does not confuse secrecy with dishonor. His ethics are real, but buried deep enough that most people mistake them for procedure.

He rarely lies directly.

This does not make him trustworthy.

It makes him efficient.

## Mannerisms

- Speaks softly, but never asks to be heard.
- Looks at doors, corners, rafters, and windows before looking at people.
- Keeps his hands still unless casting.
- Avoids standing in the center of rooms.
- Uses names only after deciding someone matters.
- Notices who reaches for a weapon and who reaches for a symbol.
- Pauses before answering questions about the Crown.
- Smiles rarely, and usually when someone else has just misunderstood something important.

## Relationship with Thranduil Stormarrow

Riven respects [[Thranduil Stormarrow]].

He also finds him inconvenient.

Thranduil moves through hostile territory like a hunting spirit given muscle and contempt. He is astonishingly capable, brutally direct, and fundamentally unwilling to pretend civilization has solved anything important.

Riven does not try to command him.

That would be stupid.

Instead, he shapes the environment so Thranduil’s violence arrives where it must and not where it merely wants to.

Their relationship is not friendship.

It is a working arrangement between two predators who hunt by different rules.

Riven’s private assessment:

> “The Count is not subtle. He is quiet. Those are different virtues.”

## Relationship with Aylassa

Riven is wary of [[Aylassa]] in the way a professional locksmith is wary of an elegant fire.

He recognizes skill immediately. He recognizes discipline. He recognizes that she has rules, standards, and a sense of theater sharp enough to draw blood.

He also recognizes that none of this makes her safe.

Aylassa likely enjoys him because he refuses to be properly unsettled by her. This may be false. He may be profoundly unsettled and simply too well-trained to give her the satisfaction.

Their conversations are probably dreadful for anyone else in the room: dry, oblique, full of half-finished thoughts and implications that sound flirtatious until one realizes they are discussing murder routes.

Riven’s private assessment:

> “She is not unpredictable. That is the dangerous part.”

## Relationship with Sir Mathren Solgard

Riven’s relationship with [[Sir Mathren Solgard]] is the best kind of awkward.

Solgard is a holy warhammer asked to join a team of scalpels.

Riven knows this.

Solgard knows Riven knows this.

Neither man says it aloud because both are professionals and because Aylassa would become intolerable if handed that kind of ammunition.

Riven does not mock Solgard. He gives him exact instructions, creates safe paths, muffles his movement, and quietly adjusts the environment around him.

This somehow makes it worse.

Solgard is used to confronting darkness face-to-face. Riven asks him to stand in it quietly and let worse people pass by.

That tension is useful.

Riven respects Solgard’s integrity. Solgard distrusts Riven’s secrecy. Both men are probably right.

Riven’s private assessment:

> “The Dawn Marshal is not clumsy. He is honest. The building objects to honesty.”

## Relationship with the Crown

Riven’s loyalty to the Crown appears firm, but its nature is unclear.

He may be loyal to [[Porthos Ryomar]] personally.

He may be loyal to the office of the Crown.

He may be loyal to an older charge established before the current crisis.

He may be loyal to someone dead.

When asked directly, Riven says:

> “The Crown has work that must be done quietly.”

That is not an answer.

It is a locked box wearing grammar.

## Role at Castle Blackvale

At [[Castle Blackvale]], Riven’s purpose is to make infiltration possible against a fortified, magically sensitive, politically unstable seat of power.

His likely tasks include:

- reading or bypassing wards
- suppressing sound
- hiding movement
- controlling light sources
- misleading guards without unnecessary killing
- detecting magical surveillance
- identifying alarm structures
- coordinating silent entry routes
- protecting the team from anti-mage contingencies
- opening paths for Thranduil and Aylassa
- keeping Solgard from sounding like a cathedral falling downstairs

Riven is not the team’s leader.

He is the condition that allows the team to exist.

## Methods

Riven’s preferred solution is prevention.

A guard who never sees the team does not need to be killed.  
A lock that opens quietly does not need to be broken.  
A ward that believes nothing happened does not need to be dispelled.  
A witness who remembers the wrong hallway is better than a corpse.

Not because Riven objects to corpses.

Because corpses create questions, reports, grief, revenge, smells, and paperwork.

He does not like paperwork.

This may be his most human trait.

## Rumors

Some rumors about Riven Halcyre:

- He once walked through the White Court during an active Pholtan truth-rite and was not recorded by any witness.
- He was trained by elves, humans, and someone neither.
- His mother was a minor noblewoman; his father was an elven exile.
- His father was a noble; his mother was the exile.
- Both are lies.
- He can remove a spoken word from a room if he hears it once.
- He does not cast invisibility on himself; he convinces attention to look elsewhere.
- He once killed a man in a crowded hall, and the victim apologized before falling.
- He once saved a noble child from assassins and left before anyone knew the child had been in danger.
- He has a sister in a monastery.
- He has no family.
- He had a different name before the Ninth Seal.
- The ring on his hand belonged to the first person he failed to save.
- The ring is a key.
- The ring is a warning.
- The ring is nothing, which would be very like him.

## The Halcyre Problem

There is no confirmed House Halcyre.

No registered noble line.  
No known elven clan.  
No mercantile house of consequence.  
No border family with that name.

This raises questions.

“Halcyre” may be:

- a false surname
- a dead family name
- an elven name translated badly into Common
- a field name assigned by the Ninth Seal
- a word taken from an old cipher
- a joke no one living understands

Riven has been asked once.

His answer:

> “It was available.”

He did not clarify whether he meant the name, the family, or the grave.

## Secrets and Uncertainties

The following are intentionally unresolved:

- Who trained Riven?
- Who does he truly answer to?
- Is the Ninth Seal a formal office, a royal cipher, or a post-war invention?
- Why does the Lantern Watch tolerate his operations?
- Has Riven worked against Gorundian nobles before?
- Did he have a role in preparing the field before the post-Vogel purge?
- Is he loyal to Porthos, Priam, the Crown, or a promise older than either?
- Why does he avoid temples except under operational need?
- Why does Solgard seem to know enough not to ask about his past?

Do not answer all of these.

Mystery is the point.

Give the players just enough rope to decide whether he is a lifeline or a garrote.

## Table Use

Use Riven when you need:

- a stealth wizard who does not feel like a fantasy burglar with cantrips
- a Crown operative whose presence suggests deeper machinery
- quiet tension with paladins and assassins
- magical support for impossible infiltration
- someone who can know too much without explaining how
- a way to make the Crown feel competent and unsettling
- a contrast to louder, more spectacular wizards

He should never over-explain.

Riven’s answers should create cleaner questions.

## Voice

Riven speaks in short, precise sentences.

He avoids dramatic threats. If he threatens someone, it should sound like an observation.

Good Riven lines:

> “Lower your voice. I have already lowered the room.”

> “That guard will remember a rat. Try to be less interesting than the rat.”

> “The ward is awake, but vain. Give me a moment.”

> “Do not touch the blue thread. It is either decorative or lethal. I dislike even odds.”

> “The door is not locked. It is waiting for permission.”

> “No, Dawn Marshal. Your left foot. The honest one.”

> “Count Stormarrow, if you kill him there, the blood runs under the door.”

> “Lady Aylassa, I would prefer that we terrify them after we leave.”

> “I did not say it was safe. I said it was quiet.”

> “I am not hiding anything from you. I am hiding you from everything else.”

## DM Notes

Riven should remain useful but not cozy.

Do not make him smug. Smug ruins him. He is not trying to impress anyone. He is trying to finish the work.

He should occasionally show flashes of principle, but only when nobody is expecting it. He may spare a servant, redirect a patrol rather than kill it, or object quietly to an unnecessary death.

This does not mean he is soft.

It means he counts costs other people miss.

He should unsettle Mathren, intrigue Aylassa, irritate Thranduil, and make the players wonder whether the Crown has had people like this all along.

The answer is probably yes.

That should worry them.

## Scene Fragment: The Quiet Hall

The corridor ahead is dark.

Not naturally dark.

Corrected dark.

The kind of dark made by someone who has decided the lamps are being unhelpful.

Riven stands beside the door with one gloved hand resting near the latch. He does not touch it yet.

Thranduil waits behind him, motionless.

Aylassa has somehow become part of the wall.

Sir Mathren Solgard stands last, silent and deeply unhappy about the floor.

Riven tilts his head.

Listens.

Then raises two fingers.

Everyone stops.

A moment passes.

Then another.

Somewhere beyond the door, boots cross stone.

One guard.

Then two.

Then nothing.

Riven breathes out.

Barely.

> “Now.”

The latch lifts without sound.

The door opens inward.

No creak.

No complaint.

No witness.

Mathren steps forward.

A board gives a faint, traitorous pop.

Everyone freezes.

Riven looks down.

Then at Mathren.

Aylassa’s smile appears in the dark like a knife deciding to be friendly.

Mathren says, very softly:

> “The floor is poorly disciplined.”

Riven considers this.

Then whispers:

> “I will note it in the report.”

Thranduil is already through the door.
