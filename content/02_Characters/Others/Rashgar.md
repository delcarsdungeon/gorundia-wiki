# Rashgar

> “You may underestimate me if you wish. I have found it saves considerable time.”

**Title:** High Magus of Furyondy  
**Race:** Half-orc  
**Profession:** Wizard, royal arcane adviser, diplomat  
**Known For:** Arcane analysis, wards, countermeasures, magical theory  
**Former Master:** [[Delcar Blackheart]]  
**Public Reputation:** Brilliant, proud, intimidating, exceptionally young for the weight of his office  

---

# Overview

**High Magus Rashgar** is one of Furyondy’s most prominent younger wizards and an increasingly important voice in matters of arcane defense, magical intelligence, and unusual supernatural threats.

He is a half-orc.

This fact is considerably more interesting to strangers than it is to Rashgar.

His preferred response to anyone visibly surprised by his title is usually to wait until they have finished being surprised.

Rashgar trained under [[Delcar Blackheart]], Count Blackheart and one of Furyondy’s most experienced archmages.

The apprenticeship produced an unusually formidable scholar.

It also produced a wizard with extremely high expectations of:

- preparation,
- technical accuracy,
- magical discipline,
- and himself.

Especially himself.

---

# High Magus

**High Magus** is a senior Furyondian arcane distinction and advisory office.

The title does not make Rashgar commander of every wizard in Furyondy.

It gives him recognized standing to advise senior authorities on matters involving:

- hostile magic,
- magical defenses,
- arcane intelligence,
- ancient or unfamiliar sorcery,
- wards and counter-wards,
- magical artifacts,
- supernatural military threats,
- and unusually dangerous magical research.

The position carries substantial prestige.

Rashgar has noticed.

---

# Appearance

Rashgar is unmistakably half-orc, though physically he takes after neither parent in scale.

Compared with his father and mother, he is something of a runt.

**Height:** 5'9"  
**Build:** Slender, lightly built, more scholar than warrior  

His features are sharp rather than heavy, with pronounced lower canines, a strong brow, and an expression that tends toward severe concentration.

He dresses well.

Not extravagantly.

Precisely.

His robes and traveling clothes tend toward severe cuts and restrained ornamentation, with the insignia of a High Magus displayed clearly enough that nobody can plausibly claim later that they did not know whom they were addressing.

Rashgar carries himself like someone who spent much of his youth entering rooms where people assumed the half-orc must be:

- a servant,
- an assistant,
- a bodyguard's clerk,
- or somebody considerably less important than he actually was.

Being physically unimposing by half-orc standards only encouraged the mistake.

He no longer corrects it immediately.

Sometimes he enjoys the next thirty seconds.

---

# Personality

Rashgar is **proud and intimidating**.

Neither quality is entirely accidental.

He knows he is brilliant.

He knows how hard he worked.

He knows exactly how many people expected less of him.

Rashgar does not feel obligated to perform humility for their comfort.

He is:

- extremely intelligent,
- analytical,
- disciplined,
- ambitious,
- academically voracious,
- blunt when irritated,
- deeply competitive,
- and considerably more sensitive to condescension than he likes to admit.

He rarely shouts.

He does not need to.

His anger usually produces greater precision.

---

# Pride

Rashgar’s pride is both strength and weakness.

He has spent much of his life proving that he belongs in rooms where some people initially assumed he did not.

Consequently, he has difficulty distinguishing between:

**a genuine challenge to his competence**

and

**someone merely disagreeing with him.**

He is getting better.

Slowly.

Delcar considers this educational.

Rashgar considers Delcar’s opinion on the matter unnecessary.

---

# Intimidation

Rashgar is not physically imposing in the conventional sense.

At 5'9" and slender, he is notably smaller than most half-orcs and considerably smaller than his parents.

This has never prevented him from being intimidating.

His presence comes from:

- absolute confidence,
- precision,
- magical authority,
- prolonged eye contact,
- and the unpleasant sensation that he has already identified the weakest part of your argument.

He rarely threatens people deliberately.

He has simply discovered that standing very still while someone explains something foolish produces excellent results.

---

# Delcar Blackheart

Rashgar was trained by [[Delcar Blackheart]].

Calling the relationship merely that of master and apprentice understates it.

Delcar shaped much of Rashgar’s approach to magic.

In particular:

> Never trust a defense whose failure you have not already considered.

Rashgar learned from Delcar to examine:

- what a spell assumes,
- how a ward fails,
- what happens when a caster is interrupted,
- which layer of a defense is actually carrying the burden,
- and how an enemy might use the defender’s own preparation against them.

Rashgar has since developed these principles aggressively.

Possibly excessively.

---

# Delcar’s Influence

People familiar with both wizards occasionally notice things Rashgar picked up from his old master.

These include:

- an unusual comfort underground;
- excellent low-light habits;
- an irritatingly broad knowledge of obscure magical traditions;
- careful treatment of names and identities;
- knowledge of magical practices most Furyondian scholars know only from texts;
- and the assumption that every magical system has another layer beneath the obvious one.

When asked where Delcar taught him some particularly strange piece of lore, Rashgar commonly answers:

> “Blackheart.”

This does not clarify much.

---

# Family

Rashgar is the son of [[Reigar]] and brother of [[Rykeena]].

His family background has contributed to a life in which identity, expectation, and other people’s assumptions have rarely been simple matters.

Rashgar does not generally discuss family with strangers.

People who mistake this reserve for embarrassment usually discover they have misunderstood him badly.

He is fiercely protective of those he considers family.

He simply does not believe that affection needs to be publicly demonstrated to be real.

---

# Scholar

For all his imposing presence, Rashgar is fundamentally bookish.

Given the choice between:

- a feast,
- a tournament,
- a fashionable court gathering,
- and an unexplored magical archive,

Rashgar will choose the archive so quickly that the other invitations may wonder whether they were ever actually considered.

He reads obsessively.

He annotates aggressively.

He argues with dead authors in the margins.

Some books in Blackheart’s libraries contain decades-old notes by Delcar followed by newer rebuttals from Rashgar.

Occasionally Delcar has responded.

This has produced scholarly arguments spanning several generations of ink.

---

# Wards

Rashgar’s strongest practical reputation is in **wards and magical defenses**.

He is particularly skilled at determining:

- what a ward actually does,
- what its creator believed it did,
- how it was constructed,
- what powers it,
- what assumptions it depends upon,
- what activates it,
- and how to make it stop doing whatever unpleasant thing it was designed to do.

He approaches magical defenses less like puzzles and more like hostile arguments.

Somebody made a claim about how reality ought to behave.

Rashgar intends to find the flaw in the argument.

---

# Arcane Analysis

Rashgar is exceptionally good at examining unfamiliar magic.

His procedure tends to be methodical:

1. Determine what is observable.
2. Separate magical effects from assumptions about their source.
3. Identify the tradition or traditions involved.
4. Determine what the caster wanted.
5. Determine what the caster feared.
6. Find the compromise between the two.

He dislikes immediately destroying magical objects merely because they are dangerous.

A destroyed spell cannot be studied.

This occasionally produces disagreements with more practically minded companions.

---

# Forbidden Knowledge

Rashgar holds a controversial position regarding forbidden magical traditions.

He believes that ignorance is not a defense.

If an enemy knows how to use a magical discipline, then someone must understand it well enough to:

- recognize it,
- counter it,
- contain it,
- and explain it.

Consequently, his library includes works that many respectable wizards would prefer remain locked away.

Rashgar is careful about this distinction:

> “Studying a weapon is not the same thing as firing it.”

Not everyone finds this reassuring.

---

# Dangerous Curiosity

Rashgar occasionally displays rather more enthusiasm for proscribed magical theory than his purely defensive argument would seem to require.

He is fascinated by magic that has been:

- forbidden,
- lost,
- deliberately suppressed,
- declared impossible,
- or remembered only through hostile accounts.

He insists this is scholarship.

Mostly, it is.

---

# Diplomacy

Rashgar has represented Furyondian interests abroad and is trusted to discuss magical matters with foreign authorities.

He is a surprisingly effective diplomat when he chooses to be.

His strengths include:

- excellent preparation,
- strong memory,
- resistance to intimidation,
- little concern for social rank,
- and the ability to recognize when another delegation is hiding behind technical language.

His weaknesses include:

- pride,
- impatience,
- enjoying intellectual victory somewhat too much,
- and occasionally forgetting that making someone feel stupid is rarely necessary even when they are.

---

# Being Underestimated

Rashgar has extensive experience being underestimated.

He has learned to use it.

When someone assumes that the half-orc standing beside the diplomats must be security, Rashgar does not immediately correct them.

When someone assumes he must be an apprentice because of his age, he waits.

When someone begins explaining elementary magical theory to him, he listens.

Then, once they have committed themselves thoroughly enough:

> “Please continue.”

The results are rarely merciful.

---

# Reputation Among Wizards

Among younger Furyondian wizards, Rashgar inspires a mixture of:

- admiration,
- envy,
- professional terror,
- and the desperate hope that he will not review their thesis.

Older wizards tend to divide into two camps.

Those who regard him as one of Furyondy’s brightest emerging magical minds.

And those who also regard him as one of Furyondy’s brightest emerging magical minds but would prefer he discover modesty before discovering anything else.

---

# Reputation Beyond Furyondy

Outside Furyondy, Rashgar is increasingly known by title before reputation:

**the half-orc High Magus.**

This sometimes causes diplomatic surprise.

Furyondian representatives have stopped apologizing for enjoying that.

His reputation is strongest among people dealing with:

- defensive magic,
- magical fortifications,
- artifact containment,
- ancient magical sites,
- and dangerous arcane research.

---

# Magic

Rashgar favors magic that provides:

- control,
- analysis,
- protection,
- countermeasures,
- information,
- and decisive advantage.

He enjoys destructive magic perfectly well.

He simply considers destruction one of the easier applications of wizardry.

The difficult part is knowing precisely **what should be destroyed and when**.

---

# Philosophy of Magic

Rashgar dislikes dividing magic into comfortable categories such as:

> safe and dangerous

or:

> permitted and forbidden.

He prefers:

- understood,
- misunderstood,
- controllable,
- uncontrollable,
- and not yet understood.

This distinction occasionally makes clerics nervous.

Rashgar regards nervous clerics as evidence that a discussion has finally become interesting.

---

# Ambition

Rashgar is ambitious.

He does not hide this particularly well.

He wants to understand magical traditions other wizards have abandoned.

He wants access to archives normally closed to scholars.

He wants to examine artifacts that prudent people have locked away.

He wants to understand why particular forms of magic were forbidden rather than merely accepting that they were.

His stated reason is simple:

> “If something is dangerous enough that nobody should study it, then we have deliberately ensured that the next person who does study it will understand it better than we do.”

This argument is difficult to dismiss.

It is also not entirely comforting.

---

# Flaws

## Pride

Rashgar knows his capabilities and sometimes overestimates what intelligence can solve.

## Intellectual Arrogance

He can believe that understanding something gives him greater control over it than is actually true.

## Curiosity

The phrase **“there are some things mortals were not meant to know”** is almost perfectly designed to make Rashgar investigate something.

## Competitive Instinct

Tell him another wizard failed to solve a magical problem and he becomes interested.

Tell him Delcar could not solve it and several responsible adults should immediately hide the relevant books.

## Inexperience

Rashgar possesses extraordinary magical knowledge.

He does not yet possess Delcar’s depth of lived experience.

Occasionally he knows everything about a problem except what it feels like when the answer goes wrong.

---

# Social Weak Spot

For all his intimidating confidence, Rashgar can still be caught badly off guard by situations outside his intellectual defenses.

Praise that he considers sincere can occasionally fluster him.

Personal attention can do worse.

He strongly prefers magical catastrophes.

They are more predictable.

# Player-Facing Summary

**High Magus Rashgar** is a half-orc wizard of Furyondy, former apprentice of [[Delcar Blackheart]], and one of the kingdom’s rising authorities on wards, magical defenses, and unusual arcane phenomena.

He is brilliant, proud, intimidating, and acutely aware that people frequently underestimate him.

Rashgar’s scholarship ranges considerably farther into obscure and restricted magical traditions than many of his colleagues find comfortable.

His explanation is that dangerous magic must be understood before it can be defeated.

This is a very reasonable argument.

Rashgar has become extremely good at reasonable arguments.
