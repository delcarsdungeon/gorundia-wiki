# Tivor Underbough

> “You command ships. I command supper. Respect borders.”

**Tivor Underbough**, commonly called **Tivor the Sauce**, is the executive chef of the [[Admiral's Vice]] in [[Bard's Gate]].

Young, exceptionally trained, and almost offensively confident, Tivor runs the Vice’s kitchen as a separate sovereign territory whose borders happen to lie inside [[Thalrik Vaultforge]]’s casino.

He is a talented food mage, although he refuses to use magic as a substitute for skill, proper ingredients, or attention.

> “Magic can hold the heat steady. Magic can keep the cream from turning. Magic can tell me whether someone poisoned the saffron.”
>
> “Magic cannot cook.”
>
> “That is why it needs me.”

## Appearance

![[Tivor the Sauce.png]]

Tivor is lean for a halfling, with strong forearms, quick hands, and the compact balance of someone accustomed to moving through crowded kitchens without ever colliding with another person.

He has dark auburn hair swept sharply back, hazel eyes, and a remarkably expressive eyebrow that tends to rise whenever someone offers an opinion about food without first demonstrating that they possess a functioning palate.

During service he wears:

- a white chef’s coat with black cuffs
- fitted dark trousers
- a heavy waist apron
- wrapped ankles and insteps to protect against heat and spilled oil
- a narrow leather bandolier holding tasting spoons, chalk, folded towels, and several small knives

His apron bears no name or title, only a small embroidered copper saucepan.

By the end of a busy evening, Tivor generally resembles a victorious battlefield commander whose enemies happened to be onions.

## Personality

Tivor is brilliant, theatrical, demanding, and entirely convinced that his confidence is justified by available evidence.

Unfortunately, he is usually correct.

He has little patience for:

- laziness
- hidden mistakes
- mistreatment of serving staff
- customers who mistake wealth for expertise
- people entering his kitchen without permission
- anyone who uses the phrase “just food”

He does not object to honest criticism. He welcomes informed criticism and may spend far too long discussing it.

Ignorant criticism receives a different response.

A noble once complained that his roast was too moist.

> “My lord, the moisture is the part you paid for.”
>
> “I prefer it dry.”
>
> “Then continue speaking. Your mouth should be ready shortly.”

Tivor is harsh with carelessness but remarkably patient with inexperience. He will verbally dismantle an apprentice for allowing a sauce to split, then personally show them how to repair it and send them home with enough food for their household.

He attacks failure only when the failure came from indifference.

## Training

Tivor trained in several respected kitchens across Gorundia and spent time working under cooks associated with [[Master Bramble Gooseberry]] and the Royal Kitchens.

His strongest influence was a halfling sauce-master who taught him that sauce was not something poured over a meal.

> “It is the final argument explaining why everything else belongs on the plate.”

Tivor also received formal instruction in:

- baking and fermentation
- kitchen logistics
- pastry construction
- meat preparation
- ingredient preservation
- ceremonial and noble service
- culinary magic
- poison and contamination detection

Master Bramble once described one of Tivor’s dishes as:

> “Acceptable.”

Tivor treats this as a mildly encouraging comment and not, under any circumstances, the proudest moment of his professional life.

## Appointment at the Admiral's Vice

Tivor was recommended to [[Thalrik Vaultforge]] after the Royal Kitchens became involved in an affair concerning a rare magical pepper.

Accounts differ regarding what occurred, who owed whom a favor, and whether a sauceboat was actually used as an improvised weapon.

Thalrik’s interview with Tivor lasted only a few minutes.

Tivor inspected the kitchen, criticized the placement of the stoves, demanded full authority over staff discipline and purchasing, and informed Thalrik that cigars would not be permitted beyond the kitchen doors.

Thalrik hired him immediately.

## The Kitchen

Within the [[Admiral's Vice]], authority is divided plainly:

- [[Thalrik Vaultforge]] owns the establishment.
- [[Rhyka Blacktray Marruk]] controls the floor.
- Tivor controls the kitchen.

Thalrik generally respects this arrangement.

The first time he entered during service without warning, Tivor struck him in the chest with a freshly baked dinner roll.

> “You command ships. I command supper. Respect borders.”

Thalrik ate the roll.

It was excellent.

## Kitchen Rules

Tivor maintains several standing rules.

1. Staff eat before the late rush.
2. No guest enters the kitchen without permission.
3. Anyone abusing a server forfeits dessert.
4. Nothing leaves the kitchen that the cook would not serve their own family.
5. Money does not transform a bad request into a good one.
6. Mistakes must be admitted immediately.
7. Hidden mistakes become crimes.
8. Magic may support technique but may never replace it.
9. No cigars.
10. [[Guaco Moe Lee]] is not kitchen staff.

The final rule was added after Guaco wandered into the kitchen, tasted directly from a simmering pot, and attempted to justify himself by mentioning a possible distant family connection.

Tivor’s reply was immediate.

> “You might be distant kin.”
>
> “You are definitely not staff.”
>
> “And you sure as hell cannot cook.”

Guaco later returned carrying his own spoon.

This did not improve matters.

> “There are spoons here.”
>
> “Those are kitchen spoons.”
>
> “This one is mine.”
>
> “That is somehow worse.”

Tivor does not dislike Guaco.

He simply believes Guaco possesses the dangerous confidence of a man who has been fed well his entire life and consequently assumes he understands how food happens.

## Food Magic

Tivor is an accomplished food mage but has strict opinions about its proper use.

He commonly uses magic to:

- maintain exact temperatures
- regulate oven heat
- control humidity
- preserve ingredients
- detect spoilage or poison
- accelerate or halt fermentation
- cool pastries evenly
- prevent delicate sauces from breaking
- isolate aromas until service
- warm plates without continuing to cook their contents
- control smoke and steam
- encourage herbs and spices to release their oils

He refuses to use magic to:

- disguise spoiled ingredients
- make poorly prepared food taste better
- create the illusion of expensive ingredients
- compel hunger or satisfaction
- conceal a failed dish
- replace ordinary kitchen work

> “Using enchantment to make bad food taste good is not cooking.”
>
> “It is fraud with garnish.”

He makes exceptions during famine, siege, disaster, or emergency relief. When feeding people becomes a matter of survival, Tivor considers pride an indulgence and will use every spell available to stretch provisions.

## Service Style

Tivor believes a fine meal should possess theater, although spectacle must never conceal weakness in the food itself.

His presentations may include:

- several covered dishes revealed simultaneously
- herb smoke released from beneath a cloche
- heated sauces poured into chilled bowls
- salt crusts cracked at the table
- brandy ignited with a snapped finger
- aromatic steam released at the first cut
- dishes presented in a deliberate sequence according to the diners’ reactions
- elaborate centerpieces carved apart with little ceremony

His principal tasting service is known as **The Captain’s Table**.

The dishes change frequently. Tivor believes a permanent signature menu is evidence that a chef has stopped thinking.

The final course is traditionally served under a blackened brass cloche.

Tivor removes it personally, watches the table take the first bite, and leaves without waiting for praise.

## Treatment of Customers

Tivor accommodates:

- allergies
- religious restrictions
- medical diets
- sensory difficulties
- honest dislikes
- reasonable substitutions made in advance

He does not accommodate vanity disguised as sophistication.

Customers may request meat cooked beyond Tivor’s recommendation, but the charge will appear on the bill as:

> **Ruined by Request**

A diner once returned a dish of roasted onions because it contained onions.

Tivor sent the following response through the server:

> “The gentleman has identified the primary ingredient.”
>
> “Congratulate him and remove the plate.”

## Staff Meals

Tivor provides proper meals for the staff of the Vice.

They are not served scraps or whatever happens to remain after the guests finish. Staff meals are prepared deliberately and usually include some combination of:

- thick stew
- roast meat or sausage
- fresh bread
- onions
- potatoes
- cheese
- seasonal fruit
- sweet rolls after especially difficult evenings

Tivor complains throughout the meal.

> “Eat that before it cools.”
>
> “You look half dead.”
>
> “Pippa, bread is not a complete diet.”
>
> “Rhyka, sit down. The building will survive six minutes.”
>
> “Thalrik, that portion was not intended for you.”
>
> “Put it back.”
>
> “I saw you.”

He always prepares more food than necessary.

Tivor insists this is the result of inaccurate inventory records.

No one believes him.

## Relationships

### Thalrik Vaultforge

Tivor respects Thalrik because Thalrik gave him authority over the kitchen and has generally honored it.

The two argue frequently.

Thalrik calls him **Sauce**.

Tivor calls him **Admiral** when pleased and **Owner** when irritated.

> “Owner, remove the cigar.”
>
> “I own the kitchen.”
>
> “You own the walls around the kitchen. The kitchen belongs to civilization.”

### Rhyka Blacktray Marruk

Tivor and [[Rhyka Blacktray Marruk]] maintain a strong professional respect.

Rhyka controls the dining room. Tivor controls the kitchen. Neither crosses into the other’s territory casually.

During service they communicate through brief gestures, expressions, and highly efficient profanity.

Rhyka is one of the few people who can tell Tivor not to confront a customer and have him listen.

He will postpone the confrontation.

He will not forget it.

### Pippa

Tivor claims [[Pippa]] steals food from the kitchen.

Pippa claims Tivor leaves food where she can reach it.

Both statements are accurate.

Small wrapped meals frequently appear before Pippa’s late shifts. Tivor describes these as inventory errors.

### Guaco Moe Lee

Tivor recognizes that [[Guaco Moe Lee]] is important, well connected, possibly distant kin, and personally trusted by Thalrik.

None of this grants Guaco permission to enter the kitchen.

Tivor considers Guaco a capable judge of food but a menace near cooking equipment.

> “Eating well is not a qualification.”
>
> “Cats eat well.”
>
> “I do not let them season the soup either.”

### Master Bramble Gooseberry

Tivor becomes noticeably quieter around [[Master Bramble Gooseberry]].

Not humble.

Merely less expansive.

Bramble can taste a single spoonful, pause for several seconds, and cause Tivor to reconsider every decision he has made since childhood.

### Uncle Fenby Goodbarrel

Tivor publicly describes [[Uncle Fenby Goodbarrel]]’s cooking as:

> “Crowd control conducted with gravy.”

Privately, he regards Fenby as one of the greatest living halfling cooks.

Fenby calls him **Saucepan**.

Tivor objects every time.

Fenby has therefore concluded that it is the correct name.

## Common Sayings

> “Salt is not flavor. Salt introduces flavor. Learn the difference.”

> “The sauce has broken because you panicked. Food can smell fear.”

> “You do not sear meat to trap juice. That is tavern mythology repeated by men who fear thermodynamics.”

> “The spell keeps the cream at temperature. It does not make the cream good.”

> “The cow did that. Show some respect.”

> “Perfection is imaginary. I demand that every mistake be interesting enough to teach us something.”

> “This is a kitchen, not a democracy.”

## Reputation

Among the wealthy patrons of the Admiral’s Vice, Tivor is regarded as one of Bard’s Gate’s most promising young chefs.

Among the Vice’s staff, he is regarded as loud, unreasonable, fiercely protective, and worth enduring because no one under his roof goes hungry.

Among other cooks, opinion is divided between admiration and a perfectly reasonable desire to push him into a flour barrel.

Tivor would consider either response evidence that he made an impression.
