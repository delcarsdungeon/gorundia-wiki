## Norwin’s Enchanter Assistants

### Pellin “Pell” Wickwire
**Role:** Lead Assistant / Stabilizer  
**Vibe:** The only one who can keep Norwin’s notes from catching fire

Thin, precise gnome with ink-stained fingers and a permanently furrowed brow. Speaks in clipped, efficient corrections mid-sentence. Keeps three parallel ledgers because Norwin’s “working notes” are a war crime. Specializes in power flow regulation and enchantment stability.

**Table Behavior:**
- Constantly mutters corrections under her breath.
- Quietly fixes mistakes unless the error is catastrophic.
- Favorite line: “That’s not how containment circles work. That’s how containment circles fail.”

**Dynamic with Norwin:**
- Deep respect.
- Deep exhaustion.
- Has saved Norwin’s life more than once and refuses to acknowledge it.

---

### Tamsin Gearloft
**Role:** Materials & Binding Specialist  
**Vibe:** Cheerful gnome with goblin-energy and a PhD in “what happens if I push it”

Bright-eyed, fast-talking, always has a tool in hand. Treats dangerous enchantments like interesting puzzles rather than threats. Specializes in embedding magic into physical objects: runes, lattices, hybrid materials, bound fragments, and other things Pell wishes had never been named.

**Table Behavior:**
- Gets far too excited about unstable items.
- Favorite line: “Okay, but what if we don’t dampen it and instead see what it wants to do?”
- Has caused at least one “incident” everyone now pretends was intentional.

**Dynamic with Norwin:**
- Sees him as a genius and kindred spirit.
- Norwin sees her as promising but concerning.
- Pell sees her as a walking liability.

### Brannik Feld
**Role:** Field Tester / Practical Application  
**Vibe:** Former student who realized theory is great until it explodes

Human, broad-shouldered, and more soldier than scholar in appearance. Keeps his gear practical, worn, and deliberately non-explosive. Specializes in testing enchantments under real conditions: combat, weather, stress, impact, fatigue, and user error.

**Table Behavior:**
- The one who asks: “What happens when this gets hit with a hammer?”
- Carries scars from testing-phase failures.
- Will refuse to deploy something if he does not trust it.

**Dynamic with Norwin:**
- Loyal, but not blind.
- One of the few people who will say, “No. Not like that.”
- Norwin actually listens sometimes.

### Maera Vellumreach
**Role:** Resonance Reader / Arcane Diagnostics  
**Vibe:** Quiet apprentice who hears the bad noise before anyone else does

Soft-spoken, serious, and unnervingly sensitive to enchantment resonance. Maera reads magic by hum, pressure, vibration, and dissonance. She can sense when an enchantment is fatigued, hungry, unstable, or interfering with another binding.

**Table Behavior:**
- Says alarming things very quietly.
- Favorite lines:
  - “That tone was not there a moment ago.”
  - “Please stop touching it. It noticed you.”
  - “The inner lattice is breathing.”
- Usually ignored only once.

**Dynamic with Norwin:**
- Norwin values her diagnostics enormously.
- She respects him, but often stands beside Brakka when giving warnings so the warning has mass.

## Security

### Brakka Thornhand
**Role:** Senior Guard / Magical Hazard Spotter  
**Race:** Half-orc  
**Vibe:** Patient, grounded, not afraid of magic because he knows exactly when to duck

A calm, broad-shouldered half-orc guard familiar with magical work but not a mage himself. Brakka knows the signs of spell failure, containment stress, apprentice panic, and Norwin curiosity. He is not afraid of magic, but he respects it like a sailor respects storms.

**Table Behavior:**
- Moves people out of danger before anyone admits danger exists.
- Favorite lines:
  - “When Master Norwin says ‘interesting,’ I count exits.”
  - “If it glows blue, we watch. If it glows green, we leave. If it glows white, I carry the small ones.”
  - “Tamsin, put down the ethically complicated hammer.”

**Dynamic with Norwin:**
- Likes and respects Norwin.
- Begins most major projects by taking one step back and evaluating what Norwin is doing.
- Has physically removed Norwin from danger at least once.

### Garrik Venn, called Old Halfhelm
**Role:** Veteran Guard / Apprentice Shield  
**Race:** Human  
**Vibe:** Terrifying ruined-face old soldier who is secretly everyone’s favorite uncle

Old Garrik looks meaner than he is. His face was badly damaged in war, and he wears a battered half full-face helmet over the ruined side, smooth metal with no eye slit. The result is frightening. The man beneath it is dry, kind, and violently protective of Norwin’s apprentices.

**Table Behavior:**

- Favorite lines:
  - “Master Norwin, I admire your courage. I question its paperwork.”
  - “Threatening apprentices is how you become educational.”
  - “I look worse than I am. Most men are the other way around.”

**Dynamic with Norwin:**
- Respects Norwin because Norwin treats apprentices like people.
- Will absolutely say, “No, sir. Not with the little ones in the room.”
- Norwin listens because Garrik’s no sounds like a door barring shut.

## Brakka and Garrik’s “Uppity Sort” Routine

## Brakka and Garrik’s “Uppity Sort” Routine

When nobles, inspectors, guild officials, petty aristocrats, or other expensive hats get rude around Norwin’s workshop, Brakka and Garrik slip into a practiced act.

Brakka becomes intensely polite.

Not just polite. **Too polite.**

Articulate, urbane, formal, almost fawning. He bows correctly. He uses the noble’s full title. He apologizes for things no one did. He speaks with the calm, careful diction of someone who has read more etiquette manuals than the noble has.

Garrik, meanwhile, becomes a catastrophe in boots.

He slumps. He mumbles. He wipes his nose on his sleeve. He squints at insignia. He mispronounces titles. He asks if “baron” is a kind of churchman. He scratches his helmet. He laughs at the wrong moments.

The trick is simple: Brakka flatters the rude noble into relaxing, while Garrik makes himself so dismissible that the noble reveals too much, says something stupid, insults the wrong person, or steps exactly where Brakka wanted him.

It is not subtle.

It still works constantly, because class arrogance is apparently a reusable trap component.

---

## Why It Works

Most uppity sorts assume Brakka is the brute and Garrik is the “proper” human authority.

So Brakka being eloquent immediately disarms them.

Garrik acting like a dumb old peasant gives them permission to underestimate him.

Then Brakka lets them feel superior while Garrik watches hands, exits, weapons, shoes, guards, seals, and who flinches when Norwin’s name comes up.

By the time the noble realizes the idiot has been counting bodyguards and memorizing signet rings, the conversation is already over.

---

## In-Scene Version

A noble steps into Norwin’s workspace and sneers at the apprentices.

**Noble:**

> “Are these… children actually permitted to handle royal-grade enchantments?”

Brakka gives a deep, polished bow.

**Brakka:**

> “My lord’s concern honors the dignity of the Crown and the safety of all present. Master Norwin’s apprentices are supervised under strict internal protocols, though naturally we welcome any lawful inquiry from a person of your distinguished station.”

Garrik snorts, wipes his nose on his sleeve, and leans toward Brakka.

**Garrik:**

> “That mean he’s complainin’?”

**Brakka:**

> “It means his lordship has questions, Garrik.”

**Garrik:**

> “Oh. Thought he was just bein’ tall indoors.”

Brakka does not react.

The noble does.

That is the trap.

---

## Brakka’s Role: The Silk Rope

Brakka’s job is to make it impossible for the rude party to claim mistreatment.

He is formal, controlled, and exquisitely polite.

He says things like:

> “Your lordship’s patience is a credit to your house.”

> “Naturally, we would never presume to inconvenience a person of consequence.”

> “I am certain your meaning was more refined than your phrasing suggested.”

> “Your concern for procedure is most admirable, especially given the speed with which you formed it.”

> “Master Norwin is occupied with a delicate matter. I would be honored to delay you with every courtesy available.”

That last one is a threat in a velvet coat.

---

## Garrik’s Role: The Mud Decoy

Garrik plays stupid, rustic, and harmless.

Bad posture. Bad grammar. Bad timing. Bad manners.

He says things like:

> “Beg pardon, m’lord, is that the hat what means you own roads?”

> “I don’t read much, sir. Mostly faces. Yours is doin’ plenty.”

> “Should I fetch Master Norwin, or is this one o’ them visits where folk just stand around smellin’ expensive?”

> “That a family crest? Looks angry. Bird owe you money?”

> “Sorry, m’lord. Helmet side’s my clever side. Can’t hear nobility from there.”

He also deliberately misunderstands insults.

**Noble:**

> “Do you even know who I am?”

**Garrik:**

> “No, sir. But Brakka does, and he’s usually good with livestock.”

**Brakka:**

> “Lineage, Garrik.”

**Garrik:**

> “Right. Fancy livestock.”

---

## When the Noble Insults Brakka

This is where the routine gets nasty.

If the noble gets racist or classist toward Brakka, Brakka becomes even more gracious. Calm. Smooth. Almost warm.

Garrik gets dumber.

Which somehow makes it worse.

**Noble:**

> “I did not expect such language from your sort.”

Brakka smiles faintly.

**Brakka:**

> “Then I am pleased to have exceeded your expectations, my lord. However modest they were.”

Garrik squints.

**Garrik:**

> “What sort’s he mean?”

**Brakka:**

> “The sort that listens before speaking.”

**Garrik:**

> “Ah. Rare sort.”

---

## When the Noble Threatens the Apprentices

The act drops halfway.

Not fully. Halfway is scarier.

Brakka remains polite.

Garrik stops mumbling.

**Noble:**

> “Perhaps the little ones should be taught their place.”

Brakka folds his hands.

**Brakka:**

> “I would advise against experimental education in this room, my lord.”

Garrik straightens slightly.

**Garrik:**

> “Aye. Children learn fast. Adults, less so.”

**Noble:**

> “Is that a threat?”

**Brakka:**

> “No, my lord. A threat would be premature.”

**Garrik:**

> “And loud.”

---

## When the Noble Pushes Too Far

The routine becomes a trapdoor.

Brakka is still polished, but stops fawning.

Garrik stops acting stupid.

**Brakka:**

> “My lord, you have mistaken courtesy for permission.”

**Garrik:**

> “Happens to folk who hear too much bowin’.”

**Brakka:**

> “You may speak with Master Norwin when he is available.”

**Garrik:**

> “Or you may continue practicing bad judgment near witnesses.”

**Brakka:**

> “We are prepared to assist with either outcome.”

---

## Their Favorite Exchange

This should be the bit the apprentices have heard a dozen times and still enjoy.

**Brakka:**

> “Garrik, his lordship is a guest.”

**Garrik:**

> “Then why’s he trackin’ arrogance on the floor?”

**Brakka:**

> “We must be gracious.”

**Garrik:**

> “I wiped me boots.”

**Brakka:**

> “Not all filth clings to leather.”

**Garrik:**

> “That one of them court sayings?”

**Brakka:**

> “It is now.”

## Brannik’s Opinion

Brannik loves this routine because he recognizes it as battlefield misdirection wearing social clothes.

He will absolutely stand there with arms crossed, watching the noble dig deeper.

**Brannik:**

> “They’ve started the routine.”

**Pell:**

> “Which one?”

**Brannik:**

> “The one where the noble thinks he’s winning.”

**Tamsin:**

> “Oh, I like that one.”

**Maera:**

> “His voice has gone shiny.”

**Pell:**

> “Brakka’s?”

**Maera:**

> “No. The noble’s. He’s about to crack.”

---

## Pell’s Opinion

Pell pretends to dislike the routine because it is inefficient.

This is a lie.

She keeps notes.

> “Brakka’s phrase ‘your meaning was more refined than your phrasing’ produced visible facial discoloration in Lord Astenvale. Potential future use.”

Because Pell is a menace with stationery.

---

## Tamsin’s Opinion

Tamsin thinks Garrik is doing actual peasant acting.

She once tried to help.

This was forbidden.

**Tamsin:**

> “I can drool on command.”

**Pell:**

> “You absolutely cannot participate.”

**Garrik:**

> “Never waste a gift, miss.”

**Brakka:**

> “Do not encourage her.”

---

## Maera’s Opinion

Maera can hear when someone’s pulse changes.

This makes the routine even more unfair.

She quietly tells Brakka when the target is angry, afraid, lying, or about to call for guards.

**Maera, softly:**

> “His heartbeat changed when Garrik mentioned the seal.”

**Brakka:**

> “Did it?”

**Garrik:**

> “What seal? I don’t know letters.”

And now the noble is dead in the water.

Socially, probably.
