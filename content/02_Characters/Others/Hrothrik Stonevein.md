# Hrothik Stonevein

“Clan-sergeant” is the word given to humans.

It is not the word the dwarves would use.

---

## First Appearance — At the Gangplank

The skyship rests at dock. Lines taut. No movement.

At the foot of the gangplank stands a dwarf.

Not blocking it.

Not guarding it.

Waiting.

---

He steps forward when [[Beringer]] approaches.

> “Hrothik Stonevein,”  
> “Clan-sergeant of the deep ranks. I speak for the stoneborn attached to your command.”

No bow. No salute.

Just fact.

---

He gestures to the courier.

> “He arrived first.”

---

Later—

after the paper is gone, after the dock breathes again—

he adds:

> “Stone waits.”  
> “Paper hurries.”

---

And then, the accounting:

> “You give orders.”  
> “I decide how many dwarves live long enough to carry them out.”

---

No threat.

No challenge.

Just truth.

---

## What Humans Hear

A veteran noncommissioned officer.

Senior.

Blunt.

Possibly insubordinate.

---

## What Dwarves Hear

A **stone-witnessed veteran**.

---

Hrothik is not appointed.

He is **acknowledged**.

---

He does not speak for the dwarves because of rank.

He speaks because no one present—

officer or otherwise—

has the standing to contradict him.

---

## The Deep Ranks

Among the stoneborn, there are formations that exist beyond formal record.

Old companies.

Broken holds reforged into marching orders.

Lineages of warriors carried forward through memory rather than parchment.

---

These are the **deep ranks**.

---

They are not raised.

They are **continued**.

---

Hrothik stands among them as one who remembers:

- Which captains spent lives like coin  
- Which lines held when they should have broken  
- Which oaths bent—and which did not  

---

He carries that memory forward.

And enforces it.

---

## Authority

On parchment, Hrothik answers to officers.

In practice—

officers explain themselves to him.

---

Because when orders fail—

when plans fracture—

when men start dying for reasons no one can justify—

---

It is men like Hrothik who decide whether the line holds.

---

Or does not.

---

## Conduct in Command

Hrothik does not interfere with orders lightly.

When he does—

it is already too late for politeness.

---

His operating doctrine is simple:

- Hold when holding matters  
- Kill when killing ends the problem  
- Ignore anything that smells like glory  

---

He has no patience for:

- Banner-chasing  
- Heroics that cost lives  
- Command decisions made for appearances  

---

## Standing Among the Stoneborn

Younger dwarves recognize him immediately.

Not by name.

By **weight**.

---

A shieldbearer straightens when he walks past.

A veteran checks his grip.

Officers lower their voices.

---

He is not feared.

He is **taken seriously**.

---

## Relationship to Command

When Hrothik says:

> “I speak for the stoneborn attached to your command.”

The meaning is clear to those who understand it:

---

The dwarves will follow.

They will hold.

They will fight.

---

But if they are wasted—

if they are thrown away—

---

Hrothik will correct the situation.

Personally.

---

## Assessment of Beringer

When presented with [[Beringer]]’s titles, Hrothik pauses.

Not out of uncertainty.

Out of calculation.

---

Dwarves do not respect titles.

They respect outcomes.

---

His conclusion:

> “Acceptable.”

---

For now.

---

## The Unspoken Truth

The presence of a dwarf like Hrothik is not routine.

---

He is not sent to fill ranks.

He is not sent to observe.

---

He is sent when:

- The stakes matter  
- The outcome matters  
- And someone wants a witness who cannot be misled  

---

Because when the fighting is done—

when the stories start—

---

There will be one voice among the stoneborn that decides what is remembered as:

> “Well fought.”

—or—

> “Wasted.”

---

Hrothik Stonevein is that voice.
