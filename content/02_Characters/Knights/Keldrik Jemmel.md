# Sir Keldrik Jemmel

> “Malik didn’t save Jemmel. He stopped him from making the last stupid decision of his life. Naturally, Jemmel hated everyone who noticed.”
> — [[Sorek Redmarch]]

Sir Keldrik Jemmel was a knight in service to [[Baron Lynwenden Cron]], and later became an infernal officer of the [[Iron Covenant]].

He was never one of the great men of [[Gravendar]]. That is the important part.

Keldrik was useful, cruel, ambitious, and painfully eager to be noticed by better men. He served Cron with the desperate loyalty of a subordinate who mistook proximity for importance. He flattered upward, kicked downward, and called the whole arrangement duty, because apparently civilization needed another way to make middle management worse.

Under Cron, Keldrik was exactly the sort of knight a brutal baron keeps close: good at enforcing quotas, good at frightening peasants, and very good at agreeing loudly with whatever his lord had just said.

He kissed ass well enough to keep his place.  
He beat efficiency out of peasants.  
He hated his wife.  
His son mocked him.  
And he carried all of it like proof the world had wronged him.

## Appearance

Before his fall, Keldrik was a well-fed household knight with expensive armor, a loud mouth, and the sour confidence of a man who had never been corrected by anyone allowed to survive it.

As **Ash-Prefect Jemmel**, he is something worse.

His body has thickened into corpulent infernal density rather than ordinary softness. His skin is ashen grey, pulled tight around jowls and cheekbones. His eyes burn red like banked coals. Small horn-buds rise from his brow, subtle but unmistakable. His ears have sharpened slightly, just enough to make his face look wrong before one knows why.

He goes unhelmed by choice.

He wants to be recognized.

His armor is blackened infernal plate, still carrying the martial lines of a Gorundian knight but warped into something crueler. Flames crawl through engraved channels in the metal, not wild and uncontrolled, but disciplined, ritual, and smug. Even his armor acts like it wants applause. Fitting, really.

## Service to Baron Cron

Keldrik served [[Baron Lynwenden Cron]] as a household knight and enforcement officer.

His duties likely included:

- enforcing rents, labor obligations, and village quotas
- escorting tax collectors and Cron’s officials
- disciplining peasants accused of idleness or defiance
- intimidating lesser gentry and local headmen
- serving as Cron’s visible mailed fist
- echoing Cron’s politics with embarrassing enthusiasm

Cron likely found him useful but not impressive. Keldrik, unfortunately for everyone nearby, wanted to be impressive.

He had a powerful case of “notice me, my lord” energy. He wanted Cron’s approval, Cron’s confidence, Cron’s recognition. Every nod from Cron became a private triumph. Every dismissal became another wound to nurse.

Keldrik admired Cron’s brutality because it looked like certainty. He admired Cron’s authority because it made cruelty legal. He wanted to be that kind of man.

He just lacked the scale.

## Domestic Life

Keldrik’s household was rotten long before Maszareth came.

He hated his wife, not because she was wicked, but because she saw him clearly. She knew the difference between honor and performance. She knew what he was when he took off the armor.

His son mocked him.

That mockery mattered. It got under the plates and stayed there. A son’s contempt is precise in a way no enemy’s insult can be. Keldrik could dismiss peasants, rivals, and lesser knights, but his own son’s disgust followed him home.

Keldrik came to believe his wife had scorned him and his son had disrespected him.

When [[Maszareth]] offered him recognition, Keldrik murdered them both as part of the bargain.

He considered it small coin.

That is the measure of him.

## Brewfest and the Dregs

Keldrik first crossed the party’s path during the [[Brewfest Tourney]] in [[Bard's Gate]].

He was visiting the city as part of the noble and knightly crowd, but wandered into the Dregs looking for “strange.” In plain speech, he went slumming, expecting poverty and fear to make people pliable.

Instead, he behaved badly enough that the locals began to gather.

The Dregs did not immediately riot, but the mood turned. A mob began forming around him. Keldrik, arrogant and rattled, reached for his sword.

That was when [[Malik Redhand]] stepped in.

Malik did not defend Keldrik’s honor. He did not praise noble blood. He did not scatter the crowd on Keldrik’s behalf.

He looked at Keldrik and told him that if he drew steel, **Malik would kill him personally**.

That was the humiliation Keldrik never recovered from.

Not because Malik threatened him.  
Not because the mob frightened him.  
But because Malik identified Keldrik as the immediate danger and checked him like a dog in the street.

Then [[Beringer]] and [[Orin Kharne]] arrived or witnessed the aftermath.

Keldrik survived.

His pride did not.

## The Broken Arm

During Brewfest, [[Beringer]] later broke Keldrik’s arm in a duel.

For most men, one public humiliation would be enough to create a grudge. Keldrik, being committed to overachievement in the field of being pathetic, collected several.

Later, [[Orin Kharne]] broke that same arm again.

The repeated injury became part of Keldrik’s private mythology. In his mind, Beringer and Orin did not merely defeat him. They marked him. They made his shame physical.

The arm healed.

The resentment did not.

## Keldrik’s Version of Events

Keldrik does not remember Brewfest honestly.

The truth is simple:

Keldrik acted like a predatory noble thug in the Dregs, nearly caused a deadly confrontation, was restrained by Malik, then was later beaten and humiliated by better men.

Keldrik’s version is different.

In his mind:

- commoners rose against noble blood
- Malik alone understood strength
- Beringer and Orin witnessed his restraint and humiliation
- Beringer was rewarded
- Orin was elevated
- Cron was destroyed
- proper men were discarded
- lesser men were dressed in honor

This is nonsense.

Naturally, he built his soul around it.

## Fall of Cron and Recruitment

When [[Baron Lynwenden Cron]] fell, Keldrik lost more than a lord.

He lost the structure that told him who he was.

Cron’s fall, Vogel’s collapse, Beringer’s rise, and Orin’s elevation all fed the same wound. Keldrik saw the old order breaking and decided the problem was not cruelty, corruption, or treason.

The problem was that men like him had not been obeyed hard enough.

That was when [[Maszareth]] came personally.

Not because Keldrik was especially useful.

Because Keldrik was easy.

Maszareth saw a resentful, vain, humiliated, cruel little knight who desperately wanted the universe to tell him he mattered. Keldrik did not need seduction. He needed permission.

Maszareth offered him recognition.

Not redemption.  
Not honor.  
Recognition.

Keldrik murdered his wife and son as part of the bargain and counted their lives as a cheap price for power.

He became **Ash-Prefect Jemmel** of the [[Iron Covenant]].

## Ash-Prefect Jemmel

As an Ash-Prefect, Keldrik is the infernal version of the man he always pretended to be.

He is stronger.  
Faster.  
Harder to kill.  
Recognized.

That last word matters most to him.

He does not think he fell. He thinks he finally rose.

He believes his wife was a burden, his son was an insult, and their deaths were the last shedding of weakness. He believes Maszareth saw what everyone else refused to see.

That makes him dangerous, but not grand.

Keldrik is not a tragic fallen hero.

He is a petty tyrant given fire.

## Current Role

The party is likely to encounter Ash-Prefect Jemmel attached to or commanding a terror unit operating in former Vogel/Cron territory.

His unit burns villages deemed “disloyal.”

These attacks are not strategic in the clean military sense. They are exemplary violence: punishment, warning, spectacle, and recruitment tool all in one. Jemmel’s unit exists to make people afraid of choosing the Crown, [[Beringer]], or the new order.

Typical targets include:

- villages that surrendered to the Lord Marshal
- peasants who gave information to Crown scouts
- former Cron holdings that accepted new authority
- communities accused of hiding deserters
- settlements where taxes or grain were redirected away from Vogel loyalists
- places where “disrespect” can be inflated into treason

Keldrik calls it discipline.

It is arson with a title.

## Personality

Keldrik is not frothing or mindless. He is worse.

He is lucid, smug, and absolutely convinced he has been vindicated.

He is:

- vain
- resentful
- classist
- cruel
- status-hungry
- obsessed with recognition
- brittle around mockery
- enraged by pity
- especially reactive to Beringer, Orin, Malik, his wife, or his son being mentioned

He wants the party to remember him.

If they do not, it wounds him more deeply than any sword strike.

## Combat and Encounter Notes

Keldrik works best as a named villain inside a larger terror unit.

He should not appear alone unless the scene is about talking before the real fight. He should arrive with fire, discipline, and screaming peasants in the background. Subtle as a brick through stained glass, but that is sort of his whole brand.

Possible unit elements:

- Ashen Wing die-hards
- Iron Covenant veterans
- chain-binders
- a censer-magus
- mounted fire-starters
- terrified local collaborators
- bound prisoners marked as “examples”

His battlefield role should be:

- command bully
- heavy infernal knight
- morale weapon
- duelist-brute
- terror officer

He should be hard to kill, but not elegant. His fighting style is force, intimidation, and punishment.

Weaknesses:

- mockery
- being ignored
- reminders of Malik checking him
- references to his wife and son
- Beringer refusing to treat him as important
- Orin breaking through his guard again, especially that same arm

## Full Monologue

> “You remember Brewfest.”

Keldrik smiles.

The flames in his armor move softly, almost lazily.

> “The Dregs. The stink of beer and sweat. Cheap paint. Cheaper courage. A crowd gathering itself into bravery because numbers let cowards pretend they are men.”

His red eyes narrow.

> “They circled. Muttered. Reached.”

A pause.

> “And I reached for my sword.”

The smile thins.

> “Then Malik Redhand spoke.”

He says the name with reverence and hatred tangled together.

> “He did not plead with them. He did not reason with gutter-filth. No. He looked at me.”

A beat.

> “At me.”

The flames brighten.

> “And said if I drew steel, he would kill me himself.”

He breathes in slowly.

> “A knight of Gorundia. Checked like a dog in the street.”

Now his eyes move to Beringer and Orin.

> “And then you came.”

Softly:

> “You saw.”

The word hangs there.

> “Not the insult. Not the disorder. Not the sickness crawling up from that district.”

His mouth twists.

> “You saw me restrained.”

The fire rises along the armor seams.

> “Then came the duel.”

He looks to Beringer.

> “You broke my arm.”

Then to Orin.

> “And later, Kharne broke it again.”

His jaw tightens.

> “The same arm. As though the gods themselves wished to write mockery into the bone.”

A pause.

> “After that, the world began rewarding absurdity. Amadis raised. Kharne ennobled. Tavern-men, half-bloods, mongrels, oathbreakers, and murderers dressed in honor while men of proper blood were told to wait.”

A harder breath.

> “Then Cron fell.”

That one hurts him.

> “My lord, brought low. His house mocked. His strength answered with treachery and called justice.”

A pause.

> “And still, I waited.”

He smiles again.

> “Then Maszareth came.”

Let that land.

> “Not a messenger. Not a whisper. Not some crawling imp with a bargain in its teeth.”

His chin lifts.

> “Maszareth himself.”

He mistakes predation for honor. Of course he does. That is basically his whole personality wearing spurs.

> “He saw what you would not. What my wife refused to honor. What my son dared mock.”

The flames flare.

> “He saw me.”

A beat.

> “And now I have been recognized.”

Soft, proud, poisoned:

> “Made stronger. Faster. Known.”

Then the confession, ugly and calm:

> “All it cost me was a wife who mistook contempt for virtue… and a son who forgot obedience.”

His hand closes around his weapon.

> “Small coin.”

The armor blazes.

> “You saw me checked in the gutter.”

A final smile.

> “Now see me unbound.”

Then:

> “Draw steel, if you like.”

A pause.

> “This time, no one will stop me.”

## Short Combat Version

> “Brewfest. The Dregs. Malik’s threat. Beringer’s duel. Kharne’s hands. Cron’s fall. My wife’s scorn. My son’s mockery. You all thought these were separate humiliations.”

The armor ignites.

> “Maszareth taught me they were a road.”

A smile.

> “And now I have arrived.”

## Death Line

If dying:

> “No…”

A wet breath.

> “I was seen.”

Another.

> “I was finally…”

Then nothing.

## If Captured

If somehow captured alive, Keldrik should be almost worse.

He will not beg at first. He will sneer, threaten, invoke Maszareth, and insist villages burned because they were disloyal.

But if stripped of armor, recognition, and terror, the old Keldrik starts leaking through.

Not remorse.

Fear.

He should not become sympathetic. He should become small.

That is the real punishment.

## DM Notes

Keldrik’s central theme is **recognition without worth**.

He is not a mastermind.  
He is not a tragic loyalist.  
He is not even especially important to Hell.

He was recruited because he was easy.

That is what should make him sting. Maszareth did not need to break a noble soul. Keldrik had already done most of the work himself.

The devil merely paid him in the one coin he could not resist.

Recognition.
