# Katherine Kendal

> “The baroness may wish for lace and manners, but what she got was a warhorse in a braid. Good thing too.”
> — Sir Aldos Verran, speaking fondly of Katherine Kendal

## Snapshot

**Dame Katherine “Kat” Kendal** is the eldest child of [[Baron Cadaras Kendal]] and [[Baroness Isolde Kendal]] of [[Barony Southmere]]. Newly knighted and only just eighteen, she stands at an uncomfortable crossroads: old enough to carry steel, young enough that everyone around her still feels entitled to decide what her knighthood means.

She currently serves as **Knight-Attendant of the Lord Marshal’s Household**, attached to [[Beringer|Sir Beringer Amadis]] after his elevation to Lord Marshal.

She is earnest, stubborn, disciplined, and visibly more comfortable in armor than in court dress. She is not polished in the old noble style, nor hardened into legend like [[Venom|Dame Venom]], [[Dana Sylvanranth|Countess Dana Sylvanranth]], or [[Braga Kharne|Baroness Braga Kharne]]. Kat is still becoming what others already insist on judging.

Her closest friendship was with [[Blue Sylvanranth]], who saw Kat as proof that goodness could be armed, noble, and still safe. Blue’s fall, judgment, and current magical entombment have left Kat in a state of suspended grief. Blue is not dead, not free, not forgiven, and not condemned. For Kat, that is in some ways worse than any clean answer.

In older alignment terms, Kat is **Lawful Good**: not flawless, not simple, but genuinely committed to duty, lawful judgment, mercy with form, and the belief that oaths should protect rather than merely bind.

## Core Identity

Kat is a young knight trying to make honor real in a political world that treats honor as either decoration or leverage.

She believes in duty. She believes in courage. She believes in the vows of knighthood. She believes in [[Beringer|Sir Beringer Amadis]], though not blindly. She has seen enough broken people around him to know that a good man can still leave wounds behind him.

Her central tension is not whether she is brave.

She is brave.

Her central tension is whether she can survive becoming a symbol before she has finished becoming herself.

## Appearance

![[Kat Kendal.png]]

Kat is tall for a young noblewoman, around 5'9", with a lean, wiry strength built from drilling, riding, and armor rather than courtly exercise. She is tanned from travel and training, with dark brown hair usually kept in a long practical braid and deep brown eyes that often look more serious than her years.

Her face still shows her youth, though she tries to bury that under posture and discipline. She carries herself like someone constantly aware of being watched.

In armor, she looks more natural: focused, alert, steady. In gowns, especially formal yellow-and-black Southmere colors, she seems restrained rather than adorned, as if waiting for permission to return to useful clothing.

Her day-to-day colors are her own, but cleanly Kendal-adjacent: black and gold, plain lines, restrained martial dignity. She does not dress as a miniature banner, but no one looking at her doubts whose house she comes from.

## Heraldry and Arms

The arms of [[House Kendal]] are stark and easily recognized: a heater shield divided vertically, gold to either side with a broad black pale down the center.
![[house kendal.png|240]]
Blazon:

> Or, a pale sable.

Motto:

> By Hand and Word.

The device is old Southmere work: plain, visible at distance, and difficult to mistake in rain, dust, or torchlight. It has none of the elaborate beasts or jeweled pretensions favored by older houses. Kendal arms speak in straight lines: keep the road, honor the contract, hold the storehouse, and stand where placed.

House Kendal is steady and trusted, but it is not counted among the great front-line warrior houses of Gorundia. Its strength lies in keeping what others depend upon: roads, grain, stores, accounts, oaths, and the practical machinery of survival.

Kat’s spear echoes the family device visually: gold flanking a dark central line, plain in shape but unmistakable in association. She has not yet wielded it in earnest. For now, it is more emblem than habit: the shape of House Kendal carried forward, not yet a weapon with a history in her hand.

## House and Family

Kat is the eldest child of [[House Kendal]], a lesser noble family of [[Barony Southmere]] under [[County Wyrmlea]]. Southmere is wealthy not through glory or ancient battlefield myth, but through trade routes, storage houses, grain brokerage, and practical mercantile power.

Her father, [[Cadaras Kendal|Baron Cadaras Kendal]], is famously pragmatic: a broad-shouldered, weather-worn noble with work-callused hands, more comfortable in a ledger hall or warehouse than a courtly salon. He earned his spurs in youth, but now leaves most martial administration to [[Aldos Verran|Knight Commander Aldos Verran]], the household Knight-Commander.

Her mother, [[Isolde Kendal|Baroness Isolde Kendal]], has long wished her daughter carried herself with more grace and less martial intensity, though that disappointment is threaded with unmistakable pride.

Kat’s younger brothers:

- [[Tomas Kendal]] — studious, already showing skill with numbers and maps.
- [[Henrick Kendal]] — boisterous, sword-happy, eager for training.
- [[Maren Kendal]] — mischievous, quick-witted, adored by the household.

Rumors suggest Baroness Isolde may be expecting another child, and has privately hoped for “a daughter with a bit more grace than dear Katherine.”

## Family Pressure

[[Isolde Kendal|Baroness Isolde Kendal]] wants to pull Kat home.

Not as punishment. Not exactly.

Isolde sees the court, the Lord Marshal’s household, the rumors, Blue’s fate, and Beringer’s shadow around her daughter, and her instinct is to remove Kat before the world takes more than pride from her. She may dress that impulse as prudence, reputation, family duty, or recovery, but beneath it is fear.

She wanted a daughter with more grace. Now she wants her daughter alive, whole, and out of reach.

[[Cadaras Kendal|Baron Cadaras Kendal]] is not a vindictive man. He is practical, steady, and not eager to turn every insult into blood.

But he is Kat’s father.

House Kendal still has a few very hard knights in its service, men who remember older customs and take family honor seriously. If the rumor keeps spreading, some of those men may begin finding formal reasons to call other men into the yard with blades. Cadaras may not order it.

He may also not stop it quickly.

## Political Background

Kat’s placement under [[Beringer]] was not merely a matter of training. Her father deliberately maneuvered her away from [[Wilhelm Vogel]] and Vogel influence, trusting Beringer more than the Duke.

At the time, this was a pointed insult. Now that Wilhelm is dead, the insult has become part of the record of who saw the rot early, who acted quietly, and who protected their children before the banners changed.

Kat’s connection to Beringer marks her as part of the wider Amadis-aligned circle, though she remains lesser nobility by birth. That makes her useful, visible, and vulnerable.

## Personality

Kat is earnest without being naive, stubborn without being theatrically defiant, and proud without being vain.

She is not good at pretending something is fine when it is not. She can keep her mouth shut, but her posture, hands, and eyes often betray the effort. She is polite because manners are armor. She is formal when uncertain. She is most comfortable when given a task, a perimeter, a horse, a blade, or a clear chain of command.

She values duty over appearance, but appearance still wounds her because court and camp both punish those who fail to perform the correct shape of dignity.

She talks more quickly when nervous. Blue once remembered her as smelling of soap and ink, speaking rapidly but with every word mattering.

Kat does not have Venom’s ancient elegance, Dana’s iron restraint, or Braga’s open defiance. She survives by standing straighter, speaking carefully, and reassembling herself before anyone important sees the pieces.

## Faith

Kat is a worshipper of [[Daern]], praying chiefly for endurance, protection, shelter, and the strength to remain standing when there is no clean victory to seize.

This is not a public problem. In Gorundian knightly culture, her devotion to Daern sits comfortably beneath the broader martial and chivalric authority of [[Heironeous]]. She honors Heironeous as the visible ideal of knighthood, courage, and righteous service, but her private prayers go first to Daern.

Her devotional token is a small iron portcullis, worn or kept close. It is plain, heavy for its size, and deliberately unornamental.

Heironeous tells her what a knight should be.

Daern helps her survive becoming one.

## Training and Knighthood

Kat was trained under [[Beringer|Sir Beringer Amadis]], first as a squire and then as a young knight tied to his circle. Her advancement has been politically visible, emotionally complicated, and socially contested.

She was formally knighted by the King himself in a quiet ceremony shortly after Beringer became Lord Marshal. The ceremony was lawful, honorable, and witnessed, but deliberately private. There was no tournament field, no great hall full of cheering knights, and no public feast to fix the moment in common memory.

Those present were the tight royal circle: the King, the Chaplain, [[Sapphire Flame]], [[The Krozen]], and Beringer.

Beringer presented her with one of his own swords.

That gift was honor, trust, and burden all at once. It marked her as his former squire, as a knight raised under his eye, and as someone he believed worthy of steel that had belonged to him. It also gave the crueler tongues at court another shape to twist.

The King’s act recognized what Kat had already been forced to become during the crisis: a young woman carrying knightly duty under grief, scrutiny, and dangerous association. She had served faithfully through the collapse of Vogel power, Blue Sylvanranth’s fall, and Beringer’s rise to command. In the King’s judgment, she had already borne the burden of knighthood before anyone placed the title upon her.

The exact words spoken by the King are still to be recorded.

To those loyal to her, the quiet ceremony was proof of royal trust.

To her enemies, it became proof of favoritism.

Her knighthood is real.

That has not stopped people from trying to make it smaller.

## Arms and Personal Effects

Kat carries one of Beringer’s own swords, given to her at her knighting. It is not merely a weapon but a visible line of trust between knight and former squire.

Her spear echoes the Kendal coat of arms: gold flanking a dark central line, plain in shape but unmistakable in association. She has not yet truly wielded it. It remains, for now, a heraldic promise rather than a proven weapon.

She also keeps a small iron portcullis of [[Daern]]. It is not displayed for admiration. It is touched in moments when she needs strength, restraint, or the reminder that a closed gate can be mercy as well as refusal.

## Current Posting

Kat currently serves as a young knight attached to the Lord Marshal’s household.

Working formal style:

**Dame Katherine Kendal, Knight-Attendant of the Lord Marshal’s Household**

Her duties are practical rather than decorative: escort, sealed messages, witness service, shield detail, and controlled exposure to command under older knights. Her position keeps her close to Beringer’s authority without making her a commander before she is ready.

It also keeps her visible.

Visibility has not been kind.

## Combat Style

Kat fights like someone trained to survive rather than impress.

She favors practical arms and armor, most likely sword and shield, with a strong defensive instinct. Her shield is not decoration; it is part of how she thinks. She is more line-holder than duelist, more protector than performer.

She is not yet a battlefield legend. Her strength lies in discipline, awareness, and willingness to stand where she is placed.

Possible combat notes:

- Shield-forward style.
- Strong riding fundamentals.
- Good armor discipline.
- Better under orders than in freeform chaos.
- Protective instincts can override caution.
- Vulnerable to emotional disruption when Blue or Beringer are involved.
- More reliable in formation than in a chaotic personal duel.
- Will hold position past good sense if she believes duty requires it.

## Relationship: Sir Beringer Amadis

Kat loves [[Beringer|Sir Beringer Amadis]].

The feeling is complicated: squirely devotion, idealized courtly love, hero worship, gratitude, and a teenage crush all tangled together. She knows enough to be ashamed of parts of it and loyal enough to insist, even to herself, that it is not “like that.”

It is not a clean romantic desire, and should not be seen as one.

Beringer is her model of knighthood, her protector, her mentor, and the man whose judgment she still trusts despite knowing he can hurt people while trying to do right. She believes his judgment may be trusted even when his temper should not be.

Beringer likely understands enough to be careful. Kat does not let him see how deeply the rumors wound her. To admit that pain to him would feel, to her, like showing weakness and making the whispers more real.

This trust is one of her strengths.

It is also one of the easiest ways to wound her.

## Relationship: Blue Sylvanranth

Blue was Kat’s best friend.

They were squires together, close enough to become sisters in everything but blood. Blue helped Kat feel less alone in a world that expected too much too quickly. Kat helped Blue with letters; Blue helped Kat clean armor. They laughed, drilled, kept watch, shared nerves, and tried to survive adults who kept mistaking movement for responsibility.

Blue idealized Kat as proof that a girl could carry a sword and still belong, that discipline did not have to mean coldness, and that nobility did not have to mean cruelty.

Kat did not know she had become that important.

That ignorance now haunts her.

After Blue’s fall, Kat said:

> “She was my best friend, and I didn’t see it. I thought she was just tired, hiding the same way I do—behind jokes, chores, noise. I saw the cracks and I told myself it was nothing, that she’d talk when she was ready. She didn’t. She kept her secrets from me too.”

Blue is currently held under an [[Entombment]] spell. Kat knows this, but does not know where. She knows the PC group chose that course and assumes [[Norwin]] performed the magic.

She resents it.

Not cleanly. Not entirely. Kat is lawful and good-hearted enough to understand why a terrible mercy may have been chosen. But understanding does not stop the ache. In her heart, Blue has been buried without dying, spared without being freed, and judged without being allowed to speak.

Blue’s judgment has been deferred. This leaves Kat trapped between mourning, waiting, fearing, and hoping.

She cannot grieve properly because Blue lives.

She cannot forgive herself because Blue has not spoken.

She cannot seek justice because judgment has not come.

If Blue wakes, Kat believes she must be allowed to speak in her own voice before anyone calls the matter justice. If Blue is guilty, let her say it with her own mouth. If she is not, let her say that too.

## Relationship: Norwin

Kat assumes [[Norwin]] performed the magic that placed Blue under [[Entombment]].

This has given her resentment a shape, though not necessarily a fair one. She does not know the details of the spell, the safeguards, or who made each part of the decision. She only knows that Blue is buried somewhere out of reach, and Norwin is the wizard-shaped answer her mind keeps returning to.

Norwin likely has not given much thought to what Kat assumes.

That may matter later.

## Relationship: Vianola Erinleaf

[[Vianola Erinleaf]] met Kat through Blue’s journals before she met Kat herself, which made the encounter dangerous for both of them.

Vianola recognized that Blue had made Kat into a symbol, but also that Kat remained “substantially real beneath Blue’s idealization.” Her conclusion was that Kat carries guilt but has not yet converted it into identity, and must be kept from becoming a monument to someone else’s wound.

Vianola’s private assessment:

> “She does not know whether to mourn Blue, wait for Blue, prepare for Blue, or forgive herself before Blue has the chance to do it properly.”

Kat thanked Vianola afterward for not making Blue smaller.

## Relationship: Dame Venom

Kat sees [[Venom|Dame Venom]] as both intimidating and admirable. Venom represents an older, stranger, more elegant model of feminine knighthood: ancient, controlled, dangerous, and emotionally guarded.

Where Venom has perfected distance, Kat is still trying not to show pain at the wrong moment.

If the rumor about Kat reaches Venom, she is likely to address it. That may not help. Venom is known to have been Beringer’s lover, and any defense from her risks feeding the same mouths it seeks to close.

## Relationship: Dana Sylvanranth

[[Dana Sylvanranth|Countess Dana Sylvanranth]] is another model Kat cannot easily imitate. Dana is iron-willed, clinical, hard-edged, and controlled almost to the point of cruelty.

Kat may respect Dana’s strength, but she does not share Dana’s emotional architecture. Kat cannot simply decide not to hurt. She can only decide what to do while hurting.

## Relationship: Baroness Braga Kharne

[[Braga Kharne|Baroness Braga Kharne]] is defiant, forceful, and openly impossible to ignore. Kat is not that.

Braga can shout a room into submission. Kat survives rooms by standing straighter.

The contrast matters. Kat’s strength is not loud defiance. It is dignity under pressure.

## Relationship: Cornelia Bordella

Kat is wary of [[Cornelia Bordella]], though the exact shape of that wariness remains unresolved.

Cornelia’s connections to Bertram, Blue, court scandal, and wider political intrigue make her difficult for Kat to read. Kat may recognize Cornelia’s kindness while still distrusting the web of consequences surrounding her.

## The Rumor

There are whispers that Kat earned her spurs “on her back” rather than on her feet.

The rumor is cruel because it twists several visible truths into a lie:

- Kat is young.
- Kat is female.
- Kat was trained by Beringer.
- Kat loves Beringer in a complicated, visible way.
- Her father placed her under Beringer’s protection for political reasons.
- Her rise happened during a period of crisis and factional upheaval.
- Her knighting was performed quietly by the King himself shortly after Beringer became Lord Marshal.
- Beringer presented her with one of his own swords.

The lie is designed to make her knighthood seem sexual, political, and unearned.

The fact that the King knighted her should have silenced such talk. Instead, among those determined to wound her, it merely changed the shape of the accusation. They imply Beringer’s favor was laundered through royal hands.

The rumor has reached [[House Kendal]]. Her family has attempted to ignore it, shrug it off, and deny it the dignity of formal response. That restraint may be wise politics, but it leaves Kat feeling exposed. Silence can look like strength from the outside and abandonment from the inside.

Kat does not let Beringer see how badly the rumor hurts her. She would see that as weakness, and worse, as making the rumor part of the space between them.

Kat’s public response is controlled silence, formal correction, or withdrawal. Her private response is sharper: shame, fury, and fear that her own feelings for Beringer make the lie harder to dismiss.

This rumor should not define her, but it follows her because it attacks the exact place where she is least able to defend herself cleanly.

## Current Emotional State

Kat is in suspended grief.

Blue’s entombment has denied her closure. She cannot mourn Blue as dead, cannot embrace her as living, cannot defend her as innocent, and cannot condemn her as guilty. She is left with memory, guilt, resentment, and waiting.

She is also carrying the burden of her own knighthood under hostile scrutiny. Her armor is both protection and argument. Every time she stands in public, she is silently insisting that her title is real.

Her central fear is not death.

Her central fear is that the people she loved needed more from her than she knew how to give.

## Public Face

To most observers, Dame Katherine Kendal is:

- young
- serious
- dutiful
- reserved under scrutiny
- practical
- plainly more soldier than courtier
- closely tied to Beringer
- marked by association with Blue’s tragedy
- the subject of ugly camp gossip
- a lesser noble suddenly made visible by royal favor and political disaster

Those who know her better see:

- intense loyalty
- self-blame
- wounded pride
- real courage
- quick intelligence
- an unsteady but sincere moral center
- a girl trying very hard to become the knight everyone already argues about

## Voice

Kat speaks formally when under pressure. With trusted friends, especially Blue, she becomes quicker, warmer, more talkative, and more openly young.

Public speech:

> “That is not true, my lord.”

> “I was knighted lawfully.”

> “I will answer any charge made properly.”

> “I would rather not discuss Dame Blue as if she were absent from herself.”

Private speech:

> “I don’t know what I was supposed to see.”

> “I keep thinking if I had asked one more question…”

> “Everyone talks about justice like it is a door. It feels more like a wall.”

> “I know what they say. I also know why they say it.”

> “If she wakes, she should speak. Not us. Not first.”
