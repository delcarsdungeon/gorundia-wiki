# Radomir Stonehand

![[90_Assets/Radomir Stonehand.png|340]]

_Bodyguard to the Lord Marshal_

---

> [!quote] “My lord breathes because I am suspicious man.”  
> — [[Radomir Stonehand]]

> [!quote] “Door does not win argument with this one.”  
> — Radomir, on [[Milena]]

## Overview

[[Radomir Stonehand]] is the personal bodyguard of [[Sir Beringer Amadis]], drawn from hard Orenic territory and carrying all the warmth of a locked gate in winter.

He is not courtly.

He is not charming.

He is not interested in clever explanations from men who appear within blade range of the Lord Marshal without permission.

Radomir’s duty is simple: stand between Beringer and everything else.

He performs this duty with the grim efficiency of a siege weight dropped from a tower.

Among the Marshal’s growing circle, Radomir has become a fixture: arms folded, eyes moving, expression flat, listening to every word and trusting almost none of them. He speaks rarely, but when he does, people tend to remember it. Not because he is poetic. Because he removes every unnecessary syllable before speaking, then uses what remains like a hammer.

He is one of the few men in Beringer’s orbit who does not care whether a visitor is noble, holy, royal, foreign, beautiful, famous, or carrying parchment with very important wax on it.

If they are too close, they are too close.

This has already caused problems.

It has also prevented several.

## Appearance

Radomir is a thick-built, scarred veteran with the battered look of a man who has been mistaken for dead several times and took it personally.

He wears heavy armor like a second hide, favoring practical protection over courtly polish. His gear is maintained, but never decorative. He carries himself with the compact readiness of a man who expects violence to happen close, fast, and without a herald announcing it first.

His face is heavily weathered and scarred, with pale, watchful eyes set beneath a hard brow. His expression usually rests somewhere between suspicion and disappointment.

He often stands with arms folded.

This is not relaxation.

It is storage.

---

## Voice and Speech

Radomir speaks Common with a heavy Orenic/Suel-border accent: clipped, hard, and compressed. His grammar is often simplified, but never foolish. He speaks like a man using a tool, not composing music.

He drops articles.  
He avoids ornament.  
He does not soften warnings.  
He does not waste threats.

Examples:

> “You are too close.”

> “No. You stop there.”

> “I do not like men who enter without sound.”

> “Sound is polite. Sound keeps blood inside body.”

> “You have seal. Good. Show seal before I show steel.”

> “If Crown sends shadow, shadow waits outside tent.”

> “You are not first clever man. Clever men die same as stupid men, only surprised.”

His accent becomes heavier when angry.

Unfortunately for visitors, Radomir is often angry.

---

## Demeanor

Radomir is quiet, not because he lacks thoughts, but because most thoughts do not improve by being shared.

He is watchful by habit and suspicious by profession. He tracks hands, doorways, weight shifts, breath control, and the tiny lies people tell with their feet before their mouths catch up.

He does not posture unless posture itself is useful.

He can stand still for an hour without fidgeting.

This unsettles squires, courtiers, and anyone with a guilty conscience, which is most of the aristocracy if we are being honest and not pretending perfume changes the nature of bone.

---

## Duties

Radomir’s duty is not to guard the Lord Marshal’s dignity.

It is to guard his life.

This distinction matters.

He is not a chamberlain, not an aide, not a political handler, and not a decorative strongman placed nearby to make Beringer look important. Radomir is close protection. He controls access, reads threats, blocks doors, and decides who gets within reach.

When necessary, he acts before asking.

This has already proven important during official audiences, including the arrival of the Furyondian delegation, where Radomir immediately identified Rashgar as the meaningful danger behind Countess Neltara Blackheart. His response was simple, physical, and absolutely Radomir: he shifted his stance, blocked the door, and said, “No.” :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

When Kat later tried to unpack what had happened, Radomir’s assessment remained brutally spare: the wizard stepped forward, wanted inside, and that was enough. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

## Role in the Lord Marshal’s Circle

Radomir is not part of the Lord Marshal’s strategy table in the formal sense.

He is something more useful and less pleasant.

He is the man who notices when the strategy table has too many knives beneath it.

Beringer’s circle includes knights, priests, mages, nobles, spies, criminals, warlords, and people who are somehow several of those things at once because apparently Gorundia is allergic to clean categories. Radomir does not attempt to understand every political layer. He classifies people by immediate usefulness, honesty under pressure, and whether they are likely to get Beringer killed.

His categories are simple.

They are also usually correct.

## The Old Witch’s Prophecy

When Radomir was young, an old witch looked at his hands, spat once into the ashes, and laughed.

> “Stonehand. Hah. They name you wrong.”

She tapped his boot with one yellow nail.

> “You do not die on stone. You die in water.”

Young Radomir answered exactly as young Radomir would:

> “Then I stay away from boats.”

The witch smiled.

> “All men think water means boat.”

Radomir has disliked prophecy ever since.

He has avoided boats, lakes, wells, rivers, stupid fountains, decorative noble ponds, and unnecessary marshes for many years. This has not made him feel safer. Only irritated.

The stronger form of the prophecy, as remembered by Radomir, is:

> “You will not die on the road, Radomir Stonehand.  
> You will not die on the wall.  
> You will die with water at your knees, holding back a door that should not open.”

This prophecy remains unresolved.

Radomir does not discuss it unless something has gone wrong enough that poetry starts looking like evidence.

## Riven Halcyre and the Ninth Seal

Radomir’s first proper encounter with [[Riven Halcyre]] is expected to be hostile.

Riven, operating under awakened Ninth Seal authority, can plausibly arrive at the Lord Marshal’s mobile camp after traveling unlisted aboard the ship. Radomir will not enjoy discovering that a half-elf operative has bypassed perimeter, watch, and camp procedure to appear near Beringer.

By “not enjoy,” read: may attempt to turn the man into a cautionary stain.

Radomir’s position is simple:

> “You do not appear beside him. You are permitted beside him.”

The tension is useful. Riven represents old hidden authority. Radomir represents immediate sworn protection. Both are necessary. Neither will like that.

Likely exchange:

> “You are very close to killing a lawful royal operative.”  
> — Riven Halcyre

> “Then he should have entered lawfully.”  
> — Radomir Stonehand

Radomir’s judgment of Riven remains pending, but his starting classification is obvious:

**Quiet man. Hidden knife. Watch hands.**

## Relationship with Kat Kendal

Radomir and [[Kat Kendal]] have developed an odd working rhythm.

Kat talks.

Radomir listens.

Kat fills silence because silence makes people uncomfortable.

Radomir maintains silence because discomfort reveals things.

During the aftermath of the Furyondian delegation’s entrance, Kat tried to process what had just happened while Radomir simply watched the door. She observed that Neltara Blackheart had not merely expected trouble, but seemed to want it. Radomir agreed without drama, noting that the Countess had been watching and that this was honest, if not comforting. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Radomir’s private assessment of Kat:

> “She gets mad, she’s listening.  
> She laughs, she’s tougher than looks.  
> She ignores me, I stop.”

This is high praise from him.

Unfortunately, it is also practical advice.

## On Milena and Amadis the Younger

Radomir’s assessment of [[Alistair Amadis the Younger]] marrying [[Milena]] is one of his warmer statements, which is to say no one was actively threatened.

He watches Milena first.

Then Amadis.

A short exhale through the nose.

> “Hah.”

A beat.

> “Lucky boy.”

His eyes return to Milena.

> “Big woman. Strong frame.”

A small nod. Approval, plain as a hammer.

> “Knows herself.”

Another beat.

> “Good.”

Almost a smile.

> “Door does not win argument with this one.”

Radomir respects physical certainty, self-knowledge, and people who do not apologize for occupying space.

Milena qualifies.

This does not make them shallow.

It makes them efficient.

No talking.

## Guaco

Radomir’s first impression of [[Guaco]] was:

> **Trap.**

Radomir’s baseline survival rule is simple:

> If it smells good, it is trying to kill you.

Guaco broke that rule immediately.

Too friendly.  
Too generous.  
Too much food.  
Food with layers.  
Food that arrives after Radomir said no.  
Food that keeps coming even when he stops chewing.

Radomir classified it as some kind of halfling ambush doctrine.

> “No one needs this much gravy.”

He later added:

> “I have survived sieges. I was not trained for pie.”

### Final Assessment of Guaco

In combat: unassuming, observant, underestimated.

Dangerous.

In politics: soft voice, iron boundaries.

Also dangerous.

At table: absolute menace.

Radomir will stand guard for Guaco without being asked. He will intervene immediately if someone disrespects him. He will eat exactly one additional bite to be polite and refuse seconds with visible strain.

His full endorsement:

> “Good man. Would trust him with my back.  
> Would not trust him with my stomach.”

Dana once noticed Radomir silently suffering through a halfling feast, accepting a second helping against all instincts.

Later, privately, she said:

> “You didn’t have to finish it.”

Radomir, flat:

> “He cooked.”

## Combat Role

Radomir is not a battlefield commander, though he has the instincts of a man who has survived bad fields and worse orders.

His role is close defense.

He excels at:

- controlling physical space
- reading imminent violence
- blocking access
- intercepting ambush
- standing still long enough to become part of the room
- making threats that require no translation
- fighting in confined spaces
- preventing clever people from becoming clever near Beringer

He is at his best in doorways, halls, tents, camp lanes, stair mouths, and anywhere else an enemy thinks numbers matter.

Radomir likes narrow spaces.

Narrow spaces mean fewer directions for idiots to come from.

---

## Personality

Radomir is not humorless.

His humor is simply dry enough to crack leather.

He notices far more than he says, and his opinions often emerge as compact verdicts. He has little patience for performance, but he does respect people who know what they are, even if what they are is dangerous.

He dislikes prophecy, hidden authority, over-seasoned food, soft-handed arrogance, decorative ponds, and men who enter quietly.

He respects discipline, plain courage, physical competence, honest anger, and anyone who can stand still without needing applause.

## Rykeena Pitt  
  
Radomir’s original assessment of [[Rykeena Pitt]] was simple:  
  
> “Warhorse pretending to be a person.”  
  
This remains accurate.  
  
Unfortunately, it has become less complete.  
  
Rykeena’s directness, physical confidence, and refusal to be intimidated have placed her in a category Radomir does not enjoy naming. She is dangerous, useful, observant, and entirely too pleased when he notices.  
  
Radomir’s revised private classification:  
  
> “Pretty warhorse. Problem.”  
  
He considers her smile suspicious.  
  
He has not decided whether this makes it better or worse.  
  
When asked if he is afraid of her, Radomir answered:  
  
> “Yes.”  
  
A pause.  
  
> “Good fear. Keeps hand steady.”  
  
Another.  
  
> “Bad fear makes man run.”  
  
His eyes remained on her.  
  
> “I am still here.”

![[Rykeena and Radomir.png|340]]

“Rykeena Pitt. Strong. Fast. Watches corners. Laughs when should not. Does not ask if danger is present, only where.”

A pause.

“Smile is trap.”

Another pause.

“May be acceptable trap.”

Sorek Redmarch:

> “Careful, Stonehand. She keeps smiling like that, you’ll have to start forming full sentences.”

Radomir:

> “Old dog should watch own bones.”

Sorek:

> “There he is.”

## Table Use

Radomir works best when used sparingly.

He should not explain plots.

He should identify pressure.

He should not debate politics.

He should reduce them to threat geometry.

He is useful whenever a scene needs:

- a hard security read
- a clipped verdict
- someone to distrust the charming visitor
- someone to make Beringer’s authority feel physically protected
- quiet tension in a hallway
- a joke that sounds like a threat and might actually be one

He is the wall beside the Lord Marshal.

Sometimes walls speak.

Usually to say no.
