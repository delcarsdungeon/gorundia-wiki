As of 10 Coldeven 1231
# Sir Garran Rook

> “Mercy is not being told you were innocent. Mercy is being made useful after guilt.”
> — Sir Garran Rook

## Snapshot

**Sir Garran Rook** is a household knight of [[House Kendal]], once a Southmere road-thief, smuggler, leg-breaker, and road-scum survivor, now one of Baron [[Cadaras Kendal]]'s hardest and most reliable men.

He was not born noble. He was not trained gently. He was not redeemed by a speech.

He was spared the gallows, placed under [[Sir Aldos Verran]], broken down by discipline, and rebuilt into something useful. Garran drank the whole cup: duty, shame, debt, service, restraint, and the particular kind of honor that comes from knowing exactly what he used to be.

He has joined the [[Lord Marshal's Host]] quietly, not as an obvious Kendal agent and not as a visible escort for [[Dame Katherine Kendal]]. He keeps away from Kat and away from the command circle. Officially, he rides as a free household knight seeking service under the Lord Marshal during unsettled times.

Unofficially, the rumors about Kat reached Southmere.

Cadaras did not send him.

Cadaras also did not stop him.

## Core Identity

Garran is what happens when mercy works.

Not soft mercy. Not sentimental mercy. The kind that says a guilty man may live, but only if he spends the rest of his life proving the judgment was not wasted.

He is not a shining knight. He does not pretend to be.

He is a guarded gate, a scar with a sword, and a reminder that House Kendal is steady, not soft.

His central tension is simple:

He knows violence. He knows law. He knows the difference.

When insult, cruelty, or disorder crosses into his reach, he has to decide whether he is acting as a knight, as a debtor, or as the criminal he used to be.

Most days, he chooses knight.

Some days, the line is thinner.

## Appearance

![[Garran Rook.png|340]]

Garran is broad, thick-handed, and built like a prison door taught to ride.

He has close-cropped iron-gray hair, a broken nose, a notched ear, and the flat, watchful eyes of a man who used to count exits before he learned to guard them. A long knife scar pulls across his left cheek, especially when he smiles.

He rarely smiles.

His armor is darkened, practical, and well-maintained. No bright plume. No courtly excess. No ribbons unless someone with authority requires it, and even then they look temporary. His surcoat is plain and weathered, bearing his own arms rather than the undifferenced Kendal device.

He looks like a man who has personally disappointed several magistrates and outlived most of them.

## Heraldry

Garran bears personal arms granted after his knighting.

Blazon:

> Sable, a rook-bird argent perched upon a portcullis Or, within a bordure embattled azure.

Plain English:

> A black shield bearing a silver rook-bird perched on a gold portcullis, surrounded by a blue battlement border.

Motto:

> Spared, Therefore Bound.

### Heraldic Meaning

**Black field:** his past, not erased.

**Silver rook-bird:** watchfulness, carrion-memory, and a criminal bird turned sentinel.

**Gold portcullis:** Daern, protection, guarded passage, and the gate he now holds rather than breaks.

**Blue embattled border:** lawful service, walls, discipline, and the boundary between the man he was and the knight he became.

The arms are deliberately plain and grim. They do not pretend Garran was always noble. They say he was spared, armed, watched, and bound.

## House Kendal and Southmere

Garran belongs to [[House Kendal]], but not by blood.

He is one of the house's hard-made men: useful, proven, and never entirely decorous. Southmere produces merchants, grain-brokers, caravan guards, road-watchers, warehouse men, and practical knights who understand that civilization is mostly logistics with swords nearby.

Garran fits Southmere better than court ever will.

He understands roads, storehouses, river cargo, grain lines, and the kind of men who gather around honest trade looking for weakness. He once preyed on those lines. Now he guards them.

That fact matters to House Kendal.

They did not cleanse him by pretending he had never been guilty.

They made him stand where guilt could be put to use.

## Criminal Past

Before he was Sir Garran Rook, he was Garran Rook of no house, no arms, and no lawful trade worth recording.

He worked roads and margins: smuggling, robbery, intimidation, bad company, worse employers. He was not a charming outlaw or tavern-story rogue. He was a hard man with a talent for ambushes and a willingness to live ugly.

He was caught after a grain-road robbery near Southmere went badly.

A cart guard died. A boy was maimed. Garran expected rope.

The record notes one complication: Garran stopped one of his own men from killing children in the wagon.

That did not make him innocent.

It made him salvageable.

[[Baron Cadaras Kendal]] stayed the sentence and gave him to [[Sir Aldos Verran]].

That was not pardon.

That was judgment by other means.

## Remaking Under Sir Aldos

[[Sir Aldos Verran]] did not redeem Garran with poetry.

He used work, discipline, drill, humiliation, road duty, night watch, escort service, and repeated instruction delivered with shield, staff, and patience Garran did not deserve.

Aldos taught Garran three things that stuck:

- violence without order is appetite
- mercy creates debt
- a man is not made clean by being spared, only responsible

Garran still calls Aldos “Sir” with the tone other men reserve for prayer.

He would not describe Aldos as a father.

He would probably hit anyone else who did.

It is still true.

## Relationship: Baron Cadaras Kendal

Garran's loyalty to [[Baron Cadaras Kendal]] is absolute and unsentimental.

Cadaras had every lawful reason to hang him. Instead, he made a different judgment and then required Garran to live inside the consequences. Garran does not mistake this for softness.

He knows Cadaras saw him clearly.

That is why the debt holds.

Garran is not a court creature. He is not one of Cadaras's smiling negotiators or ledger-hall men. He is used where roads turn dangerous, where stores need guarding, where an escort must be trusted not to panic, and where a message needs to arrive without a dozen fools asking whether the recipient will be offended by the packaging.

Cadaras trusts him with ugly work.

Not because Garran is gentle.

Because Garran is controlled.

## Relationship: Baroness Isolde Kendal

[[Baroness Isolde Kendal]] does not dislike Garran, but she has never fully relaxed around him.

She remembers what he was. She also knows what he became.

Her judgment of him is practical: useful, loyal, dangerous, not suitable for delicate rooms unless the room needs to be made less delicate.

Garran is careful around her. Not courtly, exactly. Careful.

He knows she is Kat's mother. He knows fear for a child does not always sound fair. He does not hold that against her.

## Relationship: Dame Katherine Kendal

Garran remembers Dame [[Katherine Kendal]] as a child running around Southmere Hall with ink on her fingers and bruises on her shins.

He keeps his distance from her now.

Deliberately.

He does not hover near her tent. He does not request duty beside her. He does not present himself as her protector. He does not let the camp see a hard Kendal knight standing at her shoulder like her father's leash.

That would make her look managed.

It would make her look fragile.

It would make the rumor worse.

So Garran stays away.

But he watches the edges.

He watches young knights laughing too late. He watches squires carrying jokes from one fire to another. He watches men who say “only a jest” after testing whether filth will pass unchallenged.

Kat may not know he is there at first.

When she learns, she is likely to be angry, embarrassed, and privately relieved. The relief will annoy her most of all.

Garran does not need her gratitude.

He does need her command.

If he is acting in Kendal honor, then Kendal honor may tell him where the line is.

## Relationship: Sir Beringer Amadis

Garran respects [[Beringer|Sir Beringer Amadis]] as Lord Marshal and as the man Cadaras trusted enough to take Katherine out from under Vogel influence.

He does not idolize him.

Garran's view of Beringer is practical:

- brave
- dangerous
- useful
- too visible
- too central to the rumor
- not the right man to solve every wound caused in his shadow

Garran avoids Beringer's command circle because his presence there would look like pressure from Southmere. Worse, it would make the matter about Beringer.

Garran understands that if Beringer answers the rumor directly, the rumor mutates.

That does not mean Garran thinks Beringer should do nothing.

It means Garran thinks the correct answer may need to come from Kendal hands.

## Relationship: Sophia Amadis

[[Sophia Amadis|Lady Sophia]] first becomes interested in Garran through pattern recognition.

One incident is a duel.

Two incidents are data.

Sophia follows the name from casualty note to duty roll to pay ledger to patent. She discovers the Southmere witness marks, the Kendal confirmation, and Aldos Verran's name behind Garran's knighthood.

Her conclusion is precise:

> “The first duel says a rough knight defended a lady's honor within form. The second incident says someone is making a habit of correction. A third will say men are being silenced because the rumor frightens someone powerful.”

Sophia does not dislike Garran. She is wary of the story he creates.

She also understands something important: Garran is not random. He is a Kendal answer moving before anyone has admitted there is a Kendal question.

## Relationship: Brother Anselm Crowther

[[Brother Anselm Crowther]] is the camp healer whose notes help surface the pattern.

After the second incident, Anselm treats a man with two broken fingers, a split brow, and a dislocated shoulder. He knows the difference between a fall, a duel, and correction administered by a man who knows exactly how much damage he means to do.

Anselm is not Garran's enemy.

He is a healer.

That means Garran gives him work.

Anselm does not appreciate unnecessary work.

Possible Anselm line:

> “I can mend fingers. I cannot mend whatever defect caused him to use them in that manner.”

## Faith

Garran honors [[Daern]].

Not theatrically. Not poetically. He is not the sort of man who weeps over shrine candles unless something has gone very wrong and no one else is there to see it.

Daern matters to him because Daern is the god of gates, walls, defenses, and the hard mercy of keeping danger on one side and the vulnerable on the other.

Garran's faith is built around a single idea:

He used to be the thing outside the gate.

Now he holds it.

He respects [[Heironeous]], especially as Aldos framed him: courage, order, righteous service, and the duties of arms. But Daern is the god whose symbol he understands in his bones.

The portcullis on his arms is not decoration.

It is confession.

## Combat Style

Garran fights with shield, sword, body weight, and practical contempt for ornament.

He does not fight dirty in the sense of cheating.

He fights dirty in the sense that courtly duelists complain about afterward while a healer counts their teeth.

His method is lawful, controlled, and ugly:

- shield-rim
- bind
- pommel
- boot hook
- belt grab
- shoulder pressure
- knee pin
- mud if mud is available
- terrain if terrain is available
- ending the fight before style becomes relevant

He does not waste motion on display. He closes distance fast, breaks rhythm, collapses balance, and turns the duel into work.

Aldos taught him how to fight without romance.

Garran learned well.

## The First Incident

On 9 Coldeven 1231, near the horse-lines of the [[Lord Marshal's Host]], a young knight repeated a slander about [[Dame Katherine Kendal]] loudly enough for squires to hear.

Garran required him to name his house, name his second, and either repeat the insult as charge or retract.

The knight refused to retract.

Garran challenged him.

The duel was proper: witnesses, seconds, cleared ground, insult heard, retraction refused, challenge accepted.

The fight itself was not courtly. Garran used shield-rim, bind, boot, pommel, mud, and weight. He defeated the younger knight, pinned him, and took his yield.

Garran's only recorded words after the yield:

> “Next time, speak charge or silence.”

By supper, the story had become unexpected chivalry: a rough knight defending a lady's honor within form.

Those who watched knew better.

It was proper.

It was also a beating with witnesses.

## The Second Incident

Two days after the duel, another man reached the healers with two broken fingers, a split brow, and a shoulder sitting wrong beneath the skin.

No formal duel had been recorded.

The name carried anyway:

> Rook.

This second incident changes the story.

The first was lawful. The second suggests pattern.

It brings Sophia into the matter, and through Sophia, the question becomes larger than whether Garran is right to hurt men who slander Kat.

The question becomes what story the camp will tell if he continues.

## The Rumor Around Kat

The rumor claims that [[Katherine Kendal]] earned her spurs “on her back” rather than through service, courage, and lawful knighthood.

Garran understands what the rumor is designed to do.

It is not merely an insult. It is an attempt to make Kat's knighthood seem sexual, political, and unearned. It attacks the exact place where she cannot defend herself cleanly without making the rumor more visible.

Garran's instinct is to make the cost of repeating it immediate.

Sophia's concern is that this may change the rumor's shape:

- first version: Kat was slandered
- second version: Kat is being defended
- third version: men are being silenced
- fourth version: the Lord Marshal's people are hiding something

Garran understands this once it is put plainly.

He may not like it.

But he understands.

## Current Role in the Lord Marshal's Host

Garran is presently riding with the lower and rougher elements of the [[Lord Marshal's Host]], away from Kat and away from Beringer's command circle.

He takes ordinary duties.

He keeps his horse, gear, and shield in order.

He listens more than he speaks.

He lets men underestimate him until they choose not to.

His presence should first be felt through consequences:

- a mess line going quiet when he appears
- a young knight with a swollen jaw refusing to explain himself
- a squire suddenly deciding a joke is not worth finishing
- Brother Anselm recording the same name twice
- Sophia pulling patents because two incidents are no longer coincidence

## How He Handles Insults

Garran's preferred escalation, when he has control of himself and the situation, is:

First:

> “Name your house.”

Second:

> “Say it again as charge.”

Third:

> “Then retract.”

Fourth:

> “Then draw.”

That is the lawful version.

The dangerous version happens when the insult is repeated outside formal company, by a man who knows exactly what he is doing, with no honest intention of standing behind it.

Then Garran may skip the pageantry.

That is the version that creates problems.

## Personality

Garran is quiet, blunt, and controlled.

He is not humorless, but his humor is dry enough to pass for a threat if heard from the wrong angle. He has little patience for ornament, evasions, or men who mistake polished manners for virtue.

He does not claim to be a good man.

He claims to be bound.

That distinction matters to him.

He has a strong sense of justice, but it is not gentle. His rules are simple:

- mercy creates obligation
- slander is not harmless
- a formal insult deserves a formal answer
- a man who refuses law should not be surprised by force
- the vulnerable should not pay for the mouths of the comfortable
- if you were spared, you owe proof

He can be harsh without being cruel.

He can also be cruel if he decides cruelty is the only language a man has chosen to speak.

That is the risk.

## Public Reputation

To most of the host, Garran is:

- hard
- rough
- Southmere, once the papers are found
- a former criminal, if the wrong clerk talks
- a proper knight, if the right clerk talks
- dangerous in close quarters
- not a man to test
- unexpectedly formal when insult becomes charge
- less formal when filth stays filth

To courtly knights, he may look like proof that House Kendal has strange standards.

To soldiers, he looks like a man who knows how fights actually end.

To squires, he is terrifying.

To healers, he is extra work.

To Sophia, he is a pattern.

To Kat, he is a complication she must decide whether to command.

## Private Shame

Garran does not forgive himself for what he was.

He also does not wallow in it where others can admire the shape of his guilt.

His shame has become routine:

- check the girth
- walk the road
- stand the watch
- answer the bell
- pay the debt
- repeat

He distrusts easy absolution.

If someone tells him he has “made up for it,” he will likely answer:

> “Not your account to close.”

## Voice

Garran speaks little. His sentences are short, plain, and usually final.

He does not dress threats as poetry.
