# Sir Alden Frostwind

> [!quote] Sorek Redmarch
> Honor’s a fine thing… until it gets tangled in court games and anonymous vegetable deliveries. Alden’s the only man I know who could stumble into legend with both spurs on the wrong boots.

## Overview

**Full Name:** Sir Alden Frostwind  
**Age:** 27  
**Title:** Knight of Stormarrow  
**Allegiance:** Sworn to [[Baron Orin Kharne]]  
**Former Allegiance:** [[House Cron]] of Lynwenden  
**Known For:** The infamous Squash and Spur Affair  

Sir Alden Frostwind is a sincere and honorable knight whose life was upended by one of the stranger court escapades in living memory.

Earnest, loyal, and unshakably romantic, Alden was once bound to the prideful [[Lynwenden Cron]], who forbade his knights from marrying without his leave. When Alden fell in love with a healer’s daughter, his choice between duty and love seemed doomed.

Then [[Aylassa]] got involved, because apparently destiny sometimes arrives wearing a false mustache and carrying squash.

The result was chaos, humiliation, oath-breaking, household panic, and Alden’s eventual freedom.

Alden, bless his spectacularly sincere heart, still believes the whole affair was a straightforward act of chivalric intervention.

## Appearance



Alden is handsome, tall, and broad-shouldered, with dark hair that falls loosely at the temples and striking grey eyes.

His armor is plain and serviceable, unadorned by personal sigils or vanity decoration. He carries himself like a knight who wants his deeds to speak for him, which is noble, if somewhat inefficient in a kingdom powered mostly by gossip and terrible decisions.

Beneath one bracer, he wears a woven bracelet from [[Mirelle Frostwind]], a small but constant reminder of his vows of love.

## Personality

Alden is noble-hearted, determined, and wholly sincere.

He believes in chivalry, love, and loyalty with every breath. He is baffled by politics, though this has not stopped politics from repeatedly using him as a chair.

He stands unflinching in the face of danger and carries himself with a quiet romanticism that makes him both admirable and dangerously easy to manipulate.

He is not stupid.

He is trusting.

Unfortunately, in noble society, this is often treated as a temporary form of stupidity.

## Background

Sir Alden Frostwind once served [[House Cron]] under Baron Lynwenden Cron. Cron’s household was proud, restrictive, and deeply controlling, particularly regarding marriage among his sworn knights.

When Alden fell in love with a healer’s daughter, the match was forbidden. Cron refused consent, treating Alden’s personal happiness as another piece of household property.

Alden was trapped between oath and love.

Then came the Squash and Spur Affair.

## The Squash and Spur Affair

Court still whispers about the chain of absurdities that led to Alden’s release from Cron’s service.

[[Aylassa]] disguised two prostitutes, [[Nyssa]] and [[Brielle]], as battered servants in order to bait [[Baron Orin Kharne]]’s sense of chivalry.

Orin, predictably, reacted like someone had thrown a torch into a granary.

He stormed Cron’s estate, roaring about produce logistics and demanding answers.

> [!quote] Baron Orin Kharne
> WHO ORDERS THE SQUASH?!

Cron’s household guard scattered. Cron’s champion, [[Morg]], was smashed into the dirt. The baron was humiliated. Alden was abruptly freed from his oath.

To Alden, the truth is simple:

Orin acted on principle and saved him.

He remains blissfully unaware of the ruse.

Which is probably for the best, because truth is a fine thing until it ruins a man’s entire emotional architecture.

## Current Situation

Alden now serves [[Orin Kharne]] as a knight of [[Stormarrow]].

His beloved, [[Mirelle]], has taken up residence at [[Castle Stormarrow]], where she tends herbs for the healers and offers quiet counsel to the household.

Together, Alden and Mirelle are modest but meaningful threads in the growing legend of Orin and Braga’s household.

Alden sees Stormarrow as a place where battered honor is not merely restored, but given room to breathe.

## Lady Mirelle Frostwind

Mirelle is Alden’s beloved and now his wife. Quiet, gentle, and practical, she is trained in herbs and healing work.

She is not a courtly ornament. She is one of the steady hands that keeps a growing household from becoming a parade of armed lunatics with banners.

Her presence grounds Alden. Her bracelet under his bracer is his private token of devotion, and he treats it with more reverence than some knights give holy relics.

## Court Gossip and Jokes

Aylassa continues to send baskets of squash to Stormarrow, each with cryptic notes or suspiciously innocent messages.

[[Nyssa]] and [[Brielle]], the women from the ruse, now serve [[Braga Kharne]]’s household as respectable attendants, though they remain very much on Aylassa’s string.

Braga remains oblivious to the full scope of the prank.

This is exactly how Aylassa likes it.

Among knights, Alden is spoken of as either a noble romantic or the luckiest fool in Gorundia.

Both readings are defensible, which is how history gets annoying.

## Reputation

Alden has quietly become a symbol of romantic chivalry among younger knights and sentimental household servants.

Older knights tend to snort into their cups and mutter that he has no idea how lucky he is.

Both camps agree that he is brave.

Most agree that he is honorable.

Almost everyone agrees that someone should probably tell him about the squash eventually.

No one wants to be that person.

## At the Muster

At the Muster, Alden’s gesture toward [[Ilvar Iron-Teeth]] cements him as a knight who sees worth where others do not.

This makes him a foil to cynicism, class contempt, and courtly calculation. He does not look first for pedigree, polish, or political utility.

He looks for courage.

That may make him dangerous in the best possible way.

## Roleplaying Alden

Alden should be played with complete sincerity.

Do not make him a fool. Make him earnest enough that cynical characters feel faintly embarrassed around him.

He believes people can be better than they are. He believes love is worth risk. He believes loyalty should be chosen, not extorted.

He is naïve about schemes, but not about courage.

When danger comes, he does not hesitate.

When someone vulnerable needs defending, he moves first and asks questions later.

Yes, this is exactly how Orin ended up in the squash incident. The infection spreads.

## Hooks for Play

- Alden’s earnestness makes him a moral compass in Orin’s household.
- His faith in Orin could become complicated if he learns the full truth of the Squash and Spur Affair.
- Aylassa’s continued squash deliveries may eventually force the secret into the open.
- Cron or his remaining loyalists may resent Alden’s freedom and seek to reclaim, shame, or punish him.
- Mirelle may become a quiet but important healer within Castle Kharne.
- Alden’s respect for Iron-Teeth could become a bridge between Stormarrow and rougher martial elements.
- His romantic reputation may attract admiration, mockery, or manipulation at court.

## DM Notes

Alden’s central tension is sincerity versus revelation.

If he learns the truth of the Squash and Spur Affair, his reaction could shape his arc:

- **Heartbreak:** He feels humiliated and used, questioning whether his freedom was built on lies.
- **Disillusionment:** He learns that even good outcomes can come from ugly tricks.
- **Maturity:** He accepts that Orin’s honor was real, even if the situation that summoned it was engineered.
- **Protective Loyalty:** He may decide the deception matters less than the fact that Cron’s cruelty was real and Orin acted when others would not.

Alden should not be broken by the truth unless the campaign needs that tragedy.

Better still, let him grow.

Alden is useful because he reminds the table that sincerity is not weakness.

In a campaign packed with spies, cursed bloodlines, devils, political murders, and nobles treating decency like an exotic rash, Alden is the guy who still believes a knight should mean something.

That is ridiculous.

That is also why he matters.
