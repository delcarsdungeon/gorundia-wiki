# Darien Thornbloom

> “You can always tell who the hammer is. Flash, swagger, and noise. Darien Thornbloom? He’s the anvil. You hit him, he does not move. You break yourself instead.”  
> — [[Sorek Redmarch]]

> “He is the only knight Perrin listens to, and the only one I trust to keep him in check.”  
> — Anonymous knight of [[House Cron]]


![[Darien Thornbloom.png|640]]
## Overview

**Sir Darien Thornbloom** is a veteran knight formerly of [[House Cron]], known across Grevandar as **the Anvil**: a broad, tired, immovable wall of a man who holds when better-dressed heroes start looking for exits.

Recent events around [[Baron Lynwenden Cron]]’s fall and execution have changed Darien’s place in the world. While many Cron loyalists collapsed, fled, or tried to pretend they had always been deeply loyal to the Crown — adorable, really — Darien emerged as one of the few men whose reputation survived the wreckage. He was not present for the final collapse, and Cron’s death effectively freed him from a feudal loyalty that had become politically poisonous.

He has since been absorbed into the **Lord Marshal’s entourage**, serving as a heavy knight, old soldier, and brutally practical stabilizing presence around [[Beringer]].

Darien is also remembered as the one man in Cron’s roster who could counter **[[Perrin the Colossus]]**. Not by being louder, meaner, or more theatrical, but by being completely unintimidated. Perrin might rage, loom, or threaten. Darien would simply stare at him like a tired blacksmith waiting for a mule to stop kicking the door.

## Basic Information

- **Full Name:** Sir Darien Thornbloom
- **Known As:** The Anvil
- **Age:** 46
- **Height:** 6’1”
- **Build:** Broad, heavyset, immensely strong
- **Hair:** Dark brown streaked with grey, worn short
- **Eyes:** Blue-grey, steady and unflinching
- **Former Allegiance:** [[House Cron]]
- **Current Role:** Knight in the Lord Marshal’s entourage
- **Visual Reference:** Tougher, more tired knightly John Goodman energy

## Appearance

Darien is built like a fortress that has had a bad month and still showed up to work.

He has wide shoulders, thick arms, a heavy torso, and the battered look of a man who has spent decades treating violence as weather. His face is weathered, his nose was broken and healed crooked, and his arms bear old campaign scars from border fights, shield walls, sieges, and stupid young men with sharp objects.

He is not conventionally handsome, but he has weight. Presence. The kind of man who walks into a room and makes everyone unconsciously check whether they are standing in his way.

His armor is well-maintained but plain, formerly marked in [[House Cron]] colors. Since joining the Lord Marshal’s company, he has not rushed to replace every bit of old livery. Darien is not sentimental about cloth, but he is also not a man who pretends his past never happened.

## Personality

Darien is blunt, dependable, dry, and tired in the bones.

He has very little patience for posturing, theatrical threats, or noble peacock displays. He is not humorless, but his humor is heavy, understated, and usually delivered after letting someone dig their own grave conversationally.

He is deeply patient with people, especially soldiers, squires, survivors, and anyone trying to do the right thing badly. He is exactly the man who will drag a drunken squire out of trouble, sit him down, feed him bread, then tell him he is an embarrassment to his boots.

Darien believes in **Gorundia** more than he believes in factions. He served [[House Cron]] because that was his oath and because, for many years, the Crons served the realm. He has little love for [[House Vogel]] politics and even less for court intrigue.

He is not naïve. He knows loyal men can be used by bad lords. That knowledge has made him quieter, not softer.

## Current Status

Darien has been folded into [[Beringer]]’s orbit as part of the Lord Marshal’s growing entourage.

This works because Darien brings three useful things:

1. **Physical authority** — he is a knight other hard men take seriously.
2. **Cron roster knowledge** — he knows who was merely sworn, who was rotten, and who is still dangerous.
3. **Emotional ballast** — he can drink with [[Beringer]] and [[Orin Kharne]] without turning everything into a speech, a confession, or a duel.

He is not a sycophant. He does not kiss the Lord Marshal’s ass. He simply stands nearby, drinks, listens, and occasionally says the one sentence nobody else had the guts to say.

## Relationship with Beringer

Darien is becoming one of [[Beringer]]’s drinking companions.

Not a court friend. Not a political advisor. A drinking companion in the old soldier sense: someone who understands silence, grief, duty, and the difference between a man wanting company and a man wanting questions.

Darien respects Beringer because Beringer does not treat command like theater. He also recognizes the danger in him: the grim duty, the pressure, the temptation to carry too much because no one else seems willing.

He will not flatter Beringer. That may be exactly why Beringer keeps him around.

### Darien on Beringer

> “He doesn’t need another man telling him he’s important. He needs someone making sure he eats before he falls over in armor.”

## Relationship with Orin Kharne

Darien and [[Orin Kharne]] get along because both men understand mass, force, and the sacred art of not moving when idiots expect you to.

Orin is bigger, more terrifying, and more politically central. Darien is older, drier, and more experienced in watching powerful men make mistakes.

They make good drinking companions: two heavy men with battlefield instincts, blunt humor, and a mutual dislike of ornamental nonsense.

### Darien on Orin

> “Kharne hits like a siege engine and thinks more than people notice. Dangerous combination. Good thing he married someone scarier.”

## Relationship with Lyosha and the Ohotnik Survivors

Darien has a quiet fondness for [[Lyosha Ohotnik]] and the remaining Ohotnik line.

Before the Ohotnik disgrace became a political stain, Darien had worked alongside their predecessors. He remembers them as hard, strange, useful people who went where other men hesitated. Monster-hunters, grim priests, border guardians — not polite company, but good to have when the night had teeth.

Because of that history, Darien does not treat Lyosha as tainted goods. He treats him as a man carrying a family ruin he did not earn.

### Darien on the Ohotniks

> “People liked them well enough when something was clawing at the shutters.”

## Relationship with Perrin the Colossus

Darien was widely considered the only man in Cron’s roster who could keep **[[Perrin the Colossus]]** in check.

Not because he could necessarily beat Perrin in a contest of raw brutality — though gods help the tavern that hosts that arm-wrestling match — but because Perrin listened to him.

Darien never feared him, never fed his ego, and never challenged him for sport. He treated Perrin like a dangerous tool that still needed to be cleaned, stored, and occasionally told to sit down.

### Darien to Perrin

> “You done being the biggest man in the room, or do we need to wait?”

## Relationship with Baron Cron

Darien served [[Baron Lynwenden Cron]] and Cron’s father before him.

Cron trusted Darien deeply, and for many years that trust was not misplaced. Darien was the keystone of the household knights, the man used to steady a line, cool Perrin, and keep lesser knights from getting stupid in expensive ways.

Cron’s fall complicates Darien’s legacy. He does not deny his service. He does not pretend he saw everything clearly. But he also does not accept that loyalty to a fallen lord makes every sworn man a villain.

That tension follows him now.

### Darien on Cron

> “He was my lord. That answers some questions. Not all of them.”

## Arms and Combat Style

- **Primary Weapon:** Massive iron-headed maul
- **Armor:** Heavy plate, plain and practical
- **Combat Role:** Defensive anchor, line-holder, counter-charge breaker
- **Reputation:** Strong as hell; almost impossible to move once set

Darien fights like an anvil with opinions.

He is not fast in the flashy duelist sense. He is efficient, punishing, and terrifyingly hard to shift. He absorbs charges, breaks shields, crushes doors, ruins knees, and makes aggressive men discover the limits of momentum.

His combat style is built around refusing displacement. He plants, receives, and breaks whatever hits him.

## The Battle of Blackvale Ford

During the **Border Raids of 1217 OR**, House Cron’s levy clashed with Keshite marauders attempting to cross the **Blackvale Ford**. Outnumbered two-to-one, the Cron line began to buckle under the weight of the enemy charge.

Darien anchored the center with his maul in hand.

When the ford’s shallows churned red and the shield wall began to give, Darien planted his feet on the stones and refused to fall back. Witnesses say he stood like a wall, breaking pikes, shields, and skulls alike, holding the crossing long enough for [[Baron Lynwenden Cron]] to wheel his cavalry and strike the enemy flank.

> “The ford was lost — until Thornbloom made it his ground. They came in waves, and broke against him like surf against rock.”  
> — Captain Harl Kendros, survivor of Blackvale Ford

The victory turned a potential breach into a rout. From that day forward, Darien was called **the Anvil**.

## Role in Play

Darien is useful as:

- a grounded veteran voice in Beringer’s entourage
- a bridge to surviving Cron men who are not automatically traitors
- a drinking companion for [[Beringer]] and [[Orin Kharne]]
- a physical counterweight to oversized battlefield personalities
- a sympathetic old hand with history involving the Ohotnik line
- a man who can say ugly truths without sounding like he is angling for advantage

He should not be played as comic relief, though he can absolutely be funny. He is weary, practical, and quietly kind under the armor. Less “jolly big man,” more “big man who has seen enough death to know when stew and silence are better than speeches.”

## Voice

Darien speaks plainly. Short sentences. Dry observations. He rarely raises his voice because he has spent a lifetime being heard without needing to.

### Sample Lines

> “That plan has three parts too many and two horses too few.”

> “If he wanted subtle, he shouldn’t have brought knights.”

> “Sit down before you fall down. That’s not advice. That’s scheduling.”

> “I knew your people before the songs went sour, Ohotnik. Good folk. Strange as winter thunder, but good.”

> “Perrin, breathe through your nose and stop frightening the furniture.”

> “A man can serve a bad lord without being a bad man. But he’d best not make a habit of it.”

> “Lord Marshal, permission to say the thing everyone else is polishing too hard to speak plainly?”

## Legacy

Darien Thornbloom will never be remembered for glittering tourney victories, courtly romances, or poetic charges.

He is the man soldiers remember because he was still standing when the line should have broken.

If Gorundia endures, it will not only be because of kings, dukes, and sacred blades. It will also be because of men like Darien Thornbloom: tired, broad, stubborn, and too decent to step aside when the world leans hard.
