## Sir Osric Fenwick

![[Osric.png|320]]![[Osric Fenwick.png|260]]

> **Sir Osric Fenwick is a veteran Gorundian knight whose military career ended years before he was ready for it.**
>
> Once regarded as the natural soldier of his family, Osric spent nearly a decade in the ugly little wars that followed the last great conflict with the Dominion: hunting brutal bandit companies, guarding threatened roads and settlements, and fighting suspiciously well-equipped raiders whose connection to the Dominion was always obvious and never officially provable.
>
> A battlefield injury left him permanently lame. He survived, but could no longer endure the riding, marching, and footwork expected of a campaigning knight.
>
> The army thanked him for his service.
>
> Osric discovered that being politely discarded can hurt considerably more than being insulted.

# House Fenwick

House Fenwick is an old cadet branch of [[House Veymar]].

The Fenwicks are respectable landed nobility rather than one of Gorundia's great houses. They have traditionally supplied knights, officers, administrators, and the sort of competent minor nobles upon whom larger houses depend while occasionally forgetting to thank them.

Their arms retain the Veymar bridge and eight-pointed star, differenced by a blue engrailed border:

> **Argent, in base water barry wavy Azure and Argent, thereover a bridge embattled Sable masoned Argent, in chief a mullet of eight points Gules; all within a bordure engrailed Azure.**

Osric's personal arms are further differenced by a red crescent and black martlet.

The current family line is headed by his elder brother, [[Ser Aldren Fenwick]].

---

# The Two Fenwick Brothers

Aldren and Osric received essentially the same noble education.

Both were taught history, letters, law, heraldry, estate management, religion, courtly behavior, riding, weapons, and enough arithmetic to discover that peasants become surprisingly important once someone starts discussing taxes.

But there was never much confusion about their futures.

**Aldren was the heir.**

He was expected to learn the estate, understand politics, marry appropriately, maintain the family name, and be militarily respectable enough that nobody whispered about cowardice.

**Osric was going to be the knight.**

Everyone knew it.

Osric knew it.

More importantly, Osric liked it.

He had no desire to displace his brother and no secret conviction that the elder son had stolen something from him. Aldren's road looked exhausting in entirely the wrong ways.

Osric wanted horses, soldiers, field exercises, maps, and eventually command.

He proved very good at all of them.

---

# Ser Othmar Fenwick

Their father, **Ser Othmar Fenwick**, had himself served as a knight before settling into the responsibilities of the family.

Othmar was an educated but practical man who believed both sons should know the full business of their station, even if they were destined to use different parts of it.

He was proud of Aldren.

He was also rather obviously delighted by Osric's talent for soldiering.

Nobody in the family was cruel enough to say that distinction aloud.

Nobody particularly needed to.

When Osric was knighted in **1215 OR**, Othmar gave him his own sword.

Not a newly commissioned ceremonial weapon.

Not a jeweled family relic.

**His sword.**

The one he had carried.

It was understood as both a gift and a declaration:

Osric would continue the martial part of the family tradition.

Aldren remained the heir.

Osric rode to war carrying their father's blade.

The brothers have never openly quarreled about it.

---

# Othmar's Sword

Osric still carries his father's sword.

It is surprisingly plain.

The grip is dark leather. The fittings are restrained. There are no jewels, gilded saints, engraved victories, or tiny heroic lions doing anatomically dubious things to the crossguard.

To an untrained eye, it is merely a well-kept old broadsword.

Anyone who knows weapons looks twice.

The balance is excellent. The steel is exceptional. Every part of it was made by someone who understood that a sword is ultimately supposed to go through another human being rather than impress people across a dining hall.

The blade is also enchanted.

The enchantments are similarly unobtrusive. It does not glow or announce itself. It simply handles better, strikes truer, and keeps an edge with unnatural stubbornness.

Osric has carried it for roughly half his life.

Questions about the sword do not bother him.

Questions about **why he has it rather than Aldren** occasionally produce a brief pause before the answer.

---

# The Quiet War

Osric was knighted in **1215 OR**, twenty-five years after Red Gorge had ended the last great open war with the Dominion.

Open war had ended.

Violence had not.

For most of Osric's active career, the eastern marches existed in the unpleasant space between peace and war.

There were bandits.

There were deserters.

There were mercenary companies who somehow possessed better equipment than many baronial troops.

There were raiders who crossed the border without insignia, struck a manor or caravan, and vanished again.

There were Hextorian fanatics who had apparently become lost several dozen miles inside Gorundia while carrying military-grade weapons and detailed local maps.

There were Dominion nobles whose household troops regularly wandered somewhere they supposedly had not been ordered to go.

And there were always officials on the other side willing to express profound surprise that any of this was happening.

The fighting rarely became large enough to earn a proper war-name.

That did not make it less dangerous.

---

# Campaigning, 1215–1224 OR

Osric spent approximately nine years rotating through field assignments.

Some seasons were relatively quiet.

Others were vicious.

He served in patrols, punitive expeditions, convoy escorts, fortified-road detachments, village relief forces, and the small mobile columns sent after raiders before they could disappear across a border and become somebody else's paperwork.

The enemy varied.

Sometimes they were ordinary bandits.

Some of those were bad enough.

The worst companies burned farms that could not pay, tortured people for hidden coin, kidnapped children for ransom, and murdered witnesses simply because witnesses were inconvenient.

Others were harder to classify.

Osric fought men whose officers used Dominion drill commands.

He found Hextorian charms on corpses.

He encountered raiding parties with fresh military crossbows, professional scouts, alchemical supplies, and horses too expensive for the impoverished "outlaws" supposedly riding them.

Once or twice Gorundian troops pursued such men nearly to the border and watched them disappear beneath the protection of soldiers who insisted they had never seen them before.

Osric came away from those years with very little patience for the phrase **"unconfirmed Dominion involvement."**

---

# What Kind of Officer He Was

Osric was a good swordsman.

He was a better officer.

He learned early that battlefield courage was common enough.

Keeping frightened people organized was rarer.

Osric became known for giving simple orders and making them sound achievable.

He remembered names.

He noticed who was exhausted.

He listened to scouts.

He distrusted maps once the ground began openly contradicting them.

He understood that an army eats before it fights and that a heroic charge is considerably less impressive when the horses have not been fed.

Most of his favorite campaign stories involve:

- mud
- stubborn mules
- incompetent junior officers
- camp cooks
- priests being bitten by animals
- weather defeating military planning
- soldiers doing something brilliant while their commanders were temporarily occupied elsewhere

His stories about actual fighting tend to be shorter.

His stories about the dead are shorter still.

---

# The Injury

In **1224 OR**, Osric's company was pursuing a raiding force after an attack on a road settlement.

It was exactly the sort of action he had fought dozens of times.

That was the problem.

There was no grand battle.

No famous enemy champion.

No bard waiting on a hill.

A horse went down during the pursuit.

Osric went with it.

The animal landed on him.

His leg and hip were badly crushed.

The men with him got him out alive, and a priest was eventually able to heal injuries that would otherwise probably have killed him or cost him the leg entirely.

The healing worked.

Mostly.

Bone joined.

Flesh closed.

Osric lived.

Something set badly.

Whether the fault lay in the original damage, the delay, exhaustion, or the healing itself hardly mattered afterward.

He could walk.

He could ride.

He could fight.

He could not do any of them for long enough, fast enough, or reliably enough to remain a field knight.

He was twenty-nine.

---

# After the Injury

Osric tried to return to active service.

He was not permitted to.

Nobody behaved cruelly.

He was praised for his record.

He was thanked.

He was assured his service would be remembered.

People suggested advisory work.

Administrative appointments.

Household positions.

Inspection duties.

His brother offered him a respectable place within the Fenwick household.

Osric refused most of it.

He did not want to spend the rest of his life being introduced as a veteran before being seated somewhere safe.

The matter slowly resolved itself in the traditional manner:

Everyone stopped asking what he intended to do.

Osric began drinking.

---

# The Drinking Years

Osric's drinking became serious.

Part of it was pain.

Part was boredom.

Most of it was the loss of purpose.

He had spent his entire childhood preparing for a profession, then nearly a decade becoming good at it.

At twenty-nine he was informed that he had completed that portion of his life.

There was no replacement.

He had money enough to live.

Family enough not to fall.

Rank enough to remain welcome in respectable places.

And almost nothing anyone truly **needed** him to do.

Osric discovered that a man can survive quite comfortably while still coming apart.

---

# Bard's Gate

By 1231 OR, Osric had become a familiar enough figure in [[Bard's Gate]].

He was still recognizably a gentleman and knight, though somewhat weathered around the edges.

He could behave perfectly well in noble company.

He increasingly preferred people who did not require him to.

That eventually led him to [[The Blood and Sand]].

More specifically, it led him to [[Toots]].

---

# The Blood and Sand

Osric's first serious visit to the Blood and Sand did not go entirely according to whatever plan he imagined having.

At the door he met [[Slab]].

Osric immediately calculated distance, reach, exits, draw time, and likely outcome.

The likely outcome was that Slab could kill him before his sword cleared the scabbard.

Osric therefore said:

> "Good evening."

This turned out to be the correct tactical decision.

Inside, he encountered Toots at work and very quickly realized that he had entered her world without fully understanding what that meant.

To his credit, once corrected, he listened.

When Toots told him:

> "I don't need you proving courage over me."

Osric stayed seated.

That may have impressed her more than drawing the sword would have.

---

# Toots

What exactly is happening between Sir Osric Fenwick and Toots depends greatly upon which of them is being asked.

What is obvious is that Osric came to the Blood and Sand because she was there.

He has made no serious effort to deny it.

He does not appear embarrassed by her being a half-orc.

He is occasionally embarrassed by practically everything else.

He does not try to police the men around her, buy possession of her time, or rescue her from a life she did not ask him to rescue her from.

When she tells him to sit, he generally sits.

This may be the strongest evidence yet produced that the man is trainable.

He also fits unexpectedly well among the Blood and Sand regulars.

His campaign stories play better there than they ever did at noble dinners.

Osric can make soldiers and laborers laugh without making them the joke.

And underneath the polished manners there remains enough old campaign bastard to be recognized by other veterans almost immediately.

---

# The Mugging

Not long after Osric began seeing Toots, he was attacked on his way home.

Several men jumped him from behind.

His bad leg, alcohol, surprise, and numbers settled matters fairly quickly.

They beat him badly and took his purse, cloak, and father's sword.

Osric disappeared for two days while recovering.

For a short while, Toots believed she had simply frightened him away.

Then the Blood and Sand learned what had happened.

[[Gravel]] eventually recovered the sword and cloak.

How Gravel managed this has not been described in detail.

There is a widespread belief that knowing less about Gravel's errands contributes to longevity.

Osric's reaction to the recovered sword made it fairly clear that its importance to him went well beyond its value.

When he returned to the Blood and Sand, bruised and limping, he raised a drink to the people there.

It was one of the first times the respectable knight and the Dregs tavern crowd seemed to fully understand that something had changed between them.

---

# When the Officer Came Back

The most revealing moment came later, during an accident at the Blood and Sand.

A man was badly injured.

People crowded in.

Some tried to move him.

Others shouted conflicting advice.

Osric's entire manner changed.

The hesitant gentleman disappeared.

So did the drunk.

The old field officer surfaced almost instantly.

> **"DON'T MOVE HIM."**

Then came names.

Tasks.

Clear instructions.

People obeyed.

The injured man survived.

When he later returned to thank Osric, the exchange was characteristically brief.

He told him:

> **"I believed you."**

Gravel put it another way:

> **"You give orders like people expect to live through them."**

After a pause:

> **"Still do."**

That observation has proven unexpectedly important.

---

# A New Command

Events surrounding the fall of House Vogel and the unsettled future of Fenwyck brought Osric's family back into political attention.

His elder brother Aldren is now considered one plausible future holder of Fenwyck should the disgraced Veymar line lose the barony.

That, in turn, caused people to look again at Aldren's younger brother.

Some saw a potential embarrassment.

Others saw something considerably more useful.

[[Baroness Braga Kharne]] ultimately offered Osric what nobody had thought to offer him in years:

**a real job.**

Not a ceremonial office.

Not a pension with a sword attached.

A command.

Osric has been given responsibility for roughly forty men in the west, with duties centered on training, patrol, garrison work, and local security.

It is work his damaged leg does not prevent him from doing.

More importantly, it is work at which he is very good.

---

# Current Status — 1231 OR

Osric remains lame.

He still drinks too much.

His relationship with Toots remains socially inconvenient to people who apparently have sufficient spare time to be inconvenienced by such things.

His father's sword still hangs at his side.

His brother's political future is suddenly much less predictable than it was a month ago.

And Osric has soldiers again.

The change is noticeable.

He comes back from Stormarrow exhausted.

He complains about roads.

He complains about recruits.

He complains about supplies.

He complains about officers who send written instructions to people standing twenty yards away.

He is happier than he has been in years.

# Personality

## Dry Humor

Osric's humor is understated, quick, and usually sharpest when discussing military life.

He tends to joke upward or sideways rather than downward.

Bad commanders, noble pretension, military bureaucracy, animals, weather, and Osric himself are all considered legitimate targets.

## Well Educated

Osric is not an ignorant sword-arm.

He is a properly educated nobleman who understands politics, history, weapons, military theory, and court behavior.

He can move through respectable society without difficulty.

Whether he enjoys doing so is another matter.

## Formal When Nervous

When uncomfortable, Osric becomes more polite.

This can result in him sounding like a diplomatic treaty attempting to ask a woman for a drink.

Toots has noticed.

## Soldier's Instincts

Osric automatically notes exits, weapons, distances, threats, and the behavior of people around him.

When something actually goes wrong, his humor tends to vanish.

He becomes extremely calm.

## Pride

Osric tolerates jokes about his limp far better than pity.

He especially dislikes people assuming he is helpless.

Offering practical accommodation usually works.

Hovering over him generally does not.

---

# Aldren and Osric

Osric has never shown any desire to take his elder brother's place.

By all appearances, the brothers care for one another and accepted their different roles long ago.

There are, however, a few subjects around which conversation becomes slightly more careful.

Their father's sword is one.

Osric's wasted years are another.

Aldren once tried to find his younger brother a safe, respectable place after the injury.

Osric refused it.

Both men seem capable of understanding why the other did what he did.

That has not necessarily made the memory comfortable.

---

# Reputation

Among nobles, Osric is generally remembered as:

- a promising knight whose career was cut short
- a competent veteran
- Ser Aldren Fenwick's younger brother
- a man who drinks more than is good for him
- increasingly, the knight involved with a half-orc woman from the Blood and Sand

Among soldiers who served with him, the assessment tends to be simpler:

**He was good.**

Among the Blood and Sand crew, the matter is still under review.

He has, however, recovered from "that noble sniffing around Toots" to something approaching "our noble idiot."

In the Dregs, this represents significant social advancement.

---

# The Important Thing About Osric

For years, Osric believed the injury had taken away the thing that made him valuable.

He could no longer charge.

Could no longer march all day.

Could no longer ride hard for a week and still fight at the end of it.

He could no longer be the knight everyone had spent his childhood telling him he was supposed to become.

It has taken him years to notice something other people are only now beginning to recognize.

The sword was never the best thing he brought to a battlefield.

It was the ability to make frightened men listen.

And believe him when he told them what came next.
