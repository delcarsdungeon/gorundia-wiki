# Alric DeSalle

> [!quote] Sorek Redmarch
> Alric DeSalle wears Vogel colors like armor and his own name like a bruise. That makes him dangerous, but not useless.

## Overview

**Full Name:** Sir Alric DeSalle  
**Born:** 1209 OR  
**Age:** 22  
**House:** [[House DeSalle]]  
**Current Service:** Knight in service to [[House Vogel]]  
**Current Location:** Near [[Blackbar]], on the DeSalle border  
**Father:** [[Baron Remy DeSalle]]  
**Mother:** [[Baroness Lisette DeSalle]]  
**Brother:** [[Baron Jareth DeSalle]]  
**Known For:** Vogel-trained discipline, DeSalle blood, bitter loyalty, and a clearer head than most of his family’s enemies expect  

Sir Alric DeSalle is the younger brother of [[Jareth DeSalle|Baron Jareth DeSalle]], the newly elevated head of [[House DeSalle]].

He is a knight in Vogel service and a difficult man to categorize cleanly. He wears black and gold, serves with discipline, and has learned the hard lessons of Grevandar command. Yet he is still DeSalle by blood, still loyal to his mother and brother, and still grieving the death and disgrace of his father.

Alric is not blind to [[Baron Remy DeSalle]]’s failures. He saw enough of his father’s vanity, weakness, and poor judgment to understand why the house was vulnerable. But understanding is not forgiveness. To Alric, the death of his father remains a wound, not a solved legal matter.

This makes him bitter.

It does not make him stupid.

Placed near [[Blackbar]], Alric is a natural point of contact as the [[Lord Marshal]]’s host approaches the DeSalle border: a Vogel knight who is still a real person, a DeSalle son who can speak without immediately drawing steel, and a possible voice of reason on the other side of a very tense line.

## Appearance

Alric is broad-shouldered and muscular, with the build of a young knight shaped by regular drill, campaign riding, and practical weapons work.

He has dark brown hair, kept short for the field, and hazel eyes with faint golden flecks inherited from his mother. His complexion is fair but weathered by outdoor service. A thin scar crosses his left cheek, earned in a training accident that he refuses to describe with any drama.

His posture is controlled and formal. Even at rest, he looks like a man standing for inspection.

Alric usually wears functional armor in the colors of [[House Vogel]]: blackened steel with restrained gold accents. He carries a longsword at his hip and wears a small silver pin bearing the crest of [[House DeSalle]], usually placed discreetly where it does not compete with Vogel livery.

The pin is not decoration.

It is a statement.

## Personality

Alric is dutiful, reserved, and pragmatic. He speaks carefully, watches closely, and rarely lets anger show until it has already been shaped into a decision.

He believes in loyalty, discipline, and service. He does not believe birth alone proves worth. That belief separates him from some of the weaker habits of DeSalle pride, but it also makes him severe toward failure, especially his own.

He is not warm on first meeting. He is polite, but distant. His respect must be earned through conduct rather than claimed through title.

Alric has a strong sense of honor, but it has been hardened by humiliation. His father’s fall left him with grief, resentment, and the uneasy knowledge that some accusations against Remy were deserved. That contradiction sits badly with him.

He can admit his father was flawed.

He does not easily tolerate hearing others say it.

## The DeSalle Wound

Alric is more bitter than [[Baron Jareth DeSalle]] about their father’s death.

Jareth has been forced into rule. He must think of lands, tenants, oaths, survival, and the immediate burden of keeping [[House DeSalle]] from collapsing under disgrace. Jareth’s grief has been crowded by duty.

Alric has had more room for anger.

To him, Remy DeSalle was not only a failed baron. He was his father. Weak, vain, and compromised, yes, but still the man whose name Alric carries. Alric resents the public satisfaction some took in Remy’s downfall. He resents the way the story has already hardened into a lesson told by people who did not have to bury him.

He does not deny that Remy made enemies.

He does not deny that [[House Durathen]] had cause.

But he is not ready to call the matter clean simply because it was made lawful.

This bitterness makes Alric dangerous in conversation. He may accept facts and still reject the tone in which they are delivered.

## Service to House Vogel

Alric entered service with [[House Vogel]] as a young knight, partly for advancement and partly to escape the suffocating decline of his father’s household.

In Vogel service, he found structure. The discipline suited him. The expectations were hard, but clear. A man did not need to be loved if he was useful, brave, and exact.

He learned field command, mounted combat, patrol discipline, and the cold arithmetic of Grevandar border security. He also learned how Vogel men talk about weaker houses when they think no one of consequence is listening.

That education left marks.

Alric respects Vogel discipline, but he has not surrendered his judgment to it. He knows strength can become cruelty when wrapped too tightly in certainty. He has seen Vogel knights hold order where softer men would fail, and he has seen them mistake fear for loyalty.

He remains in Vogel service because his oath matters.

He remains DeSalle because blood matters too.

That tension is the heart of him.

## Relationship with Jareth DeSalle

[[Baron Jareth DeSalle]] is Alric’s elder brother and now the head of [[House DeSalle]].

Alric loves Jareth, though the two brothers are not easy with each other. Jareth stayed closer to the center of DeSalle responsibility. Alric left and built his name elsewhere. That difference has created old friction.

Jareth likely sees Alric as useful but difficult: a brother with military training, Vogel connections, and too much anger.

Alric sees Jareth as the man now forced to carry a broken inheritance. He respects him for stepping into the role, but he may quietly judge him if he seems too quick to accept the terms imposed by others.

Their bond is real.

Their trust is strained.

If pressed from outside, they close ranks.

## Relationship with Lisette DeSalle

Alric remains deeply respectful of his mother, [[Baroness Lisette DeSalle]].

Lisette instilled in him much of his discipline and restraint. From her, he learned that survival in noble life often depends on what one does not say aloud. He writes to her when he can, though his letters are likely spare and controlled rather than openly emotional.

His mother is one of the few people who can reach the grief under his discipline.

He is protective of her reputation and safety. Insulting Remy may anger him. Insulting Lisette may make him act.

## View of Remy DeSalle

Alric’s view of [[Baron Remy DeSalle]] is complicated.

He knows his father was flawed. He knows Remy’s vanity damaged the house. He knows poor decisions led to real deaths, real resentment, and political disaster.

But Alric also remembers private things: lessons, gestures, moments of approval, stories told before the rot became impossible to ignore. He remembers wanting his father to become better and being angry when he did not.

Alric’s grief is sharpened by disappointment.

He mourns the father Remy was.

He also mourns the father Remy failed to be.

## Role at Blackbar

Alric can be placed near [[Blackbar]] as the [[Lord Marshal]]’s host approaches the DeSalle border.

His presence gives the scene immediate tension without requiring instant combat. He has reason to challenge, stall, question, or demand clarity. He also has enough discipline to avoid throwing men away just to satisfy wounded pride.

Possible roles at Blackbar:

- Commanding or advising a small Vogel-aligned border detachment.
- Escorting DeSalle retainers sent to observe the Lord Marshal’s movement.
- Acting as liaison between local Vogel forces and surviving DeSalle authority.
- Quietly assessing whether the Lord Marshal intends occupation, punishment, or negotiation.
- Watching for signs that [[House Durathen]] or its allies intend further revenge.
- Testing whether [[Beringer Amadis]] is conqueror, judge, or something less simple.

Alric should not be played as a villain on sight.

He is an obstacle because he has duties.

He is sympathetic because those duties are tangled with grief.

## Voice of Reason

Alric can serve as a voice of reason among Vogel-aligned or DeSalle-loyal forces.

He understands that open defiance of the Lord Marshal’s host could destroy what remains of his house. He also understands that cringing submission could do the same thing more slowly.

His instinct is to hold ground, demand terms, and prevent hotheads from turning a tense border meeting into a massacre.

He may say things like:

> “No one crosses angry until someone explains whose authority is being carried.”

Or:

> “If this is judgment, say so. If this is occupation, stop dressing it as courtesy.”

He does not want war on the border.

He does want dignity preserved.

That distinction matters.

## Skills and Abilities

Alric is a competent young knight with strong battlefield fundamentals.

### Strengths

- Skilled swordsman.
- Proficient in mounted combat.
- Trained in patrol command and border discipline.
- Calm under visible pressure.
- Strong grasp of military routine and chain of command.
- Respected by practical soldiers for steadiness rather than charm.

### Weaknesses

- Emotionally guarded.
- Carries unresolved anger over Remy’s death.
- Can be overly critical of himself and others.
- Struggles to separate insult from legitimate judgment when his family is involved.
- May overcorrect toward rigidity when uncertain.

## Equipment

### Weapons

Alric carries a finely crafted longsword gifted or issued through Vogel service. It bears restrained Vogel marking rather than elaborate ornament.

He may also carry a dagger, riding sword, or mace depending on patrol conditions.

### Armor

He wears blackened steel armor with gold accents, representing his service to [[House Vogel]]. The armor is functional rather than ceremonial, well cared for, and polished as a matter of discipline.

### Personal Item

Alric wears a small silver pin bearing the crest of [[House DeSalle]]. It is discreet, but always present.

He also keeps a small sketch of the DeSalle estate among his belongings, carefully folded and worn at the edges.

## Private Habits

Alric polishes his armor and weapons as a form of meditation. The routine steadies him. It gives his hands something useful to do when his thoughts turn bitter.

He also reads poetry in private, a taste inherited from his mother. He does not discuss this with other Vogel knights unless he trusts them, which is rare.

He keeps his letters organized and rarely discards family correspondence.

## Relationships

### Baron Jareth DeSalle

Older brother and newly crowned baron.

Alric is loyal to Jareth, but their relationship carries tension from years of distance and differing responsibilities.

### Baron Remy DeSalle

Father.

Alric resented Remy’s failures but remains bitter over his death and public disgrace.

### Baroness Lisette DeSalle

Mother.

Alric respects and loves her deeply. She remains his strongest emotional tie to the family.

### House Vogel

Alric serves Vogel and respects its discipline. He is trusted by some knights and viewed with suspicion by others who see him as DeSalle blood wearing borrowed colors.

### House Durathen

Alric understands Durathen grievance better than he wants to admit. He is wary of them and may resent the moral clarity others grant them.

### Beringer Amadis

Alric’s view of [[Beringer Amadis]] is likely conflicted.

Beringer helped turn the DeSalle matter into lawful judgment rather than private revenge, but from Alric’s side, that distinction may feel cold. He may respect Beringer’s authority and conduct while still holding him partly responsible for the machinery that ended Remy.

This makes Alric a useful conversation partner: hostile enough to push, disciplined enough to listen.

## Roleplaying Alric

Alric speaks with restraint. He does not waste words, and he dislikes being maneuvered into emotional display.

He is formal with superiors, clipped with fools, and careful with enemies.

He should feel like someone trying very hard not to become what grief wants him to become.

Common lines:

> “Duty is a path I chose for myself, not a legacy handed down to me.”

> “Strength is more than muscle or swordplay. It is knowing when to stand and when to walk away.”

> “I left for honor. That does not mean I stopped looking back.”

> “Speak plainly. If you came to punish my house, do not make me thank you for the courtesy.”

> “My father had failings. I am aware. I do not require them recited by men who never had to call him father.”

> “No one here benefits from a border soaked in pride.”

## Scene Use: First Contact at Blackbar

As the Lord Marshal’s host approaches Blackbar, Alric can be waiting with a small disciplined force: enough men to matter, not enough to win.

He does not block the road like a fool.

He holds the road like a knight.

Suggested opening:

> Alric DeSalle waits beside the border marker in blackened steel, Vogel gold worked across his shoulders and a small silver DeSalle pin at his throat.  
>   
> His men are drawn up behind him in good order. Not enough to stop an army. Enough to show that someone thought about trying.  
>   
> He removes his helm when the Lord Marshal’s banner comes into view.  
>   
> “My lord,” he says. “Before your host crosses, I would know whether you come as shield, judge, or conqueror.”  
>   
> His eyes flick once to the banners behind Beringer.  
>   
> “The answer will matter to men with less discipline than mine.”

## Adventure Hooks

### The Border Order

Alric has orders from Vogel command that conflict with what Jareth needs to preserve House DeSalle. He must decide which oath bends first.

### His Father’s Effects

Alric seeks the return of a personal item belonging to Remy DeSalle. It has no military value, but great emotional weight.

### DeSalle Hotheads

A group of younger DeSalle retainers wants revenge for Remy and tries to provoke the Lord Marshal’s host. Alric may ask for help stopping them without humiliating his house further.

### Vogel Suspicion

Some Vogel knights suspect Alric’s DeSalle blood makes him unreliable. They may try to force him into a public demonstration of loyalty.

### Jareth’s Message

Jareth sends Alric a private message asking him to slow the Lord Marshal without starting a fight. The message is reasonable. The timing is terrible.

### The Durathen Witness

A Durathen-linked retainer or merchant recognizes Alric and deliberately provokes him by invoking Remy’s death. Alric’s response may define whether he can become a bridge or remains a wound.

## GM Notes

Alric is not meant to replace Jareth.

He sharpens Jareth.

Jareth is the newly crowned baron trying to preserve the house. Alric is the younger brother who left, served Vogel, and now returns to find the family name damaged, the father dead, and the border about to become everyone’s problem.

He should be bitter, but not cartoonish.

He should be loyal to Vogel, but not blind.

He should love his family, but not excuse every failure.

The best use of Alric is as a human face on the other side: a man the party can dislike, understand, pressure, respect, or possibly win over.

He is a Vogel knight.

He is a DeSalle son.

Both are true.
