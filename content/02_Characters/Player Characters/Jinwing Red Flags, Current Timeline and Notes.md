## Canon decision

**Lock this:** Jinwing is **Glynlynn Sylvanranth**, born in spring **459 OR** to **Maelóthiel Sylvanranth** and **Eryndor Stormarrow**. Duskseer misheard or simplified “Glynlynn” into “Jin,” then later “Jin Broken Wing,” then “JinWing.”

This supersedes the current Jinwing Timeline file’s old core lock of **birth c. 559 OR**, **age ~672**, **found c. 562**, and **Deep Sleep c. 899-1231**. That older file explicitly says those numbers, so into the continuity shredder they go, with dignity, or at least a clipboard.

Keep from the older patch:

- Deep Sleep is a **ritual state**, not a place.
    
- The site is still **Rootbound Hollow**.
    
- Duskseer chooses her.
    
- Elunaeth influences timing, not selection.
    
- Jin is **not** a High Druid, not Duskseer’s replacement, and not incorruptible.

## Thranduil / Jin kinship lock

Use this unless you want to reopen the whole elven genealogy pit, and frankly there are cleaner ways to suffer.

**Jinwing/Glynlynn is Thranduil’s maternal half-aunt.**

The clean version:

- **Eryndor Stormarrow** fathered **Glynlynn/Jinwing** with **Maelóthiel Sylvanranth**.    
- Eryndor is also the father of **Thranduil’s mother**.    
- Therefore Jin and Thranduil’s mother are half-sisters.    
- Therefore Jin is Thranduil’s maternal half-aunt.    
- This is **not through Faelar**, not through Círdan, not through Leafwhisper lines.    
- It is through **Eryndor Stormarrow**.    

That preserves the old “maternal half-aunt” structure from the superseded timeline while replacing the vague “unknown father” with **Eryndor**. It also honors your correction: **Glynlynn is Jin.**

## The Faelar/Theron impact

The revised Faelar/Theron model works and is better.

The early arrival model means Faelar and Theron are already embedded by the time Jin is born. Faelar being “gone native” by Maelóthiel’s heartbreak makes sense if he entered the Great Forest around **c. 420-440 OR**. Theron can still become the public legal architect later. The public histories compress centuries of groundwork into “the elves came and civilized Gorundia,” because historians apparently think nuance is a disease.

So the clean phase model is:

1. **c. 420-440 OR:** Faelar and Theron arrive quietly.    
2. **449 OR:** Quiet Ones begin the Jin project.    
3. **c. 457-459 OR:** Maelóthiel/Eryndor/Glynlynn.    
4. **600-747 OR:** Jin witnesses the cultures and druidic systems that later Gorundia grows out of.    
5. **c. 700-900 OR:** Theron’s hidden influence becomes institutional law.    
6. **c. 900 OR onward:** Humans start remembering the elves publicly.    
7. **1021 OR:** Nilanthalas falls while Jin sleeps.    
8. **1231 OR:** Jin wakes into the consequences.

But he may **not** know the whole Maelóthiel/Eryndor/Glynlynn story. That reveal should not be reduced to “fun fact: your aunt is the bird lady.” Let the elves have one emotionally catastrophic family dinner. They’ve earned it through centuries of making everything weird.

## Final canon summary

Jinwing is **Glynlynn Sylvanranth**, born in **459 OR** to **Maelóthiel Sylvanranth** and **Eryndor Stormarrow**. She was lost through a fern-wrapped stone-ring event, found in **466 OR** in Silvanastra/Druid Valley by **Duskseer** and **Elarithanys**, and raised as Duskseer’s chosen daughter after a fifty-year failed search for her origins.

She trained from **516-747 OR** across mountain, plains, forest, water, beast, death, and tree paths, becoming extremely broad but not politically hardened, not a High Druid, and not Duskseer’s successor. She entered Deep Druid Sleep in **750 OR** at **Rootbound Hollow**, slept through the fall of Nilanthalas in **1021 OR**, and awakened in **1231 OR** as a preserved witness of the old balance.

She is not a civilizational messiah. She is worse, from a storytelling standpoint: a memory-bearing hinge between Celene, Grugach blood, Druid Valley, the old forest, modern Gorundia, and Thranduil’s family line. Which means every faction with a brain should either want to protect her, use her, question her, or panic quietly in a corner.
