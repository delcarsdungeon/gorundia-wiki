# Beringer Amadis Timeline - Hidden Heir to Golitsyn's Gate

> [!summary]
> This page covers the early life of [[Beringer|Sir Beringer Amadis]], from his hidden birth in 1200 OR through the war with [[House Golitsyn]] and the battle remembered as [[Golitsyn's Gate]].
>
> Born the acknowledged but hidden son of [[Duke Galmyr Vogel]] and [[Avelyne Marsten]], Beringer was rescued from the cursed ruin of [[Villa Marsten]] by [[Cwenheld|Cwenheld, the Venerated Shield of Heironeous]] and raised within [[House Amadis]] as one of their own. His youth at [[Blackbriar]] gave him the foundation that later made him both knight and Lord Marshal: discipline, mercy, service, and the stubborn belief that honor is earned rather than inherited.

## Continuity Notes

- [[Beringer]] is the secret son of [[Galmyr VogelDuke Galmyr Vogel]] and [[Avelyne Marsten]].
- Beringer is raised by [[Alistair Amadis|Count Alistair Amadis]] and [[Anna Amadis|Countess Anna Amadis]].
- Only [[Cwenheld]], Count Alistair, and Countess Anna know his true birth during his childhood.
- The Vogel signet ring is quietly passed to Anna Amadis by Cwenheld after Galmyr gives it to her.
- [[Elizaveta Golitsyn]] is the daughter of **Boyar Alexsi Golitsyn**, tyrannical former lord of House Golitsyn.
- Elizaveta's father and brother are killed during the war between Amadis and Golitsyn.
- The heir of House Golitsyn, Elizaveta's brother, is deliberately left unnamed in Amadis histories as a final insult.
- [[Dame Cwenheld]] spelling is preferred over Cwenhild.
- [[Grevandar]] spelling is preferred over Gravandar.

# Part I - The Hidden Heir
## 1200-1212 OR

## Readying 4, 1200 OR - The First Godsday of Spring

**Age:** 0

[[Beringer]] is born at [[Villa Marsten]], an estate in the wooded hills northeast of [[Castle Direhold]] in the Duchy of [[Grevandar]]. His birth falls upon the first Godsday of spring, a date long regarded by the faithful of [[Heironeous]] as an auspicious beginning.

He is the first born son of [[Galmyr Vogel]], heir to one of the oldest noble houses in [[Gorundia]], and [[Avelyne Marsten]], a skilled man-at-arms in the duke's service.

Though Galmyr and Avelyne never marry because of the gulf between their stations, Duke Galmyr openly acknowledges both mother and son. He grants Avelyne a villa in recognition of her faithful service to try and ensure that she and the child want for nothing.

Beringer enters the world carrying two inheritances:

- The noble blood of [[House Vogel]]
- The quiet strength of his mother's humble devotion

---

## The Heroes of Ashmaw

Beringer's parents first met while serving together in an adventuring company.

During their greatest campaign, the company hunted and slew the red dragon [[Ashmaw]], though victory came at terrible cost. Their companion [[Theravain the Ashkeeper]] fell in battle, while [[Dame Cwenheld]], a holy warrior of [[Heironeous]], survived only after suffering grievous wounds that permanently scarred her.

Shared hardship forged an enduring bond between Duke Galmyr and Avelyne. From that companionship, Beringer was eventually born.

---

## The Fall of Villa Marsten

Only weeks after Beringer's birth, servants of the mysterious [[Raven Queen]], a dreaded figure of Grevandaran legend, descend upon [[Villa Marsten]].

Their purpose is neither theft nor conquest.

A ritual of blood is performed over the infant, using Avelyne's suffering to place an ancient curse upon her son.

Avelyne dies defending her child, buying precious moments with her own life.

Before the ritual can be completed, [[Dame Cwenheld]] arrives, drawn to the villa by a divine vision sent by [[Heironeous]]. She destroys the Raven Queen's servants, breaks the assault, and rescues the infant from the burning estate.

Though the curse has already taken root, the ritual remains unfinished.

---

## The Last Choice of Duke Galmyr

After escaping Villa Marsten, Cwenheld reunites with Duke Galmyr only once.

Believing himself somehow compromised by unseen enemies, and deep in his heart, is afraid his wife, [[Zannara]] is behind it, Galmyr reaches a heartbreaking conclusion: if his son remains anywhere near [[House Vogel]], the child will never be safe.

Entrusting Cwenheld with his future, the duke asks only one thing.

That he never learn where his son is taken.

He parts from Beringer forever.

Before they separate, Galmyr gives Cwenheld the Vogel signet ring, asking that it remain hidden until the day his son is ready to learn the truth of his birth.

Neither father nor son will ever see the other again.

---

## Arrival at Blackbriar

Dame Cwenheld carries the infant north and east to [[County Blackbriar]], placing him in the care of [[Count Alistair Amadis]] and [[Countess Anna Amadis]], trusted allies whose household is considered among the safest in the kingdom.

Only three people know the child's true identity:

- [[Cwenheld]]
- [[Alistair Amadis]]
- [[Anna Amadis]]

The Vogel signet ring is hidden away.

Beringer is raised simply as another child of [[House Amadis]].

---

## The House of Amadis

Within Blackbriar Court, Beringer receives not refuge, but a family.

Countess Anna becomes the mother he scarcely had the chance to know. Warm, affectionate, and endlessly attentive to her children, Anna nevertheless commands enormous respect among the nobility. At court, she proves every bit as formidable as her husband is upon the battlefield.

Count Alistair offers a quieter affection.

Though duty frequently calls him away to command soldiers and govern the county, he remains a constant presence whenever possible. He personally teaches Beringer the responsibilities of leadership, the obligations of nobility, and the discipline expected of every knight.

Together they instill a lesson that will define Beringer's entire life:

> Nothing worth having is ever given.  
> The greatest things in life are earned.

---

## A New Brother and Sister

Within [[House Amadis]], Beringer grows beside two younger children.

[[Alistair the Younger]], heir to the county, quickly becomes the brother Beringer never had. Though different by birth, the two boys trust one another without reservation, sharing both their triumphs and their burdens.

Young [[Sophia Amadis]] follows behind them. Bright, endlessly curious, and fiercely devoted to her brothers, she quarrels with Beringer as only siblings can before making peace just as quickly.

Under Anna's guidance, Sophia begins developing the keen intellect that will one day make her one of the realm's greatest scholars.

For all three children, the distinction between blood and adoption simply never exists.

---

## The Hidden Curse

Unknown to anyone within Blackbriar Court, the blood curse placed upon Beringer during infancy remains buried deep within him.

Whether through the intervention of Cwenheld, the grace of [[Atroa]], or divine providence itself, the curse lies dormant throughout his childhood.

Its existence is all but forgotten.

Only years later would the seal begin to fail.

---

## 1200-1212 OR - Childhood at Blackbriar

Beringer's childhood is marked by unusual promise.

He proves naturally courageous, possesses an instinctive talent for leadership, and demonstrates a willingness to place himself before others without hesitation.

Count Alistair encourages these qualities while tempering them with discipline.

Countess Anna ensures that compassion grows beside courage.

As he matures, it becomes increasingly clear that Beringer inspires confidence in those around him, not through birth or privilege, but through quiet example.

Long before he carries a knight's sword, others begin following where he leads.

---

## Readying, 1212 OR

**Age:** 12

Recognizing both his character and potential, Count Alistair names Beringer as one of his pages.

The boy who arrived at Blackbriar as a nameless orphan takes his first formal step toward knighthood.

It is the beginning of a journey that will eventually carry him from the quiet halls of Blackbriar Court to the office of Lord Marshal of Gorundia itself.

---

# Part II - The Young Squire
## 1212-1216 OR

## Readying, 1212 OR

**Age:** 12

Following several years of preparation under the household tutors of Blackbriar Court, Beringer is formally accepted as a page to Count Alistair Amadis.

Though the appointment carries ceremony, Count Alistair wastes little time reminding the boy that pagehood is not an honor, but the beginning of service.

Beringer's days become filled with study.

He learns:

- The histories of the kingdom
- The laws of noble governance
- Heraldry
- Diplomacy
- Accounting
- Stewardship
- Military logistics
- The rites of the Church of Heironeous

Alongside his academic education, he begins the physical disciplines expected of every knight:

- Riding
- Swordsmanship
- Wrestling
- Archery
- Shield work
- Maintenance of armor and horses

Blackbriar believes a knight must first become a servant before he may command others.

---

## The Lessons of Countess Anna

While Count Alistair prepares Beringer for war, Countess Anna prepares him for peace.

She insists that a noble's greatest strength is not found upon the battlefield, but in the confidence of those he governs.

Beringer accompanies her through the villages of County Blackbriar, watching as she settles disputes between farmers, comforts grieving families, oversees harvests, and listens patiently to concerns brought before her.

Anna never permits rank to become distance.

Every stable hand, mason, merchant, and shepherd receives the same courtesy she extends to kings and bishops.

Years later, many who serve beside Beringer remark upon his uncommon habit of knowing the names of common soldiers and stable boys alike.

Those habits were learned at Anna's side.

---

## Brothers in Training

As the years pass, Beringer and Alistair the Younger become inseparable.

Though Alistair is heir to House Amadis and Beringer is not, Count Alistair refuses to educate them differently.

The two spar together.

Study together.

Fail together.

Improve together.

Where one succeeds, the other is expected to follow.

Competition exists between them, but never jealousy.

Each wishes only to become worthy of standing beside the other.

Their bond becomes one of complete trust, a brotherhood that neither politics nor blood can weaken.

---

## Sophia of Blackbriar

Young Sophia trails endlessly behind her brothers.

Still a child, she insists upon attending lessons beyond her years, often appearing in libraries where she is politely, but unsuccessfully, sent away.

Beringer delights in teasing her.

Sophia delights in reminding him that she remembers every embarrassing mistake he has ever made.

Their arguments become legendary within Blackbriar Court.

So too does the speed with which they forgive one another.

The affection between them is unmistakable.

To outsiders, they appear every bit the children of one family.

---

## The Philosophy of House Amadis

Throughout his youth, one lesson is repeated so often that it becomes the foundation of Beringer's character.

> Nothing worth having is ever given.  
> Everything worth keeping is earned.

Count Alistair teaches this upon the training field.

Countess Anna teaches it within the great hall.

Together they ensure that praise follows effort rather than talent.

Success is celebrated.

Failure is accepted.

Excuses are not.

By his fifteenth year, Beringer no longer seeks recognition.

He seeks only to become better than he was the day before.

---

## Readying, 1215 OR

**Age:** 15

Having completed his years as a page, Beringer is accepted as the personal squire of Count Alistair Amadis.

As part of the ceremony, he is entrusted with the [[Blackbriar Squire Blade]].

Unlike ceremonial weapons carried only for display, the blade is a working knight's broadsword, forged within the smithies of Blackbriar generations earlier. It has long served as the sword of the count's current squire, passing from knight to apprentice as each generation rises to knighthood.

The weapon represents trust rather than rank.

To bear it is to stand within an unbroken chain of service stretching back through the history of House Amadis.

Beringer carries the sword throughout his years as a squire, preserving it long after earning the right to bear finer weapons of his own.

---

## The Weight of Command

As Count Alistair's squire, Beringer's responsibilities expand dramatically.

He maintains his lord's arms and armor, prepares horses, oversees camp when traveling, carries messages between commanders, and attends councils where military matters are discussed.

More importantly, Count Alistair begins explaining not merely what decisions are made, but why.

Victories purchased at needless cost are condemned.

Retreats that preserve lives are sometimes praised.

Mercy, when wisely given, is shown to strengthen kingdoms more effectively than cruelty.

For the first time, Beringer begins to understand that leadership demands judgment as much as courage.

---

## An Uncle's Watchful Eye

Though [[Count Vorias Serrin]], brother to Countess Anna, resides far from Blackbriar for much of the year, he remains a familiar presence throughout Beringer's youth.

To Beringer, he is simply Uncle Vorias.

Where Count Alistair teaches command and Anna teaches governance, Vorias teaches caution.

He possesses an uncanny understanding of secrets, politics, and the dangers hidden beneath seemingly ordinary events.

Many of his lessons appear cryptic at the time.

Only years later, after entering the world beyond Blackbriar, would Beringer realize how often his uncle had quietly prepared him for dangers no sword could defeat.

---

## The Eve of Spring

By the opening months of 1216 OR, Beringer has become a capable young squire.

He has known little beyond Blackbriar.

His greatest concerns are training, duty, and earning the respect of his adoptive father.

The hidden truth of his birth remains unknown.

The blood curse sleeps silently beneath Atroa's fading seal.

Beyond the eastern frontier, however, events are already moving toward him.

Across the border, the daughter of House Golitsyn has begun searching for a path to freedom.

Within weeks, the course of Beringer's life will change forever.

---

## Legacy of the Young Squire

Historians often remember Beringer's youth for the wars and tragedies that followed.

Those who knew him best remembered something else.

They remembered a boy raised by parents who were not bound to him by blood, yet loved him without reservation.

A brother who never questioned whether he belonged.

A little sister who adored him despite pretending otherwise.

And a young squire who entered adulthood believing that honor was measured not by birth, but by the choices one made.

It was this foundation, laid quietly within the halls of Blackbriar Court, that prepared Beringer for the trials yet to come.

The next chapter would test every lesson House Amadis had ever taught him.

---

# Part III - The Spring of Hope
## 1216-1218 OR

## Readying, 1216 OR

**Age:** 16

By the spring of 1216, unrest along the Dominion frontier has become impossible to ignore.

For months, House Amadis receives increasingly secret communications from an unlikely source: [[House Golitsyn]], one of Blackbriar's oldest and bitterest enemies.

The letters come from [[Elizaveta Golitsyn]], daughter of [[Boyar Mstislav Golitsyn]].

Raised within the [[Dominion of Kesh]] under the cruel doctrine of [[Hextor]], Elizaveta has become deeply disillusioned with the violence and oppression that define her father's rule. Though renowned as a formidable warrior-priestess, she no longer believes in the cause she was born to serve.

She seeks asylum.

Countess Anna understands what such a request truly means.

Granting sanctuary will almost certainly lead to war.

Refusing it will condemn an innocent woman to a life she desperately wishes to escape.

She chooses mercy.

---

## The Blackbriar Raid

Under the command of Count Alistair Amadis, a mounted force crosses the frontier.

The operation is swift and precise.

House Golitsyn's escort is engaged, broken, and driven into disorder.

Amid the confusion, Count Alistair gives an unexpected order.

His young squire is to retrieve the refugee.

Beringer spurs his horse directly through the fighting, reaches Elizaveta's side, seizes her from her saddle, and lifts her onto his own before turning west.

Pursued by Dominion riders, he flees alongside a handful of Blackbriar knights while Count Alistair and the main force remain behind to cover their escape.

It is the first great test of his courage.

It will not be the last.

---

## First Taste of Freedom

As storms sweep across the frontier, Beringer and Elizaveta seek shelter within a lonely roadside tavern while their escort waits for the weather to pass.

For Elizaveta, the moment is unlike anything she has ever known.

No guards watch her.

No father dictates her every word.

No expectation of violence shadows every conversation.

For the first time in her life, she simply exists as a young woman free to laugh, speak, and choose for herself.

Beringer expects gratitude.

Instead, he finds companionship.

They speak not as enemies born to rival houses, but as two young people whose lives have always been directed by others.

Neither yet realizes how profoundly they understand one another.

Years later, Beringer remembers that quiet tavern not for the storm outside, but because it was where Elizaveta first smiled without fear.

---

## A Daughter of Blackbriar

Upon arriving at Blackbriar Court, Elizaveta is not treated as a prisoner or political hostage.

At Countess Anna's insistence, she is welcomed as a guest, and in time, as family.

Anna oversees her education in the customs of Gorundia, introducing her to courtly life alongside young Sophia.

The Countess never asks Elizaveta to deny where she came from.

Instead, she shows her that birth need not determine destiny.

Freed from her father's influence, Elizaveta gradually abandons the teachings of Hextor.

In their place, she finds herself drawn toward the ideals of Heironeous:

- Courage
- Justice
- Sacrifice
- Honor

The transformation is neither sudden nor forced.

It is chosen.

---

## The Courtship

As the months pass, affection between Beringer and Elizaveta quietly deepens.

Neither seeks romance.

It simply arrives.

Elizaveta sees in Beringer the first person who has ever treated her as more than a weapon or political prize.

To her, he becomes the doorway through which freedom entered her life.

Beringer, for his part, sees something equally extraordinary.

Raised to believe that honor is earned rather than inherited, he finds living proof before him.

Elizaveta demonstrates that blood does not define a person's character.

Long before Beringer learns the truth of his own birth, she unknowingly teaches him the lesson that will one day allow him to bear the name Vogel without becoming its prisoner.

Recognizing the sincerity of their affection, Countess Anna permits the two to court under her careful supervision.

Their love grows openly beneath the protection of House Amadis.

For a brief season, it seems happiness has finally found them both.

---

## 1217 OR - The Death of Countess Anna

Peace proves tragically brief.

Seeking to end the growing crisis between Blackbriar and House Golitsyn before open war erupts, Countess Anna undertakes a diplomatic mission to the Dominion frontier.

She hopes to broker peace through dialogue with Elizaveta's father.

She never returns.

Dominion assassins ambush her party during the journey home.

The Countess of Blackbriar dies upon the road she traveled in pursuit of peace.

News of her death shatters House Amadis.

Throughout County Blackbriar, church bells ring in mourning.

Villages line the roads as her body is carried home.

Those who loved her weep openly.

Those who feared her at court speak of the kingdom losing one of its greatest stateswomen.

For Beringer, it is the second mother he has lost.

---

## The Abbey of the Celestial Veil

Count Alistair leads his family in bearing Anna's remains to the [[Abbey of the Celestial Veil]], where she is laid to rest with every honor due her station.

Overcome by grief and uncertain of what the future will bring, Beringer and Elizaveta make a decision known only to themselves.

Within the abbey, before a kindly and thoroughly intoxicated prior, they exchange vows in secret.

No nobles attend.

No heralds announce the union.

No feast follows.

The ceremony lasts only a few quiet moments.

Yet before both the Church and the gods, they become husband and wife.

It remains one of the best-kept secrets in the history of House Amadis.

---

## Taken Once More

The journey home from the abbey never reaches its end.

Dominion agents strike before the company returns to Blackbriar.

Elizaveta is seized and carried back across the frontier to House Golitsyn.

The young woman who had finally found freedom is dragged once more into her father's grasp.

Beringer can do nothing to stop it.

Within a single week, he has buried his mother...

...and lost his wife.

---

## The Road to War

The assassination of Countess Anna and the abduction of Elizaveta destroy any remaining hope of peace.

Count Alistair declares war upon House Golitsyn.

Throughout the following months, raids, reprisals, and pitched engagements spread across the frontier.

What began as one woman's plea for sanctuary becomes one of the bloodiest private conflicts either house has fought in generations.

Beringer enters the campaign as a young squire.

He emerges from it a hardened warrior.

The final battle still lies ahead.

Its name will be remembered throughout Blackbriar for generations.

[[Golitsyn's Gate]].

---

## Legacy of the Spring of Hope

Later generations often describe these years as the happiest of Beringer's life.

Not because they are untouched by sorrow.

But because, for a brief moment between childhood and tragedy, he knows exactly who he believes himself to be.

He is the son of House Amadis.

A faithful squire.

A young man in love.

He has no ambition beyond earning his spurs, serving his family, and building a quiet future beside the woman he loves.

History has other plans.

---

# Part IV - Golitsyn's Gate
## 1218-1219 OR

## Spring, 1218 OR

**Age:** 18

By the spring of 1218, the war between [[House Amadis]] and [[House Golitsyn]] has raged for nearly a year.

Neither side has secured a decisive victory.

The borderlands bear the scars of constant raids, burned villages, broken fortifications, and private violence politely denied by every official who benefits from pretending not to understand what a border war looks like.

[[Count Alistair Amadis]] understands the conflict cannot continue indefinitely.

To preserve [[County Blackbriar]], House Golitsyn must be broken.

The final campaign is planned.

Its battlefield will later become known simply as [[Golitsyn's Gate]].

---

## The Fall of Boyar Alexsi Golitsyn

Count Alistair draws House Golitsyn into battle through careful deception, forcing [[Boyar Alexsi Golitsyn]] to commit his household guard before the battlefield has fully developed.

The trap closes.

Surrounded by the knights and men-at-arms of Blackbriar, the boyar fights to the last.

He falls beneath the blades of Count Alistair's household guard.

His head is taken from the field as proof that House Golitsyn has lost its lord.

The battle should end there.

Instead, it becomes even more desperate.

---

## The Charge of the Heir

Seeking either vengeance or succession, the heir of House Golitsyn gathers what remains of his strength.

Mounted cavalry surges forward beside a vast horde of chained battle slaves, men and women driven into combat through terror, cruelty, and Hextorite doctrine.

Count Alistair answers in kind.

He entrusts the countercharge to his own heir.

Though only fifteen years old, [[Alistair Amadis the Younger]] lowers the Silver Lance of Blackbriar and leads the mounted knights of House Amadis directly into the enemy's flank.

The collision shatters Golitsyn's cavalry.

In single combat, Alistair slays the heir of House Golitsyn.

History no longer remembers the young man's name.

House Amadis deliberately allows it to be forgotten.

To them, no greater insult can be given to an enemy than to deny him remembrance.

---

## Holding the Line

While the mounted knights break the enemy charge, the center of the battlefield descends into chaos.

Terrified battle slaves continue pressing forward despite the deaths of their masters.

Someone has to hold them.

Beringer steps forward.

Still only a squire, he and the infantry of House Amadis form a living wall against wave after wave of desperate attackers.

Victory demands time.

Beringer buys it with blood.

---

## The Champion of the Pits

Amid the fighting, a single warrior emerges from the enemy ranks.

Unlike the terrified slaves surrounding them, this champion advances with terrible purpose.

Clad in heavy armor, masked beyond recognition, and bound by an iron slave collar and chain, the gladiator carries a long spear stained by countless battles fought for the amusement of others.

No words pass between them.

The two meet in single combat.

The duel is brutal.

Each wounds the other.

Beringer eventually forces himself inside the spear's reach, striking with such force that his final blow shatters the slave collar fastened around the champion's neck.

At almost the same instant, the spear pierces deeply into his own body.

Both combatants fall.

Only then does the shattered mask slip away.

The face beneath belongs to [[Elizaveta Golitsyn]].

---

# The Price of Victory

House Amadis wins the Battle of Golitsyn's Gate.

House Golitsyn ceases to exist as a meaningful military power.

The victory comes at terrible cost.

Elizaveta Golitsyn, Beringer's wife though almost no one knows it, dies upon the battlefield.

She had been taken from Blackbriar, stripped of her freedom once more, chained, masked, and forced into battle as little more than a living weapon.

Neither she nor Beringer recognized the other before it was too late.

The final blow that freed her from slavery also ended her life.

For the remainder of his days, Beringer would remember Golitsyn's Gate not as his greatest victory...

...but as the day he lost everything.

---

## Between Life and Death

Beringer himself does not die upon the field.

The wound inflicted by Elizaveta's spear very nearly claims him.

He remembers little after the duel.

Only fragments.

The weight of armor.

The smell of rain and churned earth.

Voices calling his name.

When consciousness finally returns, he finds himself once more within Blackbriar Court.

Weeks have passed.

---

## A Long Recovery

Recovery proves painfully slow.

For nearly a year, Beringer's body heals while his spirit does not.

He speaks little.

Rarely leaves his chambers.

The victory celebrations held throughout County Blackbriar mean nothing to him.

His family does not force him back into the world.

Count Alistair remains patient.

Alistair the Younger visits often, even when conversation fails.

Sophia quietly fills his room with books, flowers, and letters copied from Anna's journals.

Together, House Amadis carries him through the darkest year of his life.

---

## Elizaveta's Mercy Knife

Among the few possessions recovered from Golitsyn's Gate was [[Elizaveta Golitsyn]]'s Mercy Knife, [[The Second Mercy]].

It had been made in the Orenic style, though disguised for life inside House Golitsyn.

Fashioned of true silver, its visible crossguard bore the bear and bull of House Golitsyn, allowing it to pass as a noble daughter's private blade or ceremonial keepsake.

Hidden beneath the guard, however, was a tiny Orenic maker's notch: a storm-lynx claw split by a broken chain.

The blade's inscription had been nearly polished away, but under candlelight the old words could still be read:

> No soul belongs to the chain.

Its grip remained wrapped in blue silk cut from the hem of one of Elizaveta's gowns.

Hidden beneath that wrapping rested a lock of her silver hair.

The knife had not saved her life.

But it had remained with her.

In the end, that mattered.

---

## How Elizaveta Came by It

Elizaveta never spoke openly of where she obtained the blade.

The safest version, and therefore almost certainly not the whole truth, is that it came to her through a minor Orenic-born attendant in House Golitsyn's service.

The better version is this:

The knife was passed through a chain of women who had survived Dominion households.

Servants.

Hostages.

Unacknowledged daughters.

Political prisoners.

A widowed Orenic cousin by marriage.

A chambermaid who knew which doors did not creak.

Each kept it only long enough to pass it to someone who needed the reminder more.

By the time it reached Elizaveta, the knife was already old.

It had cut slave cords.

It had opened locked prayer-books.

It had been hidden in hems, saddle padding, and once inside the false bottom of a reliquary dedicated to Hextor, which is the kind of joke that keeps border folk alive.

Elizaveta kept it not because she intended to die with it.

She kept it because, long before she had the courage to flee, the knife told her that fleeing was thinkable.

---

## Beringer's Understanding

Beringer did not understand the Mercy Knife when it was first placed among Elizaveta's recovered possessions.

At first, he saw only a keepsake.

A silver blade.

Blue silk.

A lock of hair.

A dead wife's last relic.

Only later did he understand what it truly meant.

The knife was never a token of despair.

It was the opposite.

It was the proof that even inside House Golitsyn, even under Hextorite law, even beneath the doctrine that every captive was property, Elizaveta had carried a private contradiction.

A small piece of freedom.

A hidden refusal.

A promise that tyranny had not reached all the way in.

---

## Table Use

The Mercy Knife should not be treated as a magic weapon first.

It is a symbolic object with enough metaphysical weight to matter when oaths, curses, bondage, possession, slavery, or Hextorite rites are in play.

Possible uses:

- Cutting mundane or ritual bonds.
- Breaking lesser Hextorite slave-marks.
- Serving as a focus for oaths of protection, freedom, or release.
- Providing a bonus or narrative permission against domination tied specifically to slavery, oath-binding, or Hextorite claim.
- Acting as a recognized symbol among Orenic veterans, border priests, or resistance families.
- Provoking immediate hostility from Dominion clergy or Secret Chancery agents who recognize it.

Suggested GURPS handling:

- Treat as a fine silver small knife for mundane use.
- Against Hextorite binding cords, slave brands, collar-runes, or oath-ribbons, allow it to count as a sanctified tool.
- When used as part of a sincere oath to free, protect, or release the enslaved, grant a small bonus to Religious Ritual, Exorcism, Thaumatology, or Will, as appropriate.
- It should not casually cut major curses by itself. That way lies item inflation, and then everyone wants a holy can-opener.

# The Bloodsworn Oath
## 1219 OR

**Age:** 19

When at last Beringer can stand once more, he enters the Chapel of Heironeous at Blackbriar Court.

There, before Count Alistair Amadis, he kneels at the altar.

He places Elizaveta's Mercy Knife upon the sacred stone.

He does not yet understand every part of its Orenic history.

He understands enough.

He understands that it was hers.

He understands that she carried it in defiance.

He understands that it meant freedom before it meant grief.

Before both his father and his god, Beringer swears a Bloodsworn Oath.

He vows that the life denied to Elizaveta will not end with her death.

He will live a life worthy of the woman she became.

He will carry her memory wherever duty leads him.

He will never allow grief to become an excuse for abandoning honor.

He will never mistake mercy for weakness.

He will never allow chains to become invisible simply because nobles call them law.

The oath does not erase his sorrow.

It gives his sorrow purpose.

---

# Knight of Blackbriar

Not long afterward, Count Alistair fulfills the promise he made years before.

Before the household of Blackbriar, Beringer is dubbed a knight.

The ceremony is quieter than anyone imagined when his training first began.

Countess Anna is gone.

Elizaveta is gone.

The young man who kneels before the Count is no longer the optimistic squire who rode east to rescue a frightened young woman from House Golitsyn.

He rises bearing new spurs...

...and invisible wounds that will remain with him for the rest of his life.

---

# The Road West

Soon after receiving his knighthood, Beringer makes a decision that surprises many within House Amadis.

Rather than remain at Blackbriar, he chooses to ride west.

There will be no lands.

No title.

No comfortable command.

He will become a hedge knight, offering his sword wherever it is needed most.

Count Alistair does not forbid him.

He understands.

Some roads cannot be walked for another.

As Beringer departs Blackbriar, he carries only a handful of possessions that truly matter:

- The hidden Vogel signet ring, still unknown to him
- The [[Blackbriar Squire Blade]], symbol of the family who raised him
- Elizaveta's Mercy Knife, upon which he swore his oath

The rest, he intends to earn.

---

# Legacy of Golitsyn's Gate

Historians often describe Golitsyn's Gate as the battle that destroyed House Golitsyn.

Those who knew Sir Beringer remember it differently.

It was the day a young man lost his first love.

The day a grieving squire became a knight.

And the day a promise, sworn over a silver knife before the altar of Heironeous, began shaping every choice he would make for the rest of his life.

# Part V - The Long Ride West
## 1219-1230 OR

## Late Summer, 1219 OR

**Age:** 19

Within weeks of receiving his knighthood, Sir Beringer departs Blackbriar.

He does not seek lands, title, or command.

Instead, he turns west toward the frontier, where monsters, raiders, and lawlessness constantly test the strength of Gorundia's border.

Count Alistair offers neither command nor destination.

Only a final piece of advice.

> "Go where you are needed, not where glory waits."

Beringer carries those words for the rest of his life.

---

## 1219-1221 OR - A Hedge Knight of the Frontier

The western frontier proves harsher than anything Beringer imagined.

Villages possess neither walls nor standing armies.

Many cannot afford to pay a knight at all.

Beringer accepts whatever payment people can spare.

Sometimes silver.

Sometimes a warm bed.

Sometimes little more than bread and fresh water.

More than once, he accepts nothing.

To him, protecting those unable to defend themselves is simply the duty of knighthood.

---

## The Defense of Bard's Gate

Not long after arriving in the west, Beringer answers the call to defend [[Bard's Gate]] from repeated raids by Qazaq bands of nomadic half-orcs.

Though only one knight among many, he distinguishes himself through calm leadership during the city's defense.

Veteran soldiers begin remembering his name.

Not because he seeks attention.

Because he is invariably found wherever the fighting proves hardest.

---

## The Knight Without a Home

Unlike many hedge knights, Beringer refuses long-term service beneath a single banner.

He wanders continuously.

One month escorting merchants through dangerous forests.

Another hunting trolls in forgotten valleys.

The next recovering relics from abandoned ruins.

He sleeps where fortune allows.

Roadside shrines.

Barn lofts.

Stable floors.

When contracts pay well, he enjoys warm inns, hearty meals, and good company.

When coin runs short, he accepts hardship without complaint.

---

## Three Barrels of Salted Beef

One village possesses no money whatsoever after repeated monster attacks.

Unable to pay their rescuer in coin, the villagers instead offer Beringer three barrels of salted beef.

He accepts them with sincere gratitude.

The gift sustains him for months.

By the time every barrel has finally been emptied...

...Sir Beringer never wishes to eat salted beef again.

Among friends, it eventually becomes the only food he steadfastly refuses.

---

## 1221-1224 OR - The Adventurers' Guild

As his reputation steadily grows, Beringer joins the [[Adventurers' Guild of Bard's Gate]].

Unlike noble service, the guild judges its members only by competence.

Within its halls, wandering knights fight beside priests, barbarians, druids, rogues, and hedge wizards.

For Beringer, it becomes both livelihood and community.

No two contracts are alike.

One week requires escorting caravans.

The next, recovering forgotten relics.

Then monster hunts, missing travelers, haunted ruins, or disputes between isolated settlements.

Every completed contract deepens his understanding of the frontier and its people.

---

## A Reputation Earned

Without ever seeking fame, Beringer slowly becomes known throughout the western counties.

Innkeepers begin keeping rooms available should "the young knight from Blackbriar" happen through town.

Village elders quietly recommend him whenever honest help is needed.

Merchants prefer his company upon dangerous roads.

Children remember the polite knight who always kneels to speak with them at eye level.

He never considers these things remarkable.

To him, they are simply part of the work.

---

## 1224-1228 OR - The Making of a Frontier Knight

The years pass quickly.

Each season brings new roads.

New companions.

New dangers.

Beringer hunts monsters that terrorize isolated settlements.

Escorts pilgrims across dangerous mountain passes.

Recovers lost family heirlooms.

Delivers justice where local authority cannot.

Protects villages too poor to hire anyone else.

The frontier slowly reshapes him.

He learns when steel solves problems...

...and when kindness accomplishes far more.

---

## The Lessons of Uncle Vorias

Though separated by great distances, Beringer maintains correspondence with [[Count Vorias Serrin]], brother to the late Countess Anna.

Their letters range from politics to military affairs, from philosophy to quiet family matters.

Vorias rarely gives direct advice.

Instead, he asks questions.

Questions that force Beringer to think rather than simply react.

Many years later, Beringer would credit his uncle with teaching him that wisdom often begins by admitting how much remains unknown.

---

## 1228-1230 OR - Captain of the Greencloaks

As unrest along the western frontier steadily worsens, Lord-Mayor [[Theron Sylvanranth]] invites Beringer into the [[Greencloaks]], Bard's Gate's respected ranger and militia force.

There, Beringer accepts his first permanent military responsibility since leaving Blackbriar.

Promoted to captain, he leads patrols throughout the surrounding countryside, protecting roads, villages, and travelers from increasingly organized threats.

For the first time since Count Alistair's household, other soldiers regularly look to him for orders.

He accepts the responsibility reluctantly.

He carries it well.

---

# Current State at End of Part V

By 1230 OR, Sir Beringer Amadis is no longer merely the hidden child rescued from Villa Marsten or the grieving survivor of Golitsyn's Gate.

He is:

- A knight of Blackbriar
- A proven hedge knight of the western frontier
- A veteran of Bard's Gate's defense
- A member of the Adventurers' Guild
- A captain of the Greencloaks
- A man shaped by grief but not ruled by it
- The bearer of Elizaveta's Mercy Knife
- The unknown son of House Vogel
- The living proof that honor can be chosen

He has not sought power.

Which, naturally, means power is about to start stalking him like an unpaid debt with spurs.
