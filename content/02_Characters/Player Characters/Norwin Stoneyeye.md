# Norwin Stoneyeye

> “Knowledge belongs to those willing to seek it, not merely those willing to read about it.”
> — Commonly attributed to [[Rasros Stoneyeye]], though Norwin has repeated it often enough that several students assume he coined it himself. This irritates him more than it should.

## Summary

Norwin Stoneyeye is a gnome wizard, former professor, Royal Archivist, and Sage of the Crown. Brilliant, fussy, patient, technical, and dangerously curious, he is one of the Stoneyeye triplets born in [[The Free Warren of Tinkershade]] in 830 OR.

He was raised under the eye of his grandfather [[Rasros Stoneyeye]], a Teenkdiwar-born founder, engineer-scholar, and long-lived gnomish visionary who established Tinkershade in Araisal and never moved it once founded. Rasros insisted that the Warren existed for a purpose still centuries away, because apparently even gnome family planning comes with prophetic infrastructure.

Norwin’s life divides cleanly into five broad eras:

- the wonder of Tinkershade
- the long apprenticeship under Rasros
- the quiet years after the return home
- the academic century at Gorundia’s College of Magic
- the second wandering, now tied to the Crown, the Spelljammer program, and the Gorundian Civil War

## Family and Origin

Norwin was born as one of identical triplets alongside [[Kelwin Stoneyeye]] and [[Loxwin Stoneyeye]] in Tinkershade Warren on Ready’reat 5, 830 OR.

The triplets’ birth was celebrated as a sign of unusual fortune. Tinkershade itself had been founded roughly two centuries earlier by their grandfather, [[Rasros Stoneyeye]], a gnome of [[Teenkdiwar]] heritage. Rasros established the Warren in Araisal, under the protection and influence of [[House Allaric]], and the enclave remained there. It did not migrate from the Dominion, despite later Stoneyeye travels through Dominion lands.

Tinkershade is properly styled **The Free Warren of Tinkershade**, with the older Teenkdiwar form **Tenk-Shaddir** preserved in traditional records.

The Warren developed a reputation for skilled craftsmen, engineers, hedge mages, bookbinders, clockworkers, and problem-solvers who could make a machine do six things no sane person asked it to do and then file the patent twice.

## The Triplets

![[stoneyeye brothers.png]]

### Norwin Stoneyeye

Norwin developed an early hunger for magic, natural philosophy, classification, and theory. He wanted to know why spells worked, why they failed, why they failed differently under weather pressure, and whether the answer could be archived in triplicate.

He became the most serious of the triplets.

### Kelwin Stoneyeye

Kelwin Stoneyeye is a **Sparkwright**, an engineer-mage whose craft blends clockwork, rune-current, field mechanics, and practical battle engineering.  Kelwin showed an early gift for mechanisms, dismantling and rebuilding almost anything within reach. In later life, he became senior engineer aboard [[The Second Draft]], the Crown’s hammership. He is disciplined, technically brilliant, sarcastic, and best suited to rooms full of machinery that are not currently being worshiped by overexcited engineers.

### Loxwin Stoneyeye

Loxwin Stoneyeye is a **Concordwright**, a diplomat-mage trained in languages, parley customs, oathcraft, translation magic, and the careful shaping of peaceful agreements. Among less formal company, he is sometimes called a **Wordwright**, though he dislikes the simplification.  Loxwin displayed an uncommon talent for language, diplomacy, timing, and understanding people. Where Norwin studies structures of magic and Kelwin studies structures of machinery, Loxwin studies the terrifying machinery of society, which is much less reliable and tends to talk back.

## I. The Years of Wonder

**830–877 OR**  
**Age:** 0–47

Norwin, Kelwin, and Loxwin grew up in Tinkershade under the watchful eye of their family, their Warren, and especially Rasros.

Rasros recognized Baravar Cloakshadow’s blessing upon the boys and began preparing them personally for life beyond the Warren. His education was broad, deliberate, and occasionally maddening.

Norwin received early training in:

- reading and languages
- history
- mathematics
- arcane theory
- natural philosophy
- field observation
- practical caution, though that one did not fully take
- the difference between “known,” “probable,” and “the table is on fire”

By childhood, Norwin had already shown magical aptitude. By adolescence, he had become known for relentless questions, precise corrections, and the ability to turn a simple household spell into a three-hour inquiry on sympathetic resonance.

### Milestones

- **830 OR:** Born on Ready’reat 5.
- **Childhood:** Demonstrates magical aptitude.
- **Youth:** Receives early instruction from Rasros.
- **877 OR:** Begins formal apprenticeship at forty-seven years of age.

## II. The Long Apprenticeship

**877–1127 OR**  
**Age:** 47–297

For the next two and a half centuries, Rasros led Norwin, Kelwin, and Loxwin across a tremendous stretch of the known world.

He did not believe scholarship should be confined to desks, libraries, or safe rooms with sensible chairs. He believed knowledge belonged to those willing to cross borders, get rained on, sleep badly, bargain badly, and learn from people who did not care how clever you were.

The four traveled not as conquerors, but as students.

This distinction mattered to Rasros. It came to matter to Norwin.

## The Western Roads

The first years of travel broke the brothers of several comforting illusions.

They learned to sleep beneath wagons, cross dangerous roads, keep watches, bargain with merchants, lose coin gambling, burn meals over campfires, and manage the basic indignities of long-distance travel.

Norwin discovered that theory alone could not solve every problem. Sometimes the spell was correct and the weather was not. Sometimes the math was excellent and the mule had other opinions.

### Lessons

- magic must survive bad conditions
- plans collapse faster than tents in bad wind
- merchants lie better than books
- field notes written while running are still field notes
- being correct is not the same thing as being useful

## The High Passes

The expedition’s crossings through high mountain routes became a defining part of Norwin’s magical education.

Here, references to ice and snow belong: not broad lowland tundra, but high altitude, brutal passes, wind-scoured ridges, avalanche slopes, glacier-fed ravines, and thin air that punished every mistake.

Storms, avalanches, cold exposure, rockfall, and altitude taught adaptability rather than simple endurance. These were the years in which Norwin began developing the wind and cold-working principles that later shaped his elemental philosophy.

### The Ice Worm

One of Norwin’s earliest major failures occurred in the high passes, where the group encountered an enormous ice worm.

Trying to protect the party, Norwin conjured an Ice Wall. It was theoretically sound, structurally reasonable, and absolutely not enough.

The creature shattered it almost immediately.

Rasros intervened before the failure became a funeral. Norwin survived, and the lesson stuck. In later years he would describe the incident as “an instructional collapse,” because apparently even humiliation sounds better when catalogued properly.

### Lessons

- cold is not merely an absence of heat, but a structure of pressure, brittleness, and time
- wind is movement, warning, and weapon
- defensive magic must be judged against what it is stopping, not how elegantly it is cast
- humility is cheaper than resurrection

## The Hellfurnaces

Norwin’s travels near the [[Hellfurnaces]] left a lasting impression on him.

The mountains were not merely high, cold, and dangerous. They were alive with volcanic force: fumaroles, hot springs, ash-fall, sulfur vents, basalt shelves, lava scars, and old stone that seemed to remember fire.

For Norwin, the Hellfurnaces did not teach fire as a campfire element or battlefield tool. They taught fire as geology, pressure, transformation, and stored violence. They revealed that stone and flame are not opposites, but phases in a longer argument.

This insight later shaped his more dangerous large-scale workings.

That sentence should make everyone nervous, and it has earned that right.

### Lessons

- fire can be slow
- stone can remember heat
- pressure is a form of spellwork
- the line between earth magic and fire magic is thinner than polite theory admits
- volcanoes are not accidents; they are systems

## The Sea of Dust

The expedition ventured into the blasted lands once ruled by the ancient Suel Imperium.

The Sea of Dust was not a desert of sand. It was dust: ash, powder, old ruin, powdered stone, ancient corruption, and dead imperial residue scoured into a hostile inland waste. Its nomads did not ride dunes of clean sand beneath romantic desert moons. They crossed choking dust flats, ash drifts, cinder basins, buried roads, and ruins that surfaced after storms like bones through thin skin.

The dangers were unlike those of the high passes.

They included:

- ghosts
- deathless guardians
- ancient curses
- treasure hunters
- buried Suel cities
- poisoned wells
- dust storms
- false horizons
- old magic that still thought the Imperium had not ended

Here Norwin studied fire, heat, desiccation, endurance, and the magical behavior of ash and dust. He learned that fire does not always roar. Sometimes it leaves silence behind and lets the wind do the killing.

### Ulrach of the Dust

During these years, Norwin befriended [[Ulrach]], a dust-walker mage of the Sea of Dust.

Ulrach taught him spells for calling rain, reading dry winds, shaping ash, moving dust, and surviving where water was less a resource than a temporary rumor.

Norwin’s later weather-working and environmental magic bear Ulrach’s influence, though Norwin is far more likely to call it “field adaptation” than “being saved by someone who knew the land better.”

### Lessons

- dust is not sand
- ash remembers fire
- ruins are active hazards, not scenery
- rain magic is a survival discipline
- the Suel Imperium is dead, but not quiet

## The Dominion of Kesh

The [[Dominion of Kesh]] lies due south of the Sea of Dust, not as the birthplace of the Stoneyeye enclave, but as one of the great regions through which Rasros led his grandsons.

The expedition spent significant time in Dominion lands, including periods of travel, study, danger, and political observation. This is the true Stoneyeye-Dominion connection: Rasros did not come from the Dominion, and Tinkershade did not flee from it. The brothers learned there because Rasros took them there.

The Dominion taught Norwin a different kind of fear.

Not monsters. Not cliffs. Not ash.

Institutions.

The Dominion’s courts, ministries, temples, spies, and noble houses showed Norwin that kingdoms can behave like machines built by paranoid engineers. Each gear claims to serve the whole. Each gear also wants to eat the others.

### The Dominion Court

For several years, Rasros and the brothers served as viziers, advisors, technical consultants, or tolerated gnomish experts to a Dominion noble house.

The exact house remains deliberately vague in Norwin’s own accounts, either from caution or because he enjoys making historians suffer.

This period exposed him to:

- politics
- diplomacy
- administration
- court ritual
- oath-law
- hidden archives
- the danger of being useful to powerful people
- how a kingdom can make cruelty look procedural

### Henricossus

During the Dominion years, Norwin befriended a wandering explorer named [[Henricossus]].

At the time, Norwin did not realize that Henricossus would later be remembered as one of the Dominion’s greatest explorers. To Norwin, he was simply an infuriatingly durable travel companion with excellent instincts and a loose relationship with safe routes.

### Lessons

- governments are machines with knives inside
- records can be weapons
- titles are often disguises for appetite
- courtesy is not kindness
- the most dangerous room is often the one with the best chairs

## Earth Beneath Forgotten Ruins

Across the long apprenticeship, Norwin’s earth magic matured not in a single region but in ruins, mines, buried cities, mountain cuts, and ancient foundations.

Earth, to Norwin, became the element of memory and consequence. Stone stored pressure. Ruins stored mistakes. Foundations stored political lies in masonry form.

This understanding later made him unusually effective with structural magic, fortification theory, geomancy, and catastrophic correction.

Yes, “catastrophic correction” is doing work there.

## The Long Road Home

As Rasros neared the end of his extraordinary life, the four slowly made their way back west to Tinkershade.

By the journey’s end, Norwin had mastered the classical elements through experience rather than formal academic isolation.

- **Ice:** through survival in the high passes
- **Wind:** through storms, altitude, and roads that punished certainty
- **Fire:** through the Hellfurnaces and the Sea of Dust
- **Earth:** through mines, buried ruins, and old foundations
- **Water and Weather:** through Ulrach’s rain-calling and field survival
- **Governance:** through the Dominion, which counts as an element if the element is “paperwork with teeth”

## III. The Quiet Years

**1127–1130 OR**  
**Age:** 297–300

After centuries of wandering, Rasros and the brothers finally remained at home in Tinkershade.

Rather than seeking one last adventure, Rasros spent his final years quietly mentoring his grandsons, watching the Warren flourish, and preparing them for a command he had apparently been carrying for centuries.

When Rasros died at **999 years and 364 days old**, his final instruction was simple:

> “Two go. One stays.”

Kelwin and Loxwin carried Rasros’s body west to his ancestral home of [[Teenkdiwar]].

Norwin remained behind in Gorundia, honoring Rasros’s final command despite never learning its full purpose.

Shortly afterward, Norwin formed his lifelong bond with [[Armia]], marking the beginning of a new chapter in his life.

### The Unanswered Command

Norwin still does not fully know why Rasros required one brother to remain in Gorundia.

He suspects Rasros foresaw something: a war, a collapse, a need for knowledge preserved at the right hinge of history. Rasros had long insisted Tinkershade existed for a purpose still centuries away.

In 1231 OR, with the Gorundian Civil War unfolding and Norwin drawn into the Crown’s magical infrastructure, that old command has begun to look less like family eccentricity and more like a delayed fuse.

Annoying, really, when dead relatives turn out to be right.

## IV. The Scholar

**1130–1230 OR**  
**Age:** 300–400

Searching for purpose after Rasros’s death, Norwin accepted a professorship at Gorundia’s College of Magic.

For fifty years, he taught:

- Magical Theory
- Elemental Principles
- applied spell-structure
- field methodology
- probably several courses no one officially approved

Students remembered him as brilliant, patient, absent-minded, exacting, and famously eccentric. Many quietly remarked that he taught as Rasros had taught him: not merely how to cast spells, but how to think.

This was his great academic virtue.

It was also, inevitably, the thing that made administrators nervous.

## Vianola Erinleaf

During his academic career, Norwin developed a lasting friendship with [[Vianola Erinleaf]], an expert enchanter whose skills often complemented his own magical research.

Vianola respected Norwin’s brilliance but did not regard him as harmless. Her private view, expressed in various forms over the years, is that Norwin thinks laterally, follows dangerous ideas farther than most sane scholars would, and only later remembers to ask whether the experiment should have been done in the first place.

This is not an insult.

It is a warning label.

### Vianola’s Private Assessment

> “He is brilliant. Danger-adjacent, but brilliant. If Norwin ever turns that mind fully toward influence and identity, I would advise distance.”

Norwin would likely object to the wording.

He would not successfully refute it.

## The Greenhouse Incident

Late in his academic career, the College’s botanical laboratory and greenhouse were destroyed during what became known simply as **The Greenhouse Incident**.

Accounts differ wildly.

Some insist Norwin accepted blame to shield reckless students.

Others claim he leveled the greenhouse himself after an argument with Professor Kilroy.

A few versions include animated vines, miscatalogued fertilizer, applied fire theory, extraplanar pollen, or a goat that no one can prove existed.

Norwin has never publicly explained what truly happened.

His dismissal ended a fifty-year academic career.

## V. The Second Wandering

**1230–Present**  
**Age:** 400–401

Leaving academia behind, Norwin joined [[Admiral Thalrik Vaultforge]] and became involved in the Crown’s growing Spelljammer program.

This brought him back into active service, not as a wandering student this time, but as a scholar whose knowledge could alter war, logistics, archives, enchantment, transport, and national survival.

The timing is suspiciously Rasros-shaped.

## Current Offices and Work

Over the following year, Norwin:

- became Royal Archivist and Sage of the Crown
- learned and expanded the art of magical enchanting
- took four apprentices
- helped establish the Crown’s enchanting program
- became entangled in Leena’s courier work
- became mentor to [[Blue Sylvanranth]]
- helped support the Crown’s spelljammer infrastructure
- became attached to [[The Second Draft]] and its wider crew
- found himself pulled into the Gorundian Civil War

His formal style is:

**Norwin Stoneyeye, Royal Archivist and Sage of the Crown**

Some documents also use **Sage of the Realm**, though **Sage of the Crown** is the cleaner formal honor attached to his office.

## Harrowmere

Norwin’s most infamous recent magical working is the destruction of the [[Harrowmere Enclosure]] through the creation of a volcano.

The former fortification is now more properly known as the [[Harrowmere Caldera]].

This was not random pyromania, despite how it sounds when normal people say “he made a volcano.” It was a large-scale corrective working involving geology, magical pressure, containment failure, and battlefield necessity.

It also left Baroness Nerys Valmere and Thunderhold authorities to govern the consequences, which has made the paperwork exquisite and the politics somewhat less so.

### Archivist Note

Norwin’s filings on Harrowmere were reportedly thorough, intelligent, and probably correct.

This is not the same as comforting.

## The Volcanic Air Base

Following Harrowmere, Norwin began construction planning around a fortified volcanic air base.

This connects several threads in his life:

- the Hellfurnaces’ lessons in volcanic systems
- earth and fire as linked disciplines
- Crown spelljammer logistics
- fortress design
- military infrastructure
- Rasros’s insistence that Tinkershade existed for a future purpose

The resulting base is likely to become one of the most strategically strange installations in Gorundian history, which is an impressive achievement in a kingdom currently speed-running political collapse.

## Current Entanglements

### The Crown

Norwin serves the Crown as Royal Archivist, Sage, enchanter, and magical infrastructure planner.

He is valuable because he can think across archives, systems, spells, terrain, and machines. He is dangerous for exactly the same reason.

### The Second Draft

Norwin is tied to [[The Second Draft]], the Crown’s hammership, alongside Thalrik, Kelwin, Rykeena, and others.

During the crisis surrounding the King’s purge, Thalrik was ordered to ready the ship, gather his crew, and move Guaco’s family and selected others aboard. Norwin was included among “your crew,” which places him inside the Crown’s emergency magical transport network.

### Blue Sylvanranth

Norwin is one of the principal reasons [[Blue Sylvanranth]] is still alive.

When [[Thranduil Stormarrow]] might have killed her, Norwin argued him down, pressing for restraint long enough to keep the situation from becoming a tragedy with better posture. He then saw Blue safely Entombed rather than executed, buying time in the only form available: containment.

Afterward, Norwin brought [[Vianola Erinleaf]] onto the problem, not as a courtesy consultation but because the situation had become a deeply technical magical disaster wearing a young woman’s face. Between the Maurakappi larval influence, Blue’s own nature, Sylvanranth politics, and the obvious question of “what the hell do we do next,” Norwin understood that guessing would be criminally stupid.

His relationship with Blue is therefore not merely that of mentor and student. He helped preserve her life, secured her containment, and began the process of understanding whether she could be safely restored, freed, protected, or all three.

This makes Norwin one of Blue’s quiet anchors: not sentimental, not dramatic, but essential.

### Leena and the Amber Eye

Norwin is tangled in [[Leena]]’s courier schemes and the wider shadow-politics of Bard’s Gate.

He is not primarily an underworld figure, but his archives, transport access, and faction-neutral reputation make him useful to people who prefer not to write their intentions in large friendly letters.

### Vianola Erinleaf

Norwin’s friendship with Vianola remains one of his key scholarly relationships.

They complement one another intellectually: Norwin pushes theory and structural adaptation; Vianola anchors enchantment, coercive-magic ethics, and hard skepticism. This makes them a useful pair and a terrible faculty committee.

## The Faelar Moonshade Controversy

Norwin has recently entered a sharp academic dispute with [[Lord Faelar Moonshade]], the pre-eminent scholar of the kingdom and one of the few living figures with access to truly broad research resources across elven, Allaric, royal, theological, and arcane archives.

This is not a petty faculty quarrel, though it has all the emotional dignity of one when viewed from a safe distance.

Faelar has begun issuing formal responses to Norwin’s field notes and reports, including critiques of Norwin’s treatment of gnomish peoples, gnomish religion, Stillglass, Ancestral Continuance, deep gnome speculation, and the destruction of [[Harrowmere Enclosure]]. His replies are elegant, cutting, heavily sourced, and deeply annoying in the precise way only a scholar with enough evidence can be.

The substance of the argument is real. Faelar frequently concedes that Norwin’s observations are sharp, practical, and sometimes uncomfortably correct. He recognizes Norwin as a wizard of significant talent and an unusually capable field analyst. This only makes the criticism worse, because Faelar does not dismiss Norwin; he engages him seriously.

Faelar’s recurring charges are that Norwin:

- reduces sacred or poetic terminology too aggressively
- mistakes elven language for obfuscation when it may preserve moral nuance
- treats rock gnome systems as “normal” and forest gnome relational traditions as irritating
- approaches myth and religious multiplicity with too much impatience
- sees magical function clearly but sometimes underweights civic, ecological, and political consequence
- uses mockery as a cutting tool, which works far more often than Faelar wants to admit

Norwin, for his part, is not wrong to bristle. Faelar’s scholarship is formidable, but it also carries the full weight of high elven confidence, archival privilege, and prose that sometimes arrives wearing three layers of silk and a concealed knife.

The controversy matters because Faelar is not merely correcting Norwin in private. He is creating a formal scholarly record around Norwin’s work. That record may strengthen Norwin’s standing by proving that the realm’s greatest scholar takes him seriously, or it may complicate his reputation by framing him as brilliant, useful, biased, procedurally dangerous, and insufficiently reverent toward ancient elven categories.

In practical terms, this rivalry gives Norwin both a critic and an unwilling validator. If Faelar attacks a report, the report becomes important. If Faelar concedes a point, the point becomes difficult for the kingdom’s scholars to ignore.

Norwin may hate that.

He should also use it.

## Magical Philosophy

Norwin’s elemental philosophy comes from lived experience rather than tidy College doctrine.

He does not treat the elements as separate boxes. He treats them as interacting systems.

- **Ice** is pressure, time, stillness, and brittleness.
- **Wind** is motion, warning, communication, and force.
- **Fire** is transformation, pressure-release, destruction, and renewal.
- **Earth** is memory, weight, foundation, and consequence.
- **Water** is weather, survival, flow, and adaptation.
- **Dust and Ash** are residues of catastrophe that still behave like active magical materials.

His later work in enchanting, archives, fortification, and large-scale geomancy reflects this integrated model.

## Personality

Norwin is:

- brilliant
- fussy
- patient with students
- impatient with sloppy reasoning
- technically precise
- absent-minded in ordinary matters
- strangely calm under intellectual pressure
- prone to muttering when events violate orderly expectation
- more dangerous than he looks
- not nearly as harmless as his academic manners suggest

He is often the rational counterbalance in chaotic situations, though “rational” should not be confused with “safe.” Norwin’s rational response to a broken magical enclosure may be to make a volcano.

## Table Voice

Norwin is at his best when the scene needs:

- technical explanation without pure exposition sludge
- a gnomish voice of reason
- arcane caution that still somehow ends in dangerous experimentation
- academic fussing under battlefield conditions
- someone to notice the structural implication no one else caught
- a PC ally who understands both magic and the horror of paperwork

### Speech Patterns

Norwin tends to:

- begin objections before realizing the room is already moving
- correct terminology under stress
- mutter through cascading implications
- use precise academic phrasing for terrifying things
- get increasingly fast when alarmed
- become calmer when the problem becomes genuinely catastrophic

### Sample Lines

> “That is not a tower failure. That is a pressure argument with masonry.”

> “No, no, dust is not sand. Sand is mineral patience. Dust is history after violence.”

> “A volcano is not a fire spell. It is an earth spell losing an argument with heat.”

> “If the Dominion stamped this twice, it means either the left office lied to the right office, or both offices are lying to a third office. I hate that I understand this.”

> “The wall failed because it was designed for force. The worm was not force. The worm was appetite.”

> “Please do not touch that. It is either inert, cursed, or waiting for the confidence of a fool.”

Norwin leaves academia and becomes involved with [[Admiral Thalrik Vaultforge]] and the Crown’s Spelljammer program.

### 1230–1231 OR — Crown Service

Norwin becomes Royal Archivist and Sage of the Crown.

He learns enchanting, takes apprentices, helps establish the Crown enchanting program, and becomes part of the realm’s emergency magical infrastructure.

### 1231 OR — Harrowmere Caldera

Norwin destroys the Harrowmere Enclosure by creating a volcano.

The site becomes [[Harrowmere Caldera]].

### 1231 OR — Civil War

Norwin is drawn into the Gorundian Civil War and the Crown’s purge-era emergency movements.

He is tied to the Second Draft crew and the larger question of whether Rasros’s final command has finally come due.

## Secrets and Open Questions

### What Did Rasros See?

Rasros insisted Tinkershade existed for a purpose still centuries away.

He also commanded that one brother remain in Gorundia.

The likely implication is that Rasros foresaw the current crisis in some form, but the exact nature of his foresight remains unknown.

### What Really Happened in the Greenhouse?

Possible explanations include:

- Norwin shielding students
- a failed experiment
- a fight with Professor Kilroy
- an interaction between botanical magic and elemental containment
- something stranger that Norwin has chosen not to reveal

The truth should remain table-flexible until needed.

### Why Norwin?

Of the triplets, Rasros left Norwin behind.

Possible reasons:

- Norwin’s magical aptitude
- his temperament
- his ability to bridge theory and fieldwork
- his future role in Crown archives
- his involvement in enchanting and spelljammer logistics
- his connection to Blue, Vianola, Leena, and Thalrik

### What Does Harrowmere Change?

Harrowmere proves Norwin can operate on a strategic magical scale.

That makes him valuable, frightening, and politically relevant.

It also means future enemies may target him not because he is famous, but because he understands how to make terrain itself obey.
