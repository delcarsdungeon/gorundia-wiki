# Myra Durathen

> **“Beauty opens doors. Competence decides whether anyone regrets letting you in.”**

[[Myra Durathen]] is the youngest daughter of [[Roderic Durathen]] and [[Elinora Durathen]], sister to [[Edwyn Durathen]], and cousin to [[Braga Kharne]]. Born into the proud but poor [[House Durathen]], she learned early that charm, beauty, manners, and arithmetic are all weapons if held correctly.

Myra is young, beautiful, intelligent, and ambitious.

She knows this.

She is not embarrassed by it.

Once merely the clever unmarried daughter at [[Greyford Hall]], Myra became Braga’s lady-in-waiting and has since grown into something far more useful: the effective seneschal and administrative mind behind much of [[Orin Kharne]]’s barony.

Orin may be the baron.

Braga may be the storm.

Myra makes sure the grain moves, the letters arrive, the accounts balance, the servants fear the correct things, and nobody important forgets who they owe.

## Appearance

![[Myra Durathen.png]]

Myra favors controlled elegance rather than empty display. She understands the value of a beautiful dress, a correct ribbon, a measured glance, and a precisely timed smile.

She also understands when the road calls for old boots and riding pants.

This distinction matters.

Unlike court-bred ornaments who treat practicality as a rumor spread by ugly people, Myra can dress for advantage and still survive a hard day in the saddle. Durathen poverty taught her the difference between appearance as strategy and appearance as stupidity.

## Personality

Myra is clever, resourceful, ambitious, and emotionally guarded. She has the social instincts of a courtier, the administrative discipline of a steward, and the survival reflexes of someone from a house that cannot afford many mistakes.

She is not cruel for sport.

She is not soft by accident.

Myra watches rooms carefully. She tracks who speaks, who is ignored, who reaches for wine before answering, who flinches at names, and who pretends not to know what everyone else knows.

She is very good at making herself seem like the least dangerous person in a conversation.

This is usually a lie.

## Durathen Blood

Myra is very much a Durathen.

She inherited the family’s proud poverty, its practical contempt for waste, and its ugly little habit of remembering every debt until the debtor starts to sweat.

She does not romanticize struggle. She would very much prefer wealth, influence, security, and a wardrobe that does not require creative repair. But she also understands that Durathen dignity comes from endurance, not decoration.

Myra believes a small house survives by using every tool available:

- Beauty.    
- Marriage.    
- Records.    
- Household loyalty.    
- Rumor.    
- Ledgers.    
- Timing.    
- Politeness sharp enough to draw blood.    

She does not apologize for this.

Richer houses have been doing the same thing for centuries and calling it tradition.

## Service to Braga and Orin

Myra originally served [[Braga Kharne]] as lady-in-waiting, but the role widened quickly.

Braga’s strength is force, moral clarity, and command presence. Orin’s strength is military authority, blunt judgment, and sheer physical/political gravity.

Neither of them particularly enjoys the small daily machinery of rule.

Myra does.

Or rather, Myra understands that someone must.

She has become the barony’s quiet operator: managing correspondence, organizing household needs, watching accounts, tracking servants, coordinating local concerns, and translating noble stupidity into actionable tasks before it becomes a fire in the pantry.

She is not formally the power behind the chair.

She is the one making sure the chair is paid for, placed correctly, and not being eaten by termites.

## Ambition and Beringer

Myra once threw herself at [[Beringer Amadis]], knowing full well he was likely to become a duke.

It was not girlish fantasy.

It was calculation.

She politely made it clear she was not a fool, that he could keep [[Dame Venom]] as a side attachment, and that Myra herself was unspoiled.

It was bold, transactional, socially dangerous, and very Durathen.

Myra’s ambition does not make her shallow. It makes her honest about what the world rewards. She has seen noblewomen bartered, sidelined, praised, ignored, and ruined. She has simply decided she would rather be the one doing the math.

## Stormarrow and Sarielle Redmarch

Myra was present during the attack on [[Stormarrow]] against [[Theron Sylvanranth]].

During the chaos, [[Sarielle Redmarch]], daughter of [[Sorek Redmarch]], tried to draw attention away from the Baroness. Sarielle was only seventeen: sweet, brave, naive by comparison, and armed with little more than a dagger and courage.

She died fighting one of the Raven Queen’s Raven Knights.

“Fighting” is the word people use because it is kinder than the alternatives.

Sarielle had no real chance.

She stood anyway.

Myra has never quite been the same.

## Survivor’s Guilt

Sarielle’s death weighs heavily on Myra.

The two had formed an unlikely friendship. Sarielle was younger, softer, and more trusting. Myra was sharper, more political, and more aware of how badly the world treats girls who believe courage is enough.

That contrast is part of what hurts.

Myra survived.

Sarielle did not.

Worse, Sarielle died doing something noble, simple, and brave: drawing danger away from someone more important.

Myra has turned that memory over in her mind too many times. She wonders whether she should have seen the danger sooner, moved faster, shouted differently, pulled Sarielle back, taken the risk herself, or done any of the thousand useless things survivors invent after the dead have become untouchable.

Sarielle’s death did not break Myra’s ambition.

It changed its temperature.

There is now something colder beneath it.

## Blue Sylvanranth

If [[Blue Sylvanranth]] is released from entombment, she may become another target of Durathen vengeance.

Myra’s feelings on Blue are likely to be complicated, ugly, and dangerous. Blue may be victim, symbol, complication, or blame-carrier depending on what truth emerges and how closely Myra connects her to the attack on Stormarrow, Theron’s fate, or Sarielle’s death.

Myra is not stupid enough to strike blindly.

But House Durathen does not forget names.

If Myra comes to believe Blue’s survival was purchased with Sarielle’s death, or that Blue represents an unresolved debt from the Raven Queen’s violence, she may become quietly implacable.

Not loud.

Not immediate.

Worse.

## Mara Moffet

[[Mara Moffet]] is the Kharne house mage, and Myra cannot stand her.

This hatred is mutual, personal, and extremely useful at the table.

Mara is intelligent, beautiful, ambitious, and gifted with immense magical talent. She also carries herself like the family princess she clearly believes herself to be. In a just world, according to Mara, she should have a cushy house-mage position somewhere civilized where she could attend balls, mingle with handsome eligible bachelors, wear excellent gowns, and be admired properly.

Instead, she is on the frontier.

Hicksville, with mud.

Mara respects Orin and Braga immensely. That is part of the problem. She knows they are worth serving. She also hates that serving them means enduring a barony where the roads are rude, the weather has opinions, and half the people seem to think formalwear means “the clean boots.”

Myra regards Mara as vain, spoiled, overdecorated, and insufficiently practical.

Mara regards Myra as smug, underbred in presentation, viciously judgmental, and dressed like a tax record learned to ride.

They are both right enough to be irritating.

## The Ride Through the Barony

![[Mara and Myra ready to ride.png|480]]

During a recent ride through the barony, Myra came prepared in Durathen fashion: old boots, riding pants, practical layers, and no illusions about what distance does to vanity.

Mara arrived as if the road were a receiving line.

House colors. Careful presentation. Fashion-forward. Beautifully arranged.

Mara thought she had won.

Then the saddle sores began.

By the end of the ride, Myra had not needed to say much. This was wise, because if she had spoken, it might have become murder.

The incident did not end the rivalry.

It gave Myra ammunition.

Mara, unfortunately, is too proud to let pain become humility.

## Skills and Strengths

### Administration

Myra has a sharp grasp of household management, estate logistics, correspondence, accounts, and the thousand small processes that keep a noble holding functional.

### Social Reading

She is excellent at reading rooms, assessing motives, and spotting weak points in etiquette, hierarchy, and desire.

### Practical Nobility

Myra can present herself beautifully at court and still make sensible choices on the road. This dual competence makes her much more dangerous than a merely decorative noblewoman.

### Ambition

She wants advancement, security, and influence. She is honest enough with herself to know this and disciplined enough to pursue it intelligently.

### Memory

Myra remembers debts, embarrassments, promises, and the names of the dead.

Very Durathen of her.

## Weaknesses

### Survivor’s Guilt

Sarielle Redmarch’s death remains a deep wound. Myra may overreact to situations that echo Stormarrow: young women in danger, noble sacrifices, doomed bravery, or Raven Queen agents.

### Control

Myra prefers situations she can manage. Chaos, magic, battlefield panic, and emotional sincerity all irritate her because they refuse to stay in columns.

### Jealousy

She does not enjoy being outshone, especially by women who possess the same advantages she does plus more money, magic, or social polish.

### Vengeance Instinct

Myra is capable of patience. That does not mean she is capable of forgiveness.

## Relationships

### Braga Kharne

Braga is Myra’s cousin, former mistress in service, and one of the central powers in Myra’s life. Myra respects Braga deeply, even when she thinks Braga is being blunt enough to dent furniture.

### Orin Kharne

Myra respects Orin as a baron and as a force of order, though she likely finds his lack of subtlety exhausting. She understands his value and works hard to keep his barony functioning.

### Edwyn Durathen

Edwyn is Myra’s brother. Their relationship is marked by affection, rivalry, and mutual recognition. He is the charming blade; she is the calculating hand near the ledger.

### Myreth Durathen

Myreth is a warning and an inheritance. Myra understands her aunt’s grief and admires her discipline, but Myreth also shows what happens when vengeance becomes the only surviving language.

### Sarielle Redmarch

Sarielle was Myra’s unlikely friend and remains one of her deepest wounds. Sarielle’s death is not merely a memory. It is a measure Myra uses against herself.

### Mara Moffet

Mara is Myra’s rival in the Kharne household. Their conflict is part class tension, part beauty contest, part competence war, and part frontier misery expressed through clothing choices. It is petty, sharp, funny, and potentially useful.

### Blue Sylvanranth

Blue may become a focus of Myra’s unresolved grief and Durathen vengeance, depending on how her return intersects with the memory of Stormarrow and Sarielle’s death.

## Table Use

Use Myra when the campaign needs:

- A beautiful, ambitious noblewoman who is not an idiot.    
- A competent seneschal keeping heroic chaos from ruining everything.    
- A Durathen perspective inside House Kharne.    
- A survivor of Stormarrow carrying guilt beneath polish.    
- A social rival for Mara Moffet.    
- A potential vengeance angle involving Blue Sylvanranth.    
- A reminder that not all trauma makes people softer. Some of it teaches them to count better.    

## Voice

Myra speaks with controlled politeness, dry precision, and occasional flashes of Durathen steel.

She rarely raises her voice.

She does not need to.

Common lines:

> “That is not a plan. That is a wish with boots.”

> “If we cannot afford dignity, we can at least afford accuracy.”

> “You may dress for the road however you like. The road will comment in its own time.”

> “I remember her as she was. Please do not improve her into a statue.”

> “A debt does not become smaller because the debtor becomes inconvenient.”

> “I do not hate her. I simply resent that magic rewards vanity so generously.”

## GM Notes

Myra should not be played as comic ambition alone.

The comedy is real, especially with Mara.

The ambition is real, especially around Beringer and noble advancement.

But Sarielle’s death is the emotional anchor. Myra survived a moment where a sweeter, younger, braver girl died trying to draw danger away. That has made Myra more practical, more controlled, and more ruthless about not wasting sacrifice.

She is not broken.

She is sharpened.

That is not always better.
