# Duke Glandyr Ryomar

> [!quote] Glandyr Ryomar
> **I was the spare. I remain grateful.**

> [!quote] Sorek Redmarch
> “Reliable. Reluctant. That's why he's dangerous.”

![[duke ravenster.png]]

**Duke of Ravenster**  
**Lord of Ravenhold**  
**Younger brother of King Rimmand II**
**Devout of Heironeous**

Glandyr Ryomar is grumpy, serious, profoundly loyal to Gorundia, and as apolitical as a royal duke can remain without becoming a hermit.

He was born barely after his elder brother and spent his youth close enough to the throne to understand exactly what it cost.

He has never wanted it.

No one in the royal family is happier that [[Rimmand II]] became King.

That does not prevent Glandyr from wondering whether Rimmand should remain King.

---

## The Spare

Glandyr was raised as the Crown's second answer.

He was educated in law, command, court procedure, theology, diplomacy, and succession not because anyone expected him to rule, but because disease, war, assassination, and accident have always been enthusiastic participants in monarchy.

He learned every duty of kingship while being spared its daily performance.

The experience produced neither ambition nor resentment.

It produced relief.

Glandyr prefers decisions with visible consequences: a road secured, a line held, a garrison supplied, a criminal arrested, an enemy placed firmly on the ground. Kingship offers very few such satisfactions. Its victories arrive diluted through compromise, and its mistakes survive for generations.

He does not envy his brother.

He does, however, second-guess him.

Constantly.

---

## Appearance

Glandyr looks more like a veteran commander than a royal prince.

He is broad-shouldered, powerfully built, and carries the accumulated stiffness of old tournament injuries and newer battlefield ones. His face is stern even at rest, with heavy brows, a square jaw, and the deeply unfair expression of a man who appears disappointed before anyone has spoken.

At court, Glandyr dresses correctly and without enthusiasm. In the field, he is usually armored and visibly more comfortable.

He wears the device of Heironeous openly. It is an expression of faith, not decoration.

---

## Temperament

Glandyr is stern, resolute, deeply respected, and somewhat distant.

He is not naturally charming and has never regarded this as a problem requiring correction.

### Core Traits

**Grumpy:**  
Glandyr possesses a low, steady irritation with disorder, delay, ornamental debate, and people who mistake cleverness for usefulness.

**Serious:**  
He is capable of humor, but rarely volunteers to provide it. Vladek frequently provides it for him, usually by making Glandyr angry enough to become entertaining.

**Apolitical by Preference:**  
He avoids factions, salons, influence campaigns, and court games whenever duty permits. Unfortunately, being the King's brother, Duke of Ravenster, husband of Kylesstra, and father of Elspeth means duty rarely permits.

**Loyal:**  
His loyalty is to Gorundia before comfort, reputation, or family pride. That is precisely why his doubts about his brother matter.

**A Man of Action:**  
Glandyr prefers a difficult duty to an elegant theory. When confronted with confusion over lawful command in Ironmere, he entered in strength, stabilized the situation, recognized the person actually holding it together, and deferred the succession question until facts existed.

**Reserved, Not Unfeeling:**  
He cares deeply and displays it through protection, preparation, presence, and the prevention of avoidable stupidity.

---

## Voice and Manner

Glandyr speaks in short, laconic statements with clipped weight: the conversational equivalent of setting down stones.

He does not narrate his reasoning unless someone needs to understand it.

He rarely raises his voice. Public shouting would imply that the other person has not already been given every reasonable opportunity to stop being foolish.

### Typical Habits

- Pauses before answering questions he considers badly framed.
- Looks toward Kylesstra when politics become complicated, not for permission, but because she has usually already found the knife hidden in the wording.
- Remains armored during field conferences.
- Answers the question someone is about to ask if delay would be pointless.
- Gives praise plainly and without decoration.
- Becomes increasingly concise as his temper worsens.

### Typical Phrases

> No.

> That is not command. That is noise.

> Who presently obeys whom?

> If we know, act. If we do not know, find out.

> Your men are fine.

> I did not say I approved. I said it worked.

> Ask Kylesstra. Then return and tell me what she did not say.

---

## Warrior Born

Glandyr is a warrior by instinct, education, and preference.

He was an accomplished tournament knight in his youth and became a reliable field commander rather than a decorative royal champion. He understands the practical mechanics of moving armed men, maintaining discipline, holding territory, and restoring lawful authority after the banners have passed.

He is not a brilliant theoretician in the mold of [[Alistair Amadis]].

He is the commander sent when the Crown requires someone to arrive, determine what is real, and make the situation hold together.

His soldiers respect him because:

- he shares discomfort without romanticizing it;
- he does not spend lives to improve the appearance of a report;
- he gives clear orders;
- he notices whether units are supplied;
- and he does not confuse aggression with courage.

Glandyr enjoys battle more than politics but does not worship war. Violence is a tool he understands exceptionally well. That is not the same as believing every problem deserves it.

---

## Faith in Heironeous

Glandyr is a devout worshipper of [[Heironeous]].

He believes in courage, honorable strength, disciplined service, protection of the weak, and the obligation to stand when retreat would abandon others.

His faith is practical rather than ecstatic.

He prays. He attends observances. He listens seriously to priests. He also expects the faithful to put their boots on and do the work.

His brother's paladinhood fills him with genuine pride.

It also troubles him.

Paladins are trained to recognize evil, commit themselves wholly, and act with moral certainty. Those qualities can save a kingdom.

A king must also govern people who are selfish rather than evil, mistaken rather than corrupt, frightened rather than faithless, and politically necessary despite being personally objectionable.

Glandyr worries that Rimmand's greatest spiritual strength may also become a royal weakness: certainty can end a moral crisis while deepening a political one.

> **A paladin must know where he stands. A king must know where everyone else is standing too.**

---

## King Rimmand II

Glandyr loves his brother.

He is loyal to him.

He has defended his Crown, enforced his commands, and rejected every easy temptation to convert proximity into influence.

He has also begun to question whether Rimmand's continued reign is best for Gorundia.

This is not jealousy. Glandyr does not want the throne and considers avoidance of it one of the great successes of his life.

His concern is rooted in duty:

- the King's moral certainty has hardened;
- the civil war has exposed failures of judgment and confidence;
- the council has become less willing to offer unqualified reassurance;
- and the kingdom may soon require a ruler capable of rebuilding trust rather than merely defeating enemies.

Glandyr became the moderate voice among nobles discussing what could not honestly be called a conspiracy.

There was no settled plot to remove the King.

There were questions.

There were contingency discussions.

There were people asking what duty required if loyalty to the man and loyalty to the kingdom ceased pointing in the same direction.

Glandyr discouraged panic, ambition, and premature action. He also refused to pretend the questions were treason merely because they were painful.

He would be among the last men to move against Rimmand.

That makes the possibility of his doing so considerably more dangerous.

---

## The Crown Prince

Glandyr has quietly attempted to mentor the Crown Prince.

He does not undermine the King, recruit the Prince into a faction, or suggest that succession should come early. His advice is practical and usually delivered while doing something else: inspecting troops, riding, watching a tournament, reviewing a garrison, or enduring court ceremony.

He teaches the Prince:

- that orders continue affecting people after the throne has forgotten giving them;
- that loyalty is not the same as agreement;
- that priests, generals, nobles, and merchants all describe the same kingdom differently;
- that a ruler must distinguish insult from warning;
- and that being correct does not automatically make a decision survivable.

Glandyr is preparing the Prince to rule someday.

Whether he has begun preparing him to rule soon is a question Glandyr has not answered, perhaps even to himself.

---

## Duchess Kylesstra Ryomar

Glandyr genuinely loves [[Kylesstra Ryomar]].

Their marriage is neither a cold political arrangement nor a sentimental fiction maintained for court.

They are an effective partnership because their strengths are different.

Glandyr brings:

- military authority;
- personal credibility;
- decisiveness;
- stability;
- and the ability to make a policy physically real.

Kylesstra brings:

- diplomacy;
- political perception;
- intelligence networks;
- social precision;
- and an alarming ability to identify what everyone in a room wants before they admit wanting it.

Ravenster benefits from both. Glandyr commands respect; Kylesstra inspires devotion and understands how to convert power into durable cooperation.

Their partnership becomes strained when her intelligence work is exposed.

Kylesstra is shifty because someone in their marriage must notice what polite people are concealing. Glandyr accepts that espionage and political maneuvering are necessary instruments of rule.

He is less accepting when those instruments detonate publicly and leave his wife being verbally torn open before the court.

He will not defend an indefensible act merely because Kylesstra committed it.

He will not abandon her to satisfy people who enjoy seeing her bleed.

When Vladek once suggested that Lyosha obtain the Duchess's permission before healing Glandyr, Kylesstra answered:

> Father Lyosha may proceed. My husband rarely requires permission. Only patience.

Glandyr's response was the smallest possible acknowledgment that she was correct.

That is very nearly public affection by their standards.

---

## Elspeth Ryomar

[[Elspeth Ryomar]] is Glandyr and Kylesstra's daughter.

She has repeatedly been placed near [[Beringer Amadis]] as a potential wife.

The possibility is politically attractive:

- Beringer is Lord Marshal;
- he stands near the center of the post-Vogel settlement;
- Elspeth carries royal and ducal blood;
- and the match could bind Beringer visibly to the stable, loyal branch of House Ryomar.

No one knows how much Glandyr understands about the effort.

No one knows whether Kylesstra is merely allowing it, quietly arranging it, or conducting an operation with enough moving parts to require its own campaign map.

Most importantly, no one knows what Glandyr thinks of Beringer as a prospective son-in-law.

He clearly respects Beringer as a soldier and Lord Marshal.

That is not the same standard he would use for the man expected to marry his daughter.

## Duke Vladek Orenic

Glandyr and [[Vladek Orenic]] have been tournament rivals, battlefield allies, political irritants, and public embarrassments to the Crown for most of their adult lives.

They have fought in tourneys.

They have fought beside one another.

They have also fought one another in public without the useful excuse of a tournament.

Their arguments escalate because both men are stubborn, physically fearless, and entirely confident that the other would benefit from being struck once for clarity.

The fistfights are real.

So is the respect.

Vladek thinks Glandyr is too restrained, too serious, and far too willing to let Kylesstra sharpen the political knives.

Glandyr thinks Vladek is loud, reckless, theatrical, and occasionally useful in the manner of siege artillery.

Each trusts the other in battle more than he trusts many men he finds personally agreeable.

During [[When the Council Came to Blows]], their disagreement became physical enough that King Rimmand finally reminded them:

> You are dukes of Gorundia. You will behave like it.

Neither man found this observation especially helpful.

### Their Actual Friendship

Neither is likely to call the other a friend.

Both would respond immediately if the other were in genuine danger.

They communicate affection through insults, competition, unsolicited criticism, and periodic attempts to establish physical superiority despite being far too old and important for the exercise.

---

## Beringer Amadis

Glandyr respects [[Beringer Amadis]].

Beringer is direct, militarily competent, reluctant to turn every success into a claim upon greater power, and willing to stand where the fighting is worst. These are qualities Glandyr understands.

He also recognizes the political gravity gathering around the Lord Marshal.

Beringer may not seek power. That does not prevent other people from arranging themselves around him as though he already possesses it.

The possibility of Beringer marrying Elspeth makes that gravity a family matter.

For now, Glandyr watches.

If he has reached a conclusion, he has not shared it.

---

## Orin Kharne

Glandyr's relationship with [[Orin Kharne]] began with limited patience and has developed into grudging respect.

Orin is violent, unconventional, personally enormous, and frequently surrounded by situations that would look improbable in a sober military report.

He is also loyal to his people and persistent about their welfare.

During operations in Moffet territory, Glandyr knew Orin had repeatedly sought news of his troops. He answered before Orin could ask again:

> Your men are fine.

The statement was brief because the important facts were brief:

- the troops were intact;
- they were supplied;
- their officers remained in command;
- and Glandyr had not spent them carelessly.

It was both a report and a professional courtesy.

---

## The Moffet Situation

Glandyr entered Ironmere in strength and found little organized resistance but considerable confusion over whose commands remained lawful.

He identified [[Quinn Moffet]] as the person actually keeping the region functional.

She possessed the household rolls. Officers answered her. Stores remained accounted for. Local garrisons had not been permitted to invent independent policy.

Glandyr praised her administration and treated her as the heir exercising household authority during her father's absenceâ€”nothing more.

He refused to turn practical necessity into a premature succession declaration.

His report remained exact:

- no body for Baron Ewan Moffet;
- no confirmed prisoner;
- no verified message;
- status unknown.

This is Glandyr at his best: arrive armed, determine who is doing the work, support them without granting more authority than the facts justify, and do not let uncertainty become an excuse for disorder.

---

## House Vogel Blood

Glandyr and Rimmand are sons of Allynnora Vogel Ryomar and therefore carry the blood of [[House Vogel]] through their mother.

That connection has become newly uncomfortable after Wilhelm's treason and the exposure of concealed Vogel crimes.

Glandyr neither denies the blood nor permits it to define his loyalty.

He has little patience for claims that ancestry absolves guilt or creates it.

In his view, blood explains obligations.

It does not answer them.

---

## Current Position

Glandyr remains one of Gorundia's most reliable senior nobles.

He is loyal to the Crown, active in the field, deeply respected in Ravenster, and increasingly unable to avoid the political questions he has spent his life preferring other people answer.

He does not want to be King.

He does not want his brother removed.

He does not want his wife exposed, his daughter used, the Prince unprepared, Ravenster destabilized, or Gorundia ruled by certainty unsupported by judgment.

He may not be permitted to keep all of those desires.

---

## Glandyr's Traits

### In Council He Will:

- Speak rarely.
- Ask what is known and who is actually obeying whom.
- Let Kylesstra handle implications until action is required.
- Oppose dramatic measures unless delay has become more dangerous.
- Never make him sound hungry for the throne.

### In the Field He Will:

- Remain armored.
- Notice supply, command integrity, and exhausted soldiers.
- Give blunt reassurance when reassurance is supported by fact.
- Act before explaining when lives are immediately at stake.

### Under Pressure He Will:

- Become quieter.
- Make shorter statements.
- Look personally offended by foolishness.
- If Vladek is responsible, allow diplomacy one fair opportunity before somebody moves the furniture.

### What He Wants

- Gorundia stable.
- Ravenster secure.
- The Prince prepared.
- His brother worthy of continued loyalty.
- Kylesstra safe without requiring her to become harmless.
- Elspeth treated as a daughter rather than a treaty with hair.
- To remain Duke of Ravenster and never, ever become King.

### What He Fears

- That loyalty to Rimmand and loyalty to Gorundia will finally become incompatible.
- That his moderation merely postpones an unavoidable decision.
- That Kylesstra knows more about the royal crisis than she has told him.
- That the Prince will be forced to inherit before he is ready.
- That everyone assumes the spare must secretly want the crown.
- That someone will decide he is the solution.

---

## Summary

Glandyr Ryomar is the spare who sincerely thanks Heironeous for sparing him.

He is a warrior born, a reluctant political figure, a loyal brother with dangerous doubts, a loving husband to a woman considerably shiftier than he is, and the father of a daughter being maneuvered toward the most politically significant unmarried soldier in Gorundia.

He does not seek power.

He seeks something much less attainable:

A kingdom governed well enough that he can remain outside, in armor, doing useful work.
