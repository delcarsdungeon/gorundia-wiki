# Avelyne Marsten

![[Avelyne Marsten.png|220]]

> [!quote] Cwenheld, Venerated Shield of Heironeous
> I carried Beringer out with my own two hands, covered in blood — his and theirs. Whatever happened in that villa was no accident, but I know Galmyr wasn’t behind it. He trusted Avelyne too much, cared for her too deeply. No... this was something darker, something crueler. I arrived too late to save her, but by Heironeous’ grace, I saved the boy.

## Overview

**Full Name:** Avelyne Marsten  
**House:** [[House Marsten]]  
**Status:** Deceased  
**Known For:** Warrior, man-at-arms, companion of [[Galmyr Vogel]], mother of [[Beringer|Beringer]]  
**Companions:** [[Cwenheld]], [[Galmyr Vogel]], [[Theravain the Ashkeeper]]  
**Child:** [[Beringer|Beringer]]  
**Death:** [[Death of Avelyne Marsten]]  
**Related Event:** [[Origin of Beringers Curse]]  

Avelyne Marsten was a low-born adventurer who later earned the Marsten name and title through service, steel, and reputation. She became one of the few women to serve as a man-at-arms in the orbit of [[House Vogel]].

Known for strength, discipline, and fierce loyalty, she was a natural leader on and off the battlefield. Over time, she became a trusted confidant, protector, and companion of [[Galmyr Vogel]], standing beside him in times of war and peace alike.

Their relationship went beyond duty and led to the birth of their son, [[Beringer|Beringer]].

To keep Avelyne and their son safe, Galmyr arranged for them to live in a secluded villa with a small household of loyal servants, far from the political intrigues of Castle Direhold.

The choice was meant as protection.

It became the site of one of the defining crimes of Beringer’s life.

## Appearance

No complete formal portrait of Avelyne is currently preserved in the active record.

From accounts of those who knew her, she was strong, athletic, and carried herself like a trained fighter rather than a court noble. She was remembered less for ornament than for presence: direct, capable, and hard to frighten.

The recovered vision of [[Villa Marsten]] shows her in her final moments barefoot, bloodied, and wearing only a torn summer dress, still fighting despite catastrophic injury.

That image has become one of the central recovered fragments of her story.

## Personality

Avelyne was disciplined, loyal, and physically courageous. She had the hard practicality of someone who earned her place rather than inherited it, and she seems to have carried that fact without apology.

She appears to have inspired deep trust among those who fought beside her. [[Cwenheld]] considered her a sister in arms and closest friend. [[Galmyr Vogel]] trusted her, loved her, and fathered a child with her. Cwenheld later rejected the idea that he could have knowingly ordered Avelyne’s death or endangered their son.

Avelyne was not a passive victim of politics.

She was a warrior caught by treachery.

Even surprised, unarmored, and alone, she fought like one.

## The Company of the Red Hills

In her youth, Avelyne was part of a small adventuring company with:

- [[Cwenheld]] — knight of Heironeous
- [[Galmyr Vogel]] — rising knight of [[House Vogel]]
- [[Theravain the Ashkeeper]] — elven wizard and mentor
- Avelyne herself — noble warrior of [[House Marsten]]

Their greatest known battle was against [[Ashmaw of the Red Hills]], a red dragon that attempted to claim the upland ruins later remembered as Ashfall Ruins.

The fight destroyed the company.

[[Theravain the Ashkeeper]] died countering dragonfire directly. [[Cwenheld]] was permanently scarred and partially blinded holding the line under the dragon’s breath. Galmyr and Avelyne exploited the dragon’s grounding and helped bring the beast down.

Avelyne survived the dragon.

The company did not survive the cost.

## Relationship with Galmyr Vogel

Avelyne and [[Galmyr Vogel]] became lovers after the battle with [[Ashmaw of the Red Hills]].

Their bond appears to have been genuine. Cwenheld, who knew them both, believed strongly that Galmyr loved Avelyne and would never have knowingly endangered her or their son.

Avelyne bore Galmyr a son, [[Beringer|Beringer]].

The secrecy surrounding Beringer was not merely scandal management. It was protection. A Vogel-blooded child born outside the expected channels of power was dangerous to too many people, whether as threat, tool, embarrassment, or sacrifice.

## Villa Marsten

Avelyne was hidden at [[Villa Marsten]] with her infant son and a small household of loyal servants.

The villa was secluded, distant from the political machinery of Castle Direhold, and intended to keep both mother and child safe.

The attack came anyway.

When [[Cwenheld]] arrived, she found the estate wrong: quiet, bloodless outside, and full of dead servants whose bodies showed no ordinary wounds. Inside, Avelyne had been attacked by twelve black-armored figures with no heraldry.

Avelyne had already killed four of them.

She was barefoot, bloodied, badly wounded, and still fighting.

> [!quote] Cwenheld, Venerated Shield of Heironeous
> Avelyne never stopped fighting — not once, not ever. When I found her, she was barefoot in a simple dress, surrounded by the bodies of the men who came to kill her. They sent a dozen. Caught by surprise, unarmored and alone, she killed four before they could lay her low. I made sure the rest never saw the light of day again.

Cwenheld arrived too late to save Avelyne.

She did save the child.

## The Villa Incident

Avelyne’s life came to a violent end in that quiet villa.

When Cwenheld arrived, she found blood, bodies, and the remains of a carefully arranged massacre. Avelyne and the servants had been slain. The infant [[Beringer|Beringer]] was still alive.

Cwenheld fought her way out, cutting down the remaining attackers. At the time, she believed they were Vogel retainers sent to silence the boy forever.

> [!quote] Cwenheld, Venerated Shield of Heironeous
> It wasn’t battle — it was butchery. I’ve seen enough slaughter to know the difference. Someone wanted to erase every trace of Avelyne, but they underestimated my will. I would have carried that boy through the Nine Hells if I had to.

Despite the carnage, Cwenheld firmly believed [[Galmyr Vogel]] had no part in the atrocity. His absence, combined with the villa’s remote location and his known care for Avelyne, suggested he had been kept in the dark.

If blame were to be laid within House Vogel, older suspicion pointed toward [[Zanara Vogel]], Wilhelm Vogel’s mother, whose ruthless ambition was already well known.

That was the first theory.

It was not the whole truth.

## The Recovered Vision

Later recovered vision-material concerning [[Origin of Beringers Curse]] revealed details Cwenheld did not fully understand at the time.

The black-armored figures were not acting like ordinary assassins. They moved with ritual precision, silent and coordinated. After Avelyne fell, they appeared to reset the scene like stagehands.

The recovered vision also showed a raven-cloaked woman draining a faint silvery light from Avelyne as she died. The same woman carried infant [[Beringer|Beringer]] into the nursery and placed him within a twisted silver ring.

The ring ignited with harsh silver light.

This appears to be the origin point of Beringer’s curse.

Avelyne’s death was not merely an assassination.

It was part of a ritual.

## Cwenheld’s Arrival

[[Cwenheld]] had received a divine warning two nights before the massacre. She understood it not as a dream, but as a command and a gift from Heironeous.

She rode for the villa with such urgency that her horse died near the final approach.

She continued on foot.

When she reached the villa, she did not use the door.

She broke through the wall.

Inside, she found Avelyne dying, the black-armored figures present, and the infant still alive in the nursery. Cwenheld fought through the remaining attackers and carried Beringer away.

> [!quote] Cwenheld, Venerated Shield of Heironeous
> I would have carried that boy through the Nine Hells if I had to.

## Older Suspicion

At the time, Cwenheld believed [[Galmyr Vogel]] was innocent of the massacre.

His trust in Avelyne, his care for her, his absence from the villa, and the hidden nature of the arrangement all suggested to Cwenheld that he had been kept in the dark.

Older suspicion pointed toward [[Zanara Vogel]], Wilhelm Vogel’s mother, whose ruthlessness and ambition were well known.

This remains important as historical context.

However, later recovered vision-material complicates the matter. The raven-cloaked woman, the silver-ring ritual, and the extraction of power from Avelyne indicate that the massacre was tied to a deeper supernatural design.

The older political suspicion may still matter.

It may simply not be the whole answer.

## Legacy

Though her life ended in tragedy, Avelyne’s legacy endured through her son, [[Beringer|Beringer]].

He inherited her strength, courage, and refusal to yield. Raised by others and denied the truth of his origins for much of his life, he nevertheless carried much of her spirit forward.

To those who knew her, Avelyne was not merely Galmyr’s lover or Beringer’s mother. She was a warrior who stood beside dragon-slayers, fought for those she loved, and died resisting something darker than ordinary murder.

Her death is one of the central hidden wounds beneath Beringer’s life.

Her final act was resistance.

## Table Use

Use Avelyne when the campaign needs:

- The human heart of Beringer’s hidden origin.
- A link between [[Galmyr Vogel]], [[House Vogel]], and [[Beringer|Beringer]].
- A personal cost behind the Raven Queen’s designs.
- A reason Cwenheld’s loyalty to Beringer is absolute.
- A reminder that Beringer’s curse began with his mother’s murder, not abstract destiny.
- A grounded heroic figure whose death still matters.

## GM Notes

Avelyne should not be reduced to “Beringer’s mother.”

She was a warrior, dragon-slayer, lover, and trusted companion before she became a victim of the Villa Marsten ritual.

Her death should feel like a crime with layers:

- political assassination
- ritual sacrifice
- curse origin
- cover-up
- family wound
- divine intervention arriving moments too late

Cwenheld’s early belief that Galmyr was innocent should remain emotionally important. She knew both Avelyne and Galmyr personally, and her judgment came from trust, grief, and battlefield intimacy.

The recovered vision does not erase that belief.

It widens the conspiracy.
