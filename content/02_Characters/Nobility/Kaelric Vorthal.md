## Overview

Baron Kaelric Vorthal is the young lord of [[Barony Rystwell]], a Ravenster noble of excellent reputation, disciplined habits, and inconvenient moral clarity. He is often described as _a beautiful man_, which would normally be enough to make half the court insufferable and the other half stupid. Somehow, Kaelric avoided both outcomes.

He is handsome without being delicate, refined without being ornamental, and noble without treating rank like divine permission to be useless. His beauty is the first thing people notice. His character is what they remember after the room stops whispering.

Kaelric believes lordship is not a prize, but a burden accepted in public. He has little patience for nobles who treat authority as inheritance without obligation. To him, a title is a debt owed downward: to tenants, soldiers, sworn men, family, and allies.

When word reached him that [[Sarielle Redmarch]] had died in the same attack that slew [[Theron Sylvanranth]], Kaelric dropped everything. He informed his liege, gathered his best men, and rode for [[Stormarrow]] without waiting for the neat little approvals that make cowards feel tidy. Lord’s Muster, courtly propriety, political optics — all of it took second place to family, duty, and the fact that his wife’s family had been struck.

He came to secure the Redmarch household, comfort the grieving, and help [[Orin Kharne]] in every way he could. Not as a grandstanding savior. Not as a man trying to turn tragedy into reputation. As kin, ally, and lord.

That choice has done more to define him than any court appearance.

---

## Appearance

Kaelric is striking enough that even restrained courtiers tend to stumble into poetry when describing him. Tall, well-proportioned, and graceful without seeming fragile, he has the kind of face bards overuse and sculptors lie about. His features are clean, symmetrical, and warm rather than coldly perfect: dark-lashed eyes, a strong mouth, clear skin, and a bearing that suggests discipline rather than vanity.

He does not dress like a peacock. No jeweled nonsense, no perfume-cloud entrance, no embroidered costume begging for applause from people with too much wine and too few responsibilities. His clothing is excellent, fitted, and soberly rich, favoring the colors of [[Barony Rystwell]]: deep red, dark leather-brown, polished steel, and muted noble gold.

In the field, Kaelric wears full plate in his barony colors. The armor is finely made and clearly expensive, but it is war gear first and pageantry second: burnished steel, layered plates, a deep red cloak, dark leather strapping, and a heraldic badge worn at the breast. The effect is devastatingly unfair to every unmarried noblewoman within line of sight, and therefore naturally he is already married and does not fool around. Cue the weeping, gnashing of teeth, and tragic fluttering of fans among the hopeful. Civilization is built on suffering, apparently.

His sword and helmet are practical and well-kept. His armor bears field wear, not neglect: scratches, polish, repaired edges, and the marks of a lord who actually rides with his men instead of letting painters invent his courage later.

His smile is bright, almost boyish, and dangerously misleading. Men have mistaken it for innocence. A few have regretted that.

## Personality

Kaelric is honorable in the active sense, not the decorative one. He does not merely speak well of duty. He inconveniences himself for it, which is how one can tell the disease has progressed beyond courtly performance.

He is courteous, attentive, and careful with rank, but he will not hide behind manners when action is required. He has the Ravenster instinct for disciplined order, tempered by the Redmarch influence of blunt practical decency. He can sit through council, understand supply, listen to soldiers, comfort grieving kin, and still put his hand on a sword when the room turns ugly.

He is not soft. He is kind.

Humanity, being a species determined to confuse the two, often requires clarification.

Kaelric has courage, but not the frothing kind. He is no swaggering glory-hound desperate for a mural. He does not seek danger to prove himself. The problem is worse: when danger presents itself, he tends to walk toward it because someone should. That is exactly the sort of habit that gets men remembered in songs and buried under expensive stone.

He is a decent swordsman, a capable commander, and not an idiot. Unfortunately, none of those traits prevent gallant death. They mostly make it look better from a distance.

---

## Noble Philosophy

Kaelric’s view of nobility can be summarized simply:

A lord eats last, retreats last, and explains himself first.

He believes rank exists to organize responsibility. Nobles are given land, deference, command, and wealth because they are expected to carry danger before it reaches those beneath them. If a noble cannot do that, Kaelric privately considers the title a costume.

He does not speak this doctrine loudly. He simply acts on it, which is much more annoying to worse nobles because it makes their excuses harder to decorate.

He is not radical. He is not trying to overturn the noble order. He believes in hierarchy, oath, inheritance, and duty. But he believes the higher step should be the heavier one. In Ravenster, that makes him admirable. In less functional courts, that makes him inconvenient.

---

## Military Role

[[Barony Rystwell]] is known for disciplined infantry and reliable service to [[Duchy Ravenster]]. Kaelric fits that mold cleanly. He is not the most dangerous swordsman in Gorundia, nor does he pretend to be. His strength is steadiness under pressure.

He understands shieldwalls, patrol discipline, road security, signal chains, and the difference between a brave charge and a stupid one. He listens to sergeants. He remembers names. He knows which men are brave, which are tired, which are lying, and which need to be moved before they break.

On campaign, he prefers controlled action over theatrics. He is willing to take risks, but he does not waste men for spectacle. That said, if the choice is between preserving his own escape and standing with soldiers about to be overrun, Kaelric’s instincts become painfully clear.

He stands with his men.

This is admirable.

It is also how bards get material.

## The Stormarrow Response

When Kaelric heard that [[Sarielle Redmarch]] had died in the attack where [[Theron Sylvanranth]] was slain, he did not wait for perfect information. He did not convene a polite breakfast council. He did not send a beautifully phrased letter expressing concern while secretly hoping someone else dealt with the blood and screaming.

He informed his liege lord, mustered his best available men, and rode for [[Stormarrow]].

The act carried political risk. [[Stormarrow]] was already tangled in Lord’s Muster politics, military crisis, and the dangerous orbit of [[Orin Kharne]]. A lesser noble might have waited, citing propriety. A more ambitious one might have calculated who would see him arrive.

Kaelric cared about one thing first: his wife’s family had been struck.

He came to secure the Redmarch household, comfort the grieving, and assist [[Orin Kharne]] in every way he could. Not as a grandstanding savior. Not as a man trying to turn grief into reputation. As kin, ally, and lord.

That matters.

To [[Sorek Redmarch]], it matters more than Kaelric’s title ever could.

## Relationship with Valaera Redmarch

Kaelric is married to [[Valaera Redmarch]], now Baroness Vorthal of [[Barony Rystwell]]. Their marriage is widely regarded as a good match, though court gossips initially focused far too much on his face and her Redmarch blood, because apparently grown adults require hobbies.

Their marriage binds [[House Vorthal]] to the Redmarch family and strengthens Kaelric’s informal ties to [[Stormarrow]], [[Sorek Redmarch]], and the broader circle of military loyalists who respect deeds more than pedigree.

Kaelric treats Valaera as partner, counsel, and family, not ornament. He values her judgment, especially where Redmarch instincts and courtly restraint collide. He is notably faithful, which has caused visible disappointment among several ambitious single noblewomen who had mistaken beauty for availability. This is what comes of letting hope near aristocracy.

The marriage is also part of why Sorek trusts him. Sorek does not require his son-in-law to be perfect. He requires him to be decent when no one is watching and useful when everything is falling apart.

Kaelric has cleared that bar.

So far.

---

## Relationship with Sarielle Redmarch

[[Sarielle Redmarch]] was Kaelric’s sister-in-law, and he appears to have taken that bond seriously even before tragedy made it urgent.

Sarielle died in the attack where [[Theron Sylvanranth]] was slain. When word reached Kaelric, he reacted as family. No delay. No polite distance. No careful phrasing about jurisdiction. He went to [[Stormarrow]] because the Redmarch family had been wounded and because his place, as he understood it, was with them.

This response has quietly reshaped how many people view him. Before, Kaelric was admired as a handsome and honorable young baron. Afterward, he became something more dangerous to cynics: evidence that some nobles still mean the words they say in chapel.

---

## Relationship with Sorek Redmarch

Sorek approves of Kaelric in the way Sorek approves of anything: grudgingly, sharply, and with enough insults to keep the furniture from getting sentimental.

But beneath the growl, Sorek is deeply pleased with the match. For Sorek, his daughter marrying well does not mean marrying high. It means marrying someone who will stand steady when the weather turns murderously political. Kaelric has done that.

Sorek respects that Kaelric loves [[Valaera Redmarch]] without trying to possess her, respects the Redmarch family without using them, and showed up for [[Sarielle Redmarch]] when it mattered.

His concern is not that Kaelric is false.

His concern is that Kaelric is true in exactly the way that gets good men killed.

Sorek knows the type. He has buried the type. He may have been the type before age and scars made him meaner and harder to fit into a coffin.

### Sorek’s Private Assessment

> _“He is not chasing glory. That would be easier. Glory-hounds can be tripped, mocked, or drunk under the table until the mood passes. Kaelric is worse. He sees a fire and starts counting who is still inside.”_  
> — [[Sorek Redmarch]]

---

## Relationship with Orin Kharne

Kaelric treats [[Orin Kharne]] with respect, even if Orin’s methods, reputation, and general aura of “baronial battering ram” are not always easy for polite nobles to process.

He does not need Orin to be refined. He needs him to be honest, effective, and loyal to those under his protection. Kaelric can work with blunt men. In some ways, he prefers them. Blunt men waste less time pretending their knives are paperweights.

Kaelric’s arrival at Stormarrow is also a statement of trust: he is willing to aid Orin directly rather than stand at a safe distance and let court rumor decide the shape of events.

That trust may matter later.

---

## Reputation

Among Ravenster nobles, Kaelric is admired as a model young baron: devout without being sanctimonious, brave without being loud, courteous without being weak. Younger knights look up to him. Older commanders watch him with the wary fondness reserved for promising men who have not yet learned how expensive virtue can be.

Among common soldiers, his reputation is simpler.

He stands close.

That buys loyalty faster than heraldry.

Among court gossips, he remains “the beautiful Baron Vorthal,” a phrase Kaelric tolerates with polite discomfort. People who know him better tend to use different words: steady, honest, brave, decent.

Among unmarried noblewomen, he is a small social tragedy in full plate: beautiful, titled, faithful, and already spoken for. The capital has endured famines with less theatrical despair.

Sorek uses several kinder words for him, though usually after calling him a damned fool first.

---

## Public Manner

Kaelric speaks with calm precision and rarely raises his voice. He is good at making people feel seen without seeming theatrical about it. In tense rooms, he listens first, asks one or two clean questions, and then acts.

He does not posture. He does not enjoy humiliating people. When he rebukes someone, it tends to be direct and quiet, which is often worse.

He has a talent for making recklessness look like grace because he does not rush toward danger wildly. He walks toward it as if he has already accepted the cost and is now merely keeping the appointment.

Deeply inconsiderate behavior, frankly.

---

## Combat Style

Kaelric is a competent, trained noble swordsman. He is not a legendary duelist, but he is dangerous enough that dismissing him is a mistake.

He fights with clean fundamentals: shield discipline, measured cuts, controlled footwork, and good battlefield awareness. In full plate, he is steady and patient, trusting the armor without becoming lazy inside it. He favors formation fighting and command presence over dueling flourishes. In personal combat, he stays calm, protects his line, and waits for overextension.

He is courageous under missile fire, steady under cavalry pressure, and unlikely to panic if outnumbered.

This makes him useful.

It also means he may volunteer for holding actions, rearguards, rescue pushes, and other heroic nonsense that makes priests sigh and widows rich in condolences.

## Notable Scene: The Bluff at Rystwell

The tide came in under gray canvas.

Low, broad-beamed cutters slid toward the shingle with oars muffled in leather. No painted prows. No roaring theatrics. Just men in dark mail and boiled leather, shields lacquered plain, helms unadorned.

Professionals.

Smoke rose first — tight columns from signal barns along the coast — then the distant crack of a watchtower bell cut short.

Baron Kaelric Vorthal was already riding when the second bell died.

He had a dozen with him: border patrol, light mail, no banner flying. Only he and his squire were mounted; the rest had marched the ridgeline to keep the shore in sight. When they crested the bluff, they saw the landing party fanning out with practiced spacing.

Too many.

The sergeant’s voice stayed level.

“My lord. You and the boy can break through. Ravenhold needs warning. We’ll buy the time.”

Kaelric’s red cloak snapped once in the wind and settled.

He leaned down from the saddle and clasped the sergeant’s shoulder — hard, grateful.

Then he dismounted.

“I stand with my men.”

He didn’t raise his voice.

He didn’t have to.

For a heartbeat he rested his brow against his horse’s. The animal blew warm breath into the cold air. Kaelric’s gloved hand lingered at its cheek.

“Run true,” he whispered.

He pressed the reins into his squire’s trembling grip.

“Ride like the wind. Don’t look back. Sound the alert. Tell them we held.”

“My lord—”

“That’s an order.”

The boy wheeled and spurred downslope, hooves hammering through frost and stone, vanishing into the treeline.

Kaelric turned back to the advancing line — shields lifting in unison, crossbows coming up behind them.

He drew his sword.

Clean steel caught the light.

Around him, Ravenster infantry locked shields.

The enemy advanced at a measured pace, silent but for boots on gravel.

Baron Kaelric Vorthal stepped forward to close the gap between himself and his footmen.

And as the first volley snapped toward them, he smiled — bright, fearless, almost boyish — while the line braced for impact.

## Roleplaying Kaelric

Use Kaelric as a noble who makes goodness feel active rather than naive.

He should not feel like a saint. Saints are marble and usually boring until they start bleeding from the eyes. Kaelric is a living man: warm, tired, controlled, sometimes too willing to carry burdens himself.

### At the Table

- He listens before speaking.
- He does not posture unless ceremony requires it.
- He gives orders cleanly and expects them followed.
- He thanks common soldiers by name when he can.
- He notices fear and does not mock it.
- He accepts danger as part of rank.
- He dislikes cruelty disguised as necessity.
- He can be embarrassed by compliments, especially about his appearance.
- He becomes very still when someone threatens family.
- He is courteous to flirtatious attention, but does not encourage it.
- He does not fool around.

### Speech Style

Kaelric speaks formally but warmly. He avoids florid court nonsense unless trapped in ceremony. His best lines should be plain, because plain words from a noble who means them land harder.

Examples:

> “My rank does not place me above this. It places me first.”

> “If my men stand there, then I stand there.”

> “Comfort is not weakness. It is what we owe the wounded before asking them to be brave again.”

> “I was told there were reasons to wait. I found none better than family.”

> “If this was improper, I will answer for it after the living are safe.”

> “My lady, you honor me. But my heart is already well housed.”

## GM Notes

Kaelric is best used as a contrast to corrupt, ornamental, or self-protective nobility.

He proves the system can produce genuinely good lords, which makes the bad ones more damning. He also creates tension because his virtues are real but dangerous. He is brave enough to inspire, honest enough to trust, and gallant enough to get himself killed in a meaningful scene if no one stops him.

Do not play him as foolish. He is not a golden retriever in a coronet. He understands risk. He simply believes some risks belong to him.

That is what makes him noble.

That is also what makes Sorek drink.

### Use Him For

- Reinforcing that Ravenster nobility can be genuinely honorable.
- Giving Sorek a son-in-law he actually respects.
- Showing how Sarielle’s death ripples through allied families.
- Providing Orin a noble ally who is decent without being politically useless.
- Creating future battlefield stakes: if Kaelric is present, he may volunteer for the dangerous honorable choice.
- Making players protect a good man from his own damn standards.
- Giving court flirts and scandal-mongers a beautiful target who politely refuses to become interesting in the way they prefer.

## Summary

Baron Kaelric Vorthal is a young Ravenster noble of rare beauty, real courage, and deeply inconvenient decency. Husband to [[Valaera Redmarch]], son-in-law to [[Sorek Redmarch]], and brother-in-law to the late [[Sarielle Redmarch]], he has proven through action that he treats family and duty as obligations, not ornaments.

He is honorable, brave, capable, sincere, and already married.

Naturally, this means the universe, several bards, and at least one scandalous court beauty are all sharpening knives.
