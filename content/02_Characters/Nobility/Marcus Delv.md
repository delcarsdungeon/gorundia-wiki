**Born:** 1189 OR [br]  
**House:** Minor gentry (service lineage, no significant lands) [br]  
**Rank (Former):** Ducal Road Marshal (Medallion Appointee) [br]  
**Current Title:** _Styled_ Baron of the Three Fields [br]

**Children:** Two (kept clear of the current unrest) [br]  
**Allegiance:** Crown in principle; authority in practice [br]

---

## **Overview**

Marcus Delv was one of Vogel’s **medallion officers**—a man whose authority came from function, not blood. When the system broke, he didn’t.

He kept the roads open.  
He kept grain moving.  
He kept men paid and patrols active.

When neighboring officials vanished—killed, fled, or simply stopped acting—Delv absorbed their duties without ceremony. Villages stabilized. Banditry dropped. Trade resumed in narrow, controlled lanes.

At some point, the people began calling him _Baron_.

He pushed back—once.

Then he realized the truth:

> The medallion meant nothing anymore.  
> The work still did.

He accepted the title not as elevation—but as **translation**.  
Something people could understand. Something that carried weight.

---

## **Presentation**

Marcus Delv presents like a hardened field officer who never expected to sit at the head of a table.

- Functional armor, maintained but not ornate
- Clean, direct posture—no wasted motion
- Speaks plainly, listens fully, decides quickly

He does not perform nobility.

He **embodies authority**.

Among the people:

> But you’ll eat after he gives it.”

Among soldiers:

> “You do your job, he does his. That’s enough.”

Among nobles:

> “If this stands, we’ve all got a problem.”

---

## **Leadership Style**

Delv leads like a veteran NCO forced into command:

- Clear orders
- Immediate enforcement
- No tolerance for drift once direction is set

### Strengths:

- Decisive under pressure
- Deep understanding of logistics (roads, grain, movement)
- Educated enough to grasp nuance and second-order effects

### Weakness:

- When the moment for action comes, he does not hesitate
- Can outpace politics—and patience

---

## **The Title Problem**

Delv does not particularly value the title of Baron.

But he understands what it does.

The Vogel medallions represented authority through centralized control  
That system is gone.

So he replaced it with something functional:

> A visible authority who answers when things go wrong.

### His stance:

> “Call me what you need to call me.  
> Just don’t pretend no one’s in charge.”

---

## **Forces**

Delv commands a disciplined, compact force:

- Surviving ducal troops (medallion chain remnants)
- Absorbed retainers from collapsed minor holdings
- Volunteers who chose stability over uncertainty

### Traits:

- Well-fed and regularly supplied
- Paid (rare, and noticed)
- Loyal to Delv personally

They fight like men holding a line that exists **because they hold it**.

---

## **Relationship: Olessar Veyne**

Brother-in-law, advisor, and quiet force multiplier.

Where Delv acts, Olessar **anticipates**.

- Intelligence gathering
- Early warning networks
- Counterspell support

Delv does not micromanage the magic.

He trusts outcomes.

> “If Olessar says something’s coming, it’s already late.”

## **Hooks & Use**

### **The Recognition**

Delv expects acknowledgment—not reward.

If Beringer legitimizes him, Delv becomes an anchor.

If he’s dismissed, he becomes a problem.

---

### **The Third Line**

In battle, Delv’s forces don’t charge—they **stabilize**.

He imposes order mid-chaos, which can disrupt both sides.

---

### **The Transfer of Authority**

Delv is ready—quietly—to hand control over.

But only if he believes the man taking it will not waste what’s been built.
