# Dame Quinn Moffet

> [!quote] Quinn Moffet
> **“We are Moffet. We will hold.”**

>[!quote] Sorek Redmarch
>The other Moffets get to be interesting because Quinn is busy being reliable. Guess which is harder.

![[Quinn moffet 2.png|340]]


**Eldest daughter of Baron Ewan Moffet**  
**Heir of House Moffet**  
**Knight of Ironmere**  
**Wife of Sir Kersan Hes, Master of the Shield**

Quinn Moffet is the serious one.

Every family has one eventually.

House Moffet produced theirs first.

Quinn was the eldest child, the expected example, the first subjected to every new parental fear, and the one upon whom duty settled before anyone had learned to disguise its weight as affection.

She believes Mara was spoiled.

She believes Bertram is a dreamer whose head spends too much time in the clouds.

She also loves them both and would consider anyone outside the family repeating either judgment to be volunteering for an educational experience.

---

## The Serious Moffet

People call Quinn **the Serious Moffet**.

They do not generally say it as an insult.

Mara is gifted, vivid, and emotionally transparent by Moffet standards. Bertram was idealistic enough to imagine that personal honor and a sufficiently dramatic gesture could solve problems built by generations of compromise.

Quinn learned early that someone still had to:

- inspect the stores;
- remember the names of officers;
- review the rolls;
- answer petitions;
- determine which wall required repair;
- and remain present after the speeches ended.

She became serious because seriousness kept people fed, armed, paid, and alive.

The name carries respect.

It also quietly denies her permission to be anything else.

---

## Appearance

Quinn has the solid, practical bearing of a knight who expects armor to be used rather than admired.

She is tall enough to carry a greatsword without the weapon appearing theatrical, broad through the shoulders, and conditioned by years of riding, drill, and field command. She favors her dark hair secured firmly away from her face. Any style requiring maintenance after putting on a helmet has already failed her review.

Her features are strong rather than delicate. At rest she appears stern; while concentrating she appears actively displeased. This has ended many unnecessary conversations before they began.

Quinn wears House Moffet's black and gold without embellishment. Her armor is well maintained, visibly used, and repaired instead of replaced whenever repair remains honorable.

She does not dress poorly.

She dresses as though every article must justify the trouble of carrying it.

---

## Temperament

Quinn is disciplined, blunt, watchful, and deeply protective.

She is not humorless. Her humor is dry, private, and most likely to surface around people she trusts enough not to mistake it for weakness.

### Core Traits

**Responsible:**  
Quinn assumes unattended obligations will eventually become her problem. Experience has rewarded this pessimism often enough to make correction difficult.

**Reserved:**  
She does not display family pain for public consumption. House Moffet may quarrel behind stone walls. Outside them, it stands as one.

**Judgmental:**  
Quinn forms opinions quickly and revises them slowly. She is usually fair, but fairness does not require pretending foolish behavior was wise.

**Protective:**  
Her severity is built around shelter. She wants the walls sound, the shield line closed, the household intact, and vulnerable people placed somewhere the enemy must pass through her to reach.

**Promising Leader:**  
Quinn does not inspire through brilliance or theatrical charisma. She inspires confidence that someone competent has noticed the problem and will still be present tomorrow.

**Privately Resentful:**  
A small, durable part of her remembers that her parents learned gentleness on the younger children after practicing expectations on her.

She is not proud of this resentment.

She has also not entirely forgiven it.

---

## Voice and Manner

Quinn speaks clearly, formally, and without wasted motion.

She does not use five words where three will hold the line.

In public she refers to family members correctly, acknowledges lawful authority precisely, and never gives observers the satisfaction of seeing internal strain.

Her anger becomes colder rather than louder.

### Habits

- Checks exits, guards, and lines of approach without appearing to do so.
- Reads reports completely before asking questions.
- Keeps her hands still when others become emotional.
- Uses titles in public even when furious with the person holding one.
- Corrects rumors about her family without volunteering private truth.
- Stands when delivering bad news.
- Rarely promises that everything will be fine.
- Often promises that something will be done.

### Typical Phrases

> “Stand.”

> “We do not need panic. We need numbers.”

> “That is a family matter.”

> “My sister is capable. That was not the question.”

> “Bertram meant well.”

> A pause.

> “Someone usually had to.”

> “No succession has been declared.”

> “We are Moffet. We will hold.”

## Knight of Ironmere

Quinn was trained according to the military traditions of [[House Moffet]].

Those traditions descend from the house's decisive action at [[The Battle of Red Gorge]], where disciplined withdrawal, terrain, concealed knights, and a fatal counterstroke shattered the Dominion's flank.

Moffet warfare prizes:

- formation discipline;
- shield-wall cohesion;
- controlled retreat;
- exploitation of terrain;
- patience under pressure;
- and violence delivered at the moment it becomes decisive.

Quinn embodies these values.

She is frighteningly effective with a greatsword, but her real strength is not personal destruction. It is her ability to remain aware of the larger formation while fighting inside it.

She knows when a line is bending, when frightened troops require visibility rather than exhortation, and when an officer must be relieved before his uncertainty spreads.

Her soldiers call her **Mother Hound**.

The name refers not to softness but to the war-hounds supporting House Moffet's arms: loyal, watchful, territorial, and entirely prepared to tear apart whatever comes through the gate.

## Greyspur

At [[Greyspur]], Quinn's forces faced heavy infantry beneath tattered Vogel banners.

Their armor was black, barbed, and sealed. Ordinary wounds did not stop them as ordinary wounds should.

Quinn's message to the Lord Marshal was relayed through Magus Tavin Elrow while the position remained under extreme pressure:

> “Moffet forces hold Greyspur.”
>
> “We are holding, but under great pressure.”
>
> “They do not fall as men should fall.”
>
> “We are Moffet.”
>
> “We will hold.”

The message contains the essential Quinn Moffet.

No plea.

No performance of courage.

No concealment of the danger.

She reported the facts, identified the threat, and stated what House Moffet intended to do about it.

## The Eldest Daughter

![[Young Quinn Moffet.png|340]]

Quinn was raised more harshly than Mara and with less freedom than Bertram.

This was partly because she was first.

Her parents had not yet learned which standards were necessary, which fears were reasonable, or how much weight a child could carry before competence became the only acceptable response.

It was also because she was the heir.

Every mistake acquired political meaning. Every weakness became a future vulnerability in House Moffet. Every success proved that still more could be expected.

By the time Mara came of age, the family was more secure in its methods and more willing to indulge talent, emotion, and uncertainty.

Quinn noticed.

She would never accuse her parents of loving Mara more.

She does sometimes believe they permitted Mara to be loved more easily.

That distinction has teeth.

---

## Mara Moffet

Quinn thinks [[Mara Moffet]] is a spoiled brat.

Privately.

With qualifications.

Mara is talented, brave, and far more disciplined than strangers initially assume. She is also accustomed to being forgiven for emotional reactions Quinn was trained to swallow before anyone saw them.

Quinn resents:

- that Mara received more patience;
- that Mara's gifts allowed her to escape some ordinary obligations;
- that family members protect Mara's feelings while relying upon Quinn's endurance;
- and that Mara can leave to become extraordinary while Quinn remains home making sure extraordinary people still have a home.

None of this makes Quinn disloyal to her sister.

She would defend Mara publicly without hesitation.

If someone called Mara spoiled in Quinn's presence, Quinn would first determine whether they possessed standing to discuss the family.

They almost certainly would not.

Quinn's most common criticism of Mara is not that she lacks strength.

It is that Mara does not always understand how much strength other people spend making room for her.

> [!note] What Quinn Would Actually Say
> Publicly:
>
> “My sister serves with the Lord Marshal's forces and has acquitted herself well.”
>
> Privately, to Kersan:
>
> “She is brilliant. She is brave. She is still a spoiled brat.”

## Bertram Moffet

Quinn thinks [[Bertram Moffet]] is a dreamer with his head in the clouds.

His disownment did not stop her loving him.

It did confirm several of her worst opinions about the consequences of Bertram's idealism.

Bertram believed deeply in honor, justice, loyalty, and the possibility that the correct personal act could force a corrupt world to reveal itself.

Quinn believes in all four.

She also believes someone must secure the gate while the dreamer makes his gesture.

Her resentment toward Bertram is different from her resentment toward Mara.

Mara was indulged.

Bertram escaped.

Even his disgrace carried him outward into a life defined by his choices. Quinn remained responsible for the household consequences, the quiet explanations, their father's grief, and the name Bertram left behind.

She is proud of the man he tried to become.

She is furious that trying was not enough to keep him home.

She would never discuss his disownment casually and will not tolerate strangers treating it as proof that House Moffet ceased loving him.

> **“He was disowned. He was not discarded.”**

---

## Baron Ewan Moffet

Quinn loves and respects her father, [[Ewan Moffet]].

She also carries the oldest child's clear memory of every standard he later softened.

Ewan prepared her to inherit by ensuring she understood that inheritance was not possession. It was obligation: to the household, tenants, sworn soldiers, villages, roads, stores, dead ancestors, and children not yet born.

Quinn learned the lesson.

Possibly too well.

Ewan is now missing.

There is:

- no body;
- no confirmed imprisonment;
- no verified message;
- and no reliable account of his present location.

Quinn has made no succession declaration.

She acts as the heir exercising household authority during her father's absence—nothing more.

This is legally precise, politically prudent, and emotionally necessary.

Declaring herself Baroness would feel like abandoning the possibility that he will return.

Failing to exercise authority would allow the house he built to come apart while everyone waited.

Quinn therefore occupies the narrow ground between daughter and ruler.

She holds it because no one else can.

---

## Sir Kersan Hes

Quinn is married to [[Sir Kersan Hes]], known as the **Master of the Shield**.

Kersan does not compete with her authority, soften her orders for public consumption, or attempt to become acting Baron through marriage and proximity.

He protects the space in which she can lead.

Their marriage is sturdy, private, and built upon mutual competence.

Quinn does not need Kersan to rescue her from command.

She needs one person before whom she does not have to perform certainty every hour of the day.

Kersan understands the difference.

He is among the very few people permitted to see her anger at Mara, grief over Bertram, fear for Ewan, and exhaustion with being the family member who always steps forward.

He does not answer every pain.

He provides shelter around it.

That distinction shapes their shared religious instincts.

---

## Faith

House Moffet publicly stands within the martial tradition of [[Heironeous]]. Its history, heraldry, and military culture all express honorable strength, courage, disciplined defense, and duty above comfort.

Quinn respects that tradition.

Her personal prayers with Kersan increasingly lean toward [[Stern Alia]].

This is not a formal rejection of Heironeous. It is an emphasis upon another part of the same life.

Heironeous teaches Quinn to stand.

Stern Alia gives meaning to what stands behind her shield:

- household;
- shelter;
- marriage;
- kinship;
- protection;
- and the promise that strength exists so someone else may safely sleep.

Quinn's faith is not expressive. She does not seek visions or public religious emotion.

She prays like a woman checking gates before nightfall.

Kersan's title, Master of the Shield, is not merely martial in their private understanding.

The shield is the space it keeps safe.

---

## Public Discipline

Quinn never discusses family wounds publicly.

She will confirm facts that must be confirmed:

- Bertram was disowned.
- Mara serves elsewhere.
- Ewan is missing.
- Quinn is exercising household authority.

She will not provide the emotional debris surrounding those facts.

Attempts to draw her into public criticism of her siblings usually meet one of two responses:

> “That is a family matter.”

Or, if the questioner has become offensive:

> “You appear to have mistaken recognition for standing.”

The second response means the conversation is over.

Persisting means Kersan has begun paying attention.

---

## Heir in Practice

During the collapse of clear authority in Moffet territory, Quinn stepped forward without declaring more power than she lawfully possessed.

She held the household rolls.

Officers answered her.

She kept stores from disappearing.

She stopped local garrisons from inventing independent policies.

She maintained enough continuity that [[Glandyr Ryomar]], arriving in Ironmere with Ravenster forces, judged her to be holding more of the region together than anyone else.

Glandyr treated her as the heir exercising household authority during her father's absence.

Quinn accepted the support without converting it into a claim of succession.

This restraint impressed him.

Anyone can seize authority during confusion.

Quinn demonstrated that she could use necessary authority while still respecting its limits.

That is one of the clearest signs that she may become a very good baroness.

---

## Lord Marshal Beringer Amadis

Quinn formally recognized [[Beringer Amadis]] as Lord Marshal of Gorundia.

Her letter was exact.

House Moffet recognized his lawful appointment, affirmed cooperation with commands issued within his authority, and declared that recognition freely and publicly.

It was specifically **not an oath**.

This was neither hesitation nor insult.

Quinn was establishing the correct relationship between an ancient household and a newly appointed royal officer without surrendering rights nobody had asked her to surrender.

The closing sentiment was characteristically Moffet:

> “May those who hold authority be judged by their works.”

Quinn respects Beringer.

She intends to continue judging him by exactly that standard.

## Current Position

Quinn is the effective head of House Moffet while refusing to declare herself its lawful baroness.

Her father is missing.

Her brother is disowned but not unloved.

Her sister is away with the Lord Marshal's forces and cannot reach home.

Ravenster troops have entered Moffet territory.

Officers, clerks, soldiers, and tenants increasingly look to Quinn because she is present, competent, and already performing the work.

The longer Ewan remains absent, the less meaningful the distinction between acting heir and ruler will become in practice.

Quinn knows this.

She continues refusing to hurry it.

## Quinn's Traits

### In Public She Will:

- Stand straight.
- Speak formally.
- Report facts before feelings.
- Never complain publicly about Mara, Bertram, Ewan, or Kersan.
- Correct anyone who assumes authority she has not claimed.
- Accept necessary help without becoming grateful enough to surrender leverage.

### In Command She Will:

- Ask about rolls, stores, roads, officers, and relief schedules.
- Give frightened people a task.
- Put competent subordinates where others can see them.
- Never promise an easy victory.
- Treat holding as an active military act, not the absence of attack.

### In Private with Kersan She Will:

- Let her shoulders drop.
- Admit anger before fear.
- Call Mara spoiled and Bertram impossible.
- Ask whether Ewan would approve, then become irritated that she asked.
- Allow silence to serve as comfort.

### What She Wants

- Her father found alive.
- House Moffet intact.
- Ironmere governed lawfully.
- Mara safe and forced to grow up.
- Bertram's choices to mean something.
- Kersan beside her.
- To inherit only when inheritance becomes truth rather than fear.

### What She Fears

- That Ewan is dead and everyone is waiting for her to admit it.
- That delay will damage the house she is trying to honor.
- That claiming the title will feel like taking it from her father.
- That Mara will return expecting comfort without understanding the cost of maintaining it.
- That Bertram was right about more than Quinn can afford to acknowledge.
- That competence has made her useful to everyone and known to almost no one.

---

## Summary

Quinn Moffet is what happens when a family raises its first child to withstand everything and then becomes surprised when she does.

She thinks Mara is spoiled.

She thinks Bertram is a dreamer.

She thinks her father may still return.

She thinks none of those private judgments excuse public failure.

So she keeps the rolls, holds the stores, answers the officers, refuses the title, and stands where House Moffet requires its heir to stand.

Serious is what people call her because they do not see the alternative she refuses to permit.
