# Mara Moffet

_House Mage of [[Orin Kharne]]_

> [!quote] Sorek Redmarch
> “Mara Moffet? Hah. She came to the frontier dressed like the road owed her an apology. Pretty little stormcloud, all silk, spellcraft, and wounded dignity. But don’t mistake the polish for softness. That girl’s got Moffet iron under the ribbons. She just hasn’t figured out yet that iron works better after it’s been beaten.”  

![[MARA_MOFFETT_FINAL.png|320]]
![[Mara Riding Outfit.png|320]]![[Mara Court Kharne.png|320]]

> **“I already know the answer.”**

[[Mara Moffet]] is the youngest daughter of [[House Moffet]] and serves as the sworn house mage to [[Orin Kharne]]. She is intelligent, beautiful, ambitious, and gifted with immense magical talent.

She is also vain.

This would be less irritating if she were not often correct.

Mara was raised in [[Ironmere Hold]], under the shadow of a house famous for valor, discipline, Heironean honor, and the old battlefield glory of Red Gorge. The Moffets are not rich peacocks. They are the house that holds the line while richer fools compose speeches about courage from a safer hill.

Mara absorbed that duty.

She also absorbed the entirely reasonable belief that someone of her talent, beauty, and breeding deserved a refined appointment somewhere civilized.

Instead, she is on the frontier.

With mud.

## Overview

Mara is a prodigy who has not yet fully paid the price of her gifts.

She has spent much of her life being praised for brilliance, listened to for insight, and indulged for charm. In most rooms, this worked.

Orin Kharne’s barony is not most rooms.

The frontier does not care that Mara is beautiful. The roads do not respect her house colors. Horses do not admire spell theory. Local problems arrive dirty, urgent, and often uninterested in elegant solutions.

Mara is learning that intelligence is not enough.

She hates this lesson.

She also understands it may make her formidable.

## Appearance

Mara is strikingly beautiful and knows exactly how to present herself.

She favors expensive fabrics, flattering cuts, polished details, and House Moffet colors worn with theatrical precision. Even when “dressed down,” she looks prepared to be seen, judged, admired, and perhaps painted by someone with decent lighting.

She carries herself with natural confidence bordering on vanity.

Her expressions are usually controlled, but not yet fully guarded. Annoyance, pride, and wounded dignity can still show through when the world fails to arrange itself properly.

Even her horse is extra.

This is not metaphor.

## Personality

### Surface

Mara is articulate, quick-thinking, socially confident, and accustomed to praise. She speaks like someone used to being the sharpest person in the room and generally offended when the room fails to notice.

She prefers clarity, polish, and correct answers.

She dislikes mud, improvisation, ugly inns, frontier manners, bad tailoring, bad roads, and people who confuse bluntness with wisdom.

Unfortunately for her, the frontier offers these in bulk.

### Beneath the Surface

Mara is not frivolous.

She genuinely wants to be useful, not merely impressive. Her vanity is real, but so is her competence. Her ambition is real, but so is her loyalty.

She has been sheltered by status, talent, beauty, and the affection of her family, especially her father. She has not failed often enough to be comfortable with failure. She has not suffered enough practical humiliation to stop trying to negotiate with reality.

That process has now begun.

Civilization mourns.

## House Moffet

[[House Moffet]] is a minor but prestigious baronial house of [[County Blackvale]], sworn to [[House Bordella]] and formerly under the crushing ducal weight of [[Duke Wilhelm Vogel]].

The house’s reputation rests on valor, tactical discipline, old-school knightly honor, and uncompromising integrity. Their motto is:

> **Stand. Defend. Endure.**

That is not decoration. It is family programming.

Mara grew up inside a house that treats duty like oxygen and honor like infrastructure. The Moffets do not posture as warriors. They hold lines, keep formations, and do the ugly necessary thing without turning it into theater.

This makes Mara’s vanity more interesting.

She is not vain because she is empty.

She is vain because she is gifted, loved, and angry that the world keeps failing to reward excellence in the proper aesthetic setting.

## The Squeeze Under Vogel

House Moffet suffered heavily under [[Duke Wilhelm Vogel]]’s rule.

Vogel’s favoritism toward [[Baron Lynwenden Cron]] stripped old privileges from families like the Moffets and shifted those advantages toward his preferred creature. The damage was not only symbolic. It was material.

The Moffets endured:

- Extra taxes.
    
- Extra levies.
    
- Erosion of old rights.
    
- Manpower shifted into feudal service.
    
- Military obligations warped to protect Vogel’s image of fairness.
    
- Men forced into service where “duty” became cover for despicable work.
    

The house did not break.

That does not mean it was unharmed.

The burden wore heavily on [[Ewan Moffet]], Mara’s father. A man built around code, duty, and Heironean rectitude was made to watch his house squeezed, his people spent, and his son Bertram wronged by the machinery of ducal politics.

Ewan endured because Moffets endure.

But endurance is not the same as peace.

## Father’s Favorite

Mara is widely considered Ewan Moffet’s favorite.

This is not said loudly in front of the other children unless one enjoys watching Quinn’s face do interesting things.

Ewan trusts Mara with more than he should and forgives more than he admits. He sees her brilliance, her promise, and perhaps the one child who might escape the blunt misery of frontier duty and feudal rot.

Even after hardship, even after Vogel, even as Ironmere’s burdens deepen, Ewan still sends Mara small gifts.

Little luxuries.

Fine ribbons.

A book she mentioned once.

A perfume oil too expensive to be practical.

A clasp in Moffet colors.

These gifts are not indulgence alone.

They are apology, affection, and defiance.

The duke squeezed the house. The world denied comfort. Ewan still found ways to remind his princess that she was cherished.

Mara pretends this does not move her.

It does.

## Role in House Kharne

Mara serves as house mage to [[Orin Kharne]], operating within war councils, strategic planning, magical problem-solving, and advisory work.

This should be prestigious.

It is prestigious.

It is also not the assignment she imagined.

Mara expected a life where talent placed her near courts, balls, libraries, salons, and eligible men who owned clean boots and knew the value of imported wine.

Instead, she serves in a frontier barony where the problems are practical, the roads are rude, and the locals regard fashion as something that happens to richer people before they fall into ditches.

She respects Orin.

She respects [[Braga Kharne]].

This is part of the difficulty.

If they were fools, Mara could resent the assignment cleanly. They are not fools. They are formidable people doing important work in a place Mara would very much like to improve by moving it closer to civilization.

Still, she privately admits one consolation:  
  
> “At least the house colors are amazing for fashion.”

## Dynamic with Orin

Orin values clarity, decisiveness, and action.

Mara provides analysis, explanation, and elegant magical solutions.

This creates friction.

Her plans are often correct, well-structured, and incomplete in execution. Orin’s habit of acting without waiting for perfection forces Mara into a painful lesson:

> Intelligence without timing is useless.

Mara is learning to act before certainty.

She resents this.

She is also getting better.

## Dynamic with Braga

Mara respects Braga immensely.

She also finds Braga exhausting.

Braga has the sort of blunt, armored certainty Mara associates with siege engines and people who think “formal” means the blood is dry. But Mara cannot dismiss her. Braga is not stupid, not weak, and not ornamental. She is exactly the kind of woman Mara would admire more easily if she were less likely to dent the furniture.

Braga, for her part, likely sees Mara as useful but untested. Talented, yes. Pretty, yes. Capable, possibly. Ready for actual hardship? We are all being dragged toward the answer.

## Magical Approach

Mara is not a battle-hungry mage.

She is capable, controlled, and selective. Her instinct is not to explode the largest thing in view, which already puts her ahead of several wizards and most political institutions.

Under pressure, her first instinct is to refine.

Choose the best option.

Minimize waste.

Avoid inelegant failure.

This can cost time.

Time can cost lives.

Mara’s growth depends on learning that the correct spell too late is just a beautifully reasoned apology.

Her developing philosophy:

> “I do not need to be certain. I need to be in time.”

## Relationship with Bertram Moffet

[[Bertram Moffet]] is Mara’s brother.

Mara loves him.

Mara also thinks he is an idiot.

These are not contradictory positions in a sibling relationship. They may be the foundation of one.

Mara is openly critical of Bertram’s decisions and holds him responsible for some of the political damage surrounding the family. She speaks with cutting clarity rather than loud emotional outburst.

Typical tone:

> “You made a choice. We had to live with it.”

But underneath the judgment is loyalty. Publicly, she defends him without hesitation. Privately, she dismantles his reasoning with surgical precision.

Bertram, for his part, thinks his sisters are great.

This is because Bertram is an idiot.

It is also because he loves them.

## Relationship with Quinn Moffet

[[Quinn Moffet]] is Mara’s older sister.

Mara thinks Quinn is a brute.

Quinn thinks Mara is spoiled.

Both think Bertram is an idiot.

Again, the family is functioning exactly as nature intended, assuming nature was invented by a sarcastic aunt with too many knives.

Quinn is strength, ferocity, and protective instinct. She is the “Mother Hound,” blunt and brutally effective, built to put herself between danger and family. Mara respects that more than she says.

Mara also finds Quinn crude, unsubtle, and prone to solving social problems as if they are doors that have offended her.

Quinn, meanwhile, sees Mara as overpraised, overdecorated, and dangerously convinced that cleverness is armor.

They clash because they are different forms of Moffet pride.

Quinn stands.

Mara calculates.

Neither likes being told the other method works.

## Relationship with Elise Moffet

[[Elise Moffet]] is the composed one: silver-tongued, sharp with accounts, and more restrained than either Quinn or Mara.

Mara understands Elise better than Quinn in some ways. Elise knows the value of structure, social timing, and measured language. Where Quinn charges, Elise arranges.

Mara may underestimate Elise’s quiet strength, because Mara tends to mistake visible brilliance for the only kind that matters.

This is a mistake Elise is unlikely to correct too early.

## Relationship with Ewan Moffet

[[Ewan Moffet]] is Mara’s father, patriarch of House Moffet, and the man whose approval she seeks more than she admits.

Ewan is austere, brutally honest, book-law rigid, and devoted to Heironeous. He has spent years doing everything “by the code,” even when that code became a chain under Vogel’s rule.

Mara respects him deeply.

She also sees the toll.

The squeezing of House Moffet under Vogel has worn heavily on him. The old privileges stripped away, the extra taxes, the levies, the forced duties, the political humiliations, and Bertram’s disownment have all left marks.

Mara has begun to notice inconsistencies and quiet decisions that do not fully align with the ideals she was taught.

She has not confronted him.

Not yet.

## Relationship with Ayla Moffet

[[Ayla Moffet]] is Mara’s mother, elegant, calculating, serious, and quietly formidable.

If Ewan is the code, Ayla is the spine beneath the silk.

Mara inherited more from Ayla than she may admit: presentation, strategic patience, emotional concealment, and the ability to turn social expectation into a weapon.

Ayla likely understands Mara’s vanity better than anyone.

She may even tolerate it because she sees what it is trying to become.

## Relationship with Myra Durathen



[[Myra Durathen]] and Mara Moffet hate each other.

This hatred is mutual, personal, and useful.

Mara is intelligent, beautiful, ambitious, magically gifted, and born into a house with a stronger reputation than its current comfort would suggest. She believes she should have been placed somewhere civilized, glittering, and socially rewarding.

Myra is intelligent, beautiful, ambitious, practical, and born into a poor house where survival required using every advantage without pretending the world would be fair.

Mara sees Myra as smug, under-polished, socially resentful, and dressed like a ledger learned to ride.

Myra sees Mara as vain, spoiled, overdecorated, and insufficiently practical.

They are both right enough to make it unbearable.

## The Ride Through the Barony

During a recent ride through Orin’s barony, Myra arrived with Durathen practicality: old boots, riding pants, sensible layers, and the calm superiority of someone who understands distance.

Mara arrived as if the road were a receiving line.

House colors.

Careful presentation.

Beautiful tailoring.

A full visual declaration that dignity need not surrender to dirt.

Mara thought she had won.

Then the saddle sores began.

By the end of the ride, Myra had not needed to say much. This was wise, because anything she said would have become family law within the hour and possibly murder by sundown.

Mara learned a practical lesson.

She has not accepted defeat.

The next ride will be worse, because Mara is too intelligent not to adapt and too vain not to make the adaptation look expensive.

## Reputation

### Court

> “Charming. Bright. Untested.”

Among courtly circles, Mara is admired as a promising young noble mage: beautiful, articulate, and gifted. The word “untested” follows her like a polite cough.

### Soldiers

> “Smart. Hope she learns fast.”

Soldiers respect usefulness. Mara has the tools to earn that respect but not yet the habits. If she acts in time, they will forgive the vanity. Soldiers are practical like that, when they are not busy being idiots with boots.

### Nobility

> “Promising, but still young.”

Nobles see Mara as a valuable piece: marriageable, magical, intelligent, and connected. Whether she becomes an asset or a problem depends on whether her ambition matures faster than her pride.

### House Kharne

> “Useful. Expensive in spirit.”

Within Orin’s service, Mara is recognized as talented and necessary. She is also recognized as someone who thinks the frontier is beneath her, which is true, but saying so aloud is what separates nobles from people invited back to supper.

## Character Arc

Mara is not finished.

That is the point.

### Phase 1 — Confidence

She assumes intelligence is sufficient. She speaks often, explains freely, and expects to be right.

### Phase 2 — Friction

She encounters real stakes. Plans fail because the world acts before she finishes thinking. She begins to see that timing matters as much as correctness.

### Phase 3 — Adaptation

She speaks less, acts sooner, and accepts imperfect solutions.

### Phase 4 — Competence

She balances intellect with action and becomes not merely the smartest person in the room, but the one who knows what to do with it.

## Skills and Strengths

### Magical Talent

Mara has immense magical ability and the discipline to use it precisely. Her talent is real, not decorative.

### Intelligence

She is quick, analytical, and good at grasping complex problems.

### Presentation

Mara understands appearance as power. She dresses, speaks, and moves with intent.

### Social Confidence

She is comfortable among nobles and skilled at holding attention.

### Loyalty

For all her vanity, Mara is loyal to House Moffet and increasingly to Orin and Braga.

## Weaknesses

### Vanity

Mara cares about being seen correctly. This can make her vulnerable to humiliation, rivalry, and manipulation.

### Inexperience

She has not yet endured enough true consequence to be fully steady under pressure.

### Perfectionism

She prefers the best answer, which is dangerous when the ugly answer is needed immediately.

### Class Expectations

Mara expected the world to reward her properly. The frontier is teaching her that reward is often delayed, dirty, and delivered by people with terrible manners.

### Rivalry

Myra can get under Mara’s skin with almost no effort. This is funny, useful, and potentially hazardous.

## Narrative Hooks

- Mara learns more about what House Moffet was forced to do under Vogel and must decide whether to defend her father or confront him.
    
- Mara’s gifts from Ewan become evidence of his affection, guilt, or hidden political strain.
    
- Mara and Myra are forced to cooperate on a crisis where magic and estate management both matter.
    
- Quinn and Mara clash over whether brutality or precision best protects family.
    
- Bertram’s choices endanger House Moffet again, forcing Mara to choose between judgment and loyalty.
    
- Mara gets the courtly opportunity she wanted and discovers she may no longer fit it.
    
- The frontier gives Mara her first public success, and she hates that it happened somewhere with mud.
    

## Table Use

Use Mara when the campaign needs:

- A noble mage with real talent and real vanity.
    
- A Moffet family perspective that is not Quinn’s blunt force or Bertram’s wounded heart.
    
- Sibling conflict that still reads as love.
    
- A frontier fish-out-of-water who is too smart to stay useless.
    
- A rival for Myra Durathen.
    
- A way to show how Vogel’s rule damaged even honorable houses.
    
- A young woman growing from brilliance into competence.
    

## Voice

Mara speaks elegantly, sharply, and with the confidence of someone who expects the world to be improved by her participation.

Common lines:

> “That is not what I said. It is merely what you managed to hear.”

> “There is no virtue in being muddy before noon.”

> “Quinn, some problems do not become smaller because you hit them.”

> “Bertram, I love you dearly. Please stop helping.”

> “Myra dresses like hardship is a personality.”

> “I had the answer. I am learning that answers have deadlines.”

> “Father sent it. No, it is not excessive.”

## GM Notes

Mara should be played as vain, not empty.

She is proud because she is gifted. She is spoiled because she is loved. She is frustrated because she expected a better stage. She is loyal because she is Moffet.

The joke is that she thinks she deserves civilization.

The truth is that the frontier may be exactly what makes her dangerous.

Her rivalry with Myra should stay sharp and funny, but not shallow. They hate each other because each sees in the other a rival version of herself: beautiful, ambitious, intelligent, and convinced she is the practical one.

Mara’s family dynamics should be warm underneath the cuts.

She thinks Quinn is a brute.

Quinn thinks Mara is spoiled.

Both think Bertram is an idiot.

Bertram thinks his sisters are great.

That is love, apparently.
