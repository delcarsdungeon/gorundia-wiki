# Myreth Durathen

> **“No. I am not finished with grief. I am simply finished giving it bad aim.”**

[[Myreth Durathen]] is the sister of [[Roderic Durathen]], widow of [[Aledrik Thralen]], and mother of [[Braga Kharne]] and [[Garren Thralen]].

She was born Durathen, married Thralen, lost both, and remained standing.

This has not improved her manners.

Myreth is the old wound at the heart of the Durathen-Thralen grievance. She lost her husband, then her son, then the Thralen lands, and finally nearly lost the moral right to answer any of it when grief drove her hand toward murder.

She is not a helpless widow.

She is not a courtly grandmother full of gentle lessons and boiled sweets.

She is a small, white-haired, blue-eyed noblewoman with an account book where other people keep mercy.

And now [[Baron Remy DeSalle]] is dead.

That should have solved something.

Naturally, it has made her more complicated.

## Appearance

![[Myreth Durathen.png|340]]

Myreth is short, white-haired, and blue-eyed, with the hard compact presence of a woman who has spent decades learning how not to be moved by louder people.

She dresses plainly but correctly. Her clothes are well-kept, old, and severe. Nothing about her announces wealth. Everything about her announces that she has survived wealthier fools.

She wears black often enough that people notice.

She does not wear mourning as weakness.

She wears it as jurisdiction.

Her hands are small, controlled, and often still. People who know the story of the acid bomb tend to watch those hands more carefully than they admit.

This is wise.

## Early Life

Myreth was born into [[House Durathen]] in **1165 OR**, the daughter of a proud minor noble line seated at [[Greyford Hall]].

Durathen life taught her the usual family lessons:

- Do not waste coin.
- Do not waste food.
- Do not waste loyalty.
- Do not waste anger.
- Keep records.
- Keep steel clean.
- Keep old debts where they can be found.

She grew up in a house too poor to be careless and too proud to be quiet. The Durathens were never grand, but they had dignity, discipline, and the dangerous habit of remembering exactly who had failed them.

Myreth learned early that survival was not a virtue unless it came with memory.

Otherwise, it was just breathing.

## Marriage to Aledrik Thralen

Myreth married [[Aledrik Thralen]], lord of the struggling Thralen line of Marsten.

It was alliance.

It was gamble.

It was also, by the standards of noble marriage, almost offensively decent.

Aledrik was not a blazing zealot like his sister [[Cwenheld Thralen]] or a militant church power like his brother [[Patriarch-General Clytus]]. He was steadier than that. A lord of fields, levies, books, roads, oaths, and hard weather.

Myreth respected his steadiness.

Aledrik respected her mind.

That mattered more than public sweetness. Public sweetness is what nobles perform while ruining each other over inheritance law.

Their marriage joined Durathen endurance to Thralen discipline. They did not build a rich house. They built a hard one. They built a household where duty was expected, waste was despised, and sentiment was real but rarely left unattended in public.

Myreth loved Aledrik.

She would not say it cheaply.

That does not mean she felt it lightly.

## Children

Myreth and Aledrik had two children:

- [[Braga Kharne|Braga Thralen]], later known as Braga the Brazen and Baroness Kharne.
- [[Garren Thralen]], the last real hope for the direct continuation of the Thralen line.

Myreth loved both of them with the same hard intensity she brought to every other duty. She did not raise them to be soft. She raised them to stand.

Braga inherited fire, pride, and the inability to accept shame that properly belonged to someone else.

Garren inherited duty.

That killed him.

Because naturally, in Gorundia, virtues often file their own death warrants.

## Aledrik’s Death

Aledrik died suddenly in **1216 OR**, at the age of forty-six.

The official explanations are the usual polite noble fog: sudden illness, heart failure, the body failing before the burden did.

Myreth did not collapse.

She became narrower.

Aledrik had been the man who could steady her worst instincts without insulting them. He could hear her anger and give it walls. After his death, those walls remained for a while, but the hand that repaired them was gone.

His death left Myreth with grief, debts, alliances, children, old claims, and the increasingly unpleasant knowledge that the world does not become kinder when a good man dies.

It merely becomes less embarrassed about itself.

## Garren Thralen and the Redwood Fen

[[Garren Thralen]] died because [[Baron Remy DeSalle]] wanted a glorious story.

Remy led a wasteful expedition into the [[Redwood Fen]], chasing reputation, relics, vanity, and whatever else mediocre nobles call destiny when they need peasants and better men to die for it.

There was no true military necessity.

There was no great royal command.

There was Remy DeSalle, mistaking ambition for courage.

When the expedition collapsed into panic, confusion, and Fen-born danger, Garren and his loyal men were assigned the rearguard. They held long enough for others to survive.

They died covering consequences they did not create.

That is the cleanest version.

Myreth prefers the accurate one:

Remy DeSalle spent her son’s life to protect his own failure.

## The Loss of Thralen

With Garren dead and the Thralen male line broken, the Thralen lands reverted to DeSalle control.

It was legal.

That did not make it just.

The reversion was the sort of clean legal cruelty nobles commit when they want theft to smell like parchment. Remy survived. The relic was lost. The expedition failed. House Thralen was broken. DeSalle authority absorbed what remained.

Myreth renounced the Thralen name afterward.

This was not a rejection of Aledrik.

It was not a rejection of Garren.

It was a refusal to let DeSalle define what remained of her.

She returned to Durathen blood because Durathen memory still had teeth.

## The Shape of Her Grief

Myreth’s grief is not wild.

That is what makes it dangerous.

She does not scream in halls, collapse in chapels, or ruin dinners with theatrical sobbing. She can sit quietly through a council, accept tea, correct an account, and ask after someone’s health while deciding whether that person’s house should be embarrassed, indebted, or destroyed.

Her grief became strategic.

Her hatred became patient.

Her patience became a weapon.

Sir [[Sorek Redmarch]] once described her as having the eyes of a woman who lost everything twice and still sleeps with her boots on.

That is unfair.

She does sleep.

Just not like someone who trusts the door.

## The Revenge Plot

The first plan against Remy DeSalle was not honorable.

It was not lawful.

It was murder.

Myreth and those loyal to the Durathen-Thralen grievance had reached the end of patience. They were ready to kill Remy outright and take the punishment afterward. Exile. Prison. Disgrace. Forfeiture. Execution.

All of it had been weighed.

None of it outweighed Garren.

They gathered information quietly:

- Routes.
- Habits.
- Weak moments.
- Blind angles.
- Hours when DeSalle was attended in theory but alone in truth.
- Servants who could be leaned on.
- Old favors that could be called without revealing the whole shape.
- Small obligations, never large enough to make the conspiracy obvious.

The phrase that survived from this period was simple:

> “We don’t need him weak. We need him finished.”

That was not metaphor.

Myreth did not need clean.

She needed done.

## Beringer’s Intervention

[[Beringer Amadis]] changed the shape of the story.

He recognized the danger early and alerted Braga before the Durathen-Thralen revenge became a murder that would have ruined the family politically, legally, and morally.

This earned him profound favor with Braga.

Not because he saved Remy.

No sane person says that to Braga twice.

Beringer earned favor because he gave House Durathen a road to justice that did not require them to become criminals in order to be answered.

He did not tell them to forgive.

He did not ask Myreth to swallow blood and call it peace.

He helped move vengeance into the light.

That is a different thing.

A rarer thing.

Annoyingly useful, even.

## The Acid Bomb

During the Lord’s Muster, Myreth threw an acid bomb at Remy DeSalle from the galleries.

There are many ways to describe this.

Most of them are cowardly.

The truth is simple:

A mother tried to melt the man she blamed for her son’s death in front of the assembled power of the realm.

It was not politics.

It was not rhetoric.

It was grief, rage, and intent made visible.

[[Norwin Stoneyeye]] caught the bomb.

That act saved Remy’s life, yes.

More importantly, it saved Myreth from crossing the line she had already decided she was willing to cross.

Norwin saved House Durathen from becoming the family that murdered a baron at the Muster.

He saved Braga from having to choose between blood and law.

He saved Edwyn from inheriting vengeance already spoiled.

He saved Myreth from succeeding too soon.

Gods help him, the gnome caught an acid bomb and somehow became the least ridiculous part of the story.

## The Lawful Turn

After the failed gallery attack, the Lord Marshal’s office and Beringer forced the matter into lawful shape.

Remy DeSalle was pressed, baited, and given enough room to reveal himself.

He obliged.

Remy insulted the King.

He insulted the kingdom.

He insulted the honor of better men.

By the time he finished, the Durathen grievance had become something larger than family revenge. It had become a public matter of royal dignity, knightly order, and noble responsibility.

The Lord Marshal appointed [[Edwyn Durathen]] as champion.

This was lawful.

It was also engineered.

Good.

Sometimes law requires carpentry.

## The Duel Under Theron’s Statue

The matter ended beneath the statue of [[Theron Sylvanranth]], in the courtyard of Heironeous.

[[Edwyn Durathen]] faced Remy DeSalle as lawful champion.

He did not kill him in a corridor.

He did not strike from hiding.

He did not avenge Garren with poison, shadow, or a knife between servants’ bells.

He stood before crown, court, church, nobles, witnesses, and history.

When the duel ended, Remy DeSalle was dead.

Steel went quiet.

The old debt was paid in public.

For Edwyn, it was glory sharpened by obligation.

For Braga, it was vengeance without dishonor.

For Beringer, it proved he understood the difference between stopping vengeance and redeeming it.

For Norwin, it meant the acid bomb had somehow led to the sane outcome, because apparently reality had run out of dignified options.

For Myreth, it was the death she demanded.

And then morning came.

That was the cruel part.

## After Remy

Remy DeSalle is dead.

Myreth has not become gentle.

She has become less aimed.

That is not the same thing.

For years, Remy gave her grief a name, a target, a direction, and an end point. Every memory of Garren could point toward him. Every humiliation of the Thralen reversion could be filed under DeSalle. Every sleepless night had a debtor.

Now the debtor is dead.

The account is answered.

The grief remains.

This is Myreth’s current danger. Not that she still wants Remy dead. That part is finished. The danger is that she may not know who she is when she is not moving toward that death.

Vengeance kept her upright.

Justice has taken the crutch away.

## What She Feels Now

Myreth would not describe herself as relieved.

Relief sounds too soft.

She would not describe herself as satisfied.

Satisfaction sounds too vulgar.

She would likely say:

> “It was necessary.”

That is true.

It is also insufficient.

Some part of Myreth wanted Remy’s death to change the room where Garren’s memory lives. It did not. The dead remained dead. Aledrik did not return. Garren did not walk back through the door. The Thralen lands did not reassemble themselves out of remorse and legal correction.

The duel gave the family judgment.

It did not give her back her son.

No judgment can.

That is the insult beneath the victory.

## Relationship with Braga

[[Braga Kharne]] is Myreth’s daughter, pride, argument, and surviving proof that the Thralen line did not vanish quietly.

Their relationship is fierce, loving, strained, and deeply inconvenient to everyone within hearing distance.

Braga wants justice, not mere revenge. She understands blood, but she also understands honor. She carries Aledrik’s lesson more clearly than Myreth sometimes wants to admit:

> “Hold the line.”

Myreth loves Braga.

She also resents being morally steadied by her own daughter.

This is family.

Naturally, everyone involved suffers.

Braga’s marriage to [[Orin Kharne]] has given Myreth’s blood new power, but Myreth does not treat that as comfort. She watches Kharne power carefully. She respects Orin’s strength and likely trusts him more than she expected, but she does not mistake marriage for safety.

She has buried too much for that.

## Relationship with Garren

Garren is not “dead” to Myreth in any ordinary sense.

He is present.

In the chapel.

In the empty places in conversation.

In the avoided dates.

In the armor that was not inherited.

In Braga’s silences.

In Edwyn’s glory.

In every mention of Remy DeSalle, even now that Remy is a corpse and therefore finally useful.

Myreth remembers Garren not as a symbol, but as a son.

That matters.

People trying to comfort her by making him noble, heroic, or historically meaningful may find themselves corrected with terrifying precision.

Garren was brave.

Garren was dutiful.

Garren was loved.

Garren should not have died.

All four things can be true.

Myreth does not forgive anyone who tries to make the fourth one disappear beneath the first three.

## Relationship with Aledrik

Aledrik was Myreth’s husband, partner, and the man who could once absorb her anger without trying to own it.

He is the standard against which she measures men.

This is unfair to most men.

Most men richly deserve the disadvantage.

Aledrik’s memory steadies her and wounds her in equal measure. He would have wanted lawful reckoning. He would have wanted Remy answered, but he would not have wanted Myreth damned in the answering.

Myreth knows this.

That knowledge did not stop her from throwing the acid bomb.

It may be part of why she has not fully forgiven herself.

## Relationship with Edwyn Durathen

[[Edwyn Durathen]] became the blade Myreth meant to make dirty.

That is the ugly truth beneath the lawful victory.

She admires him.

She used him.

She loves him.

She endangered him.

Edwyn’s duel with Remy gave House Durathen vindication, but Myreth understands how close she came to turning him into something worse than a champion. She may never say this directly. Direct confession is for priests, children, and fools cornered by good women.

But she knows.

Edwyn should feel her approval as warmth wrapped in iron wire.

He should also be careful.

Myreth may now see his Lord Marshal service as the next place where Durathen debts can be answered properly.

This is useful.

This is dangerous.

As usual, both things have arrived wearing the same cloak.

## Relationship with Myra Durathen

[[Myra Durathen]] sees Myreth as both warning and inheritance.

She understands her aunt’s grief.

She admires her discipline.

She also sees what happens when vengeance becomes the only surviving language.

Myreth, in turn, likely sees Myra as a sharper instrument than most people realize. Beautiful, ambitious, controlled, and practical, Myra represents another Durathen path: not the bomb, not the duel, not the open wound, but the ledger, the room, the household, the marriage prospect, the quiet pressure applied exactly where it hurts.

Myreth may approve.

That should worry people.

## Relationship with Roderic and Elinora

[[Roderic Durathen]] is Myreth’s brother and head of House Durathen.

He is cautious, pragmatic, and too inclined to see danger before opportunity.

Myreth likely finds this irritating.

She also knows his caution helped keep the house alive.

Their relationship is old, layered, and not always kind. Roderic may have looked away from parts of Myreth’s vengeance because looking directly would have required action he dreaded. Myreth may resent his caution while relying on the house it preserved.

[[Elinora Durathen]] manages the household machinery with quiet efficiency. Myreth respects competence, especially when it keeps poor nobility from becoming shabby in public.

She may not say this gently.

Again, Myreth is not a festival.

## Relationship with Beringer Amadis

Myreth’s view of [[Beringer Amadis]] is complicated.

He interfered.

He was right.

This is one of the most irritating combinations known to noble pride.

Beringer prevented her vengeance from becoming assassination. He preserved House Durathen long enough for its grievance to be answered lawfully. He protected Braga from an impossible choice. He gave Edwyn the place Myreth had wanted to seize through conspiracy.

Myreth may never thank him with warmth.

She may never forgive him for being necessary.

But she owes him a debt.

Durathens remember debts.

The trick for Beringer is surviving what Myreth considers repayment.

## Relationship with Norwin Stoneyeye

[[Norwin Stoneyeye]] caught the acid bomb.

This is absurd.

It is also central.

Myreth may resent him for stopping her hand. She may also understand, with terrible clarity, that he saved her from the part of herself that would have destroyed everything she claimed to be avenging.

Norwin did not deny her justice.

He delayed her crime long enough for justice to arrive wearing armor.

That is not a small distinction.

Myreth’s gratitude, if it exists, will not be soft.

It may appear as protection, information, silence, or one quiet correction when someone speaks too lightly of gnomes in her hearing.

Which, for Myreth, is practically a love poem.

## Relationship with Remy DeSalle

[[Baron Remy DeSalle]] is dead.

Myreth hated him with discipline.

She blamed him for Garren’s death, the destruction of the Thralen line, the loss of the Thralen lands, and years of cowardice hiding behind rank.

She was not wrong.

But hatred is a crude tool, no matter how finely sharpened. It gave her strength. It nearly took her honor. It made the account clear and the world smaller.

Now Remy is gone.

Myreth does not need to forgive him.

She does need to decide whether any part of herself was built only to kill him.

If the answer is yes, the next few months matter.

## Faith

Myreth is tied to [[Heironeous]] through House Thralen and family practice, but her faith has been battered by experience.

She respects courage.

She respects duty.

She respects judgment.

She has less patience for sermons spoken by people who have not had to decide whether justice is still justice when it arrives too late.

The duel under Theron’s statue matters to her because it restored the moral shape of the grievance. Remy did not merely die. He was judged in public beneath the eyes of crown, court, church, and memory.

That distinction matters.

It may matter more later, when Myreth has to decide what to do with the rest of her life.

## Skills and Strengths

### Strategic Patience

Myreth can wait. She can work through others, call in favors, read silences, and apply pressure over time.

She does not need speed.

She needs inevitability.

### Grievance Accounting

She remembers debts, obligations, insults, old service, broken promises, and useful shame.

Among Durathens, this is practically literacy.

### Noble Politics

Myreth understands how small houses survive near larger ones: alliance, marriage, scandal, leverage, witness, and timing.

She is especially dangerous because she knows what it is like to have no clean options.

### Emotional Discipline

Her grief is immense, but rarely messy in public. She does not give enemies the relief of seeing her collapse.

### Moral Clarity Under Damage

Myreth can still recognize justice.

That is important.

She nearly chose murder, but she understood the difference once the lawful path opened.

She may not be pure.

She is not lost.

## Weaknesses

### The Empty Target

With Remy dead, Myreth has lost the central target around which her grief organized itself.

This may leave her steadier.

It may also leave her searching for new debts.

That would be bad.

### Self-Justification

Myreth is very good at explaining why the terrible thing is necessary.

She is not always wrong.

That is the problem.

### Grief-Hardened Judgment

She can mistake mercy for weakness and patience for cowardice.

This makes her useful in crisis and dangerous afterward.

### Family Blindness

Myreth loves her family, but she can use them while telling herself she is protecting them.

Edwyn should remember this.

Myra probably already has.

Braga definitely knows.

### No Talent for Peace

Peace requires habits Myreth has not practiced in years.

It may bore her.

It may frighten her.

It may feel like betrayal.

This is how old wars keep finding new names.

## Current Role

Myreth is now the dangerous aftermath of redeemed vengeance.

She is not the woman plotting Remy’s murder anymore.

That chapter ended under Theron’s statue.

She is the woman who must decide what remains after the answer.

Possible current uses:

- A hard elder voice inside House Durathen.
- A reminder that lawful vengeance still leaves human wreckage.
- A political operator who knows too many old obligations.
- A source of pressure on Edwyn’s new role with the Lord Marshal.
- A severe influence on Myra’s developing style.
- A difficult moral mirror for Braga.
- A potential ally who is never comfortable.
- A woman trying, badly, to live after the one thing she demanded has been granted.

## Table Use

Use Myreth when the campaign needs:

- The cost of vengeance after vengeance succeeds.
- A family elder who is morally compromised but not villainous.
- Durathen memory in human form.
- Political pressure from a small house that refuses to stay small in consequence.
- Someone who can thank Beringer and resent him in the same breath.
- Someone who owes Norwin more than she wants to admit.
- A warning to Myra.
- A wound Braga cannot simply punch.
- A reminder that justice may be necessary and still fail to heal anyone.

## Voice

Myreth speaks quietly, precisely, and without wasted softness.

She rarely raises her voice.

The room usually lowers itself instead.

Common lines:

> “Do not tell me time heals. Time keeps records.”

> “He is dead. That is not the same as restored.”

> “I did not ask for mercy. Fortunately, better people were careless enough to give it.”

> “My son was not a lesson. Do not improve him into one.”

> “Justice came late. I accepted it anyway.”

> “A lawful blade cuts cleaner. It does not cut less deeply.”

> “Grief is not madness. Madness is pretending grief has no account.”

> “I remember what I nearly did. Kindly do not require me to pretend otherwise.”

> “Remy DeSalle is dead. If you think that ends the matter, you have mistaken death for arithmetic.”

## GM Notes

Myreth should not be played as a cackling revenge crone.

Too easy.

Too boring.

Too much theater, not enough knife.

She is a grieving noblewoman who nearly became a murderer, was stopped, and then saw her cause redeemed through lawful judgment. That makes her more interesting than a villain and less comfortable than a victim.

Remy’s death should not magically soften her.

It should disorient her.

For years, she knew exactly where to aim the pain. Now the target is gone, and the pain remains. That gives her three possible roads:

1. **Hard Peace** — she becomes a severe but stabilizing elder, using her mind to protect Durathen interests without further blood.
2. **Transferred Debt** — she begins finding new vessels for old grief: DeSalle loyalists, corrupt nobles, legal thieves, cowards wearing rank.
3. **Confession Without Softness** — she privately admits that Beringer and Norwin saved her from destroying the very justice she wanted.

The best version is probably all three, because people are inconvenient and refuse to stay in tidy boxes.

Myreth’s story is not “revenge is bad.”

That is nursery morality with better shoes.

Her story is sharper:

Vengeance may be understandable.

It may even be deserved.

But if no one catches the bomb, vengeance can eat the person it was supposed to vindicate.

Remy DeSalle is dead.

Myreth Durathen is still here.

That is where the page gets interesting.
