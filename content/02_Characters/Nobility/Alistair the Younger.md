# Alistair, Lord Blackbriar

![[Amadis the Younger.png|320]]![[Milena Wedding 1.png|320]]

_Also known as Amadis the Younger. Heir of Blackbriar. Champion of the Lance. A good knight, a dutiful son, and currently so besotted that several practical people are reaching for antidotes._

---

## Overview

**Alistair Amadis**, usually styled **Lord Blackbriar** and often called **Amadis the Younger**, is the heir of **[[County Blackbriar]]** and son of **[[Alistair Amadis]]**.

He is a respected knight of **[[House Amadis]]**, best known for his skill with the lance, his battlefield courage, and his uneasy position in a family full of people who cast very long shadows.

He has recently married **[[Milena Orenic]]**, niece of **[[Duke Vladek Orenic]]**.

The match appears, on paper, to be a strong political alliance between **House Amadis** and **House Orenic**.

In practice, Alistair is wildly, obviously, painfully in love with his new wife.

Possibly too much.

**[[Beringer]] Amadis** suspects a love potion.

No one has proved this.

That has not stopped anyone sensible from worrying.

## Appearance

Amadis is an imposing knight, standing roughly **6'2"** and weighing around **220 lbs**. He has the strong, scarred build of a man who has survived real battle rather than merely posing for tapestries like court idiots with cheekbones.

His brown hair is kept tidy, and his deep brown eyes often carry a thoughtful, almost forlorn expression. Those who know him well do not consider him brooding. He is more contemplative than grim, more measured than miserable.

His scars are numerous, especially from battlefield injuries and hard training. He tends to wear them without vanity, though he is not immune to taking pride in what they represent.

> [!quote] Amadis the Younger
> “A scar is a reminder of a battle fought and a lesson learned. I have many, and I’ve earned every one of them.”

---

## Personality

Amadis shares much of his father’s humility and sense of **noblesse oblige**. He believes nobility exists to serve and protect, not merely to own nicer chairs while peasants starve nearby.

Unlike his father, however, he enjoys honor openly. Tournaments, battlefield praise, and earned recognition matter to him. He does not chase glory like a fool, but he does value it.

He is:

- honorable
- brave
- humble
- quietly proud
- protective of family
- romantically idealistic
- occasionally too eager to prove himself

He is intelligent, but he knows he is not his father’s equal as a strategist, nor Sophia’s equal as a scholar. That awareness has shaped him. He has spent much of his life trying to carve out a place that is truly his own.

> [!quote] Amadis the Younger
> “To lead is not to demand loyalty but to inspire it.”

---

## Skills and Accomplishments

Amadis is best known as a **master of the lance**.

During the final battle against **House Golitsyn**, he faced and killed the Golitsyn heir in a mounted clash. The feat helped secure his reputation as one of the finest young knights of House Amadis.

Though his public fame rests heavily on jousting and cavalry warfare, Amadis is also a capable commander and trained in the broader arts of war.

Some consider him a prodigy.

Others whisper that House Amadis has been very effective at polishing his legend.

Both may be true, because politics is mostly varnish applied to blood.

> [!quote] [[Sir Beringer Amadis]], reminiscing
> “He’s good. Maybe if he learned how to parry properly, he wouldn’t look like he tried to headbutt a millstone.”

---

## Marriage to Milena Orenic

Amadis has now married **[[Milena Orenic]]**, the formidable niece of **[[Duke Vladek Orenic]]**.

The pairing developed quickly after the Orenic household began pushing the match. What began as political placement became something much more personal.

Milena herself was clear with **[[Sophia Blackbriar]]**:

> [!quote] [[Milena Orenic]]
> “They pushed. We met. We spoke. We laughed. We stopped pretending otherwise.”

She also described arranged marriage in terms Sophia found both unsettling and persuasive:

> [!quote] [[Milena Orenic]]
> “Arranged marriages are not cages. They are frameworks. Inside framework, one may still build.”

Amadis, for his part, has fallen completely for her.

He frequently describes Milena as **“delicate,”** a statement that has caused several witnesses to stare at him as if he had declared a warhorse to be made of lace.

Milena appears quietly amused.

---

## The Suspicion

Amadis’s devotion to Milena is sincere.

That is almost the problem.

He is affectionate, attentive, protective, and openly delighted by her presence in a way that has begun to feel excessive even to people who enjoy seeing nobles happy, which is already suspicious because nobles are generally at their safest when miserable.

**Beringer suspects a love potion.**

This suspicion is not an accusation against Milena. Beringer does not currently believe she knowingly enchanted him.

The concern is broader:

- House Orenic has enemies.
- House Amadis has enemies.
- A sudden marriage alliance has political consequences.
- Amadis’s emotional intensity is unusually visible.
- Alchemical meddling in noble marriages is exactly the kind of garbage ambitious people do when they have too much money and no moral spine.

For now, the suspicion remains quiet.

No public accusation has been made.

---

## Current Status: Kept from the War Host

Amadis is **not being allowed to join the main war host**.

His father, **[[Alistair Amadis]]**, has kept both Amadis and Milena away from the primary campaign forces. Officially, this is prudent protection of the heir and his new bride during a dangerous military crisis.

Unofficially, it also keeps Amadis from riding into the grinder while distracted, enchanted, lovesick, or some unholy combination of all three.

This frustrates him deeply.

Amadis wants to serve. He wants to prove that marriage has not softened him, that he remains worthy of his name, and that he can stand beside the other knights when the realm calls.

His father is not moved.

This is probably wise.

Naturally, wisdom is infuriating to young knights.

---

## Relationship with Milena

Amadis adores Milena.

He sees her gentleness before her strength, her careful manners before her size, and her mind before the obvious fact that she could probably pick him up and relocate him like inconvenient furniture.

He treats her with genuine tenderness, not merely political courtesy.

This makes the marriage unusually promising.

It also makes it politically dangerous.

If someone has manipulated his feelings, then House Amadis has been compromised through its heir.

If no one has manipulated him, then Beringer owes several people an apology, which he will presumably deliver with all the grace of a man swallowing gravel.

---

## Relationship with Sophia Blackbriar

Amadis is protective of **[[Sophia Blackbriar]]**, but he respects her intellect and independence.

Sophia’s early conversation with Milena helped settle some of the family tension around the marriage. Sophia recognized that Milena was not simply being traded, and Milena recognized Sophia as someone worth speaking to honestly.

This creates a new and potentially dangerous internal alliance within House Amadis:

**Sophia and Milena now understand each other.**

That may eventually matter more than anyone expects.

---

## Relationship with Beringer

Amadis and **[[Sir Beringer Amadis]]** grew up as brothers and rivals within House Amadis.

The revelation of Beringer’s Vogel blood unsettled him, but it did not break the bond. Amadis continues to regard Beringer as family.

> [!quote] Amadis the Younger
> “Bloodlines mean little when weighed against bonds forged in trust and love. Sir Beringer is my brother in every way that matters.”

Their relationship remains affectionate, competitive, and complicated.

Beringer’s suspicion about the love potion adds a new strain. Amadis may see it as insulting, especially if raised poorly. Beringer, being Beringer, is fully capable of raising it poorly.

---

## Relationship with His Father

Amadis deeply admires **[[Alistair Amadis]]**.

He also resents, in the quiet way dutiful sons resent impossible fathers, how difficult it is to step out from under his shadow.

His father’s decision to keep him away from the main war host is protective, strategic, and politically sound.

It also hurts.

Amadis may understand the reasoning.

That does not mean he likes being protected like an heirloom sword wrapped in felt.

## Current Story Function

Amadis currently serves as:

- the protected heir of Blackbriar
- a new bridge between House Amadis and House Orenic
- a possible victim of alchemical or magical manipulation
- a source of tension with Beringer
- a symbol of what House Amadis is trying not to lose in the coming war

He is not absent because he is cowardly.

He is absent because his father is not an idiot.

This distinction matters.
