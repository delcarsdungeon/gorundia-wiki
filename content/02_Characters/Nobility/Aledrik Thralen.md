# Aledrik Thralen

> [!quote] Sir Sorek Redmarch  
> “I barely knew him, but I knew his kind. The sort of lord who makes Gorundia strong — not by crowns or banners, but by holding the line for his people. We’d be poorer with fewer like him.”

![[Aledrik Thralen.png|340]]

**Aledrik Thralen** was lord of the struggling Thralen line, husband of [[Myreth Durathen]], and father of [[Braga Kharne|Braga “the Brazen”]] and [[Garren Thralen]].

He was born in **1170 OR** and died suddenly in **1216 OR**, at the age of forty-six.

Aledrik belonged to the militant blood of [[House Thralen]], but he never carried the burning zeal of his siblings. Where [[Cwenheld]] saw visions and Patriarch-General [[Clytus Thralen]] marched crusades, Aledrik remained at home as banner-lord, steward, father, and commander.

He was the steady one.

Naturally, fate took that personally.

## Overview

Aledrik bore the martial inheritance of House Thralen without surrendering himself to its harsher instincts.

He drilled his levies to a standard most minor houses could not match. He marched beside them when called. He kept the books when he returned. He knew the names of the men who served under his banner and the yield of the fields that fed them.

To richer nobles, this made him practical.

To poorer folk, it made him trustworthy.

To House Thralen, it made him the part of the family that kept the walls standing while everyone else chased glory, visions, and heavily capitalized holy causes.

His mother, [[Ithildra Caldrith-Thralen]], shaped his discipline with ledgers, accounts, quiet devotions, and the belief that duty was most sacred when no one applauded.

His father, [[Haldrik Thralen]], gave him the older Thralen lesson: oathbound service was not theater. It was weight.

Aledrik carried both.

## Appearance

Aledrik was remembered as a broad, severe man with long fair hair, a hard-set face, and the worn presence of a lord more comfortable in armor than embroidered court dress.

He looked like the sort of man who had been told to relax exactly once and had judged the advice unworthy of further study.

His armor was kept polished but not ornamental. His cloak was good wool, repaired when needed. His sword was a working blade, not a court toy.

Those who served him remembered his eyes most clearly: direct, appraising, and rarely surprised.

## The Durathen Marriage

Aledrik’s marriage to [[Myreth Durathen]] was both alliance and gamble.

[[House Durathen]] brought stubborn honor, local respect, and the careful survival instincts of poor nobility. House Thralen brought a dwindling inheritance, martial reputation, and dangerous proximity to the Church of Heironeous.

Together, Aledrik and Myreth built a household founded less on luxury than endurance.

It was not a courtly romance full of lute songs and moonlit nonsense.

It was better than that.

It was practical, durable, and fierce beneath the surface. They understood scarcity. They understood obligation. They understood that noble survival often meant making one decent cloak last ten winters while smiling at people wearing enough velvet to upholster a chapel.

Myreth respected Aledrik’s steadiness.

Aledrik respected Myreth’s mind.

Together, they held a house under pressure.

After his death, Myreth did not merely mourn him. She carried him as a standard of comparison, which was unfortunate for almost everyone else.

## Character

### Militant Steward

Aledrik was a fighting lord, but not a glory-hound.

He believed a noble’s sword mattered only if the fields behind it still fed people afterward. His levies were trained hard, equipped as well as the house could manage, and expected to hold ground that richer lords might have abandoned while composing impressive letters about necessary withdrawal.

He was remembered for defending farmland, roads, and fords against raiders, brigands, and border threats.

He did not speak often of honor.

He expected it to be visible in the condition of his people.

### Pragmatic Commander

Aledrik’s watchwords were simple:

> “Hold the line.”

That was not a slogan for banners.

It was an instruction.

He valued discipline over frenzy, formation over heroics, and duty over personal brilliance. He did not despise courage, but he had little patience for men who confused courage with making themselves expensive corpses.

His command style was calm, blunt, and deeply annoying to fools.

Naturally, this made him effective.

### Family First

Aledrik was not soft, but he was present.

Braga remembered him sparring with her as a child, correcting her stance with the same seriousness he would have given a squire. He did not treat her fire as a problem to be extinguished. He treated it as a blade that needed a grip.

Garren was his hope for continuity. The son who might preserve the Thralen name, recover the family’s position, and keep the house from being swallowed by stronger neighbors with better accountants and worse souls.

After Garren’s death, that hope became a wound that never closed.

### Measured Faith

Aledrik served [[Heironeous]] in the quiet Gorundian manner of a local lord.

He attended rites. He honored musters. He kept his oaths. He believed courage, order, and justice were not abstract virtues but habits practiced until needed.

Unlike Cwenheld and Clytus, he did not become a symbol of the church.

He became something less celebrated and more necessary: a dependable man.

The horror.

## Relationship with Braga

For [[Braga Kharne]], Aledrik is not simply “father.”

He is the proof that strength does not need cruelty, that command does not require vanity, and that a noble can stand beside common folk without becoming less noble.

Braga’s style is louder, brasher, and far more inclined to solve moral ambiguity with blunt steel. But underneath the theatrical violence and well-earned swagger, Aledrik’s lesson remains:

> “Hold the line.”

Not because it is glorious.

Because someone has to.

## Relationship with Myreth

Aledrik and Myreth were partners in the old hard sense.

They did not have the luxury of softness as a household policy. Their lands, obligations, and prospects required competence before sentiment. Still, affection lived there, not as public sweetness, but as trust.

Aledrik trusted Myreth’s judgment.

Myreth trusted Aledrik’s steadiness.

That mattered more than poetry.

After his death, Myreth’s grief hardened. She lost the man who could steady her worst instincts without belittling them. When Garren later died because of DeSalle’s reckless command, there was no Aledrik left to hold the house back from vengeance.

That absence shaped everything.

## Relationship with Garren

[[Garren Thralen]] was Aledrik’s son and the last real hope for the direct continuation of the Thralen line.

Aledrik trained him as heir, soldier, and future lord. Not for courtly shine. Not for tournament nonsense. For service.

Garren inherited Aledrik’s sense of duty, and that duty put him where better judgment should have kept him away: holding a rearguard during [[Baron Remy DeSalle|Remy DeSalle’s]] disastrous command.

Garren died covering consequences he did not create.

Which is about as Gorundian as tragedy gets.

## Death

Aledrik died suddenly in **1216 OR**, ending his tenure too soon.

The exact cause is usually described as sudden illness, heart failure, or one of those politely vague noble deaths that lets chroniclers avoid saying, “The world removed the load-bearing beam and acted surprised when the roof sagged.”

His death left Myreth to hold the family’s grief, debts, alliances, and vengeance.

It also left Braga and Garren without the stabilizing force that had made House Thralen more than a remnant.

Aledrik’s death did not destroy the line by itself.

It made the later disaster possible.

## Legacy

Aledrik’s legacy is not built on conquest, court appointment, or grand miracle.

It is built on memory.

His levies remembered a lord who stood with them.

His family remembered a man who held the line.

Sorek Redmarch remembered him as one of the quiet pillars of Gorundia: the kind of lord who made the kingdom strong without demanding songs for it.

That is why his death matters.

Not because he was the greatest knight of his age.

Because he was exactly the kind of man a kingdom needs more of, and somehow always has fewer of by the end of the story.

## Connection to House Durathen

Through Myreth, Aledrik became central to the Durathen-Thralen grievance.

His death, Garren’s death, and the loss of the Thralen position created the grief-engine that drove Myreth’s vendetta against [[Baron Remy DeSalle]].

Aledrik himself would likely have preferred lawful reckoning.

Myreth, left alive to clean up the world’s incompetence, became less picky.

## Role in the Campaign

Aledrik works best as a memory-anchor rather than an active plot engine.

Use him when Braga needs emotional grounding, when Myreth’s grief needs context, or when House Thralen needs to feel like more than a dead legal claim.

He should represent the lost better version of martial nobility: stern, dutiful, practical, pious without being insufferable, and deeply tied to the people under his protection.

In other words, the sort of noble whose absence makes every worse noble look even worse.

Very useful.

Deeply inconvenient.
