# Sir Mathren Solgard

![[Mathren Solgard.png|320]]

> [!quote]  
> “You ever notice how the Church sends two kinds of men?  
> Ones that make you feel better…  
> and ones that make you tell the truth.”  
>   
> “…Solgard’s the second kind.”  
> — [[Sorek Redmarch]]

> [!quote]  
> “Solgard doesn’t raise his voice. That’s how you know you’re in trouble. Men like him don’t shout at darkness. They open the shutters.”  
> — [[Sorek Redmarch]]

## Basic Information

**Born:** Unknown, likely late 1180s or early 1190s OR  
**House:** None of note  
**Faith:** [[Pelor]]  
**Order:** Third Radiant Inquiry  
**Formal Title:** Dawn Marshal of the Third Radiant Inquiry  
**Offices:** Warden of False Flame; Keeper of the Black Threshold  
**Status:** Active  
**Current Role:** Pelorite paladin, religious examiner, royal witness, and field operative in the Blackvale campaign

---

## Overview

Sir Mathren Solgard is a knight of the Dawnfather not made for ceremony, but for judgment.

He belongs to the side of [[Pelor]]’s faith most people prefer not to think about too closely.

He is not the smiling village healer.

He is not the warm priest with bread, blankets, and patient blessings.

He is what arrives when mercy has already looked at the wound, smelled rot, and reached for the knife.

Solgard’s duty is not primarily to preach.

It is to **verify**.

He is sent when whispers have already become problems, when faith has begun to rot at the edges, or when something wearing holiness may, in fact, be a lie.

He determines:

- whether a miracle is true or false
- whether a preacher serves the light or themselves
- whether a priest has bent doctrine or broken it
- whether a claimed revelation comes from grace, delusion, vanity, or corruption
- whether holy purpose has begun crossing into unholy means

He does not concern himself with comfort.

Only with truth.

Which is inconvenient, because comfort is usually what everyone was hoping for.

---

## Titles and Offices

Sir Mathren Solgard bears three major titles:

- **Dawn Marshal of the Third Radiant Inquiry**
- **Warden of False Flame**
- **Keeper of the Black Threshold**

These are not honors given for valor alone.

They are burdens carried by those sent when the Church requires certainty.

Not opinion.

Not politics.

Certainty.

### Dawn Marshal of the Third Radiant Inquiry

As Dawn Marshal, Solgard acts as a senior field authority of a Pelorite investigative order concerned with doctrinal truth, corrupted sanctity, false miracle-working, and compromised servants of the light.

The Third Radiant Inquiry is not the face of Pelor shown to farmers at harvest festivals.

It is the hand that opens locked shrine doors after midnight.

It is the order that asks why the local saint never casts a shadow.

It is the voice that says:

> “Again. This time, tell the truth.”

### Warden of False Flame

As Warden of False Flame, Solgard investigates claims of divine light, miracle-working, unauthorized revelation, corrupted Pelorite practice, and false sanctity.

This is the authority under which he examined [[Druuna Sunseeker]].

A false flame is not merely a heresy.

It is something more dangerous: a thing that gives warmth, comfort, and identity while leading souls away from the Dawnfather.

Solgard treats false sanctity as spiritual arson.

### Keeper of the Black Threshold

As Keeper of the Black Threshold, Solgard is trusted to judge cases where holy purpose risks crossing into corruption.

This office concerns:

- forbidden tools used for righteous ends
- soul-taint
- necromantic compromise
- infernal contamination
- shadow-magic
- coerced miracles
- righteous violence that has stopped remembering mercy
- the seductive lie that evil becomes clean when aimed at the correct target

It is not a rank.

It is a burden.

Solgard earned the office after testifying against his own mentor, **Dawn Captain Armand Helior**, who crossed the Black Threshold by using a soul-fueled shadow blade in the name of necessity.

Helior did not fall all at once.

He justified.

Then escalated.

Then killed unjustly.

Solgard was the one who named the crossing.

That history defines him.

He does not fear hard choices.

He fears the moment a hard choice becomes an excuse.

## Appearance

Solgard is tall, broad, and physically grounded, with the dense presence of a veteran who has worn armor more often than soft clothes.

He keeps his head shaved.

His face is hard, weathered, and scarred, with the expression of a man who has seen what happens when light arrives too late.

His eyes are steady rather than warm.

He does not glare unless he means it.

He rarely needs to.

Those who have stood before him often remark on the same thing:

He does not loom.

He **settles**.

Like stone.

His armor is practical battle-plate, dulled by travel and use rather than polished for ceremony. It bears restrained Pelorite markings: sunburst etching, oath-script, and pale gold inlays worn thin at the edges.

The sunburst upon it is worked into the steel, not displayed upon it.

He does not glitter.

He endures.

Nothing about him is decorative.

That is the point.

---

## Bearing and Temperament

Solgard is controlled, direct, and deeply uncomfortable with theatricality unless it serves a sacred purpose.

He believes mercy is holy, but not sentimental.

He does not confuse compassion with permissiveness, and he has little patience for fools who hide cowardice behind kindness.

He is not cruel.

Nor is he warm.

He is **precise**.

He does not punish weakness.

He does not excuse deceit.

He will listen longer than most expect.

He will judge faster than most prefer.

He measures before he strikes, listens before he condemns, and gives the accused every meaningful chance to stand in truth.

But once he judges, the judgment holds.

He does not raise his voice to command a room.

He simply speaks, and the room arranges itself around that.

He does not drink while on duty.

He is almost always on duty.

---

## The Inquiry at the Blood and Sand

Sir Mathren came to [[Bard’s Gate]] on quiet authority, bearing concerns regarding [[Lyosha Ohotnik]] and reports of an unordained preacher working among [[The Dregs]].

He found the latter in the common room of the [[Blood and Sand]].

The room had prepared for violence.

He did not.

He entered, observed, and began.

The woman — [[Druuna Sunseeker]] — was questioned on doctrine, authority, motive, obedience, and spiritual source.

She failed, by formal measure, in several respects:

- she could not recite doctrine cleanly
- she held no formal ordination
- she lacked proper instruction
- she possessed zeal without training
- she spoke with the intensity of the street, not the discipline of the temple

To the wrong holy man, that could have been enough.

Solgard is not the wrong holy man.

He found that Druuna did not claim what was not hers.

She did not seek coin.

She did not bind others to herself.

She fed the hungry, tended wounds, discouraged vengeance, and spoke Pelor’s name without twisting it.

Solgard listened.

Considered.

And rendered judgment:

> “You are imperfect… but you are not false.”

He then declared before witnesses:

> “Druuna Sunseeker is no heretic.”

That judgment mattered.

It protected Druuna from harsher religious consequences and, by implication, confirmed that [[Lyosha Ohotnik]]’s influence had not corrupted her faith.

Solgard ordered instruction.

He offered protection.

And he made it quietly understood that harm done to her under Pelor’s light would not go unanswered.

His private view was recorded more sharply:

> “Ignorance can be taught. Fraud can be exposed. Heresy must be cut out. The girl is the first.”

After the judgment, Druuna was placed under formal instruction and protection rather than censure.

---

## On Lyosha Ohotnik

Though Solgard did not confront [[Lyosha Ohotnik]] directly, he examined the man through records, testimony, temple accounts, and the spiritual condition of those shaped by his work.

His conclusion was recorded simply:

**[[Lyosha Ohotnik]] stands within doctrine.**

No hidden corruption.

No deviation requiring censure.

Severity, yes.

Corruption, no.

For a name such as Ohotnik, that judgment carries weight.

It does not make Lyosha safe.

It makes him recognized.

There is a difference, and Solgard would absolutely make you explain it before he let you leave the room.

## Witness to Porthos Ryomar

Solgard’s importance deepened when he was present as [[Porthos Ryomar]] stood in the light of the Dawnfather and swore himself as a paladin of [[Pelor]].

This was not a coronation.

Not a royal ceremony.

Not a political convenience.

Porthos stood bareheaded and unarmed beneath the morning sun, not as prince or heir, but as a man to be judged.

Solgard was the older, scarred Pelorite knight who stepped forward and tested him.

He told Porthos:

> “You stand in the light of the Dawnfather.”

> “Not as prince.”

> “Not as heir.”

> “But as a man to be judged.”

When Porthos swore the oaths, Solgard listened not like a courtier hearing pretty words, but like a blade laid across a scale.

Then he placed a hand on Porthos’s shoulder and said:

> “The sun does not care who you are.”

> “Only what you do.”

Then, louder:

> “Stand.”

> “Stand as a knight of the Dawnfather.”

This gives Solgard unusual religious authority in the current crisis.

He did not merely witness Porthos making an oath.

He confirmed that the oath answered something real.

When crown and church both require certainty, they send Solgard.

And they accept what he says.

Whether they like it or not.

## Current Role: Castle Blackvale

Solgard is currently part of the Blackvale operation alongside:

- [[Thranduil Stormarrow]]
- [[Aylassa]]
- an unnamed half-elf wizard of exceptional stealth
- other royal-aligned assets as needed

This is an awkward assignment for him.

He is perfectly capable of violence, endurance, judgment, and holy confrontation.

He is less comfortable moving through enemy-held halls beside people who treat silence like breathing.

[[Thranduil Stormarrow]] moves like a forest killing something that deserves it.

[[Aylassa]] moves like a murder ballad that learned lockpicks.

The half-elf wizard is quiet enough to make Solgard wonder if wizards are being manufactured incorrectly elsewhere.

Solgard, meanwhile, is a large armored Pelorite paladin trying very hard not to sound like a dropped kitchen.

He does not complain.

He does not ask to leave.

But he feels every creaking board as a personal theological insult.

---

## Relationship with Thranduil Stormarrow

Solgard respects [[Thranduil Stormarrow]] but does not entirely understand him.

Thranduil’s morality is not courtly, clerical, or civilized in the usual Gorundian sense. It is older, harsher, and more feral.

Solgard recognizes that Thranduil is not evil, but he also knows the grugach warchief’s idea of justice may involve considerably less paperwork and considerably more arterial spray.

Their working relationship is built on mutual competence rather than warmth.

Solgard does not try to command Thranduil.

Thranduil likely respects him more because of that.

Solgard’s private assessment is simple:

**Thranduil is dangerous, truthful, and insufficiently troubled by locked doors.**

That places him above many nobles.

---

## Relationship with Aylassa

Solgard is wary of [[Aylassa]].

Not because she is an assassin.

Well.

Not only because she is an assassin.

He recognizes that she has rules.

Strange rules, theatrical rules, occasionally horrifying rules — but rules.

She is not random.

She is not wanton.

She is not chaos with cheekbones.

This unsettles him more than if she were simply wicked.

Wicked people are easy.

Aylassa is something more irritating: morally legible from an angle Solgard does not enjoy standing in.

He does not approve of her.

He does not dismiss her.

And he absolutely watches where her hands are.

---

## Relationship with Riven Halcyre

Solgard respects [[Riven Halcyre]].

He hates needing him.

Riven Halcyre, called **the Veilhand**, is the stealth half-elf wizard attached to the Blackvale team: quiet, precise, controlled, and nearly impossible to read. He is a royal operative of the Ninth Seal, built for magic, secrecy, and political work too delicate to survive ordinary knightly methods.

His magic is subtle by design: invisibility, silence, sound and light manipulation, blur, haste, apportation, lock-work, anti-detection, and quiet extraction. The kind of spellwork that makes a locked door feel embarrassed for having tried.

To Solgard, Riven is unsettling because he is not merely stealthy.

He is disciplined.

That matters.

A criminal hides because he fears consequence.  
A coward hides because he fears danger.  
Riven hides because the mission requires the world to remain unaware it has already been handled.

Solgard can respect that.

He still dislikes it.

Riven can stand in a shadow and make the shadow feel overdressed. He can cross a room without disturbing the dust, speak softly enough that even guilt has to lean in, and vanish from notice with the calm efficiency of a man who has never once needed armor to feel dangerous.

Solgard understands why the Crown uses him.

He understands why [[Thranduil Stormarrow]] works well with him.

He understands why [[Aylassa]] does not appear bothered by him.

That does not make standing beside him any more comfortable.

Privately, Solgard finds Riven’s methods too close to the edge of things he is sworn to examine: concealment, manipulation, silence, hidden movement, unseen hands.

But Riven is not false flame.

He is not corruption wearing virtue.

He is a blade kept under a cloak because open steel would ruin the work.

Solgard respects the skill.

He hates needing it.

And he especially hates that Riven probably weighs less than Solgard’s shield and is still better suited to half the dangers of Castle Blackvale.

---

## Private Insecurity

Solgard’s hidden flaw is not fear.

It is displacement.

He is used to walking into darkness as judgment made flesh. He is used to being the thing that evil sees when the door opens and the lies stop working.

But Blackvale requires silence, patience, infiltration, and trust in people whose methods sit outside his natural instincts.

He feels too loud.

Too heavy.

Too obvious.

Too slow.

He will never say this.

If pressed, he will frame it as tactical concern.

But privately, he is aware that he is operating in a world where [[Thranduil Stormarrow]], [[Aylassa]], and the half-elf wizard are scalpels — and he is a consecrated warhammer wrapped in white cloth and told to “be subtle.”

---

## Religious Philosophy

Solgard’s faith is Pelorite to the bone, but it is Pelor as dawn after a massacre, not Pelor as noon in a wheat field.

To Solgard, mercy is not softness.

Mercy is the disciplined refusal to become what one destroys.

He believes evil must be confronted, but he also believes that righteous men are most endangered when they begin congratulating themselves for how necessary they have become.

His mentor’s fall taught him the shape of that road.

First comes necessity.

Then exception.

Then habit.

Then doctrine.

Then atrocity with a holy name.

Solgard’s role is to stand at that threshold and say:

**No farther.**

---

## What He Certifies

A judgment by Solgard carries unusual force because he is not known for generous approval.

If he condemns someone, the Church listens.

If he clears someone, the Church also listens — often with irritation, because Solgard’s approval tends to create political problems for people who were hoping for a clean condemnation.

He has now effectively certified:

- [[Druuna Sunseeker]] is ignorant but not false
- [[Lyosha Ohotnik]] stands within doctrine
- [[Porthos Ryomar]] has been truly claimed by Pelor’s light

That is a strange and powerful triangle.

A street preacher.

A scarred Ohotnik priest.

A crown prince.

Solgard did not set out to become a hinge in royal and religious history.

Too bad.

The hinge rarely gets asked.

## Mannerisms

- Speaks quietly.
- Holds eye contact longer than most people enjoy.
- Touches the sunburst on his armor before making a hard judgment.
- Pauses before answering theological questions.
- Never fidgets in public.
- Does not drink while on duty.
- Does not sigh, but somehow makes silence feel like a sigh.
- In stealth situations, becomes painfully still and visibly annoyed at every accidental sound.
- Watches hands during questioning.
- Uses titles precisely, and withholds them precisely.
- Does not threaten often, because he considers unnecessary threats a kind of vanity.

## Possible Scene Beat: The Board Has Confessed

[[Thranduil Stormarrow]] pauses ahead, one hand raised.

[[Aylassa]] freezes instantly.

The half-elf wizard simply stops existing in any practical sense.

Solgard stops too.

One floorboard gives the faintest creak beneath his boot.

No one moves.

No one breathes.

After three long seconds, [[Aylassa]] turns her head just enough to look at him.

Solgard says nothing.

Then, barely audible:

> “The board has confessed.”

---

## Final Measure

There are brighter paladins.

There are kinder priests.

There are louder men.

Sir Mathren Solgard is none of these.

He is what remains when faith is stripped of comfort, pride, and ornament — and tested against what is real.

And in such matters, he is very difficult to argue with.

> “If he condemns you, you are finished.  
> If he approves you… you are now everyone else’s problem.”

---

## DM Notes

Solgard should be played as morally serious, not humorless.

He can dryly acknowledge absurdity, but he never winks at it. His comedy lands because he refuses to perform it.

In Blackvale scenes, keep his insecurity understated. He is not bumbling. He is painfully aware that he has been placed among specialists whose talents make his own strengths feel crude.

That gives him a good internal arc: learning that the warhammer still matters, even when the scalpels go first.

His key table function is not “Pelor cop.”

That is too small.

He is the man who can look at a miracle, a heresy, a prince, a street preacher, or a righteous atrocity and ask the same question:

**Is this true?**

And then everyone gets very quiet.
