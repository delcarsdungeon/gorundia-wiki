# Tovan Reedmere

_Low-rank human druid, caretaker of the Great Tree of Bard’s Gate_

> “Never trust a man who can’t keep a secret from a tree. The tree might deserve to know. The rest of us usually don’t.”  
> — Sorek Redmarch

## Core Identity

**Name:** Tovan Reedmere  
**Race:** Human  
**Age:** 31  
**Role:** Caretaker-druid of the Great Tree grove in Bard’s Gate  
**Rank:** Junior Grovewarden / sworn lay-druid of the Verdant Compact  
**Public Reputation:** Harmless, sincere, useful, and constitutionally unable to shut up once someone asks the right question  
**Private Problem:** He knows more than he understands

Tovan is not a mighty archdruid. He is not one of the grand voices at Bannerhall. He is the guy who knows which moss is supposed to grow on the north root, which squirrel is secretly a malicious little bastard, which noble children carved initials into the bark, and which city reeve has been quietly dumping kitchen ash too close to the sacred runoff channel.

In other words: the guy who actually keeps the damn place alive.

## The Dryad

**Recommended lock:** **Auraleth of the Great Tree**  
**Common name among locals:** Lady Green-in-the-Boughs  
**Children call her:** Aunt Leaf  
**Older elves call her:** Auraleth Root-Sung  
**Tovan calls her:** “my lady,” “she,” or, when flustered, “her green highness,” which she pretends to dislike.

Auraleth is bound to the Great Tree itself, not merely “living nearby.” She is the spirit-presence of the tree’s surviving heartwood. She appears rarely, usually as a woman formed of pale bark, green-gold hair, and leaves that never seem to belong to the current season.

She is young by dryad standards but ancient by human ones. That gives her a weird vibe: emotionally direct, curious, playful, but occasionally possessed of memories from before half the city was stone. Dryads are like that. Cute woodland girl one minute, thousand-yard stare into the age of roots the next. Whiplash, but make it botanical.

## The Great Tree of Bard’s Gate

The Great Tree should feel like a living civic relic, not just “a park tree with magic DLC.”

It stands in a walled grove near the older sacred quarter of Bard’s Gate, where Pelorite, halfling, and Verdant customs overlap uneasily but productively. The religion file already supports that Gorundia has practical, communal religious structures, including halfling nature traditions through Sheela Peryroyl and elven Verdant Invocation echoes.

The tree is:

- an old pact-marker from before Bard’s Gate became crowded and political;   
- a civic symbol used by druids, Pelorites, halflings, and elves for different reasons;    
- one of the few places in the city where oaths sworn “by root and light” still have weight;    
- possibly older than the city walls;    
- absolutely not something the reeves should be allowed to “improve” with paving stones, because that way lies municipal war crimes.    

## Tovan’s Job

Tovan handles the boring sacred work:

- pruning deadwood without offending Auraleth;    
- keeping apprentices and drunk lovers from damaging the bark;    
- watering, mulching, sweeping, and checking root health;    
- mediating between city officials and grove custom;    
- receiving donations;    
- filing maintenance accounts badly;    
- pretending he understands noble discretion;    
- failing at that last one.    

He has minor druidic magic, enough to encourage growth, speak with birds, sense blight, calm animals, and identify pollution or spiritual rot. He is more gardener-priest than combat caster.

## Personality

Tovan is earnest, sunburned, slightly muddy, and dangerously transparent.

He has that apprentice-clergy problem where he thinks honesty and truth are the same thing. They are not. One is a virtue. The other is a loaded crossbow left on a bench.

He does not gossip maliciously. He just answers questions.

Badly.

With details.

Usually while holding a watering can.

## The Zara Donation

Zara quietly made a very large donation to the grove’s maintenance.

Not “nice lady bought flowers” money.

Actual structural money.

The gift paid for:

- new root-channel stones;    
- replacement of cracked warding-posts;    
- discreet repair of old elven boundary runes;    
- removal of iron contamination from the soil near the western gate;    
- three years of gardener stipends;    
- imported loam from Araisal;    
- quiet payment of back debts owed to city laborers;    
- a restored watercourse feeding the deep root-bed;    
- an emergency fund for “storm, fire, axe, or noble stupidity.”    

Given Zara’s county wealth is tied to stone, mines, roads, and dwarven contracts, and she often works through intermediaries like the Galibanos, this sort of donation can plausibly arrive as “anonymous civic restoration funding” routed through boring ledgers and trade houses.

### How Zara Tried to Hide It

The donation was made under the name:

**“A Friend of the Root.”**

The clerk’s receipt was sealed.

The grove ledger listed no noble house.

The workers were paid through a third-party stone-and-water maintenance contract.

There was explicitly to be no plaque.

### How Tovan Spills It

Tovan, glowing with pride:

> “Oh, no plaque. She was very clear. Very clear. No plaque, no heraldry, no formal thanks, no mention at temple breakfast. Which I thought was very humble of Countess Zara, really. I mean, for someone with a dragon on her coat of arms, that’s restraint.”

Then he realizes.

Then he slowly looks down at the watering can like it has betrayed him.

## Why Zara Did It

This works best if it is not a PR move.

Zara is cleaning up her act, and Jinwing’s mercy hit something real. The grove is a safe target for repentance because:

1. It helps something living.    
2. It does not demand public vulnerability.    
3. It is not directly political.    
4. It may soothe old draconic guilt around sacred groves and guardianship.    
5. It lets her do good without standing in front of everyone wearing the “I Am Humbled Now” sandwich board.    

Also, if she is tied to old dragon/guardian themes, grove restoration is not random. The existing dragon notes distinguish integrated druidic powers from corrupting/dominating dragons, and Zara’s arc is now hovering right over that line like a cat deciding whether to knock the glass off the table.

## Tovan’s Relationship with Auraleth

Tovan adores Auraleth, but not romantically. More like a junior priest assigned to care for a living saint who also steals his lunch apples.

Auraleth likes him because he is easy to read. She finds most city people noisy, armored in lies, and rootless. Tovan is rooted. Shallowly, maybe, but rooted.

She knows about Zara’s donation.

She also knows Zara wanted it quiet.

She has not decided whether Tovan spilling it was a mistake or the grove’s will.

That ambiguity is nice and annoying. Very druidic. Ten out of ten, no notes.

## Tovan at the Table

Use him as a soft exposition NPC who accidentally drops live grenades.

### First Impression

A lanky human in a patched green-brown robe is kneeling beside exposed roots, carefully painting a paste of clay, honey, ash, and crushed herbs into a crack in the bark. His sleeves are rolled up. His hair is full of leaves. A robin is standing on his shoulder, judging everyone.

He looks up and says:

> “Oh. Hello. Please don’t step there. That patch is sacred, fragile, and recently fertilized. In that order.”

### If Asked About the Donation

> “Anonymous. Entirely anonymous. Very generously anonymous. Noble-quality anonymous, if I’m being honest. Which I’m apparently not supposed to be.”

### If Asked Directly Whether It Was Zara

He tries to lie.

He is terrible at it.

> “Countess who? No. I mean—many countesses exist. Statistically. In the realm. Some of them red-haired. Some of them extremely specific about stone drainage.”

### If Auraleth Appears

The leaves shift though there is no wind.

Tovan immediately stands straighter.

> “My lady, I was only explaining the maintenance accounts.”

Auraleth:

> “You were explaining secrets.”

Tovan:

> “That is… also true.”

## Hooks

**The Anonymous Gift Becomes Political**  
Someone learns Zara paid for the grove. Her enemies frame it as manipulation: “dragon buys tree, news at eleven.” Her allies frame it as repentance. Auraleth refuses to be used as anyone’s moral laundering service.

**The Grove’s Wards Were Worse Than Anyone Knew**  
Zara’s money did not beautify the grove. It repaired containment. Something had been leaking through the old root wards.

**The Tree Accepted the Gift**  
This matters. If the Great Tree or Auraleth had rejected Zara’s money, the work would have failed. It did not. That does not absolve Zara, but it suggests the living world is willing to take her offering.

**Tovan Has the Receipt**  
A properly sealed receipt exists, because Tovan is bad at politics but oddly good at keeping garden accounts. Leena, Cornelia, the Lantern Watch, or a Galibano clerk might all want it.

## Drop-In Scene

Tovan is halfway inside a hedge when the party arrives.

Only his boots and backside are visible.

A muffled voice says:

> “One moment. If I pull too hard, this whole section sulks.”

There is a rustle, a curse, and he backs out covered in leaves.

He sees the party.

Then Zara.

Then Jinwing.

Then the exact expression of a man whose mouth is about to outrun his survival instincts.

> “Oh! Countess! The western root-channel took beautifully. You were right about the stone lining. Well—your people were right. Or whoever definitely wasn’t you.”

Silence.

A leaf falls.

Tovan looks at Jinwing.

> “I’ve done it again, haven’t I?”

Auraleth’s voice comes from somewhere above:

> “Yes, little reed.”

Tovan closes his eyes.

> “Right. Wonderful.”
