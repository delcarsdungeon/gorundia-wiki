# Alwenna Kestrel

> “Every Warden requires a clerk.”  
> — [[Rimmand II]]  
>
> “I am a Truthseeker, Your Majesty.”  
> — Alwenna Kestrel

**Alwenna Kestrel** is a junior Truthseeker of [[Pholtus]], assigned by [[Rimmand II]] to assist [[Thalrik Vaultforge]] in his role as [[Warden of the King's Law]].

This was not an act of mercy.

It was royal engineering.

Thalrik is brilliant, decisive, extremely effective, and occasionally inclined to solve problems with artillery, explosives, loopholes, or a degree of dwarven directness that makes lawyers reach for smelling salts. The king therefore gave him something almost as dangerous:

A Pholtine witness with a notebook.

Alwenna is not Thalrik’s clerk.

She is his legal shield, legitimacy engine, procedural conscience, and portable royal warning label.

Also, yes, she probably has the right form.

She has all the forms.

## Appearance

![[Alwenna Kestrel.png|220]]

Alwenna is the sort of woman people underestimate instantly.

She is small — barely **5'1"**, slight, and immaculately composed.

Her pale blonde hair is worn in a tight braided scholar’s knot. Round spectacles rest on the bridge of her nose and slide down when she reads.

Her robes are **temple white trimmed with narrow gold lines**, the vestments of a junior Truthseeker of [[Pholtus]].

At her belt hangs:

- a **small mace**, perfectly maintained and rarely used
- three **ink pens**
- a **wax seal kit**
- a **leather-bound codex of royal law**

A satchel filled with scroll tubes, legal notes, writs, reference tablets, and emergency documentation never leaves her side.

She writes **everything down**.

Everything.

This is not a personality quirk.

This is a threat.

## Personality

Alwenna is:

- precise
- methodical
- patient
- relentless
- observant
- unflappable
- quietly judgmental
- almost never louder than necessary

She is not timid.

She simply prefers **facts to noise**.

When arguments erupt around her, she waits.

Then eventually says something like:

> “According to Royal Charter Seven, Subsection Twelve… that is incorrect.”

She never raises her voice.

She does not need to.

Alwenna has the dreadful calm of a woman who knows the exact penalty for what you are about to do.

## Her Faith

Alwenna believes completely in the doctrine of [[Pholtus]]:

**Law reveals truth.  
Truth reveals order.  
Order reveals righteousness.**

She does not care about politics in the ordinary sense.

She cares about **correctness**.

If the Warden acts lawfully, she will defend him against anyone.

Duke.

Priest.

General.

Doesn’t matter.

If he does not act lawfully?

She will tell him so.

Immediately.

This is not disloyalty.

To Alwenna, the Law outranks everyone.

Even the Warden.

Especially the Warden.

## Rank and Office

Alwenna is best understood as a **Truthseeker of Pholtus**, operating in the same broad institutional world as Pholtine Truthkeepers, Sister-Magistrixes, Brother-Magistrates, White Advocates, Lantern Examiners, and court-priests.

She is not a high-ranking national authority.

That would make her easier to ignore in the field, oddly enough.

Instead, she is a Crown-attached Pholtine legal witness whose power comes from three overlapping authorities:

- her ordination and training under [[Pholtus]]
- her attachment to the Warden of the King's Law, [[Thalrik Vaultforge]]
- the Crown’s explicit delegation of legal authority

In practice, she functions as:

- legal witness
- royal procedure officer
- truth-work assistant
- warrant certifier
- chain-of-authority recorder
- jurisdictional examiner
- portable archive of inconvenient law
- tiny holy artillery piece in spectacles

Her presence means Thalrik’s actions are not merely done.

They are witnessed.

Recorded.

Justified.

Or condemned.

## Why the King Sent Her

Officially, Alwenna was assigned to assist [[Thalrik Vaultforge]] in the enforcement of the King’s Law.

Unofficially, the king knows exactly what he is doing.

Thalrik is:

- brilliant
- decisive
- extremely effective
- occasionally inclined to solve problems with artillery

The king therefore provided him with:

1. A walking legal authority
2. A temple witness of [[Pholtus]]
3. Someone who can say **“Yes, this is lawful”** before the cannon fires

Or occasionally:

> “No, Warden. That is technically treason.”

This is not a leash.

It is worse.

It is documentation.

## The King’s Joke

At the ceremony, the king said:

> “Every Warden requires a clerk.”

Alwenna corrected him immediately.

> “I am a Truthseeker, Your Majesty.”

The king smiled.

> “Of course you are.”

Thalrik later realized the king had handed him **a walking statute book with a holy symbol**.

This is why kings should not be allowed to be funny. They get ideas.

## Her Legal Superpower

### The Lantern Mind

Alwenna remembers **structure**, not just facts.

While others argue, she is silently building:

- timelines
- chains of authority
- contradictions between witnesses
- jurisdictional limits
- document trails
- procedural vulnerabilities
- who said what, when, and under whose authority

She does not interrogate emotionally.

She constructs **legal traps for lies**.

Example moment:

A suspect contradicts himself several times.

The crew ignores it.

Alwenna closes her notebook.

> “You previously stated you were at the harbor.”

A pause.

> “The dockmaster testified you left the district two hours earlier.”

Another pause.

> “You cannot have been in both places.”

She adjusts her glasses.

> “Which statement would you like to amend?”

The crew realizes:

**She does not hunt criminals.  
She traps lies.**

## Her Relationship with Thalrik

She addresses him always as:

> “Warden.”

Not Admiral.

Not Captain.

Not Sir.

Because in her mind, the title granted by the king carries **legal authority over all others**.

Thalrik may have collected titles the way a dwarf collects grudges, but **Warden** is the title that matters to her.

She studies him with academic fascination.

Thalrik represents **the living instrument of royal justice**, which is exactly the sort of thing Truthseekers write treatises about and then make everyone else regret having learned to read.

Sometimes he notices her taking notes while he argues with someone.

If asked why:

> “I am documenting precedent.”

## The Loophole and the Reattachment

When Thalrik was “activated” under emergency royal authority, he quickly discovered a loophole that allowed him to detach Alwenna from his immediate command structure.

This was, technically, legal.

That made it more annoying.

Thalrik’s reasoning was probably sound by Thalrik standards: move faster, carry fewer witnesses, reduce procedural drag, avoid having a Pholtine Truthseeker calmly explain why something was “actionable misconduct” while the deck was already on fire.

Alwenna did not argue.

She documented.

Then she went to [[Beringer|the Lord Marshal]].

She presented the matter plainly:

- The Warden had found a valid loophole.
- The loophole permitted temporary detachment.
- The loophole did not negate the Crown’s intent.
- The Warden’s authority would be stronger, not weaker, with a Pholtine witness attached.
- The realm was entering a period in which lawful record would be essential.

The Lord Marshal approved her reattachment.

Alwenna returned to Thalrik with the approval in hand.

A copy.

The original had already been filed.

There were three copies.

Of course there were three copies.

When Thalrik objected, she likely said something like:

> “Your prior detachment order was valid, Warden.”

A pause.

> “This supersedes it.”

Another pause.

> “I have retained the original.”

This is the kind of sentence that makes even artillery feel underdressed.

## How the Crew First Sees Her

Initial impression:

A fragile temple scholar.

Tiny. Polite. Bookish.

Second impression:

Oh no.

She remembers everything.

If someone contradicts themselves days later, she will calmly say:

> “You previously stated the opposite on the twelfth.”

The crew eventually understands that Alwenna is not harmless.

She is simply dangerous in a language most adventurers avoid learning.

## When the Crew Starts Respecting Her

### Incident One

A minor noble attempts to bully her.

> “You will address me properly, girl.”

She adjusts her glasses and recites the royal decree empowering the Warden.

Word for word.

Then calmly concludes:

> “Your refusal to comply constitutes obstruction of royal authority.”

A pause.

> “That offense carries imprisonment.”

The noble backs down.

The crew notices.

### Incident Two

During an arrest, someone threatens her physically.

The crew reacts instantly.

Not because she needs protection.

Because harming her would mean **attacking the authority of the Crown itself**.

She becomes something like:

- the crew’s legal shield
- their walking arrest warrant
- their tiny terrifying mascot

Which is undignified.

And accurate.

## The Moment She Proves Her Worth

A powerful noble refuses arrest and claims Thalrik lacks jurisdiction.

Political standoff.

Everyone watching.

Thalrik is about two seconds from solving the problem with dwarven directness.

Then Alwenna quietly steps forward.

She opens her satchel.

Three documents appear.

One by one.

> “Royal Charter establishing the office of Warden of the King’s Law.”

Second.

> “Delegation of investigative authority.”

Third.

> “A sealed writ signed by the king.”

She turns the document toward the noble.

> “Your arrest is lawful.”

Silence.

She then looks at Thalrik.

> “Warden.”

A pause.

> “You may proceed.”

That is the moment Thalrik realizes something.

The king did not give him a clerk.

He gave him **legal artillery**.

## Her Hidden Arc

Alwenna slowly begins to see Thalrik as more than a commander.

To her, he becomes something dangerous:

**The embodiment of lawful justice in the realm.**

If he proves worthy of that trust, she becomes fiercely loyal.

If he betrays that ideal?

She will oppose him.

Without hesitation.

Because to Alwenna:

**The Law outranks everyone.**

Even the Warden.

That is not a threat she would speak aloud.

She would simply file the appropriate notice.

## The King’s Real Move

By assigning Alwenna, the king gave Thalrik three things:

1. A **legal shield**
2. A **legitimacy engine**
3. A **moral compass**

But also something else.

A Truthseeker of [[Pholtus]].

Which means every action the Warden takes now has a **witness of lawful truth**.

Not a spy.

A witness.

The difference matters.

The result is often identical.

## Legal Flexibility

Alwenna is not stupidly rigid.

This is what makes her dangerous.

She is not the kind of Pholtine who thinks the first visible statute is the end of thought. She understands jurisdiction, remedy, emergency authority, delegated powers, royal exceptions, religious witness, and the terrifying amount of space hidden inside the phrase **“under proper authority.”**

Her most dangerous line is:

> “That is technically illegal.”

A pause.

> “But it can be made legal.”

This is not corruption.

This is craft.

Alwenna does not break the law.

She finds the hinge.

She will bend, fold, splice, narrow, expand, cross-reference, and weaponize lawful procedure if the result is cleaner than chaos and still defensible under Pholtus’ light.

This is why she is useful.

This is also why sane people fear her.

## The Plague Village and Zara

Alwenna was stunned by the village struck by the cult of [[Incabulos]].

Not merely offended.

Shaken.

There are moments when even a Truthseeker’s ledgers do not prepare her for what rot looks like when it has a theology.

When Countess [[Zara]] Vallytrex apparently flew off to “handle it,” Alwenna was quietly, personally pleased.

Not officially.

Not in writing.

But pleased.

Because sometimes horror is eating people alive while the law is still arranging its seals.

Unfortunately, Alwenna is also extremely sharp.

When reports arrive, when frightened witnesses describe what they saw, when timelines do not align, when the thing in the sky becomes more than rumor, Alwenna will begin assembling the truth.

Quietly.

Carefully.

Line by line.

She may be grateful the horror ended.

She may even believe the result was preferable.

But if the facts point toward Zara being something other than the official record says she is, Alwenna will notice.

And then everyone gets to enjoy the classic Pholtine problem:

**The truth may be useful.  
The truth may be dangerous.  
It remains the truth.**

## Her First Words to Thalrik

She boards his ship, bows politely, and says:

> “Warden.”

A pause.

> “I have been assigned to assist you in the enforcement of the King’s Law.”

She opens her notebook.

> “What are we arresting first?”

## Her Running Joke with the Crew

Someone eventually asks:

> “How many laws do you actually know?”

She considers the question carefully.

Then answers:

> “All of them.”

She is not joking.

## Her Most Dangerous Line

When the crew debates doing something questionable, she will look up calmly and say:

> “Warden.”

A pause.

> “That is technically illegal.”

Another pause.

> “But it can be made legal.”

## Voice

Alwenna speaks softly, precisely, and with terrifying calm.

She does not threaten unless the law already has.

Useful lines:

> “Warden.”

> “That is incorrect.”

> “Under current authority, yes.”

> “Under current authority, no.”

> “You previously stated otherwise.”

> “Would you like to amend your testimony?”

> “That is technically illegal.”

> “It can be made legal.”

> “I have retained the original.”

> “This copy is sufficient for field use.”

> “The seal is intact.”

> “You are confusing permission with authority.”

> “No, Warden. That is not the same thing.”

> “I am documenting precedent.”

> “The law does not become unclear because you dislike it.”

> “The Lord Marshal has approved the reattachment.”

> “In triplicate.”

## Table Use

Alwenna is best used as:

- a legal brake on Thalrik
- a way to make arrests feel legitimate
- a Pholtine who is actually useful rather than merely obnoxious
- a witness whose approval makes player actions harder to dismiss
- a soft-spoken procedural menace
- someone who can defend the party from noble obstruction
- someone who can later become dangerous if the party abuses royal authority

She should not be played as a scold who stops play.

She should be played as the person who says:

> “Do it this way, and they cannot touch you.”

That makes her much scarier than a hall monitor.
