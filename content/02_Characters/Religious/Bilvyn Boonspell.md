# Bilvyn Boonspell

![[Bilvyn Boonspell.png]]

> “The gods gave us wit so that courage would not have to do all the work.”
> — Master Bilvyn Boonspell

## Overview

**Bilvyn Boonspell** is a wealthy gnome cleric of [[Garl Glittergold]], trained engineer, civic administrator, and associate member of the [[Merchant's Guild]] of [[Bard's Gate]].

He is best known in recent years as the gnome sent to restore order to the Merchant's Guild after the exposure of the [[Urdlen Cult]]. Several surviving guild interests hoped for a polite figurehead, preferably one impressed by gifts, deference, and carefully folded promissory notes.

Instead, they received Bilvyn Boonspell.

By the end of his first month, three ledgers had been impounded, two warehouse contracts had been voided, seven apprentices had been quietly reassigned, and an alarming number of merchants had rediscovered both honesty and indigestion.

Bilvyn is now attached to [[Orin Kharne]]’s field force, having assembled a small but highly useful gnomish support company: two sparkwrights, another priest of Garl, and a team of sappers, surveyors, and engineers. He arrived with [[Morg]] and the half-orc volunteers, a practical arrangement neither side found especially charming.

## Canonical Name

The correct spelling is:

**Bilvyn Boonspell**

The spelling **Bilvin Boonspell** appears in some older rolls and hurried military correspondence, but should be treated as a scribal error.

Bilvyn himself notices the error every time.

He does not always correct it.

This is generally taken as a bad sign.

## Appearance

Bilvyn is an older gnome with sharp blue eyes, white hair, and a full white beard kept with the exacting neatness of a man who considers presentation part of public trust.

He favors blue traveling robes and good practical armor: polished shoulder plates, layered cloth, strong belts, and a clerical-engineer’s cap marked with tasteful metalwork. His dress is too functional to be courtly and too expensive to be mistaken for humble.

His face carries the permanent expression of a man who has read the numbers, found them wanting, and is waiting for the guilty party to stop talking.

## Public Reputation

In Bard's Gate, Bilvyn is regarded as honest, capable, and difficult to misdirect.

Merchants respect him because he understands money.

Clerics respect him because he understands duty.

Engineers respect him because he understands load-bearing stone.

Criminals dislike him because he understands all three.

His reputation among the gnomes of Bard's Gate is especially strong. He is considered a credit to temple, guild, family, and city, though some younger gnomes consider him rather severe for a priest of Garl Glittergold.

They say this quietly.

Usually behind doors.

Preferably while he is outside the building.

## Cleric of Garl Glittergold

Bilvyn’s devotion to [[Garl Glittergold]] is practical, civic-minded, and deeply gnomish. He teaches that wit is not merely amusement, but a holy defense against fear, tyranny, stupidity, and despair.

He has little patience for joyless piety. He has even less patience for using humor as an excuse for cruelty.

To Bilvyn, Garl’s laughter is the sound of survival.

A tyrant hates mockery because mockery proves the tyrant has failed to become a god. A cult hates cleverness because clever people ask where the money went. A fool hates ledgers because numbers, unlike courtiers, do not flatter.

### Common Sermon Themes

- Craft is worship when done well.
- Wealth is a tool, not a master.
- Laughter is a weapon against fear.
- Luck favors the prepared, the observant, and the properly invoiced.
- A community that cannot repair its bridges cannot defend its children.
- Evil loves confusion; therefore clarity is holy.

## The Merchant's Guild Reforms

After the rooting out of the [[Urdlen Cult]], the Merchant's Guild of Bard's Gate was left compromised, embarrassed, and full of men who suddenly became passionate advocates for “moving forward.”

Bilvyn disagreed.

He believed moving forward required first learning who had been moving money backward, sideways, underground, and into private cellars.

His review of guild affairs was cold, patient, and devastating.

He did not overturn the guild.

He reopened it like a clock.

Piece by piece.

Spring by spring.

Gear by gear.

And wherever something had been gnawed hollow, he replaced it.

### Measures Attributed to Bilvyn

- Reconciliation of old warehouse ledgers
- Inspection of bonded guild contracts
- Review of debt claims linked to compromised guild officers
- Quiet removal of suspect clerks
- Protection of apprentices trapped in cult-adjacent patronage
- Reassignment of legitimate trade licenses
- Restoration of merchant arbitration under witnessed procedure
- Closer cooperation between guild officers, temple auditors, and lawful reeves

Bilvyn’s reforms did not make him universally loved.

They did make him very difficult to remove without explaining why.

## Wealth and Standing

Bilvyn is wealthy by gnomish professional standards. His family has interests in lending, insurance, temple trusts, craft investment, and trade administration.

He is not ostentatious about money.

This should not be mistaken for indifference.

Bilvyn understands wealth as stored labor, deferred obligation, social leverage, and potential civic repair. He is generous with public works and cautious with private petitioners. He gives freely to temple causes, but has been known to make donors explain exactly why they are suddenly so charitable.

He cannot easily be bribed because he already possesses comfort, influence, and access.

Offering Bilvyn money to look away is like offering a mason a brick and asking him to forget the wall is collapsing.

## Marriage

Bilvyn is married to **Merynna Boonspell**, a respected gnomish contract-broker, temple patron, and manager of several family investments and charitable trusts in Bard's Gate.

Merynna is warm in public, precise in business, and famously difficult to trap in bad terms. She is known for remembering every clause, every cousin, every feast-day obligation, and every promise made by someone foolish enough to think a verbal assurance would vanish into the air.

Bilvyn respects her deeply.

He also fears her calendar.

This is not considered unmanly among married gnomes. It is considered evidence of survival instinct.

### Merynna's Reputation

Among guild families, Merynna is viewed as gracious, composed, and absolutely lethal in negotiation. She has a gift for making unreasonable people feel childish without ever raising her voice.

She is also one of the few people who can make Bilvyn stop working.

Not by pleading.

By informing him that his exhaustion has become inefficient.

He usually obeys.

## Family and Kinship

The Boonspell family is part temple family, part guild family, and part civic machine, as many successful gnomish families are.

Their marriages, apprenticeships, sponsorships, and business relationships interlock in ways humans often mistake for conspiracy.

This is unfair.

It is not always conspiracy.

Sometimes it is merely nepotism with excellent minutes.

## Tavryn Boonspell

Bilvyn’s nephew **Tavryn Boonspell** serves with his field detachment as a young sapper.

Tavryn is talented, quick with figures, and genuinely useful in the field. He has a strong grasp of angles, stress, undermining, and the terrifying gnomish habit of treating explosive risk as a scheduling concern.

He is also a Boonspell.

This helped.

Tavryn insists he earned his place entirely on merit. Bilvyn also insists this, though with slightly less confidence when pressed by someone rude enough to understand gnomish family systems.

The truth is more complicated and therefore more gnomish: Tavryn is qualified, promising, and probably would not have been placed so near the center of things so early if his name had not already been trusted.

Bilvyn is honest enough to be troubled by this.

Eventually.

## Nepotism and Gnomish Blind Spots

Bilvyn despises bribery, corruption, false weights, forged ledgers, cult influence, and private advantage masquerading as law.

He is less alert to the softer favoritism of his own culture.

Among gnomes, family, guild, temple, and apprenticeship ties are so tightly braided that a recommendation from a cousin may also be a professional certification, a character witness, a contractual guarantee, and a breakfast invitation.

Bilvyn does not see this as corruption.

He sees it as accountability.

He is not entirely wrong.

He is not entirely right either.

This tension is one of the few reliable ways to make him pause.

## Engineer and Administrator

Bilvyn is a trained engineer as well as a priest and administrator. He understands stone, timber, drainage, load, grade, roadbed, brace-work, foundations, and the dozen tiny failures that become disasters once soldiers begin marching over them.

He does not romanticize war.

War ruins roads, burns bridges, breaks mills, muddies wells, and teaches young fools that courage can replace planning.

Bilvyn considers that last belief particularly offensive.

### Areas of Expertise

- Field fortifications
- Siege platforms
- Catapult and trebuchet siting
- Drainage trenches
- Road stabilization
- Camp layout
- Counter-sapping
- Earthworks
- Bridge repair
- Supply accounting
- Tool distribution
- Crew rotation
- Magical labor coordination

Bilvyn’s gift is not merely knowing how to build.

It is knowing how to make other people build while tired, frightened, cold, underfed, and convinced they know better.

## Magical Training

Bilvyn has a modest but useful touch of magical training. He is not a battle wizard and does not pretend to be one.

He understands enough spellcraft to supervise sparkwrights, coordinate magical labor, and keep enthusiastic young gnomes from turning siege preparation into a smoking theological debate.

His clerical blessings tend toward craft, protection, good fortune, steadiness of hand, and the prevention of stupid accidents.

He regards the last category as among the holiest works of Garl Glittergold.

## The Boonspell Field Works

Bilvyn’s current field detachment is commonly called the **Boonspell Field Works**.

Some of the younger gnomes have called it **Boonspell’s Brightworks**.

Bilvyn pretends not to know this.

He knows.

### Known Members

- **Master Bilvyn Boonspell**, priest-engineer and administrator
- **Tavryn Boonspell**, young sapper and Bilvyn’s nephew
- Two sparkwrights
- One junior priest of Garl Glittergold
- Surveyors
- Sappers
- Field carpenters
- Rope and pulley handlers
- Stone-haulers
- Ledger clerks
- Tool stewards

The Field Works are not numerous enough to decide a battle by themselves, but they can change what kind of battle is fought.

A ditch in the right place is sometimes worth twenty swords.

This is the sort of sentence Bilvyn says aloud and then waits for the infantry to stop looking offended.

## Joining Orin's Force

Bilvyn joined [[Orin Kharne]]’s force because the matter had moved beyond court theory and into the realm of roads, forts, prisoners, hungry men, and necessary violence.

He does not claim to be a warrior.

He does claim that a just cause still needs proper drainage.

Bilvyn recognizes that Orin’s force is strange: half-orc volunteers, grugach scouts, gnomish engineers, light cavalry, sworn men, irregulars, priests, and noble retainers all being pulled into something larger than any one banner.

He finds this alarming.

He also finds it promising.

The world rarely arranges virtue into tidy columns.

That is why administrators exist, apparently as punishment for everyone involved.

## Relationship with Orin Kharne

Bilvyn respects [[Orin Kharne]].

He sees in Orin a man trying to become more than a weapon without pretending he was never made into one. Orin listens to specialists, which Bilvyn values more than courtly grace.

Many nobles ask for counsel and then ignore it.

Orin asks fewer questions, listens harder, and remembers answers.

Bilvyn has noticed.

He does not flatter Orin, but he gives him direct assessments. In gnomish professional culture, this is close to affection.

## Arrival with Morg

Bilvyn arrived with [[Morg]] and his half-orc volunteers.

The pairing drew comment almost immediately, though not because either man behaved badly.

Morg is polished, articulate, and composed, with the practiced manners of a man who has spent much of his life being watched for any excuse to dismiss him. He speaks well, dresses carefully when circumstances allow, and understands rank, tone, and public restraint.

Among rougher half-orcs, this does not always earn admiration.

Some call him a “kept halfblood” when they think he cannot hear.

He usually hears.

Bilvyn, for his part, recognizes the type at once: a capable outsider made useful to respectable society, then quietly punished by everyone for surviving it.

This does not make them friends.

It does make Bilvyn careful.

Morg and Bilvyn do not particularly like one another at first. Their friction is mild, but real. Morg finds Bilvyn precise, privileged, and too comfortable assuming systems can be repaired from within. Bilvyn finds Morg controlled, intelligent, and carrying more social pressure than he admits.

Neither man says this directly.

Both are too well-mannered for that particular stupidity.

## Relationship with Morg

Bilvyn and [[Morg]] maintain a cool, functional respect.

Morg is not crude. He is not inarticulate. He is precisely the opposite: polished, careful, and well-spoken, with the social discipline of someone who knows that a half-orc who raises his voice becomes a threat in other people’s stories before he has finished the sentence.

Bilvyn notices this.

He also notices that some of Morg’s own volunteers resent it.

To them, Morg has been accepted too often by the very people who would spit on them. He knows how to speak to nobles. He understands command structure. He can stand in a lord’s tent and be treated as a useful man rather than a tolerated beast.

That makes him valuable.

It also makes him suspect.

Morg knows this and does not waste breath explaining himself to men who have already decided that polish is betrayal.

Bilvyn’s first instinct is to classify Morg as a social bridge: a disciplined, respectable half-orc leader who can help integrate rough volunteers into Orin’s command.

That assessment is true.

It is also incomplete.

Morg is not merely a bridge. He is a man standing on one while both sides complain about the traffic.
## The Half-Orc Volunteers

Morg’s volunteers are not a single temper or class.

Some are eager to prove themselves through discipline and service. Some are angry. Some are frightened and hiding it under swagger. Some are watching Morg carefully, unsure whether he represents a future they want or a compromise they despise.

A few rougher men call him “house half-orc” behind his back.

The phrase carries more than insult. It means polished. It means trained. It means acceptable. It means someone taught him where to stand, how to speak, and which parts of himself to file down so respectable people would stop reaching for knives.

Morg does not rise to it.

That restraint irritates them more.

Bilvyn understands enough of guild society to recognize the shape of the wound. Every people has its own version of this: the one who learned the master’s language, the one who passed through the office door, the one who can argue terms instead of breaking windows.

The others may need him.

They may even follow him.

That does not mean they forgive him for making respectability look possible.
## Relationship with Norwin

Bilvyn referred to [[Norwin]] as a **“renegade mage”** without obvious judgment.

When pressed lightly by Orin, he added only:

> “Everyone has heard multiple versions of the destruction of the wing in the Mages Guild.”

Then he let the matter rest.

Bilvyn does not hate Norwin. He may even admire him.

But he treats Norwin’s reputation as one treats an unstable wall: with respect, distance, and careful bracing.

Among gnomes, Norwin is not merely a person. He is an incident with shoes.

Bilvyn is willing to work with him.

He is less willing to stand under anything Norwin has recently modified.

## Views on Bard's Gate

Bilvyn loves Bard's Gate in the irritated way civic-minded people love cities.

He sees its promise, its industry, its hunger, its fraud, its underworld, its stubborn decency, and its astonishing ability to make every reform require three meetings and a bribery investigation.

He believes Bard's Gate can be made better.

Not pure.

Not perfect.

Better.

Bilvyn does not trust utopians. They have suspiciously clean hands.

## Views on the Merchant Class

Bilvyn likes merchants when they remember what trade is for.

He dislikes merchants when they begin speaking of “market necessity” in the same tone priests use for divine revelation.

Commerce is useful. Wealth is useful. Profit is not evil.

But a guild that cannot distinguish profit from predation is not a guild.

It is a dressed-up wolf with warehouse keys.

## Views on Noble Power

Bilvyn is respectful toward lawful rank, but not impressed by it.

He understands noble authority as a fact of the kingdom, not as evidence of personal wisdom.

A good noble can preserve order, protect trade, and defend the vulnerable.

A bad noble can turn inherited land into a private extortion engine and still expect songs.

Bilvyn has met both kinds.

He prefers the first, obviously, but prepares paperwork for the second.

## Speech and Manner

Bilvyn speaks with precise courtesy. He does not ramble. He does not bluster. He lets silence become uncomfortable, then places the relevant fact in the middle of it like a brick on a table.

His humor is dry rather than sharp.

He is lighter on snark than many gnomes, but this only means his insults arrive wearing polished shoes.

### Common Remarks

> “That is not impossible. It is merely expensive, dangerous, and poorly scheduled.”

> “A plan is not improved by being shouted.”

> “The ledger has no motive to embarrass you. That is what makes it useful.”

> “No, that is not corruption. Not yet. That is where corruption hangs its coat.”

> “I am not accusing anyone. I am asking why the numbers appear to be lying.”

> “Luck is not a substitute for preparation. It is what preparation looks like when Garl approves.”

> “You may ignore the ditch if you like. The rain will not.”

> “Master Norwin’s reputation is... structurally significant.”

## Habits

Bilvyn keeps wax tablets, string measures, chalk, temple seals, spare quills, and folded correspondence in separate inner pockets.

He checks camp drainage before supper.

He inspects tool racks before prayer.

He writes to Merynna whenever courier service allows, and his letters are said to include equal parts affection, procurement notes, and complaints about avoidable inefficiency.

He dislikes undocumented tool loss with a severity most soldiers reserve for enemy cavalry.

## Sample Letter Fragment

> My dear Merynna,
>
> The roads are worse than reported, the timber is greener than promised, and one of the young men attempted to brace a throwing frame with wood I would hesitate to use for a henhouse door.
>
> Spirits remain high, largely because no one here understands enough engineering to be properly afraid.
>
> Baron Kharne listens. This places him above half the nobility of Gorundia and most guild committees.
>
> Tavryn is well. Too confident, but not yet dead, which I am choosing to call progress.
>
> I remain, as ever,
>
> Yours in craft, wit, and mild exhaustion,
>
> Bilvyn

## Current Concerns

Bilvyn’s chief concerns in the field are practical:

- keeping the engineers protected
- preventing heroic misuse of siege equipment
- ensuring ammunition supply
- establishing proper camp drainage
- tracking tool loss
- integrating half-orc labor without insult
- keeping sparkwrights from improvising too freely
- supporting Orin without becoming trapped in noble politics
- watching Tavryn without appearing to watch Tavryn

His deeper concern is moral.

He has seen what rot does when allowed to live under respectable structures. Bard's Gate taught him that corruption is rarely dramatic at first. It begins in tolerances, favors, exceptions, and the belief that no one important will suffer.

Bilvyn has come to believe that what Orin’s force is uncovering may be one of those moments when decent people either act, or spend the next decade explaining why they did not.

## Field Anecdote: The Pulley Argument

During the first day of work beside Orin’s siege train, [[Morg]] stood watching a gnomish crew rig a lifting frame.

He did not interrupt.

He studied the mechanism for several minutes, hands folded behind his back, expression unreadable.

At last he said:

> “Pulleys, load sharing, and a fixed brace. Efficient, assuming the mud does not take the rear footing.”

Bilvyn looked up from his wax tablet.

A pause followed.

One of the younger gnomes opened his mouth.

Bilvyn closed it for him with a glance.

> “Correct,” Bilvyn said. “Though I would have preferred to discover your understanding before I had already assigned two men to watch that exact footing.”

Morg’s mouth curved slightly.

> “You assumed I was merely admiring the tiny wheels.”

> “I assumed nothing so simple.”

A young sapper then dropped a wedge from the frame. It clattered down the timbers and landed in the mud.

Every gnome nearby froze.

Bilvyn turned his head.

Slowly.

> “Master Tavryn.”

The young sapper swallowed.

> “Yes, uncle?”

Bilvyn raised one white eyebrow.

> “In the field, you may call me Master Boonspell.”

A beat.

> “And you may retrieve the wedge before gravity claims formal ownership.”

Morg stepped aside just enough to let Tavryn pass.

> “Your uncle is kinder than my first engineer.”

Tavryn blinked.

> “You had an engineer?”

Morg’s smile did not change.

> “Several. Briefly.”

Bilvyn returned to his tablet.

> “Then let us endeavor to remain employed longer than your previous examples.”

Morg inclined his head.

> “A worthy ambition.”
## Assessment

Bilvyn Boonspell is not a soldier, though soldiers benefit from him.

He is not a courtier, though courts should fear him.

He is not a merchant prince, though money moves when he asks why it has stopped.

He is a priest of craft, wit, order, and stubborn communal decency, armed with engineering knowledge and the terrible power of having already checked the figures.

In Orin’s force, Bilvyn represents Bard's Gate at its best: clever, civic-minded, practical, morally awake, and just smug enough to be gnomish about it.

He came because someone had to make sure the righteous side remembered to build latrines downhill.

This, too, is holy work.
