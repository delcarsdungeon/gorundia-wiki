# The Dress Before the Giant

The tournament had filled Bard’s Gate with banners, horses, mercenaries, minor nobles, and men who had discovered that owning one polished breastplate entitled them to opinions.

Braga hated tournament week.

She also needed the money.

Those two facts governed most of her life lately.

Her family name was in the dirt. Her purse was nearly empty. Her armor needed work she could not afford, and every time Venom quietly attempted to make any of those problems disappear, Braga became personally offended by the concept of friendship.

So Braga sat on a narrow bench outside a tailor’s fitting rooms, arms folded beneath her chest, looking as though she had been brought there under armed guard.

She was not tall.

People often remembered her as taller because Braga occupied space with conviction.

Five feet and five inches of compact, thick-built woman, broad through the shoulders, heavy in the chest, powerful through the hips and thighs. She had the dense, grounded physique of someone made to carry weight, hold a line, and remain exactly where she had planted her boots.

Thranduil would later maintain, with complete sincerity, that she was a dwarf.

Braga had stopped arguing with him.

Today she wore a dark fitted blouse, belted tightly at the waist, over a layered skirt arranged to leave her legs free. The clothing was better than her road gear, but only marginally. Every piece had been selected according to Braga’s three principles of fashion:

It covered her.

It survived travel.

She could fight in it.

Venom sat beside her.

Perfectly.

Naturally.

Her short dark hair framed her face in clean, severe lines. She wore black with a high collar, fitted closely enough to acknowledge her figure without seeming to ask permission from anyone looking at it. One leg crossed over the other. Gloves folded in her lap. Composed as a portrait of someone who had already poisoned the painter.

Braga looked toward the curtained doorway.

“I’m leaving.”

“No.”

“I’m not wearing lace.”

“No one has mentioned lace.”

“I can feel it waiting.”

“That may be the first truly feminine instinct you have demonstrated.”

Braga turned her head.

Venom continued examining the room.

There were mirrors everywhere.

Braga distrusted mirrors. They showed enemies behind you, but only after someone had gone through the trouble of polishing them.

“I have clothes,” Braga said.

“Yes.”

“They fit.”

“In the sense that you have successfully forced yourself into them.”

“I can move.”

“You can also move in armor.”

“Exactly.”

“You are not wearing armor to the reception.”

“Could.”

“You are not.”

“Could become necessary.”

“It often does around you. Nonetheless, you will arrive dressed as a woman rather than a siege survivor who has wandered indoors looking for soup.”

Braga’s jaw tightened.

“I told you. I’m not taking charity.”

“And I told you this is not charity.”

“You dragged me here.”

“Yes.”

“You’re paying.”

“I am advancing the cost.”

“With what expectation?”

“That you stop complaining long enough for a talented man to work.”

“That’s charity with extra words.”

“No. Charity expects gratitude.”

Venom’s mouth moved by perhaps half an inch.

“I expect repayment.”

Braga considered this.

“How?”

“When you win.”

“And if I don’t?”

“Then you will owe me money.”

“I already owe half the city money.”

“A crowded field should make you feel less conspicuous.”

Braga looked back toward the curtain.

Somewhere beyond it, a man shouted:

> “Not cream! Cream is the color of surrender! Bring me ivory or bring me the woman’s obituary!”

Fabric flew across the opening.

An assistant ran after it.

Braga pointed.

“That man is mad.”

“That man is expensive.”

“Same thing with better shoes.”

Venom’s gaze drifted toward the front windows.

Outside, tournament traffic rolled past. Retainers. Squires. Knights in colors bright enough to frighten livestock.

Braga followed her eyes despite herself.

A large figure crossed the street beyond the glass.

Tall.

Broad.

Built like the gods had begun with a normal man and become irritated halfway through.

Orin Kharne walked beside two other adventurers, listening to neither of them. His expression suggested that anything requiring more than three words had already lost his interest.

Braga watched him pass.

Only briefly.

Venom waited.

Braga continued watching.

Venom waited longer.

Braga finally looked away.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You’re doing that thing.”

“I do several things.”

“The quiet one.”

“That narrows it very little.”

Braga frowned.

“He’s very tall.”

“So are several buildings.”

“Looks strong.”

“So does the city wall.”

“He fought yesterday.”

“I know.”

“Didn’t waste movement.”

“I know.”

“Hits like a dropped gate.”

“I was present.”

Braga glanced toward the window again.

Orin had disappeared into the crowd.

“I was only studying him.”

“Naturally.”

“For the tournament.”

“Then I pray your study of Sir Orin’s backside reveals some crucial weakness in his guard.”

Braga’s head snapped around.

“I wasn’t looking at his backside.”

“No?”

“No.”

“A pity. It is one of his more comprehensible qualities.”

Color rose through Braga’s cheeks.

Venom spared her further inspection.

This was somehow worse.

“He hasn’t noticed me,” Braga said.

“Not properly.”

“He doesn’t know who I am.”

“Not yet.”

“And I don’t care if he does.”

Venom nodded.

Braga scowled at the agreement.

“You think the dress will make him notice.”

“I think it may help him understand what he is seeing.”

“He’s not stupid.”

Venom paused.

“Let us not burden romance with unnecessary optimism.”

Braga gave a reluctant snort.

Venom’s expression softened by perhaps half a degree.

Then she added mildly:

> “If the giant does not notice, Fancy Korac will.”

Braga’s face changed.

Not much.

Enough.

“Korac notices everyone.”

“He does.”

“He’s like that.”

“Yes.”

“He flirts because he thinks it’s funny.”

Venom turned her head.

“Does he?”

“He flirts with tavern girls. Noblewomen. Married women. Priests.”

“Not exclusively women.”

Braga ignored that.

“He says things.”

“To you.”

“To everyone.”

“He brings you wine.”

“He steals the wine.”

“He remembers which kind you like.”

“He remembers where the expensive bottles are.”

“He has followed you through three kingdoms.”

“We were traveling the same road.”

“He entered a slaver camp alone because they had taken you.”

“He was robbing them.”

“Before or after he learned you were there?”

Braga opened her mouth.

Closed it.

Venom watched the small war behind her eyes with the serene patience of someone observing cavalry become trapped in mud.

“He’s Korac,” Braga said at last.

“Yes.”

“That doesn’t mean anything.”

“No,” Venom said. “Apparently it means absolutely nothing.”

Before Braga could decide whether she had been insulted, the curtains opened.

Master Ellisar did not walk into the room.

He arrived in it.

A silk shirt stood open at his throat. Rings decorated six fingers. A measuring cord hung around his neck, and three pins rested between his lips. His expression suggested he had spent the morning correcting crimes committed by fabric.

He saw Venom.

Stopped.

The pins came slowly out of his mouth.

“Oh.”

Venom inclined her head.

“Master Ellisar.”

He stared at her.

Not rudely.

Professionally, which somehow involved considerably more detail.

His gaze traveled from her boots to her shoulders, paused at her waist, returned to the line of her collar, and narrowed in personal offense at whatever tailor had last touched her clothing.

“My dear woman.”

Venom waited.

“I have wanted to get my hands on you for years.”

Braga looked between them.

Venom raised one eyebrow.

“I had assumed so.”

Ellisar placed one hand over his heart.

“Do not toy with me.”

“You began without assistance.”

“I have imagined those shoulders in six different cuts.”

“Only six?”

“Seven when lonely.”

Braga stared at Venom.

Venom remained perfectly still.

Ellisar stepped closer, hands hovering near Venom’s waist without touching.

“And this line.”

He gestured.

“This restraint. This merciless refusal to offer the eye anything it has not earned.”

“I dress myself.”

“Yes. I can tell.”

A beat.

“That was not praise.”

“I understood.”

Their eyes met.

Something passed between them.

Blades crossing without either combatant having bothered to draw.

Ellisar smiled.

Venom did not.

He appeared delighted.

Braga leaned slightly toward Venom.

“He’s barely hiding it.”

Venom’s eyes stayed on Ellisar.

“No.”

“The way he talks about getting his hands on you.”

“Yes.”

“You knew?”

Venom finally looked at her.

A small smile appeared.

Cool.

Private.

Deeply unhelpful.

“Braga.”

“What?”

“Master Ellisar’s interest in me is entirely professional.”

Ellisar called toward the rear room:

> “And if anyone suggests otherwise, remind them I only do men on Moonday.”

Braga looked at him.

Then at Venom.

Venom’s smile widened by a fraction.

Braga sat back.

“Oh.”

Ellisar turned his attention toward Braga.

The delight died.

His gaze passed over her once.

Boots.

Skirt.

Belt.

Blouse.

Chest.

Shoulders.

Hands.

The posture of a woman who had arrived prepared to resist arrest.

“No.”

Braga stared.

Venom said, “You have not examined her.”

“I have eyes.”

“You also have debts.”

“I can live with debts.”

“You cannot live without my custom.”

“That is emotional blackmail.”

“It is ordinary commerce.”

Ellisar circled Braga once.

She turned with him.

He stopped.

“Do not track me.”

“I don’t like people behind me.”

“Then we shall give you mirrors.”

“I don’t like those either.”

He looked at Venom.

“She objects to geometry.”

“She is having a difficult year.”

“I can see that.”

Braga stood.

She did not tower over Ellisar.

She did not need to.

She came off the bench in one solid motion, compact and unmistakably present. Broad, heavy-set, and built with the sort of dense strength that made people reconsider whether they actually wanted the argument they had just begun.

“What’s wrong with me?”

Ellisar looked at her.

Nothing in him flinched.

“Nothing.”

“Then why’d you say no?”

“I did not say no to you.”

He gestured at all of her.

“I said no to the argument you are having with your own body.”

Braga’s brow furrowed.

“My body isn’t arguing.”

“Darling, your body is shouting from a rooftop while your clothing attempts to have it arrested.”

Venom looked away.

Her mouth had curved.

Braga caught it.

“You think this is funny.”

“Not entirely.”

Ellisar reached toward Braga’s shoulder.

She caught his wrist.

Fast.

The assistants froze.

Ellisar looked at her hand around him.

Then down at her.

“Strong.”

Braga released him.

“I’ve been told.”

“Yes, almost certainly by men immediately before regretting something.”

He touched her shoulder again.

This time she allowed it.

He pressed along the muscle, lifted her arm, turned it slightly, and frowned at her blouse as if it had concealed evidence.

“Excellent structure.”

Braga glanced at Venom.

Ellisar continued around her.

He pressed two fingers against the line of her waist, stepped back, tilted his head, and finally saw the whole of her rather than the defenses she had wrapped around it.

“Oh.”

Braga’s eyes narrowed.

“What?”

“There you are.”

He pointed toward her with two ringed fingers.

“Not tall. Everyone always thinks height is the trick. It isn’t.”

Another slow inspection.

“You are built like a fortress that learned how to glare.”

Braga looked at Venom.

Venom offered no assistance whatsoever.

Ellisar continued.

“Compact. Strong. Marvelously top-heavy. Waist present, whether you have been cooperating with it or not. Hips with intent. Legs like you kick doors open recreationally.”

“I have kicked doors open.”

“Yes. Your clothes have been spreading the news.”

He stepped around her again.

“And that face.”

“What about it?”

“It says you have never accepted help graciously in your entire life.”

“I accept help.”

Venom made a quiet sound.

Braga turned toward her.

Venom’s expression was innocent enough to constitute an insult.

Ellisar nodded to himself.

“Delicious. Difficult. Expensive.”

“I’m broke.”

“I gathered that from the belt.”

Braga looked down at it.

“What’s wrong with my belt?”

“It has been repaired with harness leather.”

“It holds.”

“So does a prison gate. I do not recommend wearing one to dinner.”

Ellisar pointed toward the fitting platform.

“Up.”

Braga did not move.

He pointed again.

“Platform.”

“What are you going to do?”

“Discover whether silk can be taught military discipline.”

“I’m not wearing silk.”

“You are broke, disgraced, angry, built like a heroic tavern song, and staring at a large knight as though you intend to climb him during a natural disaster.”

Braga went scarlet.

Ellisar continued without mercy.

“You will wear whatever I tell you.”

“I wasn’t staring at him.”

“Then whichever man you were not staring at should be profoundly grateful.”

Braga looked at Venom.

Venom had not lost control.

Quite.

The smile remained only in her eyes.

Ellisar clapped once.

Three assistants emerged from behind separate curtains.

One carried measuring cords.

One carried wine.

One carried an armful of dark fabric trimmed in warm bronze and old gold.

Ellisar took the wine.

Naturally.

He pointed at Braga.

“Strip.”

Braga looked at Venom.

Venom crossed her legs the other way.

“I shall wait here.”

“You knew.”

“I suspected.”

“You brought me here knowing.”

“Yes.”

Braga looked toward the assistants.

Then toward Ellisar.

Then toward the door.

Outside, somewhere in the tournament crowd, Orin Kharne remained blissfully unaware that several dangerous people had begun arranging his future.

Ellisar raised his glass.

“Come along, darling.”

A wicked smile spread across his face.

> “Let us make the giant stupid.”

## The Reveal

The fitting took most of the afternoon.

Braga objected to the pins.

Then the stays.

Then the neckline.

Then the fact that Ellisar kept slapping her hand away whenever she tried to pull the neckline upward.

At one point an assistant fled the room carrying a torn sleeve and the stunned expression of someone who had just witnessed a siege engine reject formalwear.

Ellisar did not surrender.

He adapted.

When Braga finally emerged, the room became quiet.

The dress was dark enough to appear black until it caught the light.

Warm brown and antique-gold embroidery followed the collar, sleeves, and structured edges. The neckline was low but deliberate, framed by a high architectural collar and a fitted bodice that acknowledged Braga’s chest without pretending it could be hidden or apologized for.

The fabric drew close at her waist, then opened enough below the hips to let her walk, turn, or kick someone if civilization failed.

It did not make her look taller.

It made height irrelevant.

Her dark hair had been loosened and arranged around her face. A small red stone rested at her throat, the only bright color in the whole composition.

Braga stared at herself in the mirror.

For once, the mirror did not show a disgraced daughter.

It did not show a broke adventurer in mended clothes.

It did not show a woman waiting for someone to remember what her family had become.

It showed Braga.

Strong.

Dark.

Beautiful.

Dangerous.

She shifted uncomfortably.

“My breasts are out.”

Ellisar stood behind her, exhausted but triumphant.

“Your breasts have always been out. Your old blouse simply made them look angry about it.”

“They’re very noticeable.”

“Yes.”

“Everyone will look.”

“Yes.”

Braga turned toward Venom.

Venom had risen from the bench.

For once, there was no mockery in her expression.

Only satisfaction.

Braga looked back at herself.

“What if he doesn’t notice?”

Venom approached and adjusted the small red stone at Braga’s throat.

“The giant?”

Braga said nothing.

Venom’s fingers lingered for a moment, making the placement exact.

Then she stepped back.

“If Orin Kharne fails to notice you in that dress, we shall have learned something medically significant.”

Ellisar lifted his glass.  

“I should be so lucky - and if the giant proves blind, bring me Fancy Korac.”

Braga frowned.

“Why?”

Ellisar smiled.

“Because I wish to see how quickly he can run while carrying you.”

Venom’s eyes brightened with quiet amusement.

Braga looked between them.

Neither explained.

Human courtship, as usual, advanced through denial, tailored fabric, and the growing possibility that someone was going to be punched.
