## Vignette — Wait. They're Brothers?

It started with an innocent question.

Which, with Orin, was frequently how trouble started.

They were sitting outside the command tent after supper. Braga had one of his sword-belts across her knees, replacing a buckle he had apparently intended to continue using until it achieved some higher state of spiritual collapse.

Orin was watching the camp.

Or pretending to.

“Why do they hate them so much?”

Braga did not look up.

“Who?”

“Heironeans. Hextorites.”

That got a glance.

Orin shrugged.

“I know why _we_ hate them. Dominion. Raids. Slavery. All the usual reasons to put an axe in somebody.”

A pause.

“But Heironeans get strange about it.”

Braga’s eyebrow rose.

“Strange.”

“You know what I mean.”

“I very much want to hear what you mean.”

Orin ignored the warning.

“Most of them are calm.”

He gestured vaguely toward the Heironean portion of camp.

“Measured. Honorable. Lots of rules about when you can hit somebody and how hard.”

Braga stared at him.

“An inspired summary of the faith.”

“But mention Hextor and suddenly everybody starts checking the oil on the Inquisition.”

That earned him the faintest twitch at one corner of her mouth.

“So why?”

Braga returned to the buckle.

“Because Hextor is not merely another god of war.”

Orin nodded.

He was listening.

Mostly.

“He represents strength without mercy. Discipline without conscience. Victory made its own justification. He takes everything honorable in war and asks what would happen if you removed the reason for restraint.”

“Mm.”

“Heironeous teaches that strength exists to defend what is just.”

“Right.”

“Hextor teaches that strength proves what is just.”

“Right.”

“They contest the same ground. Courage. War. Duty. Authority. Discipline. They simply disagree about what any of those things are _for_.”

“Right.”

Braga stopped working.

Slowly looked at him.

“You are not listening.”

“I am.”

“What did I just say?”

Orin considered this.

“Something about discipline.”

Braga stared.

Orin wisely added:

“And conscience.”

She went back to work.

“Furthermore, their hostility is ancient. Personal. Heironeous and Hextor are sons of Stern Alia, and their opposition has defined both cults since before half the kingdoms on this continent existed.”

Orin was quiet.

Braga kept talking.

“For a Heironean, Hextor is not some foreign principle that can simply be dismissed. He is the corruption standing beside the virtue. The argument that says honor is weakness, mercy is foolishness, and power needs no justification beyond possessing it.”

Nothing from Orin.

Braga glanced up.

He was staring at her.

Not blankly.

Processing.

Then:

“Wait.”

Braga closed her eyes.

“Orin.”

“Hextor and Heironeous are **brothers?**”

She opened them.

“Yes.”

“And Stern Alia is their mother?”

“Yes.”

A pause.

Orin’s face underwent the slow, terrible transformation of a man discovering that several unrelated facts have secretly been one fact the entire time.

“Huh.”

Braga waited.

Orin looked across the camp toward the Heironean banners.

Then back to her.

“So this whole thing is basically two brothers who agree that war, strength, discipline and duty are the most important things in the world…”

Braga narrowed her eyes.

“…but one thinks strength means protecting people, and the other thinks strength means they belong to you.”

The glare eased slightly.

“Yes.”

Orin thought some more.

“And neither one can stand the fact that, to everybody else, they look a lot alike.”

Braga stopped.

The buckle remained suspended between her hands.

For one brief moment she appeared caught between theological outrage and the irritating realization that her husband had accidentally found the point.

Orin saw it.

“Oh, gods.”

He grinned.

“They **do**, don’t they?”

**WHAP.**

The back of Braga’s hand struck his upper arm.

On most men it would have constituted an assault.

On Orin it mostly made a noise.

“I have been explaining this to you for ten bloody minutes.”

“I was listening.”

“You just discovered the central familial relationship of two of the largest war cults in the world.”

“I knew they were related.”

“You did not.”

“I knew Stern Alia was involved.”

“She is their **mother**.”

“I know that now.”

Another backhand.

Orin laughed.

Braga pointed the broken buckle at him.

“And do not reduce the ancient struggle between righteous valor and tyranny to two brothers having an argument.”

Orin managed to get himself under control.

Mostly.

“All right.”

A beat.

“So why are their worshippers more rabid about each other than anyone else?”

Braga opened her mouth.

Stopped.

Because now he was actually asking.

She leaned back.

The irritation remained, but something more thoughtful settled underneath it.

“Because strangers can be wrong about things you do not care about.”

She tapped the buckle against his chest.

“Your brother knows exactly what you care about.”

Another tap.

“He knows the words.”

Tap.

“The traditions.”

Tap.

“The promises.”

Then she pointed across the darkened camp.

“And he can take every one of them, twist it half a turn, and make something you recognize.”

Orin's smile faded.

Braga nodded.

“That is why.”

A moment passed.

Orin looked toward the distant banners again.

“Hextor isn't the opposite of Heironeous.”

Braga gave him a sideways look.

“No.”

“He's what Heironeous thinks happens when you get all the important parts wrong.”

Now she smiled.

Small.

Fierce.

“Congratulations.”

She handed him the repaired belt.

“You have successfully completed the first five minutes of the conversation we just had.”

Orin took it.

“Think I did pretty well.”

Braga backhanded his arm again.

“Idiot.”

But she was smiling when she said it.
