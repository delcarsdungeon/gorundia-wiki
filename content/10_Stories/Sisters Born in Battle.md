# Sisters Born in Battle

![[Sisters born in battle.png]]

## Venom's Tale

In a quiet, reflective moment, Venom grows wistful.

[!quote] Venom
> I have known Braga for ten years now, and she makes me proud.

Braga’s bravery was never in doubt, not even that night in Boulder Hollow.

She had the heart of a lion, even if her hands trembled as she gripped that battered woodsman’s axe. The village was in chaos when I arrived—firelight painting the dark sky in terrible streaks, the air thick with the iron tang of blood and the acrid sting of burning wood. I had heard rumors of orc raiders in the region, but this was worse than any whispered tale. Orcs do not simply raid; they descend like a flood, leaving nothing but ruin behind.

And there was Braga, planted in the heart of it all. Alone. Standing against the church doors like a pillar of defiance, covered in blood—orc blood, her own, it did not matter. Ten dead orcs lay around her, her sword shattered, but she had not yielded a single step.

She was brave. So brave, it broke my heart.

I did not waste time assessing the carnage; there was no room for hesitation. A lance in my hand, I spurred my horse straight into the fray, letting out a battle cry that sent the nearest orcs scattering. My lance took the first two before it splintered; the others fell to my sword in quick succession.

Braga’s eyes caught mine then—her defiance faltered for the briefest moment, replaced by unmistakable relief.

When my horse reared back, I sent it off to draw some of the orcs away. It was a fine steed, trained for battle, and clever enough to stay beyond their reach.

Meanwhile, I slid from the saddle and moved to Braga’s side.

“Shoulder to shoulder,” I said.

She nodded.

We fought together, two against many. The orcs came in droves, their savagery unchecked. Braga held firm, axe swinging in brutal arcs, while I worked with sword and shield to keep them from overwhelming us.

When her wounds slowed her and she faltered, I stepped closer, guarding her flank. Not because she needed saving—she had already earned every inch of her survival that night—but because no warrior should stand alone if it can be helped.

Still, I could not help but feel protective.

She was fierce—but she was also exhausted, bleeding, and so very young.

When the sun finally rose, the orcs withdrew, leaving only silence and the stench of death behind.

Braga and I collapsed against the church door, bloodied and battered. The priest came to tend our wounds as best he could. I handed her the dagger I carried—a small token, but a bond forged in blood.

“We will see this through,” I told her. “But you must promise me something.”

She looked at me, pale but resolute. “Anything.”

“If the worst happens, you do not let them take me alive,” I said quietly. “And I will grant you the same mercy.”

She embraced me with surprising strength.

That was Braga. Fragile and unyielding all at once.

We fought again that night when the orcs returned. Shoulder to shoulder, back to back, like two stones standing against a rising tide. And when the last of them finally slipped back into the darkness, we were still alive.

Bruised, battered, but alive.

Braga saved that village.

She would never claim it, but without her, Boulder Hollow would have become a graveyard.

She is not merely my sister in arms.

She is my hero as well.

## Braga's Tale

One chill evening, Braga felt especially talkative.

[!quote] Braga
> “She’s my sister, Orin.”

Seeing the confused look on his face, she smiled and flicked his nose.

“Not literally, of course. Hell, she’s my hero, my sister, my mentor in so many ways—what I’ve always wanted to be.”

She sat on the edge of the bed, her feet dangling.

“We’re coming up on ten years now. It was just after I was freed from service.”

“I was wandering around, mostly aimless, and ended up in a little nowhere village called Boulder Hollow.”

She sighed.

“Orcs.”

“Orcs scare me, Orin. The Empire pushes cattle into their lands, and every so often they’re ‘encouraged’ to raid into Gorundia. An orcish summer isn’t a pretty thing.”

“Anyway, the village had seen signs—dead livestock, runes on hides and skulls. They sent word for help, but Baron Kalethar never had many knights.”

“So I got to work.”

“The first night they killed three families before we could get everyone into the church.”

She rubbed her eyes.

“They don’t come one at a time. Usually a whole hand will pick a target. I was doing alright until they killed Cabbage.”

She frowned as Orin tried not to laugh.

“Yes, the horse was named Cabbage.”

“Once I was on foot, it was bloody work. Orcs love fighting, but they love breaking things even more. My armor saved me a dozen times that night.”

“I killed ten before my sword broke.”

“So there I was, back against the church doors with a woodsman’s axe when—WHAM.”

She snapped her fingers.

“Lightning bolt made of elf.”

“She came crashing through them like Heironeous had thrown her out of the sky. Killed two with a lance before it snapped, scattered the rest like kobolds.”

“She jumped off her horse and that damned beast just ran off and started dragging orcs around the village chasing it.”

“Smart horse.”

“Shoulder to shoulder we fought. I got hurt pretty bad, and she nearly got carried off when an orc yanked her helmet strap loose.”

“We fought till dawn.”

She lit a cigar.

“When the priest patched us up she gave me this.”

Braga produced the elvish dagger.

“She made me swear something.”

[!quote] The Oath of Boulder Hollow
> “I won’t let them take you alive. You promise me the same.”

“I hugged her so tight I nearly cracked her ribs.”

“They came back that evening. The priest had given me a new sword.”

“We stood there while the village burned around us.”

“They smashed against us like waves on rocks.”

“And then… just like that… they were gone.”

She hugged Orin suddenly.

“Watch over her in that damned forest, will you?”

## Chronicler’s Note

>[!note] Later parish records recovered from [[Boulder Hollow]] offer a small but intriguing detail regarding the events described above.
>
>The priest’s burial ledger from that season records **sixteen orc bodies recovered near the churchyard alone**, not counting those later found in the surrounding lanes and fields after the villagers returned to clear the wreckage.
>
>Orc warbands in the region are known to organize themselves into **“hands” of five warriors**, a structure observed repeatedly by frontier scouts and confirmed in several campaigns against raiders from the eastern hills.
>
>If the attackers that night followed their usual practice, the raiding party would likely have numbered **twenty or perhaps twenty-five orcs**.
>
>How many fell to [[Braga Kharne| The Warden of the West, Baroness Westhold, Braga Kharne]] and [[Dame Venom]] during the fighting is impossible to say with certainty.
>
>The surviving accounts agree on only two things:
>
>The church doors never opened.
>
>And when the sun rose, the village was still standing.
