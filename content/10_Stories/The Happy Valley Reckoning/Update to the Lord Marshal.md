20 Coldeven, 1231

Arlen Corvis came into the Lord Marshal’s command tent fast enough that the last three steps became a disagreement with friction.

One boot hit the rug.

The other tried to continue without him.

He caught himself against the edge of the map table, twisted sideways, and narrowly avoided scattering three cavalry markers and what remained of the western road network across the floor.

“Lord Marshal.”

Beringer looked up.

Arlen straightened, dignity arriving several seconds after the rest of him.

“Message from Silvershire.”

That got Beringer’s attention.

“Aylassa?”

“No.”

Arlen crossed the remaining distance and held out a folded sheet of signal paper.

“That is why I am here.”

Beringer took it but did not open it immediately.

“Who sent it?”

“Jessa Ardell.”

A pause.

“Who?”

Arlen pointed at him.

“Exactly.”

Then remembered who he was pointing at.

His hand dropped.

“Sorry, my lord.”

Beringer looked at the folded message.

“You don’t know her.”

“I do not. She identifies herself as attendant to Lady Aylassa, and says Lady Aylassa specifically directed her to report to you.”

Arlen drew a breath.

He was good at voices.

Not magically. Unfortunately, human beings had apparently managed to develop useful skills without requiring a wizard every single time.

Signals work meant listening.

Really listening.

A messenger gasping through a report sounded different from one pretending not to be frightened. A lieutenant reading prepared words sounded different from one choosing them carefully. Arlen could usually hear exhaustion, drink, pain, coercion, rehearsed confidence, and the particular flat calm of men who had just watched something go badly wrong and had not yet decided what they felt about it.

Then there was Aylassa.

Arlen had received messages from her before.

Her voice bothered him.

Not because it was unpleasant.

Quite the opposite.

Silky. Warm. Almost musical. And always with the faintest suggestion of a giggle waiting underneath, as though every sentence had arrived carrying some private joke.

That would have been charming if she used it to discuss weather.

Aylassa used it to report military buildups, encounters with dangerous men, and old acquaintances whose hands she had once cut off.

The content said:

**Dominion forces are preparing for something large.**

The voice said:

**You will never guess what happened at luncheon.**

Arlen had spent years learning to judge danger from voices.

Aylassa made the entire profession feel fraudulent.

Jessa Ardell was different.

Very different.

Arlen had taken the spell-stone message once.

Then listened again while transcribing.

Then a third time to confirm exact wording.

He had not enjoyed the process.

Beringer unfolded the page.

Before he could read, Arlen said, “One observation.”

Beringer looked over the paper at him.

“The speaker is composed.”

“That sounds useful.”

“Yes.”

Arlen hesitated.

“Composed in the way Master Stonefoot’s account books are probably composed.”

Beringer waited.

“She reports coerced servants, manipulated alchemy, two prisoners, probable foreign intelligence activity, and one dead knight.”

Another pause.

“She sounds like she is inventorying linen.”

Beringer looked down.

Arlen had written the message in his clean signal hand:

---

**Lord Marshal,**

**Lady Aylassa has asked that I report directly.**

We have been three days at Silvershire.

The trouble at Larkmeadow is real and organized. Servants have been entered in the household books as paid companions after refusing such service. At least one woman, Annel Rook, is now safely removed from the house and under local protection.

Bailiff Oswin Tallowmere is alive and secured. Dame Ysabet Corven is also secured. Both possess information Lady Aylassa considers more useful than their immediate deaths.

Sir Malrec Thornby attempted to interfere with the inquiry and threaten a witness.

**He is dead.**

There is evidence the house has been used to collect debts, private scandals, political information, and compromising details concerning guests. The alchemical cordials have also been deliberately combined or altered to worsen judgment and memory.

Lady Aylassa believes this exceeds local corruption.

She suspects Dominion involvement.

We have not yet identified the full chain.

She asks that you do nothing publicly with Silvershire until she has followed it farther.

My lady also instructed me to tell you that she is, in her words, **“being extremely responsible.”**

I was not instructed to confirm this.

**Jessa Ardell**

---

Beringer read the page once.

Then again, slower.

Arlen stood waiting.

“Malrec Thornby,” Beringer said.

“Yes, my lord.”

“Dead.”

“Yes.”

“Oswin and Ysabet alive.”

“Yes.”

“Dominion involvement suspected.”

“Yes.”

“And Aylassa wants silence.”

“Yes.”

Beringer stared at the report.

Arlen watched him do it.

Then Beringer looked up.

“Anything else?”

“No, my lord.”

Arlen turned toward the exit.

Made it three steps.

Stopped.

Beringer closed his eyes briefly.

“What?”

Arlen looked back.

“I have now professionally listened to Lady Aylassa and the woman Lady Aylassa apparently selected to accompany her into this investigation.”

Beringer said nothing.

“Lady Aylassa sounds amused while discussing people she may shortly kill.”

“Yes.”

“Jessa Ardell sounds mildly inconvenienced while discussing people already killed.”

Another silence.

Arlen nodded toward the paper.

“I wanted that distinction noted.”

“Why?”

“Because I work in communications.”

Arlen reached the tent flap.

“And I would like both women to continue communicating from somewhere else.”

Then he was gone.
