# Larkmeadow House — Who’s Who

Larkmeadow is a discreet country retreat in the Summer Lawns: affairs, gambling, expensive cordials, private entertainments, and aristocrats exercising their ancient right to make terrible decisions somewhere their spouses cannot see them.

Something has gone wrong here.

Exactly what has gone wrong is less obvious.

---

## Lady Aylassa Silvershire

The absentee owner of Silvershire, lately arrived without warning.

High elf, minor noblewoman, notorious eccentric, and known in certain circles to be considerably more dangerous than her title suggests.

She has spent years treating the holding largely as passive income and an occasional refuge.

She is currently being **very pleasant** about what she is finding.

This has not reassured anyone.

---

## Jessa Ardell

Aylassa’s traveling maid, borrowed from the Sylvanranth household at Taur-Endorath.

Human, composed, impeccably formal, and apparently capable of standing silently in a room until people forget she exists.

She carries luggage, pours wine, notices exits, and gives absolutely no indication why Aylassa chose **this particular maid** for the journey.

---

## Oswin Tallowmere

Bailiff and acting steward of Larkmeadow House.

A narrow, polished administrator who handles rents, accounts, staffing, guest arrangements, and the thousand small decisions an absentee landholder would rather never think about.

He is unfailingly courteous.

He has an answer for everything.

Somehow this is not comforting.

---

## Dame Ysabet Corven

A well-connected gentlewoman and unofficial social fixture at Larkmeadow.

Ysabet knows who is sleeping where, who is feuding with whom, which invitations should never be written down, and which indiscretions require tactful smoothing afterward.

Elegant, socially indispensable, and almost impossible to surprise.

---

## Sir Malrec Thornby

Larkmeadow’s resident security man.

A hard, practical knight who handles troublesome guests, unwanted visitors, late-night disturbances, and anything Oswin prefers not to deal with personally.

He knows every corridor and apparently has keys to most of them.

---

## Harl Brenn

One of Malrec’s heavies.

Not a gentleman, despite spending a great deal of time around them.

Big, watchful, and usually somewhere nearby when Malrec expects difficulty.

Several guests know Malrec rarely handles a genuinely troublesome matter alone.

---

## Sir Cadric Vayneholt

A regular guest with money, charm, and a strong enthusiasm for cards, wagers, dares, horses, and complicated arrangements between friends.

Cadric is the sort of man who remembers exactly what everyone owes him and somehow makes remembering seem convivial.

---

## Lord Perrin Vaust

Wealthy, sociable, generous, and forever discovering promising singers, artists, minor courtiers, and young people who might benefit from his patronage.

He likes being thought of as a benefactor.

He also likes being thanked.

Preferably at length.

---

## Lady Mirabel Vaust

Perrin’s wife.

Cool, composed, observant, and considerably less impressed by her husband than he is.

She has spent enough time around Larkmeadow to distinguish its regular scandals from genuinely bad behavior.

Exactly what she thinks of either is her own business.

---

## Lady Rosienne Caldray

A widowed lesser noblewoman in her early forties.

Warm, witty, experienced, and entirely unashamed of enjoying the freedoms widowhood finally purchased for her.

A regular enough visitor that the servants know how she takes her tea.

Rosienne has the unusual aristocratic habit of treating servants and professional companions as people.

---

## Dame Celestine Arbray

A knight’s widow and minor landholder.

Graceful, private, and noticeably less frivolous than Larkmeadow’s reputation might suggest.

She values the house precisely because people traditionally did **not** ask what happened behind closed doors.

---

## Mira Vell

A professional companion who works the Summer Lawns by choice.

Intelligent, experienced, expensive, and not remotely confused about the nature of her profession.

She knows a remarkable amount about how Larkmeadow actually functions, because people say extraordinary things around women they have decided are furniture.

---

## Mira Voss

Guest-stores and apothecary assistant.

Quiet, efficient, forgettable.

She handles cordials, bath oils, perfumes, linen-water, recovery draughts, and the other small luxuries requested by guests.

Precisely the sort of employee nobody remembers until they need something at two in the morning.

---

## Annel Rook

A young human servant employed for ordinary household work.

Quiet, nervous around some of the guests, and visibly more comfortable among junior staff than around the manor’s senior retainers.

She has recently become difficult to locate.

---

## Sheriff Tomlow Burdock

Sheriff of nearby Sweetbarrow.

Round, genial, hospitable, and possessed of the deeply misleading appearance of somebody’s favorite uncle.

He thinks the Summer Lawns have been economically good for his village.

He is less enthusiastic about some of the people attracted by them.

---

## Mother Hessa Goodhill

Sweetbarrow’s chapel matriarch.

Warm face. Iron spine. Excellent jam.

She does not appear especially scandalized by drinking, affairs, gambling, working women, or adults making fools of themselves.

She does, however, have strong opinions about hospitality and the obligations of a host.

---

# The Halfling Staff

Larkmeadow has always drawn a large portion of its ordinary household staff from nearby Sweetbarrow.

The village has prospered from it.

Cooks, laundresses, gardeners, grooms, serving girls, musicians, drivers, and chamber staff have traditionally made good coin working the Summer Lawns.

Older visitors remember something different from the present arrangement.

There used to be a great many young halfling women working the house.

Cheerful chambermaids.

Pretty serving girls.

Young laundresses and kitchen workers.

Girls from neighboring farms earning seasonal money before marriage or setting up households of their own.

Not anymore.

Nobody seems able to say exactly when the change happened.

The current Sweetbarrow staff are overwhelmingly male.

Older men.

Married men.

Broad-shouldered kitchen lads.

Grooms who work in pairs.

Footmen with calloused hands.

Uncles.

Widowers.

Men who look perfectly capable of carrying a wine tray and equally capable of carrying someone out by the ankles.

They are polite.

Exceptionally polite.

They smile at the guests.

They keep the tables full.

They keep the fires lit.

They also watch hands.

A visitor who remembers the old staffing might reasonably wonder where all the young women went.

Nobody at Larkmeadow seems particularly eager to explain it.
